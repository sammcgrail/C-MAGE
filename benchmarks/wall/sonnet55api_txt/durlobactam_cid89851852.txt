I read the structure as a bicyclic urea (a 1,6-diazabicyclo[3.2.1]octene core) carrying an N-sulfooxy group on one nitrogen.

**Connectivity**
- The sulfate group is S(=O)(=O)(OH) linked through an O to the lower nitrogen, N(a).
- N(a) is bonded to the ring C=O carbon, which is also bonded to the right-hand nitrogen, N(b).
- N(b) is bonded to a CH2 and to the carboxamide-bearing CH.
- That CH2 connects to the bridgehead CH, which is also bonded to N(a).
- The six-membered ring runs N(b), CH(CONH2), C(CH3)=CH, bridgehead CH, CH2. It contains one C=C, and the methyl sits on the carbon next to the CH(CONH2).
- The five-membered ring is N(a), C=O, N(b), CH2, bridgehead CH.

**Stereochemistry**
- Two stereocentres are marked: the CH bearing the carboxamide (wedge to the amide carbon) and the bridgehead CH (hashed H).
- Working through the neighbour order in the SMILES against the drawn wedge and hash, both centres come out as `@@`.

SMILES: NC(=O)[C@@H]1N2C[C@@H](C=C1C)N(OS(=O)(=O)O)C2=O
