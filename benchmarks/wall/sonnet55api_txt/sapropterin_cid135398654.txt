I read this as tetrahydrobiopterin (the 6R, L-erythro form). I assigned the stereochemistry from the wedges and hashes, taking each carbon in turn.

**Skeleton**
- A pyrimidinone ring: C(=O)–NH–C(NH2)=N–C=C. The C=C is the fusion bond.
- A saturated ring fused to it: the fusion carbons, then NH, a stereocentre CH, CH2, and NH.
- A side chain on that stereocentre: CH(OH)–CH(OH)–CH3.

**Stereocentres**
- **Methyl-bearing carbon:** the methyl is on a hashed bond, so it points away from the viewer. This gives S, written `C[C@H](O)...`.
- **Middle carbon:** the OH is on a wedge, so it points toward the viewer. This gives R, written `[C@H](O)`.
- **Ring carbon:** the CH2 is on a wedge. This gives R, written `[C@H]1`.

These agree with the (1'R,2'S) side chain and 6R ring centre of natural tetrahydrobiopterin.

SMILES: C[C@H](O)[C@H](O)[C@H]1CNC2=C(N1)C(=O)NC(N)=N2
