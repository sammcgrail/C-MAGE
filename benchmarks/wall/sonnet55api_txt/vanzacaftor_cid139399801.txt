I read the structure as a macrocyclic acyl sulfonamide built from these pieces:

- **Macrocycle linkage:** an S(=O)(=O)–NH–C(=O) acyl sulfonamide joins a pyridine on the sulfonyl side to a second pyridine on the carbonyl side. The sulfonyl-side pyridine carries an NH–CH2CH2CH2 chain on the carbon next to its ring nitrogen.
- **Pyrrolidine:** the chain ends at a stereogenic CH (drawn with a wedged H) that is part of a pyrrolidine ring. The ring is CH–CH2–C(CH3)2–N–CH2, and its nitrogen is bonded to the carbonyl-side pyridine at the carbon next to the ring N.
- **Pyrazole side chain:** the carbonyl-side pyridine also carries a pyrazol-1-yl group at its other α position. The pyrazole has an O–CH2CH2– substituent at C3, which ends in a dispiro-fused tricyclic carbon framework. A central cyclopropane carries the CH2CH2 attachment on one carbon and a spiro-fused cyclopropane at each of the other two carbons.
- **Stereochemistry:** the wedged H on the pyrrolidine CH is the only stereo element drawn. Neighbour order in the SMILES is chain carbon, H, ring-closure CH2, ring CH2. Viewed from the H side the three carbon neighbours run clockwise, and moving H to the front costs one swap, so the tag is `@`.
