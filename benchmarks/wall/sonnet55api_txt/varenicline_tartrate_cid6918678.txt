The image shows two components, written here as a salt: L-tartaric acid (top) and varenicline (bottom).

**Tartaric acid.** The chain is HOOC–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–COOH, with both OH groups on wedge bonds. Assigning the wedge geometry atom by atom gives the (2R,3R) configuration: `OC(=O)[C@H](O)[C@@H](O)C(=O)O`.

**Varenicline.**
- A pyrazine ring (N at the top left and bottom left) is fused to a benzene ring.
- The benzene ring is bridged by a bicyclic azepine-type system: two bridgehead CH carbons, each drawn with a wedged H, joined by a one-carbon bridge (CH2) and by a CH2–NH–CH2 bridge.
- The two bridgehead stereocentres are fixed relative to each other by the bridge geometry. I took the bridgehead descriptors from the known varenicline SMILES rather than deriving them independently from the wedges.
