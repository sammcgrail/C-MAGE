Reading the drawing from right to left:

- The methyl carbon on the right is bonded to a carbonyl carbon (C=O), which is bonded to an NH. This is an N-acetyl group.
- The NH is bonded to the central stereocentre carbon.
- The stereocentre carries a CH2–SH group on a wedge bond (pointing toward the viewer), so the implicit H points away.
- The stereocentre's remaining substituent is a carbonyl carbon (C=O) bearing an NH2, which is a primary amide.

The molecule is N-acetylcysteinamide.

**Stereochemistry:** The CIP priorities at the stereocentre are N > CH2SH > C(=O)NH2 > H. The CH2SH group outranks the amide carbon because S beats O at the first point of difference. With H pointing away, the sequence N (upper right), CH2SH (bottom), C(=O)NH2 (upper left) runs clockwise, so the centre is R (the L-cysteine configuration).

For the SMILES `CC(=O)N[C@@H](CS)C(N)=O`, the neighbour order at the stereocentre is N, H, CH2S, C(=O). The `@@` tag means H → CH2S → C(=O) runs clockwise when viewed from N. Moving H to the front is one swap, giving `@`, which corresponds to R. This matches the drawing.
