I read the drawing as a 3-methyl-1,2,4-thiadiazol-5-yl group attached to the triazole of a fused triazolo-piperazine. The piperazine nitrogen carries a 4-fluorobenzoyl group, and the adjacent ring carbon bears a hashed methyl.

**Reading the drawing**
- **Left ring:** a five-membered ring with the atoms C(CH3)=N–S–C=N, which is a 3-methyl-1,2,4-thiadiazole. Its carbon next to S is the one bonded to the triazole.
- **Middle fused system:** a 1,2,4-triazole fused to a saturated six-membered ring. The six-membered ring contains the triazole N and carbon at the fusion, then CH2, CH2, the amide N, and a CH(CH3).
- **Right side:** the amide N is acylated by C(=O) attached to a benzene ring bearing F in the para position.

**Stereochemistry**
- Only the CH(CH3) carbon has a wedge/hash, and its methyl is drawn hashed (pointing away from the viewer).
- Its neighbours are the amide N (upper right), the triazole carbon (upper left), the methyl (down, hashed) and an implicit H pointing toward the viewer.
- Viewed from the H side, N → triazole C → CH3 runs counterclockwise.
- In the SMILES neighbour order N, H, triazole C, CH3 this corresponds to `@@`, so the centre is written `[C@@H]`.
