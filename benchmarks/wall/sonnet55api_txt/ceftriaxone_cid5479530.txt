I read this as a cephalosporin skeleton with a methoxyimino aminothiazole side chain, which is the ceftriaxone structure. The SMILES below follows the drawing region by region.

**Core.** The bicyclic β-lactam–dihydrothiazine has a carboxylic acid on the ring double-bond carbon. A CH2–S– linker leaves the neighbouring ring carbon.

**Triazine ring.** The linker sulfur attaches to a six-membered ring: C(=N–C(=O)–C(=O)–NH–N(CH3)–). This is a 1-methyl-5,6-dioxo-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-3-yl group.

**Acyl side chain.** The β-lactam NH is acylated by C(=O)–C(=N–OCH3), which carries a 2-aminothiazol-4-yl group.

**Oxime geometry.** On the drawing, the OMe oxygen sits on the same side of the C=N as the amide carbonyl and opposite the thiazole. That makes it Z, written as `CO/N=C(\C(=O)...)/Th`.

**Ring-fusion stereochemistry.**
- At C7 (the carbon bearing NH), the hashed bond puts N behind the plane. With neighbour order N, H, C8, C6 this gives `[C@H]`.
- At C6 (the carbon bearing S), the wedged H points toward the viewer. With neighbour order C7, H, N, S this gives `[C@H]`.
