The drawing is a 1,4-diene-3-one steroid (ring A on the right) with 9-fluoro, 11-hydroxy, 17-hydroxy, a 17-hydroxyacetyl side chain (C(=O)CH2OH), and methyl groups at C10, C13 and C16.

The ring system is drawn mirror-flipped relative to the usual steroid layout (ring A on the right, ring D on the left). That reverses the usual wedge/hash reading, so I read the stereochemistry in the flipped frame:

- The C13 and C10 methyls are hashed, which corresponds to β.
- 17-OH, 9-F and the C16 methyl are wedged, which corresponds to α.
- 11-OH and 8-H are hashed (β), and 14-H is wedged (α).

A 16α-methyl with 9α-fluoro and 11β-hydroxy is the pattern of dexamethasone, not its 16β epimer betamethasone. I wrote the SMILES from this assignment, matching it to the known dexamethasone stereochemistry rather than deriving each centre's @/@@ from scratch.
