I read this as a glucopyranose-type ring joined through a glycosidic oxygen to a six-carbon open-chain polyol (a hexitol, as in maltitol or lactitol). I did not try to name the sugar from the drawing.

**Ring (left):** a six-membered ring with five carbons and one ring oxygen.
- The anomeric carbon carries the glycosidic oxygen on a hashed bond.
- The next carbon has its OH on a wedge.
- The following two carbons each have an OH on a hashed bond.
- The carbon next to the ring oxygen carries a CH2OH on a hashed bond.

**Chain (right):** six carbons, all bearing OH.
- The first carbon carries the glycosidic oxygen on a hashed bond.
- Going up the chain, the next carbon has its OH on a hash and ends in a CH2OH.
- Going down the chain, the next two carbons have their OH groups on wedges, and the chain ends in a CH2OH.

I assigned each @/@@ by working out the screen orientation of the neighbours at every stereocentre, with the implicit H taken as pointing opposite the wedge or hash.
