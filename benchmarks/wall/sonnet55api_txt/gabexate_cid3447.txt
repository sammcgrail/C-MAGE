This is the structure of gabexate, and I read it region by region.

- **Ethyl ester:** an ethyl group (CH3–CH2) is attached to an oxygen, which is attached to a C=O group.
- **Aromatic ring:** that carbonyl carbon is bonded to a benzene ring. The ring is para-substituted, with the other substituent being an oxygen.
- **Second ester:** this ring oxygen is bonded to a C(=O) group, which continues into a chain.
- **Chain:** counting the carbons after the carbonyl carbon, there are 4 CH2 groups. The chain runs from the carbonyl carbon at (730, 700) through CH2 at (818, 670), CH2 at (890, 727), CH2 at (978, 697), and CH2 at (1050, 752), then a final CH2 at (1140, 720) that attaches to N. That makes 5 CH2 groups in total, so the acid is a 6-carbon acid, hexanoate.
- **Guanidine end:** the last CH2 is bonded to N, which is double-bonded to C(N)N, so the terminus is N=C(N)N.

Writing the SMILES from the ethyl end: CCOC(=O)c1ccc(cc1)OC(=O)CCCCCN=C(N)N. The chain after C(=O) has 5 carbons (CCCCC) before N, which matches the count above.
