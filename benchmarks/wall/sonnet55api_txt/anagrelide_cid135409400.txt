This drawing matches the structure of quazinone-type compounds (anagrelide). Anagrelide is 6,7-dichloro-1,5-dihydroimidazo[2,1-b]quinazolin-2-one.

Reading the drawing atom by atom:
- The carbonyl carbon (C=O) bonds to NH and to a CH2.
- That CH2 bonds to the ring N.
- The ring N bonds to the amidine carbon and to a second CH2, which attaches to the aromatic ring.
- The NH bonds to the amidine carbon, which is double-bonded to the N=.
- That N= attaches to an aromatic carbon.
- The benzene ring carries two adjacent Cl atoms. One Cl is on the carbon next to the carbon bearing the CH2, and the other is on the following carbon.

SMILES: O=C1CN2Cc3c(ccc(Cl)c3Cl)N=C2N1

Checking the ring: c3 (attached to CH2), then c(...) bearing N=, which is the other fusion carbon. The path "Cc3c(ccc(Cl)c3Cl)N=C2N1" places the first aromatic atom c3 as the CH2-bearing carbon. The next atom, c(, is the N-bearing carbon, and its branch runs c, c, c(Cl), c3Cl. That makes the ring c3, c(N), c, c, c(Cl), c(Cl) closing back to c3, which is six atoms. The Cl-bearing carbon c3Cl is adjacent to the CH2-bearing carbon, which matches the drawing.
