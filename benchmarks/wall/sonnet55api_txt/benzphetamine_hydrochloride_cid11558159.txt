The image shows benzphetamine hydrochloride. I read it region by region:

- **Right side:** a phenyl ring joined to a CH2, which is joined to the stereocentre.
- **Stereocentre:** it carries a methyl on a hashed bond (pointing away from the viewer), the CH2 toward the upper right, the nitrogen toward the left, and an implicit H.
- **Nitrogen:** tertiary, bearing an N-methyl and a benzyl group (CH2 joined to the left phenyl ring).
- **Counterion:** HCl, written as a separate component.

For the stereochemistry, the hashed methyl means the implicit H points toward the viewer. Seen from the front, N → CH2 → CH3 runs clockwise. Seen from the side opposite H, as the CIP rules require, it runs counterclockwise. With priorities N > CH2Ph > CH3 > H, this gives the S configuration. That matches the known (S)-configuration of benzphetamine. In SMILES, with neighbour order CH3, H, CH2, N, this is written `@@`.

SMILES: C[C@@H](Cc1ccccc1)N(C)Cc1ccccc1.Cl
