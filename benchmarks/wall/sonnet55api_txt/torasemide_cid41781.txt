This is torsemide, a pyridine sulfonylurea. I read it region by region.

- **Pyridine ring:** The ring nitrogen is at the top. Going around the ring, N connects to a CH, then to the carbon bearing the sulfonyl group (C3). Next is the carbon bearing the NH-aryl group (C4), then two CH carbons that close the ring back to N.
- **Sulfonylurea chain:** The sulfonyl sulfur carries two =O groups and is bonded to NH. That NH connects to C(=O), then to NH, then to an isopropyl group.
- **Aryl group:** The pyridine C4 is bonded to an NH, which attaches to a benzene ring. The benzene has a methyl group meta to the NH attachment. Tracing the ring: the carbon attached to NH (call it a1) is bonded to a CH (a2), then to the carbon bearing the methyl (a3), then through three more ring carbons back to a1. The methyl is therefore at the meta position, a 3-methylphenyl group.

SMILES: Cc1cccc(Nc2ccncc2S(=O)(=O)NC(=O)NC(C)C)c1
