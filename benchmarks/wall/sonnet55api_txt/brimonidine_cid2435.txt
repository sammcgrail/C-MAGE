This is brimonidine, a quinoxaline bearing an imidazoline ring.

**Imidazoline ring (left):** The ring is CH2–CH2–N=C(–NH)–NH, closed back to the first CH2. The carbon on the right of the ring is double-bonded to the ring N at the lower left and single-bonded to the ring NH at the top. It also carries an exocyclic NH that links to the aromatic ring.

**Quinoxaline (right):**
- The benzene ring carbon attached to the exocyclic NH is adjacent to the carbon bearing Br.
- That Br-bearing carbon is bonded to the ring-fusion carbon, which is bonded to the lower N.
- The pyrazine ring runs N–CH=CH–N between the two fusion carbons.
- The upper fusion carbon connects to the CH=CH part of the benzene ring.

**SMILES:** Starting from the Br carbon: Brc1c(NC2=NCCN2)ccc2nccnc12. Checking the ring closures:
- c1 is the Br carbon.
- c(NC2=NCCN2) is the carbon bearing the amino group.
- cc are the two benzene CH atoms.
- c2 is the upper fusion carbon.
- n, c, c, n are the pyrazine atoms.
- c12 is the lower fusion carbon, bonded to the Br carbon and closing ring 2.
