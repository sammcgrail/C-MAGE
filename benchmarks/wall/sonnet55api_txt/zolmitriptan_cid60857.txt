I read this as zolmitriptan, a tryptamine-type molecule, and worked through it region by region.

- **Indole core:** The fused six- and five-membered rings carry an N-H, with the nitrogen in the five-membered ring.
- **Side chain on the five-membered ring:** A two-carbon chain ends in N(CH3)2, a dimethylamino group.
- **Substituent on the benzene ring:** A CH2 group is attached through a wedge bond to a stereocentre. That carbon belongs to a five-membered oxazolidin-2-one ring: C(H)–CH2–O–C(=O)–NH, closing back onto the stereocentre.
- **Stereochemistry:** The stereocentre has the following substituents.
  - NH has priority 1.
  - The CH2–O carbon has priority 2.
  - The CH2–aryl carbon has priority 3, and it is on the wedge.
  - H has priority 4 and points away from the viewer.
  - Viewed from the front, going from NH (lower left) to CH2O (lower right) to CH2Ar (up) is counterclockwise, so the centre is S.
  - In the SMILES `C[C@H]3COC(=O)N3`, the neighbour order is CH2Ar, H, N (ring closure), CH2O. With `@` this gives S, which matches the drawing.
