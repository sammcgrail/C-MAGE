I read the drawing as a 1-methylindole joined through its 3-position to a ketone C=O. The carbonyl carbon is attached to a CH carbon on a tetrahydrobenzimidazole ring. This matches ramosetron.

- **Indole part:** an N-methyl indole (N bearing CH3), with the carbonyl attached at C3.
- **Ketone:** C(=O) links the indole C3 to the stereocentre, and the wedge bond is drawn on this C–C(=O) bond.
- **Left bicycle:** a six-membered carbocycle fused to an imidazole. The imidazole has one =N– (top) and one NH (bottom). The ring carbons are CH(stereocentre)–CH2–CH2–C(=C)–…–C–CH2.
- **Stereochemistry:** viewed from the front, with the implicit H pointing away, the carbonyl carbon, the upper CH2 and the lower CH2 run counterclockwise. Writing the neighbours in SMILES order (carbonyl C, H, ring-closure CH2, upper CH2) gives `@@`.

The imidazole ring is aromatic in this SMILES, with `[nH]` at the bottom nitrogen.

SMILES: Cn1cc(C(=O)[C@@H]2CCc3nc[nH]c3C2)c2ccccc21
