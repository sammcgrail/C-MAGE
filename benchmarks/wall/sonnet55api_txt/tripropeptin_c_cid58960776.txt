I read the structure as a cyclic lipopeptide: a branched C12 fatty acid, 3-hydroxylated, whose hydroxyl forms the ring ester. The ring closes through an ester to the C-terminal residue. Stereochemistry is omitted because the wedges and hashes cannot be read reliably.

**Lipid tail:** an iso-branched chain, `CC(C)` followed by nine CH2 groups, then the CH that carries the ring ester oxygen. A CH2 and a C(=O) follow, and that carbonyl is the acyl link to the first amide NH. The tail is therefore 3-hydroxy-11-methyldodecanoic acid.

**Ring, going around from the lipid carbonyl:**
1. Threonine: NH–CH(CH(OH)CH3)–C(=O)
2. Proline: N-acylated pyrrolidine
3. Proline: a second N-acylated pyrrolidine
4. Arginine: NH–CH with the side chain CH2CH2CH2–N=C(NH2)NH2
5. 3-Hydroxyaspartate: NH–CH(CH(OH)COOH)
6. Serine: NH–CH(CH2OH)
7. 3-Hydroxyproline: the ring N is acylated, and the OH is on the carbon two atoms from the alpha carbon
8. 3-Hydroxyaspartate: NH–CH(CH(OH)COOH)–C(=O)–O, which closes the ring ester back to the lipid CH
