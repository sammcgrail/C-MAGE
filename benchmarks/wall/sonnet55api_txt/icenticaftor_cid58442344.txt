I read the structure as a pyridine ring carrying four substituents, plus an amide side chain ending in a stereogenic carbinol carbon.

**Ring.** Going around the pyridine ring:
- The ring nitrogen sits next to a carbon bearing OMe.
- Next is a carbon bearing CF3.
- Then comes a CH.
- Then a carbon bearing NH2.
- The last ring carbon carries the carboxamide, C(=O)NH.

**Side chain.** The amide nitrogen connects through CH2 to a quaternary carbon. That carbon bears OH, CH3 (drawn as a wedge) and CF3.

**Stereochemistry.** The wedged methyl points toward the viewer. The other three substituents are OH (upper left), CH2 (lower left) and CF3 (lower right). Viewed from the methyl side, OH → CH2 → CF3 runs counterclockwise, so the centre is written `C[C@](O)(CN…)C(F)(F)F` with methyl first. Rewriting with the CH2 neighbour first (`NC[C@](C)(O)C(F)(F)F`) is an even permutation of the neighbour order, so the `@` is unchanged.

SMILES: COc1nc(C(=O)NC[C@](C)(O)C(F)(F)F)c(N)cc1C(F)(F)F
