I read the structure as two substituted benzene rings joined by a thioether–propyl–ether linker.

**Left ring.** Going around the ring:
- C1 carries the acetyl group, CC(=O).
- C2 carries OH.
- C3 carries a propyl group.
- C4 carries S.
- The two remaining positions are CH.

**Linker.** S–CH2CH2CH2–O, a three-carbon chain from the sulfur to the oxygen on the right ring.

**Right ring.** Going around the ring:
- The ring carbon bonded to the linker O (call it Ca) is adjacent to the carbon bearing the propyl group (Cb).
- Cb is adjacent to the carbon bearing the O–CH2CH2CH2–COOH chain (Cc).
- Cc is adjacent to the carbon bearing the acetyl group (Cd).
- Cd is adjacent to a CH, then another CH, which closes back to Ca.

**SMILES assembly.** Starting from the left acetyl:

CC(=O)c1ccc(SCCCOc2ccc(C(C)=O)c(OCCCC(=O)O)c2CCC)c(CCC)c1O

Checking the right ring against this string: c2 is Ca (bonded to the linker O), then c, c, then c(C(C)=O) is Cd, then c(OCCCC(=O)O) is Cc, then c2CCC is Cb, which closes the ring to Ca. This matches the drawing.

Checking the left ring: c1 (acetyl) c c c(S…) c(CCC) c1O. The ring runs C1(acetyl), CH, CH, C(S), C(propyl), C(OH) back to C1, which is correct.
