The drawing shows a sodium salt (Na+) of a sulfonate (S with two =O and one O−).

The sulfonate sulfur is attached to a quaternary carbon that carries a methyl group. That carbon is bonded to a C=O carbon and to a CH2 group. The CH2 is bonded to a second C=O carbon. Both carbonyl carbons are fused to a benzene ring. This gives a 2-methyl-1,4-dioxo-tetralin-2-sulfonate, which is the menadione sodium bisulfite structure.

The ring is C2(CH3)(SO3−), C1(=O), the aromatic fusion carbons, C4(=O), and C3H2. The aromatic ring has six carbons, two of which are the fusion carbons.
