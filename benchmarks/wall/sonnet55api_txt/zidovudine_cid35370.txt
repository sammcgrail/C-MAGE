The drawing shows zidovudine (AZT), a thymine nucleoside analogue.

- **Base:** a thymine ring with a methyl on the ring carbon, two C=O groups and one N–H. Its N1 is attached to the sugar.
- **Sugar:** a five-membered ring made of one oxygen and four carbons. The carbon next to the ring oxygen that bears CH2OH has a wedge bond. The anomeric carbon bonded to the thymine nitrogen also has a wedge bond. The ring CH2 sits between the anomeric carbon and the azide-bearing carbon.
- **Azide:** N=[N+]=[N-] on the ring carbon drawn with a hashed bond. This is the 3′ position, so the 3′-OH is replaced by azide.
- **Stereochemistry:** the base and CH2OH are both wedged, so they are on the same face (β), and the azide is hashed on the opposite face. This is the natural D-configuration pattern and matches the known stereo-SMILES for AZT, which I used.
