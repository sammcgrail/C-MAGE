The image shows two separate species.

- On the left is a lithium atom with a dot, which is a lithium radical: [Li].
- On the right is an isopropyl skeleton: a central carbon with three bonds to carbons, one up, one lower left and one lower right. Hmm, the central carbon carries a dot, so it is a radical. Its three bonds are the two methyl groups plus the upward carbon. That gives a central carbon bonded to three methyl groups, with one unpaired electron: the tert-butyl radical.

Counting the atoms: the vertical line goes up to a terminal carbon, and the two lower lines each go to a terminal carbon. The central carbon therefore has three carbon neighbors and one radical dot, so it is C(C)(C)C with a radical, written [C](C)(C)C.

The two pieces are drawn as separate components, so I write them with a dot between them:

SMILES: [Li].C[C](C)C
