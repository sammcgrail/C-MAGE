I read this as a 14-membered macrolactone (erythromycin-type) carrying two sugars and a spiro-epoxide. I assigned each @/@@ from the wedge and hash bonds in the drawing, but I did not check the result with a toolkit.

**Macrolactone ring.** Going round from the lactone C=O:
- The lactone carbonyl carbon bonds to a CH(CH3), then to a CH carrying the cladinose-type sugar through oxygen.
- Next come a CH(CH3), then a CH carrying the desosamine-type sugar through oxygen, then another CH(CH3) and a CH2.
- A spiro carbon follows, bearing an epoxide (CH2–O).
- Then a ketone C=O, a CH(CH3), a CH(OH), a CH(CH3) and finally a CH(CH3) bonded to the ring oxygen that closes the lactone.

**Cladinose-type sugar** (right side), a six-membered ring: an anomeric carbon bearing the glycosidic oxygen, a CH2, a CH–OCH3, a CH–OH, a CH–CH3, and the ring oxygen.

**Desosamine-type sugar** (upper left), a six-membered ring: an anomeric carbon bearing the glycosidic oxygen, a CH–OH, a CH–N(CH3)2, a CH2, a CH–CH3, and the ring oxygen.
