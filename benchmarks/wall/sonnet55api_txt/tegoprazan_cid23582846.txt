I read the molecule as a 2-methylbenzimidazole core, a chroman ring and a dimethylamide, joined as follows.

- **Benzimidazole core:** it carries a methyl at C2 and an NH, shown as the N–H drawn on the left. On the benzo ring, one carbon next to the ring fusion bears the ether oxygen, and the carbon two positions further round bears the C(=O)N(CH3)2 amide.
- **Chroman ring:** the ether oxygen links to C4 of a 5,7-difluorochroman. C4 is the stereocentre, followed in the ring by CH2, CH2, O, then the fused difluorobenzene ring with fluorines at C5 and C7.
- **Stereochemistry:** the only stereo bond is the hashed one from C4 to the ether oxygen, so the oxygen points away from the viewer and the implicit H points toward the viewer. On the page the neighbours run O (up-left), aromatic carbon (right), CH2 (down-left), which is clockwise when seen from the H side. In the SMILES order (O, H, aromatic C, CH2) this corresponds to `@`.

SMILES: Cc1nc2c(O[C@H]3CCOc4cc(F)cc(F)c43)cc(C(=O)N(C)C)cc2[nH]1
