I read the drawing as a conjugated diene, CH3–CH=C(Ar)–C(Ar)=CH–CH3, where each Ar is a 4-hydroxyphenyl ring. This is the dienestrol skeleton.

- **Aryl rings:** Each ring has six carbons with an OH on the carbon para to the attachment point to the chain.
- **Core:** The two central carbons are joined by a single bond, and each carries one aryl ring.
- **Double bonds:** Each central carbon is double-bonded to a CH that ends in a CH3.
- **Geometry:** From the coordinates, on both double bonds the terminal CH3 and the aryl ring on the neighbouring carbon lie on the same side. The other central carbon is therefore on the opposite side from each CH3.

I encoded this as `C/C=C(\Ar)/C(=C/C)Ar`. I checked that the bond marks agree: the CH3 and the other central carbon are trans on the left double bond, and the same holds on the right one.
