The drawing shows econazole, built from these parts:

- An imidazole ring attached through N1 to a CH2 group.
- That CH2 is bonded to a CH carbon, which carries the ether oxygen and a 2,4-dichlorophenyl ring.
- The oxygen is bonded to a CH2 that leads to a 4-chlorophenyl ring (a 4-chlorobenzyl ether).

The CH carbon is a stereocentre by connectivity, but the drawing shows only plain bonds, so I give no stereo marks.

Writing the SMILES from the imidazole outward: Clc1ccc(COC(Cn2ccnc2)c2ccc(Cl)cc2Cl)cc1.

Atom check on the dichlorophenyl ring: the carbon attached to the CH is the ring carbon at about (1070, 797). Moving around the ring, the carbon at (1033, 946) carries a Cl, which is ortho. The next carbon at (1141, 1051) has no substituent. The carbon at (1288, 1011) carries a Cl, which is para to the attachment point. So the ring is 2,4-dichloro. In the SMILES, c2ccc(Cl)cc2Cl starts at the attachment carbon, then goes through c, c, c(Cl), c, and ends on c2Cl, the ortho carbon. That puts the Cl atoms at the para and ortho positions, as drawn.

The imidazole is N1 with a CH2 substituent. Going round the ring: N1 to C2 (the carbon between the two nitrogens), then N3, then C4 and C5. This gives n2cncc2, which is equivalent to the form I wrote above. In the drawing, N(left) connects to the CH at the bottom (1265, 597), which connects to N(right), then to the top-right CH, then the top-left CH, and back to N(left). So the ring is n2cncc2. The earlier form, n2ccnc2, would place the two nitrogens next to a shared carbon in a different order, so I use n2cncc2.
