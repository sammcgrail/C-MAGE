The drawing shows a steroid skeleton (four fused rings, 17 ring carbons), and I matched it to boldenone by reading it region by region:

- **Ring A:** a cross-conjugated dienone, with C1=C2, a C3 ketone (C=O) and C4=C5.
- **C10:** a hashed methyl group, which is the 19-methyl.
- **C13:** a hashed methyl group, which is the 18-methyl.
- **C17:** a hashed OH on the five-membered ring D.
- **Ring-fusion hydrogens:** a wedge H at C9, a hashed H at C8 and a wedge H at C14.

The molecule is drawn mirrored left to right (ring A on the right, ring D on the left), which swaps wedges and hashes. Once that is accounted for, every stereocentre matches the natural configuration: 8β-H, 9α-H, 14α-H, 10β-methyl, 13β-methyl and 17β-OH. This is 17β-hydroxyandrosta-1,4-dien-3-one.

I did not derive the @/@@ marks atom by atom from the drawing. I wrote the SMILES from the standard testosterone SMILES, changed ring A to the 1,4-dien-3-one, and relied on that to carry the natural stereochemistry.
