I read this as the testosterone propionate skeleton, drawn with every stereocentre inverted relative to natural testosterone propionate.

**Skeleton**
- The left side is a propionate ester: CH3-CH2-C(=O)-O- attached to C17 of the five-membered ring.
- The steroid core is the usual four-ring system. The right-hand ring is a cyclohexenone with a C3 ketone and a C4=C5 double bond. A methyl sits on C10 and another on C13.
- Counting the vertices gives the 19 core carbons of the steroid plus the 3-carbon propionyl group.

**Stereochemistry as drawn**
- The C17–O bond, both methyls (C10 and C13) and the H on C8 are hashed.
- The H on C9 and the H on C14 are wedged.
- In natural testosterone propionate, with this same ring orientation, the O and methyls are wedged and the C9 and C14 hydrogens are hashed. Every stereo bond here is therefore the opposite of the natural compound.
