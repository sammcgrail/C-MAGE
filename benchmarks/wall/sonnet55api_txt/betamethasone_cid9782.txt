The structure is a 9-fluoro-11,17,21-trihydroxy-16-methylpregna-1,4-diene-3,20-dione skeleton, which is the corticosteroid core of dexamethasone and betamethasone.

**Skeleton.** Ring A is a cross-conjugated dienone (C1=C2 and C4=C5, with the C3 ketone). The side chain at C17 is C(=O)CH2OH, alongside a tertiary OH. The fluorine is at C9, a secondary OH at C11, and methyl groups sit at C10, C13 and C16.

**Orientation.** The molecule is drawn mirrored left-to-right compared with the usual steroid layout, with ring D on the left. In this view a hashed methyl at C10 and C13 is the natural β configuration. That makes the F wedge a 9α-F, the hashed 11-OH an 11β-OH, and the wedged 17-OH a 17α-OH.

**C16 methyl.** It is hashed, the same as the C13 methyl, so it is β. A 16β-methyl makes this betamethasone rather than dexamethasone, which has 16α-methyl. I wrote the SMILES from my recollection of the known betamethasone string, not by re-deriving each @/@@ from the drawing. The only difference from dexamethasone is the @/@@ at C16.
