The drawing shows an anthraquinone core: two benzene rings joined through a central ring that carries a C=O at the top and a C=O at the bottom.

There is an OH on each benzene ring. Each one sits on the carbon next to the ring-fusion carbon that is bonded to the top carbonyl carbon. Both OH groups are therefore peri to the top C=O, which makes this 1,8-dihydroxyanthraquinone (danthron).

Writing the SMILES: O=C1c2c(O)cccc2C(=O)c2cccc(O)c12

Atom-by-atom check:
- C1 is the top carbonyl carbon, written O=C1.
- c2 is the fusion carbon on the left ring. The next atom, c(O), is the carbon bearing the OH. The ring then continues ccc, then c2, which is the fusion carbon bonded to the bottom carbonyl.
- C(=O) is the bottom carbonyl.
- The right ring starts at its fusion carbon c2, runs through ccc, then c(O), and closes with c12, the fusion carbon bonded to C1.
- The right-ring OH carbon is adjacent to the fusion carbon that closes to C1, which is the top carbonyl. Both OH groups are therefore on the top-carbonyl side, as drawn.
