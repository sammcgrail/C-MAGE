The drawing shows an anthraquinone core: two benzene rings fused to a central ring that carries two C=O groups, one at the top and one at the bottom.

Each OH sits on a ring carbon adjacent to the ring-fusion carbon that bonds to the top carbonyl. Both OH groups are therefore on the same side as the top C=O, which makes this 1,8-dihydroxyanthraquinone (danthron).

Writing the SMILES from the top carbonyl:
- Top carbonyl carbon: O=C1
- Right ring-fusion carbon, then the right ring: c2c(O)cccc2
- Bottom carbonyl: C(=O)
- Left ring-fusion carbon, then the left ring: c2cccc(O)c12

Full SMILES: O=C1c2c(O)cccc2C(=O)c2cccc(O)c12

Checking the connectivity: C1 (top carbonyl) bonds to the right ring-fusion carbon c2, which bonds to c(O), then ccc, then c2 (the other fusion carbon on that ring). That carbon bonds to the bottom C(=O), which bonds to the left ring-fusion carbon c2. The left ring then runs cccc(O), and c12 closes both the left ring and the bond back to the top carbonyl. The OH on the left ring is on the carbon adjacent to the fusion carbon that bonds to the top carbonyl, as in the drawing.

SMILES: O=C1c2c(O)cccc2C(=O)c2cccc(O)c12
