I read the molecule as four parts joined together.

- **Isoxazole:** the ring runs O–N=C–C=C. Its C3 carries the NH, so it is an isoxazol-3-yl group, written `c2ccon2`.
- **Linker:** the NH connects to a CH2, which is attached by a hashed bond to C5 of the oxazolidinone.
- **Oxazolidin-2-one:** the ring runs O, C(=O), N, CH2, CH. The ring nitrogen is bonded to the aryl ring.
- **Aryl ring:** it has three fluorines and one CH. Going round from the carbon bearing the oxazolidinone N, the substituents are CH, F, N (the dihydropyridinone), F, F. The dihydropyridinone is N1–CH=CH–C(=O)–CH2–CH2, i.e. `N2C=CC(=O)CC2`.

For the stereocentre at C5, the substituents are the ring O (up), the ring CH2 (lower right), and the CH2NH (lower left, hashed, pointing away). The implicit H therefore points towards the viewer. From the H side, O → ring CH2 → CH2N runs clockwise. Reordering to the SMILES neighbour order (O, H, CH2N, ring CH2) is an even permutation, so the centre is written `[C@@H]`.

SMILES: O=C1O[C@@H](CNc2ccon2)CN1c1cc(F)c(N2C=CC(=O)CC2)c(F)c1F
