This is milrinone: a 2-pyridone ring carrying a methyl, a 4-pyridyl group and a nitrile.

**Pyridone ring** (six atoms):
- C(=O) at the lower right, bonded to the ring NH.
- The NH is bonded to the C bearing the methyl.
- That methyl-bearing C is double-bonded to the C bearing the pyridyl group.
- The pyridyl-bearing C is bonded to a CH.
- The CH is double-bonded to the C bearing C#N.
- The C#N-bearing C closes the ring back to the C=O.

**Pyridyl substituent:** a 4-pyridyl ring attached through its para carbon, with the ring N at the far left.

Reading the pyridyl ring from the attachment carbon (522,739): it connects to the carbon at (462,529), then to (250,477), then to N (100,635), then to (158,844), then to (371,896), and back to the attachment carbon. The ring N is therefore para to the attachment point, which makes it a 4-pyridyl.

SMILES: Cc1[nH]c(=O)c(C#N)cc1-c1ccncc1
