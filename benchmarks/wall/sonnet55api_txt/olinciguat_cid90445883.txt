I read the molecule as a 2-fluorobenzyl-substituted pyrazole carrying an isoxazol-3-yl group, joined to a fluoropyrimidine that bears a chiral hydroxy-trifluoromethyl amide side chain.

1. **Benzyl ring:** a benzene ring with one fluorine ortho to a CH2 that is bonded to a pyrazole nitrogen.
2. **Pyrazole:** N1 carries the 2-fluorobenzyl group. C5, next to N1, carries an isoxazol-3-yl group, written `c1ccon1` so that the attachment carbon sits next to N. C3 is bonded to the pyrimidine.
3. **Pyrimidine:** C2 is bonded to the pyrazole. C4 carries an NH and C5 carries F.
4. **Side chain:** NH–CH2–C(OH)(CF3)–C(=O)NH2. The OH is drawn on a wedge.
5. **Stereochemistry:** with the OH toward the viewer, the CH2 (left), CF3 (up) and C(=O)NH2 (right) run clockwise as seen from the OH side. That gives `@@` for the neighbour order OH, CH2, CF3, C(=O). Rewriting in the SMILES order CH2, OH, CF3, C(=O) swaps the first two neighbours, which flips this to `@`.

SMILES: NC(=O)[C@](O)(C(F)(F)F)CNc1nc(-c2cc(-c3ccon3)n(Cc3ccccc3F)n2)ncc1F
