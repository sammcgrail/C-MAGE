I read the molecule as a symmetric bis-urea. Each end is a 6,8-dichloro-2-methyl-tetrahydroisoquinolin-4-yl group attached at the meta position of a benzenesulfonamide. The two ends are linked through the following chain:

- sulfonamide NH, then CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2
- NH–C(=O)–NH
- (CH2)4
- NH–C(=O)–NH
- CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2, then the second sulfonamide NH

**Stereochemistry:** each tetrahydroisoquinoline C4 carries a wedge or hash to the phenyl ring. The left end uses a hashed bond and the right end a bold bond, but the right end is also the mirror image of the left, so the two flips cancel and both centres have the same configuration.

I assigned CIP priorities at C4 as C3 (attached to N) > C4a > phenyl > H. On the left, the hashed phenyl points away, so H points toward the viewer. Tracing C3, C4a, phenyl then runs clockwise on the page, and with H toward the viewer that reverses to counterclockwise, giving S. Both centres are therefore S. In the SMILES below, this corresponds to `[C@@H]` when the neighbours are written in the order phenyl, H, ring closure to C4a, C3.
