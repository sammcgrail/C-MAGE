I read the structure as a 28-membered macrolactone ring (a filipin-type polyol with a conjugated pentaene), and wrote the SMILES by walking around the ring from the lactone carbonyl.

**Ring and substituents**
- The lactone is C(=O)–O, and the ring oxygen is attached to a CH bearing a wedged methyl.
- Next is a CH(OH) with a hashed OH, then the pentaene.
- The five C=C bonds are all drawn trans. The last one, C(Me)=CH, has its methyl on the side opposite the polyol chain.
- After the pentaene comes a run of contiguous CH(OH) centres and CH₂ groups: three adjacent CH(OH), then CH₂ / CH(OH) repeated five times, ending at the CH(OH) next to the ring carbon that carries the side chain.
- That ring carbon bears a CH(OH) (hashed OH) followed by a straight pentyl chain, making a 1-hydroxyhexyl side chain.

**Stereochemistry**
I assigned each centre from its wedge or hash and the 2D positions of its neighbours. Neighbours are taken in SMILES order with the implicit H opposite the wedge (or on the viewer's side for a hash). The ring carbon that bears the side chain has a hashed bond to the carbonyl carbon, which I treated as placing its H toward the viewer.
