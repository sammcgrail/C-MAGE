The drawing shows a benzene ring with three substituents.

- **Ester side:** the ring carbon at the upper right carries a C(=O)O group, an ester. The ester oxygen is bonded to CH2CH2N(CH2CH3)2, a 2-(diethylamino)ethyl group.
- **Amino group:** the ring carbon at the upper left carries NH2.
- **Butoxy group:** the ring carbon at the lower left carries an O-butyl group (OCCCC). I counted the chain from the oxygen: O to CH2, CH2, CH2, then the terminal CH3, which makes four carbons.

The ring positions relative to the ester carbon (C1, upper right) are:
- C2, the top-right vertex, is CH.
- C3, the top-left vertex, is CH.
- C4, the left vertex, carries NH2.
- C5, the bottom-left vertex, carries the butoxy group.
- C6, the bottom-right vertex, is CH.

The ester carbon sits on the right vertex of the ring. The ring runs from the C(=O) carbon to the CH at the bottom right, then to the carbon bearing OBu, then to the carbon bearing NH2, then to two CH carbons at the top. So the amino group is para to the ester and the butoxy group is meta to it.

This corresponds to oxybuprocaine (benoxinate).

SMILES: CCCCOc1cc(C(=O)OCCN(CC)CC)ccc1N
