The drawing is a six-membered ring with one nitrogen (labelled N) and five carbons, which is pyridine.

Going around the ring from N:
- N to the upper carbon: single bond.
- Upper carbon to the next carbon along the top: double bond (inner line drawn along the top edge).
- That carbon to the right-hand carbon: single bond.
- Right-hand carbon to the lower-right carbon: double bond (inner line on the right-hand lower side).
- Lower-right carbon to the lower-left carbon: single bond.
- Lower-left carbon to N: double bond (inner line drawn along the N–C bond at the bottom left).

This gives the Kekulé form C1=CC=NC=C1, which is pyridine with alternating double bonds and no substituents.
