The drawing shows 1-methyltryptophan: an indole whose ring nitrogen carries a methyl group, with the C3 position joined through a CH2 to an alpha carbon bearing NH2 and COOH.

- **Indole:** the five-membered ring contains the N-methyl nitrogen, the CH at C2 and the substituted C3. It is fused to a benzene ring.
- **Side chain:** C3 connects through CH2 to the alpha carbon, which carries NH2 on a hashed bond and COOH.
- **Stereochemistry:** The alpha carbon has NH2 hashed (pointing away), COOH to the lower left, CH2 to the lower right, and an implicit H pointing toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are N > COOH > CH2 > H. N, then COOH, then CH2 runs counterclockwise from the front. Because the H points toward the viewer, the true sense is reversed, so the centre is clockwise, which is R (the D-tryptophan configuration).
- **SMILES:** For tryptophan, L is `N[C@@H](Cc1c[nH]c2ccccc12)C(=O)O`, so the D form uses `[C@H]`. Adding the N-methyl gives the string below.
