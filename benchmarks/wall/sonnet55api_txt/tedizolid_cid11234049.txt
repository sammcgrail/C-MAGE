This molecule is tedizolid, an oxazolidinone antibiotic. I read it region by region:

- **Left:** a 2-methyl-2H-tetrazole, with N-methyl on the ring nitrogen, joined through its C5 carbon to the pyridine.
- **Middle:** a pyridine linked at its 2-position to the tetrazole and at its 5-position to a benzene ring. The benzene carries a fluorine ortho to the pyridine bond.
- **Right:** the benzene's para position is attached to the nitrogen of an oxazolidin-2-one, which has a C=O and ring O. The ring carbon next to O carries a CH2OH group drawn on a hashed bond.

For the single stereocentre, the hashed CH2OH points away from the viewer, so H points toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are O > CH2OH > CH2N. Viewed from the front the sequence runs counterclockwise, which reverses to clockwise once H is accounted for, giving R. The SMILES below, with `[C@H]` written in the order CH2N, H, CH2OH, O, also works out to R, so it matches the drawing.

SMILES: Cn1nnc(n1)-c1ccc(cn1)-c1ccc(cc1F)N1C[C@H](CO)OC1=O
