The drawing shows a symmetric dication: two 1-methylpyridinium-3-yl carbamate units joined by a hexamethylene chain. This is the structure of demecarium-like compounds, but with a six-carbon chain and N-methyl groups on both carbamate nitrogens (distigmine/hexamethylenebis).

Chain count: the path from the left N to the right N runs through these vertices: (578,858), (655,920), (750,885), (830,946), (922,910), (1003,972). That is 6 carbons, so the chain is hexamethylene.

Left half: the N-methyl pyridinium ring has N+ attached to a CH3, to C2 (the CH at the bottom, 248,705) and to C6 (the upper CH at 183,541). C3 carries the O and is joined to C2 by a double bond. The other ring bonds are C4 (357,568)=C5 (278,505) and C6=N+. In SMILES: C[n+]1cccc(OC(=O)N(C)...)c1, written aromatic.
