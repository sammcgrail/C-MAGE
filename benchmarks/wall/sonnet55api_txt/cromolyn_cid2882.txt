This is cromolyn (cromoglicic acid). Each half is a chromone: a 4-oxo-4H-chromene-2-carboxylic acid with the ether oxygen attached at C5. The two halves are joined through O–CH2–CH(OH)–CH2–O.

Reading the left half: the carboxylic acid carbon is attached to C2, which is double-bonded to C3. C3 connects to C4 (C=O), then to C4a, which is part of the benzene ring. C5 carries the ether oxygen, C8a is bonded to the ring oxygen O1, and O1 closes back to C2.

SMILES for one half: OC(=O)C1=CC(=O)c2c(OC...)cccc2O1

Full molecule: OC(=O)C1=CC(=O)c2c(OCC(O)COc3cccc4OC(C(=O)O)=CC(=O)c34)cccc2O1

Check on the second half: c3cccc4OC(C(=O)O)=CC(=O)c34. Atom c3 is C5, bonded to the ether oxygen. Then c, c, c are C6, C7 and C8, and c4 is C8a, which bears O1. O1 is bonded to C2 (bearing COOH), which is double-bonded to C3. C3 connects to C4 (C=O), which connects to c34, the C4a atom that closes both rings. This is correct.
