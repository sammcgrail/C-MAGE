I read the molecule as a tricyclic cyclopenta[b]indole core carrying an acetic acid side chain and a benzyl ether, and the stereocentre was assigned from the wedge bond.

- **Core:** a benzene ring fused to a pyrrole (the NH) and then to a cyclopentane. This is a 1,2,3,4-tetrahydrocyclopenta[b]indole.
- **Acid side chain:** the stereocentre is the cyclopentane carbon next to the NH-bearing carbon. It carries a CH2–COOH group on a wedge bond.
- **Ether:** an O–CH2 link goes from the benzene ring of the core to a second benzene ring.
- **Second benzene ring:** it has a cyclopentyl group at the para position relative to the CH2. A CF3 group sits ortho to the cyclopentyl.
- **Stereochemistry:** the acid CH2 is wedged toward the viewer, so the implicit H points away. Going acid CH2 → N-bearing ring carbon → ring CH2 runs counterclockwise from the front. Reordering to the SMILES neighbour order (acid CH2, H, N-bearing carbon via ring closure, ring CH2) gives `[C@H]`.

SMILES: OC(=O)C[C@H]1CCc2c1[nH]c1ccc(OCc3ccc(C4CCCC4)c(C(F)(F)F)c3)cc21
