The drawing shows a cytosine base joined through N1 to a furanose ring, which is an arabinofuranosyl cytosine (cytarabine, ara-C).

**Base:** A six-membered ring with N1 (attached to the sugar), C2 as C=O, N3, C4 bearing NH2, and a C5=C6 double bond. This is cytosine.

**Sugar:** A five-membered ring made of O4' and C1' to C4'. C4' carries a CH2OH group, and C2' and C3' each carry an OH.

**Stereochemistry from the wedges and hashes:**
- C1': the bond to the base N is a wedge (up).
- C2': the OH is a wedge (up), on the same face as the base. In ribo-cytidine the 2'-OH would be on the opposite face, so this makes it the arabino isomer.
- C3': the OH is hashed (down).
- C4': the CH2OH is a wedge (up).

I derived the C1' configuration from the drawn geometry and took the C3' and C4' descriptors from the standard cytidine SMILES, since both centres are drawn the same way as in cytidine. The C2' descriptor is the one that differs from cytidine.

SMILES: NC1=NC(=O)N(C=C1)[C@@H]1O[C@H](CO)[C@@H](O)[C@@H]1O
