This is ondansetron hydrochloride, which I identified from the drawing's features.

The structure has these parts:
- A 2-methylimidazole ring joined through its N1 to a CH2 group.
- That CH2 is attached to a ring carbon of the saturated six-membered ring.
- The six-membered ring carries a ketone (C=O) and is fused to an indole. The indole nitrogen bears a methyl group.
- The benzene ring is fused to the pyrrole ring.
- HCl is drawn as a separate component.

I read the connectivity as follows:
- Imidazole: N1 connects to C2, which carries the methyl group. C2 is double-bonded to N3. N3 connects to C4, which is double-bonded to C5, and C5 closes back to N1.
- The CH2 on N1 attaches to the CH of the cyclohexanone-type ring. That CH is bonded to the C=O carbon and to a CH2–CH2 chain. The chain leads to the indole C2, which is bonded to the N-methyl nitrogen.
