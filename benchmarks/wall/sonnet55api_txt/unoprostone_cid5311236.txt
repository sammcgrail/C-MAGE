The drawing is a prostaglandin F-type skeleton: a cyclopentane ring with two hydroxyls, a carboxylic acid chain carrying a cis double bond, and a lower chain containing a ketone.

**Upper chain.** Starting from the HO–C(=O) end, there are three CH2 groups, then a cis CH=CH, then one CH2 joined to the ring carbon with the bold wedge. This gives OC(=O)CCC/C=C\C–ring.

**Ring.** The five ring carbons, in order, are:
- C8, carrying the upper chain on a wedge bond.
- C9, carrying OH on a wedge bond.
- C10, a plain CH2.
- C11, carrying OH on a wedge bond.
- C12, carrying the lower chain on a hashed bond.

**Lower chain.** From C12 there are two CH2 groups, then C=O, then seven more carbons. These form an unbranched chain ending in a terminal CH3, so the ketone chain is longer than in natural prostaglandins.

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned @/@@ at the four ring stereocentres from the wedge and hash bonds, with the implicit H taken as pointing opposite the drawn wedge or hash. The double bond is cis, written as `/C=C\`.
