The drawing shows sulbactam, a penicillanic acid sulfone: a β-lactam fused to a five-membered ring that contains the sulfone.

**Connectivity**
- A gem-dimethyl carbon is bonded to the sulfone sulfur (S(=O)=O) and to the carbon that carries the carboxylic acid (C(=O)OH).
- That carboxyl-bearing carbon is bonded to the ring nitrogen.
- The nitrogen is part of the four-membered β-lactam ring: N, C=O, CH2, and the ring-fusion CH.
- The ring-fusion CH is also bonded to the sulfur.

**Stereochemistry**
- The ring-fusion CH has a hashed H (pointing away from the viewer). Its other substituents are S (up), N (lower right) and CH2 (lower left). Viewed from the front, S → N → CH2 runs clockwise. In the SMILES atom order S, H, N, CH2 this is `[C@@H]`.
- The carboxyl-bearing carbon has a hashed COOH, so its implicit H points toward the viewer. Viewed from the front, CMe2 → COOH → N runs clockwise. In the SMILES atom order N, H, CMe2, COOH this is `[C@H]`.
