The drawing shows a 3-amino-1H-indazole core with a para-substituted phenyl at C4, and that phenyl carries a urea linked to a 2-fluoro-5-methylphenyl ring.

**Indazole.** The five-membered ring is N=C(N)–C3a...C7a–NH, written as Nc1n[nH]c2cccc(c12)-.... The amino-bearing carbon (C3) is bonded to N2, which is double-bonded to it, and to C3a. C3a is fused to the benzene ring, and C4 of that ring carries the aryl group. Its neighbour C7a bonds to the NH.

**Central phenyl.** It is para-substituted, with the indazole at one end and NH–C(=O)–NH at the other.

**Terminal aryl ring.** The carbon attached to the urea NH has fluorine on one adjacent carbon, and the other adjacent carbon is CH. Going around the ring from the NH-bearing carbon (C1): C2 carries F, C3 is CH, C4 is CH, C5 carries the methyl (the lower carbon), and C6 is CH, which sits between C1 and C5. The ring is therefore 2-fluoro-5-methylphenyl.
