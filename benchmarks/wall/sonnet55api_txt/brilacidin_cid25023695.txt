I read the molecule as a pyrimidine-4,6-dicarboxamide whose two amide nitrogens are each bonded to the same substituted aryl group.

**Core:** the pyrimidine has C4 and C6 carrying the C(=O)NH groups, with C5 as CH between them and N1/N3 flanking C2 (CH).

**Each aryl ring** has these substituents, with the ring-carbon neighbours in this order:
- the carbon bonded to the pyrimidine amide NH
- a CH
- a carbon bearing CF3
- a CH
- a carbon bonded to NHC(=O)(CH2)4–N=C(NH2)2 (a 5-guanidinopentanamide; I counted four CH2 groups between the C=O and the guanidine N)
- a carbon bearing O-pyrrolidin-3-yl, which sits between the two N-bearing carbons

**Stereochemistry:** each pyrrolidin-3-yl ether carbon is drawn with one wedge or hash bond to O (a hash on the left, a wedge on the right). The priorities are O > C2 (the CH2 next to N) > C4 > H. Working through the geometry, both centres come out as R. In the SMILES, `O[C@@H]1CCNC1` encodes R for each.

SMILES: NC(N)=NCCCCC(=O)Nc1cc(C(F)(F)F)cc(NC(=O)c2cc(C(=O)Nc3cc(C(F)(F)F)cc(NC(=O)CCCCN=C(N)N)c3O[C@@H]3CCNC3)ncn2)c1O[C@@H]1CCNC1
