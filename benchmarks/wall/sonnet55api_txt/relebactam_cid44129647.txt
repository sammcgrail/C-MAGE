I read the molecule as a relebactam-type structure: a 1,6-diazabicyclo[3.2.1]octan-7-one core carrying an N-sulfooxy group and a carboxamide to a piperidin-4-yl group.

**Regions of the drawing**
- **Left ring:** a piperidine with an NH, attached through its 4-position carbon to an amide NH.
- **Amide:** the NH is bonded to a C=O carbon, which is joined by a wedge bond to the bicyclic core.
- **Bicyclic core:** the left nitrogen (N1) is bonded to the carboxamide-bearing carbon, to a CH2 bridge, and to the ring C=O. That C=O is bonded to the right nitrogen, which carries the O–SO3H group. The right nitrogen is also bonded to the bridgehead CH, which is drawn with an H wedge. The CH2 bridge links N1's side to this bridgehead carbon, and a two-carbon CH2–CH2 chain links the bridgehead to the carboxamide carbon.
- **Sulfate:** O–S(=O)(=O)–OH on the right nitrogen.

**Stereochemistry**
There are two stereocentres, both drawn with wedges.
- The carboxamide-bearing carbon has the wedged carbonyl toward the viewer and an implicit H behind. Working through the neighbour order gives `[C@@H]`.
- The bridgehead carbon has a wedged H toward the viewer. Working through its neighbour order also gives `[C@@H]`.
