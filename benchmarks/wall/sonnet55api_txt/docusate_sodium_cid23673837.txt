The drawing shows docusate sodium (sodium bis(2-ethylhexyl) sulfosuccinate).

Reading the structure region by region:
- The central chain is a succinate backbone. One carbon carries the sulfonate group, S(=O)(=O)[O-], and is bonded to a carbonyl carbon. The next carbon is a CH2, which is bonded to the second carbonyl carbon.
- Each carbonyl is an ester, C(=O)O–CH2–CH(CH2CH3)–CH2CH2CH2CH3, which is a 2-ethylhexyl group.
- Left chain: O–CH2–CH, with an ethyl branch (CH2–CH3) and a butyl chain (CH2CH2CH2CH3). That makes 6 carbons in the main chain plus the 2-carbon ethyl branch, so 2-ethylhexyl.
- Right chain: the same 2-ethylhexyl group.
- The counterion is Na+.
- No wedges or hashes are drawn, so no stereochemistry is included.
