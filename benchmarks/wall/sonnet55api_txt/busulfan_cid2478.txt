The image shows busulfan, a butane-1,4-diyl bis(methanesulfonate).

Reading the drawing from left to right:
- A methyl group is attached to a sulfur with two double-bonded oxygens (a sulfonyl group).
- That sulfur is bonded to an oxygen, which connects to a chain of four carbons.
- The chain ends in another oxygen bonded to a second sulfonyl sulfur, which carries two double-bonded oxygens and a terminal methyl.

I counted the chain carbons along the zigzag: the bond from O goes up to C1, down to C2, up to C3, then down to C4, which bonds to the right-hand O. That gives four carbons.

The SMILES is CS(=O)(=O)OCCCCOS(=O)(=O)C. It has no stereocentres, so no stereo marks are needed.
