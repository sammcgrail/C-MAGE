The drawing shows a methyl group bonded to selenium, then a CH2, then a stereocentre carrying NH2 and a carboxylic acid (C=O with OH). This is Se-methylselenocysteine, CH3–Se–CH2–CH(NH2)–COOH.

The NH2 is on a hashed bond, so it points away from the viewer, and the implicit hydrogen points toward the viewer.

CIP priorities at the stereocentre are:
1. N
2. CH2Se (Se outranks O)
3. COOH
4. H

Viewed from the front, N (bottom) → CH2Se (upper right) → COOH (upper left) runs counterclockwise. Because H points toward the viewer, the sense is reversed, so the centre is R. This is the L-configuration, as in L-cysteine.

For the SMILES, I compared against L-cysteine, N[C@@H](CS)C(=O)O. Writing the neighbours in the order CH2, H, N, COOH swaps N and CH2 relative to that string, which flips the chirality tag. The result is C[Se]C[C@H](N)C(=O)O, which is the R/L form.

SMILES: C[Se]C[C@H](N)C(=O)O
