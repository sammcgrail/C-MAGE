This is medetomidine: a 1-(2,3-dimethylphenyl)ethyl group on an imidazole ring.

**Imidazole ring.** The ring atoms are NH, a carbon bearing the side chain, a CH, a second N, and a CH between the two nitrogens. The substituted carbon is bonded to NH and double-bonded to the CH. That CH is bonded to the unprotonated N, which is double-bonded to the CH between the nitrogens. This gives the ring c1c[nH]cn1 pattern, written below as C1=C(NC=N1)... (4-substituted imidazole).

**Side chain.** The substituent is CH(CH3) attached to the benzene ring.

**Benzene ring.** The carbon bonded to the CH(CH3) has a neighbouring carbon bearing a methyl, and the next carbon along also bears a methyl. This is a 2,3-dimethylphenyl group.

**SMILES check.** CC(c1ccc[nH]1)... would be wrong, so I write the imidazole explicitly: CC(C1=CN=CN1)c1cccc(C)c1C.

- The imidazole carbon C1 is bonded to NH (closing the ring), and double-bonded to CH.
- That CH is bonded to N, which is double-bonded to the CH, which is bonded to NH. This is consistent with the drawing.
- The aromatic part c1cccc(C)c1C starts at the attachment carbon, runs through three CH atoms, then the methyl-bearing carbon, then the ring-closing carbon bearing the other methyl, which is adjacent to the attachment carbon. This matches the 2,3-dimethyl pattern.
