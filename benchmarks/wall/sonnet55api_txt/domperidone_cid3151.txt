This structure is domperidone, so I read it as two benzimidazolone units joined through a propyl–piperidine linker.

**Left half.** A benzimidazol-2-one with an NH at the top and a substituted N at the bottom. The substituted N carries a three-carbon chain (CCC) that runs to the piperidine nitrogen.

**Middle.** A piperidine ring. Its nitrogen takes the propyl chain, and the carbon at position 4 (opposite the nitrogen) bonds to the right benzimidazolone N.

**Right half.** A benzimidazol-2-one with Cl on the benzene ring. The N attached to the piperidine is at the upper left of the five-membered ring, and the NH is at the lower right. The ring fusion carbon next to the substituted N (the upper-left carbon) is bonded to the aromatic CH, which leads up to the CH–CH(Cl) side.

Tracing the right benzene ring: the fusion carbon attached to N(piperidinyl) connects to a CH (upper left), then a CH (top), then C–Cl (top right), then a CH (right), then the fusion carbon attached to NH. So Cl is para to the fusion carbon bearing the substituted N… checking against the standard domperidone SMILES, O=C1Nc2cc(Cl)ccc2N1C3CCN(CCCn4c5ccccc5[nH]c4=O)CC3, the NH-side fusion carbon is followed by a CH and then C(Cl), and this matches the drawing.

No stereochemistry is drawn.
