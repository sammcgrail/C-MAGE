This structure is tezacaftor. I read it region by region:

- **Indole core:** a fluorine sits on the benzene ring next to the amide NH, and the ring nitrogen carries a side chain.
- **C2 substituent on the indole:** a quaternary carbon bearing two methyl groups and a CH2OH, i.e. C(C)(C)CO.
- **N-substituent:** CH2–CH(OH)–CH2OH. The OH on the middle carbon is drawn with a hashed bond, so this is the only stereocentre.
- **Amide:** NH–C(=O) attached to a cyclopropane carbon, which also carries the 2,2-difluorobenzo[1,3]dioxol-5-yl group.

For the stereocentre, the priorities are OH > CH2OH > CH2N > H. The hashed OH points away from the viewer, so the H points toward the viewer. In the drawing, OH (east) → CH2OH (upper left) → CH2N (lower left) runs counterclockwise. Because H faces the viewer, I reverse that to clockwise, giving R. This matches the known (R) configuration of tezacaftor.

Writing the centre as n1C[C@@H](O)CO, the neighbour order is CH2N, H, O, CH2OH. That is one swap from the PubChem form N1C[C@H](CO)O, so @@ is correct here.
