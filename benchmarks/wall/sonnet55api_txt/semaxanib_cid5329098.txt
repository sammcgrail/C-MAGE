The drawing shows a 3,5-dimethylpyrrole joined through a =CH– linker to the 3-position of an oxindole (indolin-2-one). This is the semaxanib (SU5416) skeleton.

- **Pyrrole ring:** an NH-pyrrole with a methyl on the carbon next to NH and another methyl on the next ring carbon. The ring carbon bearing the linker is the other carbon adjacent to NH.
- **Linker:** the pyrrole C2 is bonded to a CH, which is double-bonded to C3 of the oxindole.
- **Oxindole:** C3 is attached to the C=O carbon and to the fused benzene ring. The lactam N–H is attached to the benzene ring carbon.
- **Double-bond geometry:** the pyrrole carbon and the C=O carbon lie on the same side of the C=C bond in the drawing. On the CH, the pyrrole outranks H. On C3, the C=O carbon (O,O,N) outranks the aromatic carbon (C,C,C). The higher-priority groups are on the same side, so the bond is Z.

In SMILES, a `/` followed by a `\` across the double bond puts the pyrrole and the C=O carbon cis, which gives Z.

SMILES: Cc1cc(C)c(/C=C2\C(=O)Nc3ccccc32)[nH]1
