I read the structure as a macrocycle built from three parts joined in a ring.

- **Pyrazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidine core:** the ring has a bridgehead N, an N–C(NH2) pyrazole portion, and a fused pyrimidine ring with one N.
- **Substituent at the pyrimidine carbon next to N:** an N-ethyl amine, whose CH2 links to the aryl ring.
- **Aryl ring:** carries the CH2 link, an adjacent C≡N, then F, and a ring O on the carbon next to the CH2-bearing carbon.
- **Linker back to the core:** the ring O is bonded to a stereocentre CH(CH3), then CH2, NH and C(=O), which attaches to the core carbon next to the C–NH2 carbon.

For the stereocentre, the methyl is on a wedge, and the other neighbours are O (upper right), CH2 (left) and an implicit H pointing away. With H behind, O → CH3 → CH2 runs clockwise on the page, which gives `O[C@@H](C)CN` when written from the O side.

I checked the ring closures atom by atom against the drawing. Label 1 closes the pyrimidine C–N(Et) ring, label 2 is reused after the aryl ring closes, and label 3 is the bridgehead N.
