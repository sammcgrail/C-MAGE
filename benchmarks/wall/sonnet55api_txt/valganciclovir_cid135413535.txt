The drawing shows valganciclovir, the L-valine ester of ganciclovir. I read it in three parts.

- **Purine part:** a guanine-type ring system, with a C=O and an NH in the six-membered ring, an NH2 on the carbon between the ring nitrogens, and N9 of the five-membered ring carrying the side chain.
- **Side chain:** N–CH2–O–CH(CH2OH)–CH2–O–C(=O), so the central CH carries one free hydroxymethyl and one ester-linked CH2.
- **Amino acid part:** the ester carbonyl joins an α-carbon bearing NH2 and an isopropyl group.

Stereochemistry: only the α-carbon has a wedge, on the NH2 bond. With the implicit H pointing away, the priority order N > C(=O)O > isopropyl runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S, which is L-valine. In SMILES this is `CC(C)[C@H](N)C(=O)O...`. The side-chain CH is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label.

SMILES: CC(C)[C@H](N)C(=O)OCC(CO)OCn1cnc2c1nc(N)[nH]c2=O
