I read this as fluvastatin, drawn as the (3S,5R) enantiomer. Reading the drawing region by region:

- **Acid end:** a carboxylic acid (C=O and OH) is attached to a CH2.
- **Diol chain:** the CH2 leads to a carbon bearing a wedged OH (C3), then another CH2, then a second carbon bearing a wedged OH (C5).
- **Alkene:** C5 is attached to a trans CH=CH, which joins the indole at C2.
- **Indole:** the ring nitrogen carries an isopropyl group, and C3 of the indole carries a 4-fluorophenyl ring. The benzo ring is fused in the usual way.

For the stereocentres, the OH groups are wedged up and the implicit H is behind. Applying CIP priorities to the drawing gives C3 = S and C5 = R. That is the mirror image of the marketed (3R,5S) fluvastatin, but I encoded it as drawn. The alkene is trans.
