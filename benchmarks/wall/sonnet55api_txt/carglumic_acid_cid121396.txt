The image shows N‑carbamoylglutamic acid (carglumic acid), drawn as the (S)-enantiomer.

**Connectivity**
- A chain of two CH2 groups carries a terminal carboxylic acid (HO–C=O) on the left.
- The central stereocentre carries a second carboxylic acid on its upper right.
- It also carries an NH that is bonded to a urea-type carbonyl, C(=O)NH2.

**Stereochemistry**
- The only wedge runs from the stereocentre to the CH2 of the chain, so that CH2 points toward the viewer and the implicit H points away.
- The CIP priorities are N > COOH > CH2 > H.
- Viewed from the front with H at the back, N (bottom) → COOH (upper right) → CH2 (left) runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S.
- Written as `OC(=O)CC[C@H](NC(N)=O)C(=O)O`, the neighbour order at the stereocentre is CH2, H, N, COOH. I checked this against L-glutamic acid, `N[C@@H](CCC(=O)O)C(=O)O`, which is also S. Moving N to the CH2 position is one swap, which turns `@@` into `@`, so `@` is correct here.
