I recognised this as zotarolimus (ABT-578), the rapamycin analogue in which the C40 hydroxyl is replaced by a tetrazol-1-yl group. I wrote the SMILES from that recognition, using the rapamycin framework as a template. I did not re-derive every stereocentre from the wedges and hashes in the drawing, so the stereo descriptors are the least certain part, especially at the tetrazole-bearing cyclohexane carbon.

The drawing shows the following features, and the SMILES contains each of them:
- A macrolide ring containing a hemiketal carbon bearing OH, with a tetrahydropyran ring fused to it.
- A pipecolate (piperidine) unit with an α-keto amide and an ester.
- A conjugated triene with a methyl-substituted alkene.
- A second trisubstituted alkene near the lower OH and OMe groups.
- Several ketones, methoxy groups and methyl branches.
- A side chain leading to a cyclohexane that carries an OMe group and the N1-linked tetrazole.
