This is anastrozole. The drawing shows a benzene ring with three substituents in a 1,3,5 pattern.

- Two of the substituents are C(CH3)2–C≡N groups, one at the upper left and one at the bottom.
- The third is a CH2 group attached to N1 of a 1,2,4-triazole.
- In the triazole, N1 is bonded to the CH2, to an adjacent N (the lower N), and to a CH carbon (top). That top CH is double-bonded to the right N. The right N connects to a CH, which is double-bonded to the lower N.

So the ring is n1cncn1 with the substituent on N1, which gives Cn1cncn1 style connectivity for the triazole.

The SMILES is N#CC(C)(C)c1cc(Cn2cncn2)cc(C(C)(C)C#N)c1.
