The drawing shows pantothenic acid (vitamin B5) as its D-(R) enantiomer.

- **Left end:** a quaternary carbon carries two methyl groups and a CH2OH, and is bonded to the stereocentre.
- **Stereocentre:** it carries an OH on a wedge bond and is bonded to the amide carbonyl carbon.
- **Amide link:** C(=O) joins to NH.
- **Right end:** NH is followed by CH2CH2 and a terminal carboxylic acid, C(=O)OH.

**Stereochemistry:** the CIP priorities at the stereocentre are OH, then C(=O)N, then C(CH3)2CH2OH, then H. With the OH wedged toward the viewer, H points away. Going from OH (top) to C=O (lower right) to the quaternary carbon (lower left) is clockwise, so the centre is R. I checked that `[C@@H]`, written after the quaternary carbon as `CC(C)(CO)[C@@H](O)C(=O)...`, encodes R.

SMILES: CC(C)(CO)[C@@H](O)C(=O)NCCC(=O)O
