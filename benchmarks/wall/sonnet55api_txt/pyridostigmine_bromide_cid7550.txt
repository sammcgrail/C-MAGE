The drawing shows pyridostigmine bromide, a pyridinium cation with its bromide counterion.

Reading the ring: N+ carries a methyl group. Going around from N+, the atoms are N+, CH (upper left), CH, CH, then the carbon bearing the O substituent (C3), then a CH back to N+. That is a six-membered ring: N+, C (left, bottom), C (top left), C (top right), C (O-bearing), C (bottom right), and back to N+.

The substituent on the ring is O–C(=O)–N(CH3)2, a dimethylcarbamate.

The bond pattern is:
- N+=C (left bottom), a double bond between the N+ and the left bottom carbon
- C(top left)=C(top right)
- C(O-bearing)=C(bottom right)

SMILES for the ring: C[n+]1cccc(OC(=O)N(C)C)c1. Written in Kekulé form from the drawing: C[N+]1=CC=CC(OC(=O)N(C)C)=C1.

The bromide is written as a separate ion: [Br-].
