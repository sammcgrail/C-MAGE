I read this as a cyclic undecapeptide of the cyclosporin type, with Thr at position 2 (cyclosporin C-like). The "C" label next to the N-methyl nitrogen on the lower edge is just the CH2 of sarcosine.

I traced the ring clockwise from the top D-Ala and found 11 residues:

1. D-Ala (wedged methyl)
2. L-Ala (hashed methyl)
3. N-Me-Leu
4. Val (the only N–H Val in the ring)
5. N-Me-Leu
6. Sar (the labelled "C")
7. Thr
8. MeBmt, the unsaturated hydroxy amino acid with the (E)-CH2–CH=CH–CH3 chain
9. N-Me-Val
10. N-Me-Leu
11. N-Me-Leu, which closes onto the D-Ala carbonyl

The peptide N–H groups are on D-Ala, L-Ala, Val and Thr, and the other residues carry N-methyl groups.

Stereochemistry follows the wedges and hashes. In the SMILES each α-carbon is written with its own carbonyl as the preceding atom, so the chirality tags follow that order. The MeBmt double bond is trans.

I did not run a validity check on the SMILES; I checked it by hand against the traced ring.
