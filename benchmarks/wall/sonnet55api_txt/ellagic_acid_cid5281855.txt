This is ellagic acid, a dilactone of hexahydroxydiphenic acid: two benzene rings joined by a biaryl bond and bridged by two lactone (–C(=O)–O–) links.

Reading the drawing:

- **Upper-left ring:** it contains a CH, then C(OH), C(OH), then a ring carbon bonded to the lactone O (this is the lower lactone O), then the biaryl carbon, then the carbon bearing the upper C=O.
- **Upper-right ring:** it contains the carbon bonded to the upper lactone O, then C(OH), C(OH), a CH, the carbon bearing the lower C=O, and the biaryl carbon.
- **Lactone bridges:**
  - The upper C=O carbon sits on the left ring (at the carbon next to the CH) and links through O to the right ring's carbon next to C(OH).
  - The lower C=O carbon sits on the right ring (next to the CH) and links through O to the left ring's carbon next to C(OH).

Writing the SMILES, starting at the upper carbonyl:

O=C1Oc2c(O)c(O)cc3C(=O)Oc4c(O)c(O)cc1c4c23

Checking this atom by atom:

- C1 (the upper carbonyl) is bonded to O, which is bonded to c2 (the right-ring carbon next to C(OH)).
- Continuing round the right ring: c(O), c(O), c (the CH), then c3, which bears the lower C(=O).
- That carbonyl is followed by O, then c4, a left-ring carbon, then c(O), c(O), and c (the CH).
- That CH is followed by c1, the left-ring carbon bearing the upper carbonyl, which closes ring 1 to the upper C=O. Then c4 is the left-ring carbon bonded to the biaryl bond, written as "c4" closing ring 4, and then c23.
- Left ring: c4(O-attached), c(O), c(O), cH, c1 (bearing C=O), and the closing carbon "c4". That is only 5 atoms plus the closing atom. I count them as: c4 (O-bearing), c(O), c(O), c(H), c1, and the final c4. That is 6 atoms, which is correct.
- Right ring: c2, c(O), c(O), cH, c3, and c23 at the end. That is 6 atoms, which is correct.
- The final atom c23 closes rings 2 and 3, so it bonds to c2 and c3 (biaryl bond with c4 preceding it). This is consistent with the biaryl link between the two rings.
