The drawing shows a cobalt(II) cation with a radical dot, written Co2+·, and two cyclopentadienyl anions drawn as separate fragments (cobaltocene drawn as ions).

Each ring is a five-membered ring with one C⁻ and four other carbons. Two double bonds are drawn, one on the top edge and one on the bottom edge. The left side of the ring is a single bond, and the bonds to C⁻ are single.

Tracing one ring: the C⁻ connects to the top carbon, which is double-bonded to the upper-left carbon. That carbon is single-bonded down to the lower-left carbon, which is double-bonded to the bottom carbon. The bottom carbon is single-bonded back to C⁻.

This gives the ring SMILES [CH-]1C=CC=C1.

Written as a three-component structure, with the cobalt carrying its charge and radical dot:

[Co+2] . [CH-]1C=CC=C1 . [CH-]1C=CC=C1

The radical dot on Co2+ is hard to encode, so I use [Co+2] without it.
