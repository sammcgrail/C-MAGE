The drawing shows glutamine: a primary amide (H2N–C=O) at one end, a carboxylic acid (C(=O)OH) at the other, and two CH2 groups between the amide carbonyl and the central carbon. The central carbon carries an NH2 on a wedge bond and an implicit hydrogen.

To assign the stereocentre, I ranked the substituents by CIP priority: NH2 > COOH > CH2 > H. The wedged NH2 points toward the viewer, so the hydrogen points away. Going from NH2 (bottom) to COOH (upper right) to CH2 (upper left) runs counterclockwise, which gives S. That matches L-glutamine.

I checked the SMILES stereo tag against L-alanine, N[C@@H](C)C(=O)O, which has the same neighbour order and the same S configuration, so `@@` is correct here.
