I read the molecule as a ribonucleoside of a pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidin-4-one base, which is allopurinol riboside (the pyrazole analogue of inosine).

**Base:** The pyrazole N1 carries the sugar. N1 is bonded to N2, and N2 is double-bonded to C3. C3 joins C3a, which is fused to C7a. The pyrimidinone ring then runs C3a–C4(=O)–NH–CH=N–C7a, and C7a closes back onto N1.

**Sugar:** This is a furanose ring with a CH2OH on C4′ (wedge), OH groups on C3′ and C2′ (both hashed), and the base on C1′ (wedge).

**Stereochemistry:** I worked out each centre from the drawn wedge and hash geometry, treating the implicit H as pointing opposite to the drawn wedge or hash. This gave the same pattern as β-D-ribose in inosine:
- C4′ is `[C@H]`.
- C1′ is `[C@@H]`.
- C2′ is `[C@H]`.
- C3′ is `[C@@H]`.
