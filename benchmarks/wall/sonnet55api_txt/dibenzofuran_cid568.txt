The drawing shows dibenzofuran. It has a central five-membered ring made of one oxygen and four carbons. The two carbons bonded to oxygen are fused to two benzene rings. The other two carbons of the five-membered ring are joined to each other by a single bond.

Each benzene ring has six carbons, so the molecule has 12 carbons and one oxygen.

I wrote the SMILES by starting at the oxygen, going through the left ring, and closing back through the right ring:

c1ccc2c(c1)oc1ccccc12

Atom-by-atom check:
- c1 is the first ring atom, followed by c and c, then c2, a fusion carbon.
- c(c1) is the other fusion carbon, with c1 closing the left benzene ring. That ring is c1, c, c, c2, c, c1, which is six atoms.
- The fusion carbon c(c1) is bonded to o, and o is bonded to c1 (a new ring label 1), the first carbon of the right ring.
- Continuing c c c c gives four more carbons, and c12 closes both rings. The right ring is c1, c, c, c, c, c12, which is six atoms.
- Ring closure 2 bonds the last carbon to the first fusion carbon, which gives the C–C bond in the five-membered ring. That ring is c2, c, o, c1, c12, which is five atoms.
