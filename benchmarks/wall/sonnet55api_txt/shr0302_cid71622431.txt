I read the molecule as a 3-methoxy-1,2,4-thiadiazol-5-yl urea. The urea nitrogen is the ring nitrogen of a cis-fused bicyclic octahydrocyclopenta[c]pyrrole. On the cyclopentane carbon of that bicycle sits an N-methyl-N-(7H-pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidin-4-yl)amino group.

- **Left ring:** a 1,2,4-thiadiazole carrying OMe on one carbon and the NH–C(=O) link on the other.
- **Bicycle:** the urea nitrogen is part of a pyrrolidine ring that shares its two fusion carbons with a cyclopentane ring.
- **Stereochemistry:** both ring-fusion hydrogens are drawn as wedges, so the fusion is cis. Working out the neighbour order for each fusion carbon gives `@@` for both. The carbon bearing the N-methylamino group has only plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label.
- **Right ring system:** the pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine is attached through its C4 to the N-methyl nitrogen.
