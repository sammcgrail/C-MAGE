The drawing shows three parts, read from left to right.

1. A five-membered 1,3,2-dithiarsolane ring made of As, S, CH2, CH and S. The CH carries a CH2OH group, drawn as HO–CH2.
2. A para-substituted benzene ring. One end is bonded to As and the other to an NH group.
3. A 1,3,5-triazine ring. The NH is attached to one ring carbon, and the other two ring carbons each carry an NH2.

The triazine has alternating bonds, so I wrote it as the aromatic ring c1nc(N)nc(N)n1.

The full SMILES is OCC1CS[As](S1)c1ccc(Nc2nc(N)nc(N)n2)cc1.

Checking the ring closures:
- In the dithiarsolane, C1 is the CH, followed by CH2, S, As, and then S closing back to C1. That gives 5 atoms: CH, CH2, S, As, S.
- The benzene ring is c1ccc(...)cc1, which has 6 atoms.
- The triazine is c2nc(N)nc(N)n2, which has 6 atoms: 3 carbons and 3 nitrogens.
