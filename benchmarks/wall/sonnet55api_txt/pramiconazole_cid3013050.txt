I read the molecule as an itraconazole-like analogue. An N-isopropyl imidazolidin-2-one replaces the triazolone, and there is no sec-butyl group.

**Regions, left to right**
- An isopropyl group is on N1 of an imidazolidin-2-one (5-membered ring with a C=O), and N3 of that ring is bonded to a para-disubstituted benzene.
- That benzene carries a piperazine through one of its nitrogens, and the other piperazine nitrogen bonds to a second para-disubstituted benzene.
- The second benzene bears an O–CH2 link to C2 of a 1,3-dioxolane.
- The other dioxolane carbon (C4) is quaternary and bears a 2,4-difluorophenyl group and a CH2–(1,2,4-triazol-1-yl) group. The triazole attaches through N1, which is bonded to both a ring carbon and a ring nitrogen.

**Stereochemistry**
- Both stereocentres are on the dioxolane ring, and each has one wedge bond.
- The CH2OAr carbon (C2) has a wedge to its CH2 substituent. Applying the CIP priorities to the drawing gives S, which is `[C@H]` in the SMILES below.
- The quaternary carbon (C4) has a wedge to its CH2-triazole. This gives R, which is `[C@]` in the SMILES below.

I derived both tags by hand from the drawing rather than checking them with software.
