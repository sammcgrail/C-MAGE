The drawing shows a benzene ring with three substituents, read as follows.

- **Ester side chain:** a diethylamino group (N with two ethyl groups) is attached through a CH2CH2 linker to an ester oxygen. That oxygen is bonded to a C=O carbon, which is bonded to the ring.
- **Ring substituents:** at the ring carbon next to the one bearing the ester carbon, there is an O-propyl group (OCH2CH2CH3). The NH2 sits on the ring carbon para to the ester-bearing carbon.

Numbering the ring from the ester-bearing carbon:
- C1 carries the C(=O)O group.
- The ring carbon at lower right (C2) carries the O-propyl group.
- C3 is the CH next to it.
- C4 carries NH2, at upper right.
- C5 and C6 are the remaining CH carbons.

This gives 4-amino-2-propoxybenzoate, the diethylaminoethyl ester (the compound known as propoxycaine).

SMILES: CCCOc1cc(N)ccc1C(=O)OCCN(CC)CC
