The structure is folic acid (pteroyl-L-glutamic acid). I read it in three parts:

- **Pterin end (right):** a bicyclic pteridine with a 2-amino group and a 4-oxo group. The ring NH sits between the C=O and the amino-bearing carbon.
- **Linker (middle):** a CH2 joins the pteridine to an NH, which attaches to a para-substituted benzene ring. The ring carries a C(=O)NH amide on the other side.
- **Glutamic acid end (left):** the amide nitrogen is bonded to the stereocentre. That carbon carries a COOH and a CH2CH2COOH chain, with the CH2 drawn on a hashed bond.

For the stereocentre, the priorities are N > COOH > CH2 > H. With the CH2 hashed (pointing away), the implicit H points toward the viewer. On the page, N (right), then COOH (down), then CH2 (upper left) runs clockwise. Because H faces the viewer, the true sense is reversed, so the centre is S. This is the natural L-glutamate configuration, and it matches `N[C@@H](CCC(=O)O)C(=O)O` when N is written first.

SMILES: Nc1nc2ncc(CNc3ccc(cc3)C(=O)N[C@@H](CCC(=O)O)C(=O)O)nc2c(=O)[nH]1
