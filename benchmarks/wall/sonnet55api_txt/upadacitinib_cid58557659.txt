I read this as an upadacitinib-type skeleton: a pyrrolidine carrying an ethyl group and a fused tricyclic heteroaromatic, with an N-(2,2,2-trifluoroethyl)urea on the ring nitrogen.

- **Pyrrolidine:** the ring nitrogen is acylated as a urea, N–C(=O)–NH–CH2–CF3. The ring carbon at the top bears an ethyl group on a wedge bond. The adjacent ring carbon bears the heteroaromatic on a wedge bond.
- **Heteroaromatic:** the fused tricycle is an imidazole (C attached to the pyrrolidine, then CH, N, a fusion carbon, and a bridgehead N). That imidazole is fused to a pyrazine (CH=N), which is fused in turn to a pyrrole (NH–CH=CH).
- **Stereochemistry:** I worked out each centre from the drawn geometry, with the implicit hydrogen pointing away from the viewer. Both centres come out as `@@` in the SMILES atom order below. Since both substituents are on wedges, the two substituents are cis, which may make this a different diastereomer from the marketed drug. I followed the drawing rather than the known drug.

SMILES: CC[C@@H]1CN(C[C@@H]1c1cnc2cnc3[nH]ccc3n12)C(=O)NCC(F)(F)F
