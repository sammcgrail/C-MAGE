This is calcium gluconate: one Ca²⁺ ion and two identical gluconate anions, each drawn as a six-carbon chain.

**Chain connectivity (each anion):** a carboxylate carbon (C=O and O⁻) is bonded to four CH(OH) carbons in a row. The chain ends in a CH2OH group.

**Stereochemistry:** I read each centre from the wedge or hash on its OH, taking the implicit H as pointing the opposite way. Neighbours are ordered as in the SMILES (preceding carbon, H, O, next carbon).

- C2: OH hashed (back), so `[C@H]`.
- C3: OH hashed (back), so `[C@@H]`.
- C4: OH hashed (back), so `[C@H]`.
- C5: OH wedged (front), so `[C@H]`.

Each anion is therefore `O=C([O-])[C@H](O)[C@@H](O)[C@H](O)[C@H](O)CO`. The full formula is two of these plus `[Ca+2]`.
