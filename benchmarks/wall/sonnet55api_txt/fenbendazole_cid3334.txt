The drawing is a benzimidazole carbamate with a phenylthio substituent, which matches the structure of fenbendazole.

- **Carbamate:** a methyl ester (CH3–O–C(=O)) attached to an NH.
- **Imidazole ring:** that NH is bonded to C2 of the ring. C2 is double-bonded to N, which is bonded to the fused benzene carbon (C3a). The other ring nitrogen is NH, bonded to the second fused carbon (C7a).
- **Fused benzene ring:** the carbon attached to S is two bonds away from the fusion carbon on the NH side.
- **Thioether:** S links the benzimidazole to a phenyl ring.

Tracing the fused benzene from the drawing:
- Top-left carbon at (708, 645) is bonded to N= (the fusion carbon C3a).
- Bottom-left carbon at (785, 763) is bonded to NH (C7a).
- From C7a the ring continues to (925, 757), then to (989, 630), which bears S.
- It then continues to (913, 512), then to (773, 519), and back to C3a.

So from C7a (attached to NH), the next carbon is CH, and the one after that bears S. This is the 5(6)-substituted tautomer.
