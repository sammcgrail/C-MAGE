I did not derive the stereochemistry independently from the wedges and hashes. I recognised the structure as doramectin (25-cyclohexyl avermectin B1a) and built the SMILES from the known abamectin B1a SMILES, which I recalled from memory and did not check against a database, swapping the sec-butyl group for cyclohexyl. The stereodescriptors are therefore those of the natural product, not read atom by atom from the image.

The features visible in the drawing that match this assignment are:
- a 16-membered macrolactone containing the C=O and the ring oxygen;
- a spiroketal at the top left, with a cyclohexyl group and a methyl-bearing, ring-fused alkene;
- a hexahydrobenzofuran unit at the bottom, bearing a tertiary OH, a secondary OH and an alkene methyl;
- a disaccharide of two 2,6-dideoxy sugars, the inner one with a 4-OMe and the outer one with a 4-OMe and a free 4-OH.

Replacing the sec-butyl with cyclohexyl keeps the neighbour order at that carbon, so its chirality descriptor stays unchanged.
