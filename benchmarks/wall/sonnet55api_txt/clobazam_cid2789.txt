The drawing shows a 1,5-benzodiazepine-2,4-dione (clobazam-type) skeleton, which I read as follows.

- The seven-membered ring contains N(CH3), C(=O), CH2, C(=O), N(Ph), and two fused aromatic carbons.
- The N-methyl nitrogen is on the left, bonded to the carbonyl carbon at the upper left and to the aromatic carbon at the lower left.
- The N-phenyl nitrogen is on the right, bonded to the carbonyl carbon at the upper right, to the phenyl ring, and to the aromatic carbon at the lower right.
- Chlorine is on the fused benzene ring, on the carbon para to the carbon bearing the N-methyl nitrogen. Going round the ring from the carbon attached to N-phenyl (call it C-a), the next carbon is CH, and the one after that carries Cl.

Writing the SMILES: CN1C(=O)CC(=O)N(c2ccccc2)c2cc(Cl)ccc21

Checking the ring order: N1 (methyl) is bonded to the aromatic carbon c21, which is the last atom of the fused ring. The fused ring runs c2 (attached to N-phenyl), then c (CH), then c(Cl), then c, then c, then c21 (attached to N-methyl). This matches the drawing, where the carbon bearing N-phenyl is bonded to a CH, which is bonded to C-Cl.
