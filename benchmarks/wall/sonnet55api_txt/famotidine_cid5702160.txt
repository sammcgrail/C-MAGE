This is the famotidine skeleton, read as follows.

Left side: a guanidine carbon bearing two NH2 groups is double-bonded to an N (N=C(N)N). That N is attached to C2 of a thiazole ring.

Thiazole ring: C2 is bonded to the ring S and double-bonded to the ring N. The ring N connects to C4, and C4 is double-bonded to C5, which bonds back to the ring S. C4 carries the CH2 substituent.

Chain: from C4, CH2 – S – CH2 – CH2 – C(NH2)=N – S(=O)(=O) – NH2.

The guanidine is drawn as N=C(N)N, with the double bond from the guanidine carbon to the N attached to the ring.

Thiazole SMILES: C2 is written as c1nc(cs1), which gives N=C(N)Nc1nc(CSCCC(N)=NS(N)(=O)=O)cs1. In the drawing the bonds are Kekulé-style, but the aromatic form is equivalent.

SMILES: NC(N)=Nc1nc(CSCCC(N)=NS(N)(=O)=O)cs1
