The drawing shows a benzene ring with four substituents, plus a five-membered imidazoline ring.

**Benzene ring.** Going around the ring from the top:
- The top-left carbon carries Cl.
- The next carbon, bottom-left, carries the NH link to the imidazoline.
- The bottom carbon carries Cl.
- The right-bottom carbon is CH.
- The right-top carbon carries NH2.
- The top carbon is CH.

So the NH-bearing carbon sits between two Cl-bearing carbons, and the NH2 is para to the NH. This is 4-amino-2,6-dichlorophenyl attached to the imidazoline through NH, which is the clonidine analog with a 4-amino group (apraclonidine).

**Imidazoline ring.** The ring is C(=N–CH2–CH2–NH), with the exocyclic NH attached to the C2 carbon.

**SMILES.** Writing the aryl ring first: Nc1cc(Cl)c(NC2=NCCN2)c(Cl)c1. Reading it back against the drawing:
- N is the NH2.
- c1 is the ring carbon bearing NH2.
- c is the CH.
- c(Cl) is the carbon bearing Cl.
- c(NC2=NCCN2) is the carbon bearing the NH–imidazoline.
- c(Cl) is the second Cl-bearing carbon.
- c1 is the final CH, closing the ring.
