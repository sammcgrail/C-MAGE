The drawing shows an azo compound: two benzene rings joined by an N=N bond. Each ring carries an OH and a COOH, so this is 5,5'-azobis(salicylic acid) (azodisal).

**Left ring.** The azo nitrogen attaches at the ring carbon at (568, 760). Going around the ring:
- (568, 760) bears the N.
- (457, 677) is CH.
- (330, 729) bears the COOH.
- (313, 866) bears the OH.
- (422, 950) is CH.
- (550, 897) is CH.

The COOH and OH sit on adjacent carbons, and the azo carbon is para to the OH carbon. Counting from the OH carbon (313, 866), the azo carbon at (568, 760) is three bonds away through (422, 950) and (550, 897), so it is para.

**Right ring.** The azo nitrogen attaches at (932, 739). Going around the ring:
- (932, 739) bears the N.
- (950, 601) is CH.
- (1078, 549) is CH.
- (1187, 632) bears the OH.
- (1169, 770) bears the COOH.
- (1042, 822) is CH.

Here too the azo carbon is para to the OH carbon.
