I read this as a tacrolimus-type 23-membered macrolide, and I derived each stereocentre from the wedges and hashes in the drawing.

**Macrocycle, traced from the allyl-bearing carbon:**
- The allyl-bearing CH (wedge to the allyl group) leads to the ketone C=O, then a CH2.
- Next come CH(OH) with a hashed OH, then CH(CH3) with a wedged methyl.
- Then comes the ester-bearing CH (wedged bond to the side chain), followed by the ester O and C(=O).
- This reaches the pipecolate CH (wedged H), whose ring N carries the C(=O)–C(=O) α-keto amide.
- The α-keto amide connects to the hemiketal carbon (hashed OH).
- The hemiketal carbon sits in the tetrahydropyran ring, which is made of the hemiketal C, the ring O, a CH, a CH(OMe), a CH2 and a CH(CH3).
- The macrocycle continues from the ring CH through CH(OMe) (wedge), CH2, CH(CH3) (wedged methyl), CH2 and C(CH3)=CH back to the allyl-bearing CH. This trisubstituted alkene has the CH2 and the allyl-bearing CH trans across it.

**Side chain on the ester-bearing carbon:**
- It is a C(CH3)=CH– alkene, drawn trans, attached to a cyclohexane ring.
- On that ring, the attachment CH has a wedged bond, the adjacent CH(OMe) has a wedge, and the CH(OH) has a hash.
