I read the structure as a duocarmycin SA-type alkylating unit (a cyclopropane-spiro cyclohexadienone fused to a pyrrole carrying a methyl ester). Its ring nitrogen is acylated by a 5,6,7-trimethoxyindole-2-carbonyl group.

**Fragments**
- **Ester and pyrrole:** a methyl ester (`COC(=O)`) sits on C2 of an NH pyrrole. The pyrrole is fused to the six-membered ring.
- **Six-membered ring:** the ring carries a ketone (C=O) next to the pyrrole carbon bearing NH. It continues through a CH=C(N) unit and reaches the spiro carbon.
- **Spiro cyclopropane:** the spiro carbon is bonded to the cyclopropane CH₂, the pyrrole-side ring carbon, the C(N) carbon, and the cyclopropane CH. That CH bears an H and is bonded to the CH₂ that leads to the amide nitrogen.
- **Amide and indole:** the nitrogen bears the C(=O) of the 5,6,7-trimethoxyindole-2-carbonyl group.

**Stereochemistry**
- The wedge on the spiro carbon points to the cyclopropane CH₂. With that bond toward the viewer, the other three neighbours run clockwise (pyrrole-side carbon, then C(N), then CH). That gives `[C@@]`.
- The hashed H on the cyclopropane CH puts H away from the viewer. The remaining neighbours then run counterclockwise (N–CH₂, then spiro carbon, then cyclopropane CH₂). In my SMILES atom order that gives `[C@H]`.
