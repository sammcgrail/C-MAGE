I read this as cefpodoxime proxetil, a cephalosporin prodrug, and worked through the drawing region by region.

- **Left side:** an isopropyl carbonate, (CH3)2CH–O–C(=O)–O–, attached to a CH(CH3) carbon. That carbon is bonded to the ester oxygen of the carboxylate (the "proxetil" group).
- **Bicyclic core:** a cephem. A dihydrothiazine ring (S, CH2, C(CH2OCH3)=C, N, and the S-bearing CH) is fused to a β-lactam ring (N, C=O, CH–NH, and the S-bearing CH). The carboxylate ester sits on the ring alkene carbon next to N, and the methoxymethyl group is on the other alkene carbon.
- **Side chain:** an NH–C(=O)–C(=N–OCH3) amide whose imino carbon carries a 2-aminothiazol-4-yl ring.

**Stereochemistry:**
- At the S-bearing CH, the H is on a wedge. Checking the neighbour order S, H, N, C7 gives @@.
- At the CH–NH carbon, the NH bond is hashed, so the H points toward the viewer. Checking the neighbour order C6, H, N, C8 gives @.
- In the oxime, the amide carbon and the OMe are on the same side of the C=N bond, which is the Z geometry. It is written as `/C(=N\OC)`.
