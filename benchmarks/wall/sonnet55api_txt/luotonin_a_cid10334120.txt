This looks like the tryptanthrin-like alkaloid batracylin-type scaffold, specifically a quinazolinone fused to a pyrrolo[2,1-b]quinazoline-like ring. I read it from the drawing as follows.

**Left part:**
- A benzene ring is fused to a six-membered ring.
- That six-membered ring contains the aryl carbon bearing C(=O), the carbonyl carbon, N (the bridgehead nitrogen), a C (amidine carbon), and N= (attached to the aryl carbon).

**Middle and right part:**
- The amidine carbon C(a) is bonded to the bridgehead N and double bonded to the left N. It is also bonded to C(b).
- C(b) is double bonded to the right N and bonded to C(c).
- C(c) is bonded to CH2 and double bonded to CH.
- CH2 is bonded to the bridgehead N.
- The five-membered ring is therefore N–C(a)–C(b)–C(c)–CH2.
- The right N is attached to an aryl carbon of the right benzene ring, which carries the CH at its fused position. This gives a quinoline-type ring: C(b)=N–Ar…Ar–CH=C(c).

**SMILES construction:**
O=C1c2ccccc2N=C3N1CC4=Cc5ccccc5N=C34

**Check on ring closures:**
- C1 is the carbonyl carbon.
- c2ccccc2 is the left benzene ring, and its last aromatic atom is bonded to N.
- N=C3 is the left imine nitrogen and C(a).
- N1 closes the ring to the carbonyl and is the bridgehead nitrogen.
- C is the CH2.
- C4 is C(c), and =C is the CH.
- c5ccccc5 is the right benzene ring.
- N=C34 gives the right N double bonded to C(b), which closes ring 3 to C(a) and ring 4 to C(c).

C(c) has a double bond to CH, a single bond to CH2 and a single bond to C(b), so its valence is fine. C(b) has a double bond to N, and single bonds to C(a) and C(c), so it is fine too.
