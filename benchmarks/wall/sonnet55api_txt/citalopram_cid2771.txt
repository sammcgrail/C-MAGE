The drawing shows citalopram, read as follows:

- A dimethylamino group (N with two methyl carbons) is attached to a three-carbon chain.
- The chain ends at a quaternary carbon that carries a 4-fluorophenyl ring.
- The same quaternary carbon is bonded to an oxygen and to a fused benzene ring, which together with a CH2 form a dihydroisobenzofuran (five-membered ring: quaternary C, O, CH2, and two aromatic carbons).
- A nitrile (C#N) sits on the fused benzene ring.

For the nitrile position, the benzene ring atoms run from the carbon bonded to the quaternary C (C3a), through the ring-fusion carbon bonded to the CH2 (C7a), then a CH, then the carbon bearing C#N, then two more CH carbons. The C#N carbon is therefore separated from the CH2-side fusion carbon by one CH. That places it para to the quaternary-C-side fusion carbon, as in citalopram.

The quaternary carbon has no wedge or hash bonds in the drawing, so I include no stereochemistry.
