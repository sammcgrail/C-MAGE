The drawing shows a four-carbon chain with these features:

- A methyl carbon on the left, joined by a wedge bond to a carbon that carries an OH group.
- That carbon is bonded to a second carbon, which carries NH2 on a wedge bond.
- This second carbon is bonded to a carboxylic acid group, C(=O)OH.

This is threonine, so I assigned the stereochemistry from the wedges (hydrogens implicit, pointing away from the viewer).

**Carbon bearing OH:** The priorities are OH, then the carbon bearing N, then CH3, then H. Viewed from the front, OH (up), the N-bearing carbon (lower right) and CH3 (lower left) run clockwise. This gives R.

**Carbon bearing NH2:** The priorities are N, then the carboxyl carbon, then the OH-bearing carbon, then H. Viewed from the front, N (down), the carboxyl carbon (upper right) and the OH-bearing carbon (upper left) run counterclockwise. This gives S.

The molecule is therefore (2S,3R)-2-amino-3-hydroxybutanoic acid, which is L-threonine. I checked that the SMILES below encodes R at the OH carbon and S at the NH2 carbon.

SMILES: C[C@@H](O)[C@H](N)C(=O)O
