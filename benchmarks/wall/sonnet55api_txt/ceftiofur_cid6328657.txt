I read this as ceftiofur: a cephalosporin core with a 2-aminothiazole, a methoxyimino group and a furoyl thioester on the side chain.

- **Left side:** a 2-aminothiazole is attached through its C4 to a C=N–OMe group. That carbon is bonded to the amide C(=O)NH.
- **Oxime geometry:** in the drawing the OMe is on the opposite side of the C=N from the thiazole, so I wrote it as `CO/N=C(/C1=CSC(N)=N1)`.
- **Core:** a β-lactam (the C=O at the bottom) is fused to a six-membered ring containing S, CH2, C(CH2S…)=C, and N. The C=C carbon next to N carries the COOH.
- **Right side:** the CH2–S–C(=O)–2-furyl thioester.
- **Stereocentres:** the two ring-fusion carbons are the only ones with wedge/hash bonds.
  - The carbon bearing NH has a wedge to NH, which I wrote as `[C@H]1`.
  - The carbon bonded to S has a hashed H, which I wrote as `[C@H]2`.
  - I derived both from the drawing rather than copying a known SMILES.
