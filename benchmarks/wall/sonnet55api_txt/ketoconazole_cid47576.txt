I read this as the ketoconazole skeleton, with both stereocentres taken from the hashed bonds.

**Skeleton**
- An acetyl group on a piperazine nitrogen (the left end).
- The other piperazine nitrogen bonded to a para-substituted benzene ring.
- That ring carries an O–CH2 link to C4 of a 1,3-dioxolane.
- The dioxolane C2 carries a 2,4-dichlorophenyl ring and a CH2 linked to an imidazole N1.

**Stereochemistry**
- **Dioxolane C4:** the hashed bond goes to the CH2O group, so that group points away from the viewer and the implicit H points toward the viewer. Working through the neighbour order in the SMILES gives `[C@@H]`.
- **Dioxolane C2 (ketal carbon):** the hashed bond goes to the CH2–imidazole group. Working through the neighbour order in the SMILES gives `[C@]`.

I derived both labels from the drawing's geometry rather than from a remembered ketoconazole SMILES. The result may therefore be the enantiomer of the commonly quoted cis-(2S,4R) form.

SMILES: CC(=O)N1CCN(CC1)c1ccc(cc1)OC[C@@H]2CO[C@](Cn3ccnc3)(O2)c2ccc(Cl)cc2Cl
