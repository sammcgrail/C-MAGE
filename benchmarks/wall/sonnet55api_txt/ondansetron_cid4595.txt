This is ondansetron, drawn with plain bonds and no wedges, so no stereochemistry is included.

Reading the drawing region by region:

- **Imidazole:** the ring has an N= (upper left), a C bearing a methyl group, and an N attached to the CH2 linker, plus two CH carbons. The ring runs C(CH3)=N–CH=CH–N.
- **Linker:** the imidazole N connects through a CH2 to the CH of the cyclohexanone ring.
- **Cyclohexanone ring:** CH–C(=O)–C(=C)–…, where the C(=O) joins the C3 carbon of the indole. The other indole carbon (C2, attached to N) joins a CH2–CH2 that returns to the CH.
- **Indole:** the N carries a methyl group and is fused to the benzene ring.

SMILES: Cc1nccn1CC1CCc2n(C)c3ccccc3c2C1=O

I wrote this as the ring-closure form: C1 is the CH, then CC gives the two ring CH2 groups, c2 is the indole C2 bonded to n(C), c3ccccc3 is the benzene ring, c2 is the indole C3, and C1=O closes the ketone back onto the CH.
