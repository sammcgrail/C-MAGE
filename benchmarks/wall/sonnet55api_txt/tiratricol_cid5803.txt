The drawing shows a diaryl ether with an acetic acid side chain, which is the thyroid-hormone-like skeleton of tiratricol-type compounds (here, 3,3',5-triiodothyroacetic acid).

**Right ring.** Starting from the carbon bearing the CH2COOH group (the para position), the ring has an H-bearing carbon, then a carbon bearing I, then the carbon bearing the ether O, then a second carbon bearing I, then another H-bearing carbon. The ether O sits para to the CH2COOH group and is flanked by the two iodines.

**Left ring.** The carbon attached to the ether O has an H-bearing carbon on one side and a ring CH on the other. Going around the ring: the carbon bearing I, then the carbon bearing OH, then two CH carbons back to the start. So the OH is para to the ether O, with one iodine ortho to the OH.

**Side chain.** CH2 then C(=O)OH.

Written as SMILES:

OC(=O)Cc1cc(I)c(Oc2ccc(O)c(I)c2)c(I)c1

Atom check: the right ring c1 (bearing CH2), c (H), c(I), c(O-aryl), c(I), c1 (H) gives 6 carbons. The left ring c2 (bearing O), c (H), c (H), c(O) (bearing OH), c(I), c2 (H) gives 6 carbons. This matches the drawing.
