The image shows zinc pyrithione drawn as separate ions: two pyridine N-oxide rings, each carrying a thiolate, plus one Zn2+.

Reading each ring:
- Each ring has six vertices: N+ and five carbons.
- N+ carries an O- substituent.
- The carbon next to N+ carries S-.
- In the top ring, the double bonds are N+=C (upper left), C=C (left side), and C=C (bottom, to the S-bearing carbon).

Writing one ring as SMILES, starting from the thiolate:
[S-]c1cccc[n+]1[O-]

In the Kekulé form this is [O-][n+]1ccccc1[S-]. With explicit bonds it becomes [O-][N+]1=CC=CC=C1[S-].

Checking the drawn bonds against this:
- N+=C(top) is a double bond.
- The top carbon is single-bonded to the upper-left carbon.
- The upper-left carbon is double-bonded to the lower-left carbon (the left-side C=C).
- The lower-left carbon is single-bonded to the bottom carbon.
- The bottom carbon is double-bonded to the S-bearing carbon.
- The S-bearing carbon is single-bonded to N+.

This matches [O-][N+]1=CC=CC=C1[S-].
