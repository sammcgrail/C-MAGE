The structure is DTPA-conjugated octreotide (pentetreotide). Its parts are a DTPA chelator acylating the N-terminus of D-Phe, then the cyclic peptide Cys–Phe–D-Trp–Lys–Thr–Cys with a disulfide bridge, and a C-terminal threoninol.

I assigned the stereochemistry from the standard octreotide configuration (L-Cys, L-Phe, D-Trp, L-Lys, L-Thr, L-Cys, L-threoninol, D-Phe at the N-terminus). I did not check each wedge and hash in the drawing individually against this.

Reading from the left:
1. DTPA: three N atoms joined by two ethylene links, carrying five CH2COOH arms. The fifth arm is the amide carbonyl, CH2C(=O)–, attached to D-Phe.
2. D-Phe, with its benzyl side chain.
3. Cys, whose CH2–S forms the disulfide to the second Cys.
4. L-Phe.
5. D-Trp, with its indole side chain.
6. L-Lys, with its (CH2)4NH2 side chain.
7. L-Thr.
8. Cys, closing the disulfide ring through the S–S bond.
9. Threoninol, ending in CH2OH and CH(CH3)OH.

I wrote the disulfide as ring-closure label %10 on the two sulfur atoms.
