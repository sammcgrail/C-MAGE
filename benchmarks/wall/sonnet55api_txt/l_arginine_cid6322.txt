Reading the drawing from left to right:

- A carboxylic acid (HO–C=O) is on the left.
- It is attached to the α-carbon, which carries an NH2 on a wedge bond.
- A three-carbon chain (CH2–CH2–CH2) runs from the α-carbon to a nitrogen.
- That nitrogen is double-bonded to a carbon bearing two NH2 groups, which makes a guanidine group.

This is arginine. The only stereocentre is the α-carbon.

For its configuration, the carboxyl carbon is lower left, the wedged NH2 is up and toward the viewer, and the chain CH2 is lower right. The implicit H therefore points away from the viewer. Written as OC(=O)[C@@H](N)CCCN=C(N)N, the neighbour order is COOH, H, N, CH2. With the H at the back, COOH → N → CH2 runs clockwise on the page, which corresponds to @@ in this order. This matches L-arginine.

SMILES: OC(=O)[C@@H](N)CCCN=C(N)N
