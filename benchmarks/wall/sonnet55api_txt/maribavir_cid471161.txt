I read the drawing as a 5,6-dichlorobenzimidazole carrying an isopropylamino group at C2 and a ribofuranose on N1. This matches maribavir.

- **Benzimidazole:** The fused bicycle has two ring nitrogens, one of which is substituted (N1, bonded to the sugar). Two Cl atoms sit on adjacent carbons of the benzene ring.
- **C2 substituent:** The NH is bonded to a CH that carries two methyls. One methyl is at the lower left, and the other is drawn upward and overlaps the lower OH label, so it is an isopropyl group.
- **Sugar:** The five-membered ring has one ring O, with CH2OH at C4' and OH groups at C2' and C3'.
- **Stereochemistry:** The N1 bond at C1' and the CH2OH bond at C4' are both hashed, so they lie on the same face, which makes it β. Both OH groups are wedged, on the opposite face.
- **Handedness:** Going C1'→C2'→C3'→C4'→O is clockwise on the page. In the standard D-ribose Haworth view the base and CH2OH point toward the viewer, but here they point away. So this is the L-sugar, the mirror image of the D-ribonucleoside pattern.
