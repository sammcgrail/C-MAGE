I read the structure as Val-boroPro (talabostat): a valine residue joined by an amide to a pyrrolidine ring that carries a boronic acid, B(OH)₂, at C2.

**Skeleton**
- The left side is an isopropyl group (two CH₃ on one CH). That CH is bonded to the α-carbon, which carries NH₂ and a C(=O) group.
- The C(=O) is bonded to the ring nitrogen. The ring is N, CH₂, CH₂, CH₂, then the CH that bears B(OH)₂ (five atoms in all).

**Stereochemistry (two wedged centres)**
- The α-carbon has NH₂ on a hashed bond (pointing back), so the implicit H points toward the viewer. Working through the neighbour order iPr, H, N, C(=O) gives `[C@H]`. This matches L-valine.
- The ring CH has a bold wedge to boron (pointing toward the viewer), so its H points back. Working through the neighbour order CH₂, H, N, B gives `[C@H]` when the ring is written as `N1CCC[C@H]1B(O)O`.
