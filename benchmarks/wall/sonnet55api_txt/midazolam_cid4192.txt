This structure is midazolam, which I identified from the drawing and then wrote out atom by atom.

**Imidazole ring (left):**
- A methyl group is attached to a carbon (C2) that is double-bonded to N (N3) and single-bonded to the bridgehead N.
- N3 connects to CH (C4), which is double-bonded to C5.
- C5 bonds to the bridgehead N and to the CH2 carbon.

**Diazepine ring:**
- The bridgehead N bonds to a benzene carbon.
- The two fused benzene carbons are joined to C(=N), which bears the fluorophenyl group.
- The N of C=N bonds to the CH2, which bonds back to C5.

**Chlorobenzene ring:**
- Going around the ring from the fusion carbon bonded to N (ring atom a): a, then CH, then CH, then C-Cl, then CH, then the fusion carbon bonded to C=N.
- In the image, the fusion carbon at (549,765) bonds to N, and the fusion carbon at (743,804) bonds to the C=N carbon.
- Cl sits on the carbon at (811,469), which is attached to (874,656), and that carbon attaches to (743,804). So Cl is on the carbon meta to the fusion carbon bearing C=N, reached via one CH.
