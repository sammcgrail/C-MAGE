This image shows pyrantel pamoate as two separate components, pamoic acid and pyrantel.

**Pamoic acid.** Two naphthalene units are joined by a CH2 bridge. Each has OH at C2 and COOH at C3, with the CH2 attached at C1.

For one half, the SMILES fragment is OC(=O)c1cc2ccccc2c(Cc...)c1O.

Full pamoic acid: OC(=O)c1cc2ccccc2c(Cc2c(O)c(C(=O)O)cc3ccccc23)c1O

**Pyrantel.** A 1-methyl-1,4,5,6-tetrahydropyrimidine ring carries a 2-vinyl-thiophene substituent at C2. The double bond is drawn with E geometry, because the ring and the thiophene lie on opposite sides of the C=C.

Written with E geometry: CN1CCCN=C1/C=C/c1cccs1

Checking the stereo notation: in C1/C=C/c1, the ring carbon C1 comes before the first vinyl carbon with a "/" bond, and the thiophene comes after the second vinyl carbon with a "/" bond. That is trans, so E, which matches the drawing.

The two components are combined as one dot-separated SMILES.

SMILES: OC(=O)c1cc2ccccc2c(Cc2c(O)c(C(=O)O)cc3ccccc23)c1O.CN1CCCN=C1/C=C/c1cccs1
