I read the molecule as a pyrrolidine whose nitrogen is bonded to a benzene ring. The ring carbon next to the nitrogen carries a CH(OH)CF3 group.

**Aryl ring:** The benzene ring carries a nitrile (C≡N) on one carbon, and a CF3 group on the adjacent carbon. The pyrrolidine nitrogen sits on the ring carbon that is meta to the nitrile and para to the nitrile-bearing carbon's neighbour, i.e. para to the nitrile carbon's position across the ring from the CF3 side (SMILES: `N#Cc1ccc(cc1C(F)(F)F)N...`).

**Pyrrolidine:** The five-membered ring contains N and four carbons. The carbon adjacent to N (C2) bears the side chain, and its wedge bond runs to the ring CH2 (C3).

**Side chain:** C2 is bonded to a carbinol carbon bearing OH (wedge bond) and CF3.

**Stereochemistry:** Only these two centres are drawn with wedges, so only they get stereo marks.
- At C2, with the implicit H pointing away, the sequence N → C3 → carbinol C runs counterclockwise. In the SMILES neighbour order (C3, H, N, carbinol C) this corresponds to `@@`.
- At the carbinol carbon, with H pointing away, the sequence C2 → OH → CF3 runs clockwise. In the neighbour order (C2, H, O, CF3) this corresponds to `@@`.

SMILES: N#Cc1ccc(cc1C(F)(F)F)N1CCC[C@@H]1[C@@H](O)C(F)(F)F
