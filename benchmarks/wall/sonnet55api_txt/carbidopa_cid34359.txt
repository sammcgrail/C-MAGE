I read the drawing as carbidopa. It has a central quaternary carbon carrying four groups:

- a methyl group on a hashed bond;
- a hydrazine group, NH–NH2;
- a CH2 linked to a 3,4-dihydroxyphenyl ring;
- a carboxylic acid, C(=O)OH.

The ring is a benzene with its two OH groups on adjacent carbons, one para and one meta to the CH2 attachment point.

For the stereocentre, the methyl is hashed, so it points away from the viewer. Seen from the front, the order N (upper right) → CH2 (lower right) → COOH (lower left) runs clockwise. SMILES chirality is read looking from the first neighbour (the methyl) toward the centre, which is from behind the page. From there the same sequence appears counterclockwise, which gives `@` for the neighbour order methyl, N, CH2, COOH.

As a check, the CIP priorities are N > COOH > CH2 > CH3. With the methyl at the back, N → COOH → CH2 runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S, which matches carbidopa.

SMILES: C[C@](NN)(Cc1ccc(O)c(O)c1)C(=O)O
