I read the structure as an isavuconazonium-type molecule, a 1,2,4-triazol-4-ium prodrug. I assembled the SMILES region by region and then worked out the two stereocentres from the wedge and hash bonds.

**Regions of the drawing**
- **Left side chain:** a sarcosine-type ester, CH3-NH-CH2-C(=O)-O-CH2, attached to the 3-position of a pyridine.
- **Pyridine linkage:** the 2-position of the pyridine carries N(CH3)-C(=O)-O-CH(CH3)-, a carbamate that attaches to the triazolium nitrogen N4⁺.
- **Triazolium ring:** five atoms, N1(chain)–CH=N⁺(substituted)–CH=N–back to N1. The chain on N1 is a CH2 leading to the quaternary carbon.
- **Quaternary carbon:** it carries OH on a wedge, a 2,5-difluorophenyl group, the CH2 to the triazole, and the CH(CH3) carbon.
- **CH(CH3) carbon:** its methyl is on a hash, and it carries a thiazol-2-yl group. The thiazole bears a 4-(4-cyanophenyl) substituent.

**Stereochemistry**
- **CH(CH3) carbon:** the methyl is hashed, so H points toward the viewer. Taking the neighbours in the order methyl, H, thiazole, quaternary C, this gives `[C@@H]`.
- **Quaternary carbon:** the OH is wedged. Taking the neighbours in the order CH, O, CH2, aryl, this gives `[C@]`.

The triazolium carbon bearing the CH(CH3) group is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label.
