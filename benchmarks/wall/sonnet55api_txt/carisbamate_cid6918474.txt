I read the drawing as a 2-chlorophenyl ring attached to a stereogenic carbon that bears an OH, a CH2–O–C(=O)NH2 carbamate chain and an implicit hydrogen. This is the carisbamate skeleton.

**Structure**
- The benzene ring has six carbons, with Cl on the carbon next to the one carrying the side chain (ortho).
- The side chain is CH(OH)–CH2–O–C(=O)–NH2, a carbamate ester.

**Stereochemistry**
- The OH on the stereocentre is drawn with a hashed bond, so it points away from the viewer. The implicit H therefore points toward the viewer.
- CIP priorities are OH > CH2O > aryl carbon > H. The CH2O carbon (O,H,H) outranks the aryl carbon (C,C,C) because O beats C at the first point of difference.
- On the page, OH (top), then CH2 (lower right), then aryl (lower left) runs clockwise. The H faces the viewer, so the true sense is reversed, which gives counterclockwise, i.e. (S).
- In the SMILES below, `[C@@H]` with neighbours in the order CH2, H, O, aryl also works out to (S), so it matches the drawing.

SMILES: NC(=O)OC[C@@H](O)c1ccccc1Cl
