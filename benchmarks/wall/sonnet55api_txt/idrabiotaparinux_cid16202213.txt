I read this as a biotin–aminohexanoyl-linked pentasaccharide with a methyl glycoside at the far end. It looks like a fondaparinux-type skeleton with extra methyl ethers and O-sulfates. The stereocentres were assigned from the wedges and hashes by hand, so some could be wrong. I also did not re-check the biotin core against the drawing and used the natural biotin configuration.

Reading the structure left to right:

1. **Biotin end:** a bicyclic cyclic urea fused to a tetrahydrothiophene, carrying a four-carbon chain to an amide C=O.
2. **Linker:** that amide nitrogen is attached to a chain of five CH2 groups ending in a second C=O. This is a 6-aminohexanoyl spacer, and it acylates the nitrogen on the first sugar.
3. **Residue A (aminosugar, left):** 2-NH-acyl, 3-OMe, 4-OMe and a 6-CH2OSO3H.
4. **Residue B (uronic acid):** COOH, 2-OMe and 3-OMe, linked through its C4 to residue A's anomeric oxygen.
5. **Residue C (glucosamine-type sugar):** 2-OSO3H, 3-OSO3H and 6-CH2OSO3H, linked through its C4 to residue B's anomeric oxygen.
6. **Residue D (uronic acid):** the same pattern as B (COOH, 2-OMe, 3-OMe).
7. **Residue E (terminal sugar):** methyl glycoside at C1, 2-OSO3H, 3-OSO3H and 6-CH2OSO3H.

Each stereocentre was assigned from its wedge or hash bond, with the implicit H taken as the opposite face.
