I read the molecule as a 14-membered macrolactone fused to a dihydroxybenzene ring, with the ring built up as follows.

- **Lactone side:** the ring oxygen is attached to a CH bearing a methyl group, drawn on a bold wedge. The lactone carbonyl (C=O) sits on the aromatic ring.
- **Chain from the methyl-bearing CH:** three CH2 groups lead to a CH bearing OH, which is drawn on a hashed bond.
- **Chain from the CH–OH to the aromatic ring:** five CH2 groups lead to the aromatic carbon.
- **Aromatic ring:** it carries two OH groups, both meta to the CH2 attachment. One OH is ortho to the carbonyl-bearing carbon and the other is para to it. This is the zeranol/zearalanol skeleton.

**Stereochemistry**
- The methyl-bearing carbon has the methyl on a wedge and the implicit H at the back. Its priorities are O > ring CH2 > CH3, which gives S. This is written `[C@H]` in the SMILES order below.
- The carbinol carbon has the OH on a hash and the implicit H toward the viewer. The CH2 branch on the lactone side outranks the other because it reaches the C(O)(C)H carbon first, so the priorities are OH > lactone-side CH2 > aromatic-side CH2. That gives R, which is written `[C@H]` in the SMILES order below.

SMILES: C[C@H]1CCC[C@H](O)CCCCCc2cc(O)cc(O)c2C(=O)O1
