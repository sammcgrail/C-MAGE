I read the structure as aclidinium, a quaternary ammonium cation built on a 1-azoniabicyclo[2.2.2]octane core.

- **Left side:** a phenyl ring is joined through an ether oxygen to a three-carbon chain (O–CH2CH2CH2), which is attached to the N⁺.
- **Core:** the N⁺ carries the propyl chain and three ring CH2 branches. Two of these run through CH2CH2 to the bridgehead CH. The third is a CH2 leading to the CH that bears the ester oxygen. This is the quinuclidine skeleton with the ester at C3.
- **Right side:** the ester is O–C(=O)–C(OH) with two thiophen-2-yl groups on the carbinol carbon.
- **Stereochemistry:** the only wedge is the H on the ester-bearing carbon (C3). The priorities are O > CH2–N⁺ > bridgehead CH > H. With H pointing toward the viewer, O → CH2N⁺ → bridgehead CH appears counterclockwise, so the true sense is clockwise and the centre is R. The SMILES below, with [C@H] in that atom order, also works out to R. The bridgehead CH and the carbinol carbon have no wedges or hashes, so they carry no stereo marks.

SMILES: O=C(O[C@H]1C[N+]2(CCCOc3ccccc3)CCC1CC2)C(O)(c1cccs1)c1cccs1
