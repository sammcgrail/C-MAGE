This is nitrofurantoin: a 5-nitrofuran linked through a CH=N bond to the N-amino group of a hydantoin ring.

**Furan ring.** The ring atoms are O, C5 (bearing the nitro group), C4, C3 and C2. C2 carries the CH=N substituent. The nitro group is drawn charge-separated, as [N+](=O)[O-].

**Linker and hydantoin ring.** The CH=N unit connects to N1 of the hydantoin. N1 is bonded to C2(=O), which is bonded to NH. NH is bonded to C4(=O), which is bonded to CH2, closing back to N1.

**Stereochemistry.** The C=N bond is drawn with plain bonds, but its geometry is visible. Going left to right, the furan attaches to the CH carbon at about (620, 903) and the CH is at about (783, 908). The N is at about (868, 772) and the next N at about (1010, 775). The furan substituent on the CH sits lower-left of the CH, while the N1 substituent on the imine N continues to the right at roughly the same height as that N. The furan is therefore at the bottom-left and N1 at the upper-right, which are on opposite sides of the C=N bond. This is the E configuration, matching nitrofurantoin.

In SMILES, `/C=N/N` means the substituents before the C and after the N are trans. I write the furan carbon as `c1.../C=N/N1...`, which gives the E isomer.
