I read the structure as a tenatoprazole-like molecule: an imidazo[4,5-b]pyridine core joined through a sulfoxide and a CH2 to a substituted pyridine.

**Pyridine ring (left):**
- The ring atoms are N, then CH, then C(CH3), then C(OCH3), then C(CH3), then C(CH2S), which closes back to N.
- Going around from the CH2-bearing carbon: C2 carries CH2, N1 is next, C6 is the CH, C5 carries the methyl at the far left, C4 carries OMe, and C3 carries the methyl pointing down-right.
- Written out: Cc1cnc(CS(=O)...)c(C)c1OC.

**Sulfoxide linker:**
- The CH2 connects to S(=O), and S connects to C2 of the imidazole.

**Imidazopyridine (right):**
- C2 of the imidazole is bonded to N (the "N" label) and to NH.
- The N joins C3a, which is bonded to the pyridine-type N. That N is bonded to C(OMe), then CH, then CH, then C7a, which bears the NH.
- Written out: c1nc2nc(OC)ccc2[nH]1, with the sulfoxide attached at c1.

**Full SMILES:**
Cc1cnc(CS(=O)c2nc3nc(OC)ccc3[nH]2)c(C)c1OC

Checking the ring closures: c2 (imidazole C2), n, c3 (C3a), n, c(OC), c, c, c3 (C7a, closing the ring at C3a), [nH], and the closure back to c2. The fused ring system is consistent.
