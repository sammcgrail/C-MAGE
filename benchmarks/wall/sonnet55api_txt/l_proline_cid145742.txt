The drawing shows proline: a five-membered ring made of one NH and four carbons, with a carboxylic acid (C=O and OH) on the ring carbon next to nitrogen.

**Ring and substituents**
- The ring has N–H at the top, two plain CH2 carbons on the left and bottom, and the substituted carbon at the right.
- The substituted ring carbon carries the COOH group, drawn with a single wedge. This is the only stereocentre.

**Stereochemistry**
- The wedge is narrow at the ring carbon and widens toward the carboxyl carbon, so the COOH points toward the viewer and the implicit H points away.
- CIP priorities at the stereocentre are N > COOH > ring CH2 > H.
- On the page, N is at upper left, COOH at right and CH2 at lower left. N → COOH → CH2 therefore runs clockwise with H in the back, which gives R. This is D-proline.

**SMILES check**
- For `OC(=O)[C@H]1CCCN1`, the neighbour order at the stereocentre is COOH, H, N (via ring closure 1), CH2.
- `@` means H → N → CH2 runs counterclockwise when viewed from COOH. Swapping to put H first turns this into `@@`, so COOH → N → CH2 is clockwise when viewed from H.
- Viewed with H pointing away, that rotation reverses, so N → COOH → CH2 is clockwise, which is R. This matches the drawing.
