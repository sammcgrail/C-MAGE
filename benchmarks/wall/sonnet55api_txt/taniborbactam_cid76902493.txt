I read the drawing as a benzoxaborine core (a benzene ring fused to a six-membered ring containing O and B) carrying three substituents.

1. **Left chain:** H2N–CH2–CH2–NH– is attached to a cyclohexane ring. The ring's 1,4-position carries a CH2–C(=O)–NH– group, which links to the stereocentre of the core.
2. **Fused ring:** the ring runs C(H)(NHC=O)–CH2–aromatic C, then the aromatic C bearing the ring oxygen, then O, then B(OH), and back to the C(H).
3. **Aromatic ring:** a COOH sits on the aromatic carbon next to the one bearing the ring oxygen.
4. **Stereochemistry:** the C–N bond at the ring carbon next to boron is a wedge. The cyclohexane has plain bonds, so it gets no stereo marks. Looking with the implicit H pointing away, N (wedge), CH2 and B run N → CH2 → B clockwise. Written as N[C@H]1C…B1, this corresponds to `[C@H]`.
