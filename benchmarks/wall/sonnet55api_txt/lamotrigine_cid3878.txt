The drawing is a 1,2,4-triazine ring carrying two amino groups and a dichlorophenyl group. This matches lamotrigine.

**Triazine ring.** Going around the ring from the left:
- A carbon at the left bears NH2 (C3).
- That carbon is double-bonded to the upper N, which is bonded to the upper-right carbon bearing NH2 (C5).
- C5 is double-bonded to the carbon at lower right (C6), which carries the aryl group.
- C6 is single-bonded to the lower-right N, which is double-bonded to the lower-left N.
- The lower-left N is single-bonded back to the left carbon.

This gives the ring Nc(N)... written as: C3(N)=N–C5(N)=C6(Ar)–N=N–C3.

**Aryl ring.** The attachment carbon is bonded to a carbon bearing Cl (ortho), and the next carbon also bears Cl (meta). That is a 2,3-dichlorophenyl group.

**SMILES.** Writing the triazine as aromatic: Nc1nnc(-c2cccc(Cl)c2Cl)c(N)n1.

Checking the ring order: c1 (C3, NH2), n, n, c (C6, aryl), c (C5, NH2), n (the upper N) closing back to c1. This matches the drawing.

For the aryl ring, c2cccc(Cl)c2Cl starts at the attachment carbon. The last atom, c2Cl, is the ortho carbon bearing Cl, and the c(Cl) before it is the meta carbon bearing Cl. This is correct.
