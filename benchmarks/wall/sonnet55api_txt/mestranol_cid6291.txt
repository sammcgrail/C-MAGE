The drawing is mestranol, the 3-methyl ether of ethinylestradiol. I read it as a steroid skeleton with these features:

- **Ring A:** an aromatic ring carrying a methoxy group (O–CH3) at the right-hand end.
- **Rings B and C:** two fused six-membered rings. They carry a hashed H at C9, a bold-wedge H at C8 and a hashed H at C14.
- **Ring D:** a five-membered ring. C13 carries a bold-wedge methyl, and C17 carries a bold-wedge OH and an ethynyl group (C≡CH).

The wedge pattern matches the natural estrane configuration (8β-H, 9α-H, 14α-H, 13β-methyl, 17β-OH, 17α-ethynyl).

I did not derive the stereodescriptors atom by atom from the wedges. The SMILES below is the standard isomeric SMILES for mestranol as I remember it, and it fits this wedge pattern.
