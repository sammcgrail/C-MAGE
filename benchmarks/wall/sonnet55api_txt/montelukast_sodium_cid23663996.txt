The image shows montelukast as its sodium salt. I read it region by region:

- **Left aryl group:** an ortho-disubstituted benzene carrying a C(CH3)2OH group and a CH2CH2 chain.
- **Stereocentre:** a carbon bonded to sulfur, the CH2 chain (hashed bond), a 1,3-disubstituted benzene, and an implicit H.
- **Thioether side:** S–CH2 attached to a cyclopropane carbon that also bears CH2–COO⁻.
- **Right side:** the benzene's other substituent is a trans CH=CH linked to the 2-position of a 7-chloroquinoline.
- **Counter-ion:** Na⁺.

For the stereocentre, the priorities are S > aryl > CH2 > H. The hashed CH2 points away, so the implicit H points toward the viewer. On the page, S (lower left) → aryl (lower right) → CH2 (top) runs counterclockwise. Because H faces the viewer, the true sense is reversed, giving clockwise, which is R.

In the SMILES, the neighbour order at the stereocentre is CH2, H, S, aryl. Checking this ordering gives `@@` for R, so that is the tag used below.
