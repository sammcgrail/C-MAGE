I read the drawing as a cephalosporin (cefaclor-type) skeleton. It has a β-lactam fused to a six-membered dihydrothiazine ring that carries Cl and a carboxylic acid. The 7-amino position bears a phenylglycyl amide side chain.

Stereochemistry comes from the three drawn stereo bonds:
- The side-chain carbon has a hashed NH2, so the NH2 points away from the viewer and the implicit H towards the viewer. This gives `N[C@H](c1ccccc1)C(=O)...` in the written atom order.
- C7 of the β-lactam has a wedged N, giving `N[C@@H]1C(=O)...`.
- The ring-fusion carbon C6 has a hashed H, giving `S[C@H]12`.

Connectivity check: C7 is bonded to the amide N, the lactam C=O and C6, and C6 closes onto both C7 (ring 1) and the lactam N (ring 2). The sequence N–C(COOH)=C(Cl)–CH2–S–C6 completes the six-membered ring.

SMILES: N[C@H](c1ccccc1)C(=O)N[C@@H]1C(=O)N2C(C(=O)O)=C(Cl)CS[C@H]12
