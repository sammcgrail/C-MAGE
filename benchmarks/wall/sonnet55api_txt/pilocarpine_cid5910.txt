I read the molecule as pilocarpine: a γ-butyrolactone ring carrying an ethyl group and a (1-methylimidazol-5-yl)methyl group.

**Skeleton**
- The five-membered lactone ring consists of the ring oxygen, a CH2, two CH carbons and the C=O carbon.
- The ethyl group is attached to the CH next to the carbonyl.
- The CH–CH2 link carries the imidazole, which is bonded through its carbon next to the N-methyl nitrogen. That nitrogen bears the methyl group, and the second ring nitrogen is drawn with a double bond to its neighbouring carbon.

**Stereochemistry**
Both ring CH centres have a hashed bond, one to the ethyl CH2 and one to the CH2 of the imidazolylmethyl group, with the implicit H pointing toward the viewer. Working through the neighbour order in the SMILES string for each centre, both come out as `@@`.
