I read the drawing as a cyclic lipopeptide, a lipidated peptide with a ring of ten residues. I traced it from the lipid tail around the ring and assigned each stereocentre from its wedge or hash.

**Lipid tail:** an iso-branched chain (terminal isopropyl, then 7 CH2) ends in a cis CH=CH, then CH2 and the C(=O) of an amide. This acyl group sits on an Asn residue.

**Ring, in order from the Asn:**
1. Asn: its side chain is CH2C(N)=O, and its backbone C(=O) links to residue 2.
2. 2,3-Diaminobutyric acid (Dab). Its α-N carries the Asn, its C(=O) goes to the pipecolic N, and its β-carbon (with CH3) has an NH that goes to the proline C(=O). This closes the macrocycle.
3. Pipecolic acid (six-membered ring), whose C(=O) links to residue 4.
4. 3-Methylaspartic acid, with the methyl and COOH on its β-carbon.
5. Asp, with a CH2COOH side chain.
6. Gly.
7. Asp, with a CH2COOH side chain.
8. Gly.
9. A second Dab, with its β-carbon bearing CH3 and a free NH2.
10. Val.
11. Pro, whose C(=O) closes back onto the first Dab.

**Stereochemistry:** I assigned each centre from its wedge or hash and from the position of the implicit H. That gives L-Asn, L-Pro and L-Asp. The pipecolic α-carbon comes out with the opposite (D-type) configuration from L-Pro in my reading of the drawing.
