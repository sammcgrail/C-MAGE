I read the molecule as a 1-(4-cyanophenyl)urea whose second nitrogen is attached to C4 of an N-methylpiperidine, with a benzimidazol-2-yl group on C2 of the piperidine.

**Fragments**
- A para-substituted benzene ring carries a nitrile (C≡N) at one end and an NH at the other.
- That NH is bonded to a C=O, which is bonded to a second NH. This is the urea.
- The second NH is attached by a bold wedge to C4 of the piperidine ring.
- The piperidine ring has six vertices: N1 carries a methyl, C2 carries the benzimidazole, C3 and C5 are CH2, and C4 carries the urea NH.
- The benzimidazole is attached through its C2 by a hashed bond to piperidine C2. Its ring has one NH and one =N.

**Stereochemistry**
Only the two wedged or hashed centres are stereocentres in the drawing.
- Piperidine C4 has the urea N on a wedge, so the implicit H points away. Viewed from the front, N → C3 → C5 runs clockwise. In the SMILES neighbour order N, H, C3, C5 this gives `@@`.
- Piperidine C2 has the benzimidazole on a hash. Viewed from the H side (the front), N1 → C3 → benzimidazole runs clockwise. In the SMILES neighbour order N, H, benzimidazole, C3 this also gives `@@`.
