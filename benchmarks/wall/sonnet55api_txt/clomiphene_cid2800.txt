Reading the drawing gives a diethylaminoethoxy group (Et2N–CH2CH2–O–) on a para-substituted benzene ring. That ring is attached to a C=C double bond, whose other carbon carries a chlorine and a phenyl. The first alkene carbon also carries a second, unsubstituted phenyl. This is the clomiphene skeleton.

For the double-bond geometry, the drawing places the Cl and the alkoxyphenyl ring on the same side of the C=C. On the chlorine-bearing carbon, Cl outranks phenyl. On the other carbon, the alkoxyphenyl outranks phenyl because its para carbon bears an O. The two higher-priority groups are therefore cis, so the double bond is Z.

In the SMILES, `Cl/C(...)=C(\Ar)...` puts Cl and the alkoxyphenyl cis.

SMILES: Cl/C(c1ccccc1)=C(\c1ccc(OCCN(CC)CC)cc1)c1ccccc1
