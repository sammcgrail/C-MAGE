This structure is raltegravir. I read it region by region.

- **Central ring:** a pyrimidinone. N1 carries a methyl and is bonded to C2 and to the C=O carbon. C2 bears the C(CH3)2 group and is double-bonded to N3. N3 connects to C4, which carries the carboxamide. C4 is double-bonded to C5, which carries the OH. C5 connects to C6, the carbonyl carbon, which closes back to N1.
- **Right side:** the C4 carboxamide is C(=O)NH–CH2–C6H4–F, a 4-fluorobenzyl group.
- **Left side:** C(CH3)2–NH–C(=O) leads to a 1,3,4-oxadiazole. The ring carbon attached to the carbonyl is bonded to O and double-bonded to N, then N–N, then a C(CH3) that closes to O.

Writing the SMILES from this:

Cn1c(=O)c(O)c(C(=O)NCc2ccc(F)cc2)nc1C(C)(C)NC(=O)c1nnc(C)o1

Checking the oxadiazole ring: c1(carbonyl-bearing) is bonded to O and to N; N–N; then c(C) bonded to O. In the SMILES, c1nnc(C)o1 gives C(carbonyl)–N, N–N, N–C(Me), C(Me)–O, and O back to C1. This matches the drawing.
