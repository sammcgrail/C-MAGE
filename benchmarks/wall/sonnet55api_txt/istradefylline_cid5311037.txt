This is a xanthine core with a styryl group, i.e. istradefylline (KW-6002), except that the N-ethyl groups are on N1 and N3 instead of the usual (E)-8-(3,4-dimethoxystyryl)-1,3-diethyl-7-methylxanthine pattern. Reading it from the drawing:

- **Purine core:** The six-membered ring has N(Et) at top right, C(=O), N(Et) at right, C(=O) at bottom, and two fused carbons. The five-membered ring has N= at the top left, C(styryl), and N(Me) at the bottom.
- **Substituents:** The N at top is ethylated, the N at right is ethylated, and the N at the bottom of the imidazole is methylated.
- **Styryl group:** The C8 carbon carries a CH=CH link to a benzene ring bearing two OMe groups. The ring attachment is at the position para to the lower OMe. The upper OMe is at the position meta to the attachment, and the lower OMe is para to it, giving the 3,4-dimethoxy pattern.
- **Alkene geometry:** The double bond is drawn as trans, so it is E.

SMILES, written from N3 (top ethyl N): CCN1C(=O)N(CC)C(=O)c2c1nc(/C=C/c1ccc(OC)c(OC)c1)n2C

Check on the ring closure: N1 (top, ethyl) is bonded to C(=O), and that carbonyl carbon is bonded to the right N(Et). That N is bonded to the bottom C(=O), which is bonded to c2, the fused carbon next to N-Me. The other fused carbon, c1, is bonded to the top N and to the imidazole N=. Then "nc(...)n2C" closes the five-membered ring. This matches the drawing.
