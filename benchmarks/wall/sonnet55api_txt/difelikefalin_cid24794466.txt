I read the molecule as a linear pentapeptide-like chain: Phe, Phe, Leu, Lys, then a 4-aminopiperidine-4-carboxylic acid whose ring nitrogen is acylated by the Lys carbonyl.

**Connectivity**
- The top-right Phe has a free NH2 on its alpha carbon and a benzyl side chain. Its carbonyl is bonded to the NH of the second Phe.
- The second Phe carries a second benzyl group and its carbonyl is bonded to the Leu NH.
- Leu has an isobutyl side chain (CH2–CH(CH3)2) and its carbonyl is bonded to the Lys NH.
- Lys has a four-carbon chain ending in NH2, and its carbonyl is bonded to the piperidine nitrogen.
- The piperidine ring has six vertices (one N, five C). The carbon opposite the nitrogen carries both NH2 and COOH.

**Stereochemistry**
I assigned each alpha carbon from its wedge or hash bond using CIP priorities (N > C=O > side-chain CH2 > H):
- Phe 1 has a wedged NH2 and comes out R.
- Phe 2 has a hashed CH2 and comes out R.
- Leu has a wedged CH2 and comes out R.
- Lys has a hashed CH2 and comes out R.

All four are D-configured. For this atom ordering (N written first, then H, side chain, C=O), R corresponds to `@`. The piperidine quaternary carbon has two identical ring arms, so it is not a stereocentre.
