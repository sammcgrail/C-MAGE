I read the molecule as a 2-methylpyrazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidine-6-carboxamide whose amide nitrogen carries a CH2–C(CH3)2–NH–CH2–C(=O)– chain ending in a 2-cyanopyrrolidine amide.

**Fused bicycle (left).** The pyrazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidine has a methyl on the pyrazole carbon next to the ring nitrogen. The carboxamide C(=O)N sits on the pyrimidine carbon between the CH= next to the left-hand ring N and the CH= next to the bridgehead N.

**Linker (centre).** The amide NH is followed by CH2, then a quaternary carbon with two methyls, then a secondary NH. That NH is followed by CH2 and a C(=O) that acylates the pyrrolidine nitrogen.

**Pyrrolidine (right).** It is a five-membered ring (N plus four carbons). The carbon next to N bears a nitrile on a wedge bond, so it is the only stereocentre drawn with stereochemistry.

**Stereochemistry.** With H pointing away, the substituents run N → CH2 → CN clockwise. The priorities are N > C#N > CH2, so N → CN → CH2 runs counterclockwise, which makes the centre S. Written as N3CCC[C@H]3C#N, the neighbour order is CH2, H, N, CN, and this corresponds to @.

SMILES: Cc1cc2ncc(C(=O)NCC(C)(C)NCC(=O)N3CCC[C@H]3C#N)cn2n1
