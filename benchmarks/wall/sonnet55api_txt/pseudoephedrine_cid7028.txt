I read this as a phenylpropanolamine skeleton: a benzene ring on a carbinol carbon bearing OH, next to a carbon bearing a methyl group and an NH–CH3 group. This is the ephedrine/pseudoephedrine skeleton.

**Connectivity**
- The ring is a plain phenyl group with three alternating double bonds.
- The ring is attached to a CH carrying OH, drawn on a hashed bond pointing down.
- That carbon is bonded to a second CH, which carries a hashed methyl and an NH–CH3 group.

**Stereochemistry**
- The CH carrying the methyl and NHMe has priorities N > C(OH) > CH3 > H. The methyl is hashed, so H points toward the viewer. N → C(OH) → CH3 runs clockwise on the page, so reversing for H gives S.
- The CH carrying OH has priorities O > C(N) > phenyl > H. The OH is hashed, so H points toward the viewer. O → C(N) → phenyl runs clockwise on the page, so reversing for H gives S.

This gives (1S,2S), which is pseudoephedrine. I then checked that the SMILES below encodes S at both centres.

SMILES: C[C@H](NC)[C@@H](O)c1ccccc1
