I read this as a venetoclax-type structure in which the gem-dimethyl group on the cyclohexene is replaced by a spiro-cyclobutane. Region by region:

- **Left end:** a 4-chlorophenyl group sits on a cyclohexene carbon. That carbon is double-bonded to a second carbon bearing a CH2 that leads to the piperazine. The ring continues through two CH2 groups to a spiro carbon, which carries a four-membered ring (cyclobutane) and links back through one more CH2.
- **Centre:** the piperazine N-aryl nitrogen connects to a benzamide ring. That ring carries a 1H-pyrrolo[2,3-b]pyridin-5-yloxy group ortho to the carbonyl.
- **Acyl sulfonamide:** the amide C(=O)NH–SO2 links to a second benzene ring. This ring has a nitro group and an NH–CH2 substituent ortho to each other.
- **Right end:** the CH2 attaches to the 2-position of a 1,4-dioxane ring. The hashed bond on this CH2 gives the only stereocentre.

For the stereocentre, the hashed CH2 points away from the viewer, so the implicit H points toward the viewer. Seen from the H side, the order CH2N → ring O → ring CH2 is clockwise. Written as `NC[C@H]4COCCO4`, with neighbour order CH2N, H, ring-closure O, ring CH2, this corresponds to `@`.
