The drawing shows a tri-ring system: two piperazine-type rings joined through a central ring, with two spiro quaternary nitrogens, and two chloride counterions (this resembles pipobroman-derived "prospidium"-like structures).

Reading the rings:
- Left ring: N(acyl), CH2, CH2, N+, CH2, CH2, closing back to the acyl N. This is a piperazinium.
- Central ring: the two N+ atoms are linked by two separate CH2CH2 bridges. Top: N+ – CH2 – CH2 – N+ (left N+ to right N+). Bottom: the same. So the central ring is a piperazine-1,4-diium with both nitrogens spiro.
- Right ring: N+, CH2, CH2, N(acyl), CH2, CH2, back to the N+.

Each acyl nitrogen carries C(=O)CCBr, a 3-bromopropanoyl group.

SMILES for the left N-acyl ring and left spiro N+: BrCCC(=O)N1CC[N+]2(CC1)CC[N+]3(CC2)CCN(C(=O)CCBr)CC3

Check: N1 – C – C – [N+]2, then (CC1) closes the left ring with 2 carbons. Next, from [N+]2, CC leads to [N+]3 and then CC2 back to the first N+, which gives the central ring: N+2, C, C, N+3, C, C, back to N+2 (6-membered, correct). Then [N+]3 continues with CCN(...)CC3, which is the right ring.
