This structure is a risperidone-type molecule, here with a 3,4,6,7,8,9-hexahydro-pyrido[1,2-a]pyrimidin-4-one core (no hydroxyl, so it is not paliperidone).

**Benzisoxazole part:** a fluorine sits on the benzene ring. The ring fuses to an isoxazole made of O, N and C3, and C3 carries the piperidine substituent. This is 6-fluoro-1,2-benzisoxazol-3-yl, written as `Fc1ccc2c(c1)onc2`.

**Linker:** the piperidine is attached at its 4-position to C3 of the benzisoxazole. Its nitrogen carries a CH2CH2 chain that runs to the bicyclic core.

**Bicyclic core:**
- The ring atoms are C(=O), N, C(=N), N=, C(CH3) and the carbon bearing the chain.
- The pyrimidinone ring is C(CH3)=C(chain)–C(=O)–N–C(=N–) closing back to C(CH3).
- The fused six-membered ring is N–CH2CH2CH2CH2–C, giving four CH2 groups.

Written as SMILES: `Cc1nc2CCCCn2c(=O)c1CCN1CCC(CC1)c1noc2cc(F)ccc12`

Check on the benzisoxazole: `c1noc2cc(F)ccc12` has c1 (C3), n, o, c2 (C7a), then c (C7), c(F) (C6), c (C5), c (C4), and c12 (C3a), which closes to both C3 and C7a. F is on C6, the carbon next to C7, which is attached to C7a, which is attached to O. This agrees with the drawing, where F-bearing carbon is two bonds from the O-bearing carbon.
