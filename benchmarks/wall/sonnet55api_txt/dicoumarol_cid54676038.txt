The image shows dicoumarol: two 4-hydroxycoumarin units joined through their 3-positions by a CH2 bridge.

Each coumarin unit is a benzene ring fused to a lactone ring. In the lactone ring, C4 carries the OH and is double-bonded to C3. C3 bears the CH2 bridge and is bonded to C2, the carbonyl carbon (C=O), which is attached to the ring oxygen. That oxygen connects to the benzene ring at C8a.

The SMILES for one unit, written from the CH2 bridge, is C3(=C(O)c4ccccc4OC3=O). The full molecule is:

OC1=C(Cc2c(O)c3ccccc3oc2=O)C(=O)Oc2ccccc12

Checking the atoms: C1(O)=C(...)C(=O)Oc2ccccc12 gives C4(OH)=C3–C2(=O)–O–C8a, then the benzene ring closing back to C4a, which matches the drawing. The other half, c2c(O)c3ccccc3oc2=O, is the same coumarin written aromatically: c2 is C3, c(O) is C4, c3ccccc3 is the benzene ring, o is the ring oxygen, and c2=O is the carbonyl carbon. Written aromatically, that half is valid.
