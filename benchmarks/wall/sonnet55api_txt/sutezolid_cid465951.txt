The drawing shows the thiomorpholine analogue of linezolid. I read it as follows:

- **Left side:** an acetamide, CH3–C(=O)–NH–CH2–.
- **Centre:** a 2-oxazolidinone ring. Its ring O sits next to the C=O carbon, which is bonded to the ring N. The stereocentre bears the CH2NHAc arm and the ring CH2 that leads to N.
- **Aryl group:** a benzene ring on the oxazolidinone N, with F ortho to the thiomorpholine nitrogen.
- **Right side:** a thiomorpholine ring (N, CH2, CH2, S, CH2, CH2) attached through its N.

For stereochemistry, the CH2NHAc bond at the oxazolidinone stereocentre is hashed, so it points away from the viewer and the implicit H points toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are ring O, then the ring CH2 (attached to N), then the exocyclic CH2N, then H. Seen from the front, O (up) to ring CH2 (lower right) to exocyclic CH2N (lower left) runs clockwise. Because H faces the viewer, the true sense is reversed, so the centre is S, as in linezolid. The SMILES neighbour order matches the standard linezolid SMILES, so `[C@H]` gives the same S configuration.

SMILES: CC(=O)NC[C@H]1CN(c2ccc(N3CCSCC3)c(F)c2)C(=O)O1
