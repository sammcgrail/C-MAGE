This is the doravirine structure, read from the drawing as follows.

- **Left ring:** a benzene ring carrying Cl, a nitrile (C≡N) and an aryl-O substituent, all in a 1,3,5 pattern.
- **Central ring:** a pyridin-2-one. The ring nitrogen N1 is bonded to C2(=O). C3 carries the aryl-O group, C4 carries CF3, and C5=C6 close the ring back to N1.
- **Linker:** N1 is attached to a CH2 group.
- **Right ring:** the CH2 connects to a triazolone. Its ring atoms are C(=N), N, NH, C(=O) and N-methyl.

Writing the SMILES from the nitrile end:

N#Cc1cc(Cl)cc(Oc2c(C(F)(F)F)ccn(Cc3n[nH]c(=O)n3C)c2=O)c1

The triazolone ring is C3(=N–NH–C(=O)–N3(CH3)), which matches the drawing: the carbon bonded to CH2 is double-bonded to N, then NH, then C=O, then N-Me closing back to the first carbon.
