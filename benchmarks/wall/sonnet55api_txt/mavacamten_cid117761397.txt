I read the molecule as a uracil-type ring (a pyrimidine-2,4-dione) with three substituents:

- **Ring:** N3 carries an isopropyl group and sits between two C=O carbons. The ring then continues through a CH=C bond to N1–H, which closes the ring through the other C=O.
- **Amino side chain:** the ring carbon next to N1–H bears an NH, which is bonded to a CH(CH3)–phenyl group.
- **Stereocentre:** the CH carbon has a wedged methyl, so the hydrogen points away from the viewer. Seen from the front, methyl → phenyl → N runs clockwise. Written as N[C@@H](C)c…, this order (N, H, CH3, phenyl) corresponds to `@@`.

SMILES: CC(C)N1C(=O)C=C(N[C@@H](C)c2ccccc2)NC1=O
