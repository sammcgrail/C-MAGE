I read the drawing as ceftizoxime, a cephalosporin with a 2-aminothiazole side chain.

- **Side chain:** a 2-aminothiazole (ring N, S, an NH2 on the carbon between them) is attached through its C4 to a C=N–OCH3 oxime carbon, which carries a C(=O)NH amide.
- **Oxime geometry:** the methoxy group on the oxime nitrogen sits on the same side as the amide carbonyl carbon. That carbonyl carbon outranks the thiazole carbon in priority, so the double bond is Z.
- **Bicyclic core:** the β-lactam (N, C=O, and two CH carbons) is fused to a six-membered ring. In that ring the sequence is N–C(COOH)=CH–CH2–S–CH. There is no substituent on the ring CH2, so there is no acetoxymethyl group as in cefotaxime.
- **Stereochemistry:** the NH-bearing carbon has a wedge bond and the ring-fusion CH has a hashed H. I assigned the stereo tags from the known natural (6R,7R) configuration of ceftizoxime rather than deriving them from the wedge geometry, so these two tags are the least certain part.
