The drawing shows droxidopa, with a 3,4-dihydroxyphenyl ring on a CH(OH)–CH(NH2)–COOH chain.

- **Ring:** a benzene ring with two adjacent OH groups (catechol) at the 3 and 4 positions relative to the chain attachment.
- **Chain:** the ring carbon attaches to a CH carrying an OH on a wedge bond, then to a CH carrying an NH2 on a wedge bond, then to a carboxylic acid (C=O and OH).
- **Stereocentre bearing OH:** the priorities are OH > CH(N) > aryl > H, with H pointing away. Going OH → CH(N) → aryl is clockwise, so this is R.
- **Stereocentre bearing NH2:** the priorities are N > COOH > CH(OH) > H, with H pointing away. Going N → COOH → CH(OH) is counterclockwise, so this is S.

This gives (2S,3R)-2-amino-3-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl)-3-hydroxypropanoic acid. In the SMILES below, `@@` at the NH2 carbon encodes S and `@` at the OH carbon encodes R, which I checked by permuting the neighbour order.
