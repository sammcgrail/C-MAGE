I read this as an ergoline skeleton (an indole fused to a tetracyclic ring system) carrying an N‑propyl group on the ring nitrogen and a CH2–S–CH3 group on the ring carbon at the bottom right. This resembles pergolide.

**Connectivity**
- The aromatic ring and the pyrrole ring bearing the NH make up the indole part.
- The ring nitrogen carries a three‑carbon chain (propyl).
- The ring carbon at the bottom right carries a CH2–S–CH3 group.

**Stereochemistry**
Three centres are marked in the drawing:
- The carbon bearing CH2SCH3 has a hashed bond to the CH2S group, so the implicit H points toward the viewer. Tracing the neighbours gives `@` in my SMILES atom order.
- The ring‑fusion carbon at the bottom has a wedged H. This gives `@`.
- The carbon next to the nitrogen has a hashed H. This gives `@`.

Pergolide itself is usually written with `@@` at the CH2SCH3 carbon. My reading of the hashed bond gives `@` there, so this drawing may show the C8 epimer. I kept the result of my own derivation rather than the literature form.
