The drawing shows benzoyl peroxide. Reading it region by region:

- Two phenyl rings, one on the left and one on the right, each drawn as a six-membered aromatic ring.
- Each ring is attached to a carbonyl carbon, C(=O).
- Each carbonyl carbon is bonded to an oxygen, and the two oxygens are bonded to each other (an O–O peroxide link).

This gives Ph–C(=O)–O–O–C(=O)–Ph. There are no wedges, hashes or charges, so no stereochemistry is needed.
