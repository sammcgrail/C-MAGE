The drawing shows a benzoxaborole (a 1-hydroxy-1,3-dihydro-2,1-benzoxaborole) carrying a 4-cyanophenoxy group, similar to crisaborole.

**Oxaborole ring.** B carries an OH and is bonded to the ring O and to an aromatic carbon. The ring O connects to a CH2, and the CH2 connects to the other fused aromatic carbon.

**Fused benzene ring.** Reading around the ring, the vertices are:
- C1 (394,732), bonded to B
- C2 (403,584)
- C3 (537,519)
- C4 (659,601), bonded to the ether O
- C5 (651,749)
- C6 (517,815), bonded to the CH2

**Substituent.** The ether O links C4 to a para-substituted phenyl ring bearing a C≡N group.

**Position check.** C1 (B-bearing) is bonded to C2 and C6. C6 (CH2-bearing) is bonded to C5. C4 (O-bearing) is bonded to C3 and C5. So the O sits on the carbon that is meta to C6 and para to C1. In crisaborole, the 4-cyanophenoxy group is at position 5 of the benzoxaborole. Position 5 is para to C7a, which is the carbon bonded to B (C7a–B–O1–C3–C3a). That matches this drawing.

**SMILES.** Starting from the hydroxyl: OB1OCc2cc(Oc3ccc(C#N)cc3)ccc21

Checking the ring closures: B1, O, CH2, then c2 (C6), then c (C5), then c(OAr) (C4), then c (C3), c (C2), and c21 (C1, closing to the aromatic ring start and to B). This is consistent with the connectivity above.
