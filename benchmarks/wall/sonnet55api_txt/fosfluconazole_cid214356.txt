The drawing shows fosfluconazole, a fluconazole phosphate prodrug.

**Central carbon:** It carries four substituents: an aryl ring, two CH2 groups and an O–P(=O)(OH)2 group.

**CH2 groups:** Each CH2 is attached to a 1,2,4-triazol-1-yl ring.

**Aryl ring:** It is a 2,4-difluorophenyl group. The ipso carbon is bonded to the central carbon, the ortho carbon bears F, and the para carbon bears F. Going around the ring from the ipso carbon, the top-left carbon has F (ortho), then CH, then the carbon with F (para), then two more CH carbons. The fluorines are therefore at positions 2 and 4.

**Triazole rings:**
- Top ring: N1 (attached to the CH2) is bonded to N2, then C3 (=N), then N4, then C5 back to N1. In the image, N1 connects to the top N, which is double-bonded to a CH, which connects to the left N. The left N is double-bonded to the bottom CH, which connects back to N1. This matches the 1,2,4-triazol-1-yl pattern n1cncn1 written as n1cncn1, i.e. n1ncnc1 (N1, N2, C3, N4, C5).
- Bottom ring: N1 is attached to the CH2 and bonded to the left N (N2), then CH (C3), then the bottom N (N4), then C5 back to N1. This is the same 1,2,4-triazol-1-yl pattern.

**SMILES:** The aryl ring is written as c1ccc(F)cc1F, starting from the ipso carbon.
