I read the molecule as a benzene ring carrying three substituents or fusions, plus a fused boron-containing ring and a cyclopropane.

- **Benzene ring:** it carries a COOH group, an F atom, and a ring oxygen.
- **Boron ring:** the ring oxygen links to a boron bearing an OH. That boron bonds to a cyclopropane CH, and the cyclopropane's other CH is bonded to the aromatic ring, closing a six-membered ring.
- **Cyclopropane:** two CH carbons and one CH2. Each CH has an H drawn as a wedge toward the viewer.

I assigned the two stereocentres from the wedge geometry.

- The CH attached to the aromatic ring has its H toward the viewer, with the other three neighbours (aromatic C, cyclopropane CH, CH2) running clockwise. This gives `[C@H]`.
- The CH attached to boron has its H toward the viewer, with the other three neighbours (CH2, B, other CH) running counter-clockwise when viewed from H. Given the order in which they appear in the SMILES, this gives `[C@@H]`.
