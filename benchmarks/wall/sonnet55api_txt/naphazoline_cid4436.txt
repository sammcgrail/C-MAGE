The drawing shows naphazoline: a 4,5-dihydro-1H-imidazole (imidazoline) ring joined through a CH2 group to a naphthalene at the 1-position.

**Imidazoline ring.** The ring atoms are NH, C(=N), N, CH2 and CH2. The NH is at the top left, the C2 carbon carries the CH2 substituent, and the N at the bottom is double-bonded to C2. This gives C1(=NCCN1).

**Linker.** One CH2 connects C2 of the imidazoline to the naphthalene.

**Naphthalene.** The CH2 attaches to a ring carbon at (792, 735) that is adjacent to the ring-fusion carbon at (1003, 679). This is the 1-position (alpha) of naphthalene.

**SMILES.** Writing the naphthalene as c1ccc2ccccc2c1 with the substituent on the carbon next to the fusion atom gives C1(=NCCN1)Cc1cccc2ccccc12.

Checking the naphthalene part, c1cccc2ccccc12: the substituted atom c1 is bonded to the fusion carbon c12 at the end, then runs c, c, c to the other fusion carbon c2, then four more aromatic c atoms, closing at c12. That makes 10 aromatic carbons, so it is valid.
