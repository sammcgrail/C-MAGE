The drawing shows dibenzothiophene: two benzene rings fused to a central five-membered ring that contains one sulfur atom.

The central ring has S, two aromatic carbons that bond to S (one from each benzene ring), and two carbons joined by the bond between the rings.

Each benzene ring has six carbons, so the molecule has 12 carbons and one sulfur.

Writing it as an aromatic SMILES: c1ccc2c(c1)sc1ccccc12.

Checking the ring closures:
- `c1ccc2c(c1)` opens ring 1, then ring 2, and the `(c1)` closes the first benzene ring after six atoms: c1, c, c, c2, c, c1.
- `s` follows the branch carbon, so that carbon bonds to sulfur.
- `c1ccccc12` starts a new ring 1 with six atoms, and the last atom closes ring 1 and ring 2, bonding to the first benzene's c2 atom.
