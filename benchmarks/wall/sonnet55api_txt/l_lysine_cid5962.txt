The drawing shows lysine, written here as the L-enantiomer.

- **Chain:** Starting from the H2N group on the left, there are four chain carbons (vertices at roughly x = 346, 514, 681, 848) before the stereocentre at about x = 1014. That gives four CH2 groups.
- **Stereocentre:** The α-carbon carries a hashed bond to NH2. Its other substituents are the CH2 chain and the carboxylic acid, plus an implicit H.
- **Acid end:** The carbon at about x = 1182 is a carboxylic acid, drawn with C=O and OH.

**Stereochemistry:** The hashed NH2 points away from the viewer, so the implicit H points toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are N > COOH > CH2 > H. Viewed from the front, N (up) → COOH (lower right) → CH2 (lower left) runs clockwise. Because H faces the viewer, the true sense is reversed, giving counterclockwise, which is S. S is the L configuration for lysine.

Writing this as SMILES from the chain end gives NCCCC[C@H](N)C(=O)O. It is the same as N[C@@H](CCCCN)C(=O)O, the usual L-lysine SMILES.
