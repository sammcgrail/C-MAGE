The drawing is asparagine: a four-carbon chain with a carboxylic acid at one end, a primary amide at the other, and an amino group on the alpha carbon.

- **Left end:** a carbon with a double-bonded O and an HO label, so it is a carboxylic acid, C(=O)O.
- **Alpha carbon:** it carries an NH2 on a hashed bond (pointing away from the viewer), so it is the only stereocentre.
- **Middle:** one CH2 joins the alpha carbon to the amide carbon.
- **Right end:** a carbon with a double-bonded O and an NH2, so it is a primary amide, C(N)=O.

For the stereochemistry, the implicit H on the alpha carbon points toward the viewer. Seen from the front, the sequence N (down), then COOH (upper left), then CH2 (upper right) runs clockwise. Reversing that because H faces the viewer gives counterclockwise, so the centre is S, which is L-asparagine.

I wrote the SMILES by analogy with L-alanine (N[C@@H](C)C(=O)O) rather than by tracing the chirality tag independently.

SMILES: N[C@@H](CC(N)=O)C(O)=O
