I read the structure as three pieces joined in a chain: a substituted anilide, a para-disubstituted benzene, and a pyridyl acrylamide.

**Left ring.** The ring carbon bearing the amide NH has an NH2 on the adjacent carbon. Going round the ring from the NH carbon (C1): C2 carries NH2, C3 is CH, C4 carries F, C5 and C6 are CH. So the amine is ortho to the amide N and the fluorine is para to the amide N.

**Central ring.** A benzene ring with the C(=O)NH group on one side and a CH2 on the para position.

**Right side.** The CH2 connects to NH, then C(=O), then CH=CH, then a pyridin-3-yl group. In the drawing, the alkene substituents (the C=O side and the pyridyl) lie on opposite sides of the double bond, so it is trans (E). The pyridine N sits at the top; the ring carbon attached to the alkene is separated from N by one CH, so the attachment is at position 3.
