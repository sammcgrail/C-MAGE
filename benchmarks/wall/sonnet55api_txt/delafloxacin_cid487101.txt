The structure is a quinolone (4-oxo-1,4-dihydroquinoline-3-carboxylic acid), which I read in three parts.

**Quinolone core.** N1 bears a pyridine substituent. C2 is a CH. C3 carries the COOH and C4 is the ketone. C4a and C8a are the ring-fusion carbons. C5 is a CH. C6 carries F, C7 carries the azetidine N, and C8 carries Cl.

**Azetidine.** The ring is N–CH2–CH(OH)–CH2, a 3-hydroxyazetidin-1-yl group attached at C7.

**Pyridine substituent on N1.** The pyridine ring atoms, going around the ring, are:
- C attached to N1 of the quinolone
- C–F
- CH
- C–F
- C–NH2
- ring N, which closes back to the first carbon

From the drawing, the attachment carbon (call it C2') bonds to the ring N and to C(F) at the right. That C(F) connects to CH, then to C(F) at the top, then to C(NH2), then to the ring N. So the ring is C2'(N-quinolone), C3'(F), C4'H, C5'(F), C6'(NH2), N1'. The double bonds are C2'=C3', C4'=C5' and C6'=N1'.
