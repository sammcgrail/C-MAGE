I read the drawing as a 2-chlorothioxanthene with an exocyclic double bond at C9. That C9 carries a three-carbon chain ending in a piperazine, and the piperazine's other nitrogen bears a 2-hydroxyethyl group. This looks like zuclopenthixol (the Z isomer of clopenthixol).

- **Thioxanthene core:** a benzene ring with Cl at position 2, bridged through S to a second unsubstituted benzene ring, with C9 between the two rings.
- **Side chain:** C9 is double-bonded to a CH, which connects to CH2, then CH2, then the piperazine nitrogen.
- **Piperazine:** the other nitrogen carries CH2CH2OH.
- **Double-bond geometry:** in the drawing, the chlorinated ring and the CH2 of the chain lie on the same side of the C9=CH bond. The chlorinated ring outranks the unsubstituted ring in CIP priority because of the Cl at the fourth sphere, so the higher-priority groups are cis and the bond is Z. In the SMILES this is written `CC/C=C1\c2cc(Cl)ccc2...`.

SMILES: OCCN1CCN(CC1)CC/C=C1\c2cc(Cl)ccc2Sc2ccccc12
