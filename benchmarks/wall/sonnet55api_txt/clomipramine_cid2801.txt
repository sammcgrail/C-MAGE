This is clomipramine: an imipramine skeleton with a chlorine on one benzene ring.

**Reading the structure**
- The side chain is N(CH3)2–CH2CH2CH2–N, with the terminal dimethylamino nitrogen at the lower left.
- The ring nitrogen sits between two aromatic rings and carries the propyl chain.
- The two aromatic rings are bridged by a CH2–CH2 unit, making the seven-membered ring.
- Chlorine is on the upper ring, at the carbon para to the ring carbon attached to the CH2 bridge.

**Checking the chlorine position**
The upper ring atoms, with approximate coordinates, are:
- C1 (892,475): bonded to the CH2 bridge
- C2 (795,650): bonded to N
- C3 (595,653)
- C4 (492,480): bears Cl
- C5 (590,305)
- C6 (790,302)

C4 is para to C1 (C1→C2→C3→C4 is three bonds), so Cl is para to the CH2-bearing carbon and meta to the N-bearing carbon, as in clomipramine.
