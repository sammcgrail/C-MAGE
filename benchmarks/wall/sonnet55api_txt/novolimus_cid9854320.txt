This reads as 16‑O‑demethylrapamycin (sirolimus with a free OH in place of one methoxy), and I wrote the SMILES by adapting the known rapamycin SMILES to what the drawing shows. I did not re-derive each stereocentre from the wedges and hashes; I kept rapamycin's.

Reading the macrocycle from the pyran ring:
- **Pyran ring:** an O‑bridged ring carries a methyl on a wedge, and the hemiketal carbon bears an OH.
- **Amide/ester section:** the hemiketal carbon is followed by two adjacent C=O groups, then the piperidine N. The piperidine carbon next to N bears a hashed H and is bonded to a C(=O)–O ester.
- **Ester side:** the ester O leads to a CH that carries the side chain and the ring CH2 leading to a ketone.
- **Side chain:** CH(CH3)–CH2 leads to a cyclohexane ring bearing OMe and OH.
- **Ketone to alkene:** the ketone is followed by CH(CH3) and a trisubstituted C=C(CH3).
- **Hydroxyl and methoxy carbons:** next come CH(OH) and CH(OMe), then a C=O.
- **Methylated section:** this C=O is followed by CH(CH3), CH2 and CH(CH3).
- **Triene:** CH(CH3) leads into a conjugated triene, whose last carbon is the C(CH3)= that meets the next CH(OH).
- **Closure:** CH(OH) then CH2 closes onto the pyran carbon.

The carbon next to the pyran CH2 carries a free OH, not the OMe that rapamycin has at that position. So I changed the terminal `OC` of the rapamycin SMILES to `O`.
