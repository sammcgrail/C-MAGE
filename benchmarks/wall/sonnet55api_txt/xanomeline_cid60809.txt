This is xanomeline: a pentyl chain on an ether oxygen, attached to a 1,2,5-thiadiazole, which is attached to an N-methyl tetrahydropyridine.

**Chain.** Starting from the left, the vertices are CH3 (75,593), CH2 (186,704), CH2 (337,662), CH2 (449,771), CH2 (600,731) and CH2 (711,840), which then joins O. That is six carbons, so the group is hexyloxy, not pentyl.

**Thiadiazole.** The ring atoms are C(O) at (973,908), C at (1128,884), N at (1200,1022), S at (1090,1138) and N at (950,1062). The double bonds are C(O)=N and C=N, so the ring is C1=NSN=C1, with S between the two nitrogens.

**Tetrahydropyridine.** The ring atoms are:
- C (1198,743), attached to the thiadiazole carbon
- CH2 (1112,613)
- N (1185,470), carrying a methyl
- CH2 (1338,464)
- CH2 (1424,596)
- CH (1354,735)

The double bond runs between C(1198,743) and CH(1354,735).

**SMILES.** Written from the hexyl chain through the thiadiazole to the ring: CCCCCCOc1nsnc1C1=CCCN(C)C1. Here the ring carbon bearing the substituent is C1, with =C, then CH2, CH2, N(C) and CH2 closing the ring.
