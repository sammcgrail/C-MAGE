This structure is pantoprazole, a benzimidazole sulfoxide. I read it region by region.

**Pyridine ring (left).** The ring atoms are N, C2, C3, C4, C5 and C6.
- C2 carries the CH2 that leads to the sulfoxide.
- C3 carries an OMe.
- C4 carries an OMe.
- C5 and C6 are plain CH.

**Linker.** The CH2 is bonded to a sulfoxide S, written S(=O), which is bonded to C2 of the benzimidazole.

**Benzimidazole (right).** C2 is bonded to N (imine) and to NH. The fused benzene ring bears an OCHF2 group.

**Assembled SMILES.** COc1ccnc(CS(=O)c2nc3ccc(OC(F)F)cc3[nH]2)c1OC

**Atom check on the benzimidazole.**
- The ring N (no H) is attached to the fused carbon at the upper left of the benzene ring (around 880,640). From there the benzene runs to the top carbon (955,543), then 1075,560, then 1122,675, which bears the O, then 1047,770, then 925,755, which is bonded to NH.
- In the SMILES, c3 (bonded to n) is followed by c, c, c(OC(F)F), c, c3[nH]. This gives the fused carbon, CH, CH, C–O, CH and the fused carbon bonded to NH.
- The O-bearing carbon is therefore the third ring atom from the N-side fusion carbon, and it is adjacent to the CH that sits next to the NH-side fusion carbon. This matches the drawing.
