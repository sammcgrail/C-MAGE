The drawing is a fused bicyclic ring system with a nitrogen at the ring fusion, which is indolizine.

**Atoms and rings**
- The five-membered ring has N, C(a) at the upper left, C(b) at the far left, C(c) at the lower left, and C(d) at the fusion carbon below N.
- The six-membered ring has N, C at the top, C at the upper right, C at the lower right, C at the bottom, and the fusion carbon C(d).

**Double bonds**
- In the five-membered ring: C(a)=C(b) (inner line on the upper left) and C(c)=C(d) (inner line on the bottom).
- In the six-membered ring: top C=upper-right C (inner line on the upper right) and lower-right C=bottom C (inner line on the lower right).
- The N–C(d) bond is single, and N has three connections, so no charge is needed.

**SMILES**
Starting at N: n1 connects to C(a)=C(b)–C(c)=C(d), and C(d) closes back to N. The six-membered ring continues from C(d) to the bottom carbon, then the lower-right and upper-right carbons, then the top carbon, and back to N.

Written with explicit double bonds, this is N1C=CC=C2C=CC=CN12, which has the following problem. C(d) would have a double bond to C(c) and a single bond to the bottom carbon, with the bottom carbon double-bonded to the lower-right carbon. The correct form is C1=CC2=CC=CC=CN2... which needs rechecking, so I use the aromatic form instead.

Aromatic form: c1ccn2cccc2c1. This is indolizine, C8H7N, and it matches the drawing.
