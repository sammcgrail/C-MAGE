The drawing shows a quaternary ammonium (N⁺) carrying three methyl groups. The fourth substituent is a CH2 that leads to a CH. That CH bears a methyl group and an oxygen, and the oxygen is bonded to a carbamate carbon, C(=O)NH2. This is bethanechol.

The CH carbon is a stereocentre by connectivity, but it is drawn with plain bonds, so I give no stereochemistry.

SMILES: C[N+](C)(C)CC(C)OC(N)=O
