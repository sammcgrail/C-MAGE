I read this as phenmetrazine tartrate, drawn as two separate components.

**Tartaric acid component (top).** It is a four-carbon chain with a carboxylic acid at each end and two CH(OH) carbons between them. Both OH groups are on wedge bonds.
- Starting from the left carboxyl, the lower-left stereocentre has its OH on a wedge pointing down, which gives `[C@H]`.
- The upper-right stereocentre has its OH on a wedge pointing up, which gives `[C@@H]`.
- This gives `O=C(O)[C@H](O)[C@@H](O)C(=O)O`.

**Phenmetrazine component (bottom).** It is a morpholine ring with the atoms in the order O, CH2, CH2, N(CH3), CH(CH3), CH(Ph).
- The CH bearing the methyl group has the methyl on a wedge, which gives `[C@@H]`.
- The CH bearing the phenyl group has the phenyl on a hashed bond, which gives `[C@H]`.
- The N carries a methyl group, and the phenyl is an ordinary six-carbon aromatic ring.
- This gives `C[C@@H]1N(C)CCO[C@H]1c1ccccc1`.

I worked out each @/@@ assignment by hand from the wedge and hash geometry, so the stereo marks are the part most open to error.
