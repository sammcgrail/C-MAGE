Reading the drawing, I get a 19-nortestosterone (nandrolone) skeleton.

- **Ring A:** a cyclohexenone with a C=O on the right and a C=C in the ring next to the ring-fusion carbon. There is no methyl on this ring fusion, which is what makes it a 19-nor steroid.
- **Rings B and C:** two fused six-membered rings.
- **Ring D:** a five-membered ring carrying an OH on the carbon next to the quaternary carbon, which bears a methyl.

**Stereochemistry:** the drawing shows the following.
- The methyl and the OH are both hashed.
- The H at the ring-D/C fusion is a wedge.
- The H at the ring-C/B fusion (adjacent to the ring-D fusion) is hashed.
- The H at the next ring fusion is a wedge.
- The H at the ring-A/B fusion is hashed.

In natural nandrolone the methyl and 17-OH are wedged, so this drawing is the enantiomer: every stereocentre is inverted. I wrote the SMILES by taking the natural nandrolone SMILES as I remember it and inverting every centre. I did not derive the descriptors independently from the drawing, so the chirality marks depend on that recollection being right.
