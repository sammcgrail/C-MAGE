The drawing shows a 1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one, which matches delorazepam (the N-desmethyl analogue of diazepam, with a 2-chlorophenyl group).

**Seven-membered ring**
- The lactam NH is bonded to the fused benzene carbon and to the C=O carbon.
- The C=O carbon connects to a CH2, which connects to N.
- That N is double-bonded to the C bearing the aryl group, and this C is bonded to the other fused benzene carbon.

**Fused benzene ring**
- Going around from the NH-bearing carbon (C9a): C9a, then CH, then CH, then C(Cl), then CH, then C5a, which carries the C=N carbon.
- The Cl sits on the carbon that is para to the NH-bearing carbon. In the image, the NH carbon is at (480, 754) and the Cl carbon at (783, 446). The NH carbon neighbours (423, 546) and (689, 808), so the ring order is NH carbon, (423, 546), (574, 392), (783, 446) with Cl, (840, 655), (689, 808). The Cl carbon is therefore para to the NH carbon, as in the SMILES below.

**Pendant ring**
- A 2-chlorophenyl group is attached to the C=N carbon.

SMILES: O=C1CN=C(c2ccccc2Cl)c2cc(Cl)ccc2N1

Checking the benzene ring in this SMILES, c2cc(Cl)ccc2N1 runs: c2 (C5a, attached to C=N), c (CH), c(Cl), c, c, c2 (attached to N). C5a is bonded to CH, then C(Cl). C(Cl) is meta to C5a and para to the N-bearing carbon, which agrees with the image.
