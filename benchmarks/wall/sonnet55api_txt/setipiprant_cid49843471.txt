The drawing shows a tetrahydro-γ-carboline core (a pyrido[4,3-b]indole) with three substituents.

**Core.** An indole nitrogen (N, right side) carries a CH2COOH group. The benzene ring carries F. The six-membered ring contains a second N, which is acylated and bonded to two CH2 groups.

**Ring connectivity.**
- The indole N is bonded to C2 (the carbon at about 1020,913), which is also bonded to the CH2 at about 958,1044.
- That CH2 connects to the CH2 at about 813,1052, which connects to N (730,935).
- N connects to the CH2 at about 795,803, which connects to C3 (941,794).
- C3 is double-bonded to C2 and bonded to the benzo carbon at about 1030,680.
- The other benzo carbon (1165,730) is bonded to the indole N.

**Benzene ring.** The atoms run 1030,680 → 1007,538 → 1118,445 (bearing F) → 1252,494 → 1277,637 → 1165,730. The F carbon is therefore meta to the C3-attached carbon, which gives a 6-fluoro pattern on the tetrahydro-γ-carboline.

**Acyl group.** N–C(=O)– is attached to the naphthalene at its 1-position, since the attachment carbon is adjacent to the ring-fusion carbon (363,831).

- C2 is the carbon attached to the CH2CH2 side, and C3 is the carbon attached to the CH2 that leads to N. In the SMILES, C2 (written "C2=") closes ring 2 with the CC2 end, and "C(CN(CC2)...)" is C3 with its CH2–N. This matches the drawing.
- Naphthalene: c2cccc3ccccc23 gives a 1-naphthyl group. The attachment atom c2 is followed by cccc, then c3 (the fusion carbon), then ccccc23 closing the ring back to the attachment carbon, so the attachment carbon is adjacent to the fusion carbon. This is correct.
