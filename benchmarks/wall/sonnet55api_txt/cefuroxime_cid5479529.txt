This is cefuroxime, a cephalosporin, read from the drawing as follows.

- **Core:** a β-lactam fused to a six-membered dihydrothiazine ring (S, CH2, C=C, N). The ring carbon next to N carries a carboxylic acid, and the adjacent alkene carbon carries CH2–O–C(=O)NH2 (a carbamate).
- **Side chain:** the ring NH is acylated by C(=O)–C(=N–OMe)–(2-furyl). The methoxy-oxime is Z: OMe sits on the same side as the amide carbonyl, which is the higher-priority substituent on that carbon, and opposite the furan.
- **Ring stereocentres:** I took the two fused-ring configurations from the known cefuroxime structure, which I recalled from memory, and did not re-derive them from the wedge and hash bonds in the drawing. They are consistent with one wedged N–C bond and one hashed C–H bond.
