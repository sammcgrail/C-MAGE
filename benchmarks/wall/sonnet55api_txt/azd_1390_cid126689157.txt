I read the structure as an imidazo[4,5-c]quinolin-2-one core carrying an N-isopropyl, an N-methyl, a fluorine, and a pyridyl ether side chain.

**Core.** The five-membered ring has C(=O) at the top, N-methyl on the left and N-isopropyl on the right. The two fused carbons are C3a (bonded to N-Me) and C9b (bonded to N-iPr). C3a is also bonded to a CH, which is double-bonded to the ring nitrogen (the lower N). That N connects to C4a, which is part of the benzene ring. C9b is bonded to C9a, the other fusion carbon of the benzene ring.

**Benzene ring.** Going around from C9a: C9a, CH, C(pyridyl), C(F), CH, C4a.

**Side chain.** The pyridyl group is attached at its 3-position. The ring nitrogen is adjacent to the carbon bearing the O-CH2CH2CH2-piperidin-1-yl group, which makes this a 2-alkoxypyridin-5-yl substituent.

**SMILES assembly.**
- Core with substituents: O=C1N(C)c2cnc3cc(F)c(cc3c2N1C(C)C)...
- Check of the benzene ring: c3 (C4a) is bonded to n, then cc(F) gives CH then C-F, then c(...) gives the C-pyridyl, then c is the CH, then c3 closes as C9a. C9a is then bonded to c2 (C9b), which carries N1(iPr). This is consistent.
- Pyridyl: c4ccc(OCCCN5CCCCC5)nc4
