I read this as a glucosamine-type pyranose with a phosphate and three 3-(decanoyloxy)tetradecanoyl chains, plus three sodium counterions. This is a lipid A mimetic.

**Structure read from the drawing**
- **Ring:** a six-membered pyranose with ring oxygen, bearing a CH2OH at C5, a phosphate at C4, an acyloxy group at C3, an acylamino group at C2, and an exocyclic ether oxygen at the anomeric C1.
- **Serine unit:** the C1 oxygen is linked to CH2–CH(NH–acyl)–COO⁻, i.e. an O-glycosylated serine carboxylate.
- **Acyl chains:** three identical 3-(decanoyloxy)tetradecanoyl groups. One is on the C3 oxygen, one on the C2 nitrogen, and one on the serine nitrogen.
  - Each is C(=O)CH2–CH(O–decanoyl)–(CH2)10CH3. The decanoyl group is C(=O) followed by 9 carbons, and the tetradecanoyl tail after C3 is 11 carbons.
- **Phosphate and counterions:** the phosphate carries two negative charges and the carboxylate one, balanced by three Na⁺.

**Stereochemistry**
I assigned each centre from the wedges and hashes at the ring carbons, the serine α-carbon and the three acyl C3 carbons. All three acyl C3 centres work out as R (the natural configuration). The wedge directions at the serine α-carbon and the acyl C3 centres were hard to make out in the drawing, so those assignments are less certain.
