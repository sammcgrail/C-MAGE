The drawing shows a hydantoin ring (imidazolidine-2,4-dione) with these features:

- An ethyl group is attached to the ring nitrogen at the lower left (N-CH2-CH3).
- That nitrogen sits between two carbonyl carbons. One is the upper C=O, which is also bonded to NH (a urea carbonyl). The other is the lower C=O, which is also bonded to the CH carbon (an amide carbonyl).
- The NH is bonded to the CH carbon, which carries a phenyl substituent.
- The CH carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so I give it no stereochemistry.

Writing the SMILES from the ethyl group around the ring: CCN1C(=O)NC(c2ccccc2)C1=O. The ring closure works as follows: N1 bonds to C(=O), then NH, then CH(Ph), then C1(=O), which closes back to N1.
