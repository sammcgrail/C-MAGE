I read this as a cardenolide: a digitoxigenin-type steroid with a butenolide at C17, a 14-hydroxyl, and a chain of three 2,6-dideoxy sugars on the 3-oxygen. The 5-H, 8-H, 9-H, both angular methyls, the 14-OH, the C17 lactone bond and the 3-O bond are all drawn with wedges or hashes, so I assigned chirality at every one of those centres. For each stereocentre I took the wedge/hash geometry from the drawing and converted it to @/@@ by a signed-volume test. This gives the stereochemistry as drawn, which differs from the usual digitoxin configuration at several centres. I did not check it against a reference structure.

**Steroid core.** Ring A is a cyclohexane, ring B a cyclohexane with the 5-H, and ring C a cyclohexane carrying the 14-OH. Ring D is a cyclopentane. There are methyls on C10 and C13. The lactone is C1=CC(=O)OC1 joined at C17.

**Sugar chain.** Each sugar is a tetrahydropyran with a 6-methyl and a ring CH2. The terminal ring carries 3-OH and 4-OH. The two inner rings each carry a 3-OH and a glycosidic oxygen on C4. They are linked 1→4 to each other and to the aglycone's 3-oxygen.

In the SMILES, the sugar rings use digits 1–3. The steroid uses digits 4–8 so that no ring-closure digit is reused while it is still open.
