The drawing shows two pyridinium rings joined by a single bond between their 4-positions. Each ring nitrogen carries a positive charge and a methyl group, so the molecule is the paraquat dication (1,1'-dimethyl-4,4'-bipyridinium).

Reading the left ring from the bonds drawn: N+ is double-bonded to the upper carbon, which is single-bonded to the next carbon. That carbon is double-bonded to the ring-junction carbon (the one attached to the other ring). The junction carbon is single-bonded to the lower carbon, which is double-bonded to the next carbon, and that carbon is single-bonded back to N+.

The right ring is read the same way. The junction carbon is double-bonded to the lower carbon (the lower inner line) and single-bonded to the upper carbon. The upper carbon is double-bonded to the next carbon, which is single-bonded to N+. N+ is double-bonded to the lower carbon, which is single-bonded to the junction carbon.

Written out, the left ring is C[N+]1=CC=C(C=C1)… and the right ring is …C2=CC=[N+](C)C=C2. Each ring has alternating double bonds in a valid pattern, so the SMILES below is consistent with the drawing atom for atom.

SMILES: C[N+]1=CC=C(C=C1)C1=CC=[N+](C)C=C1
