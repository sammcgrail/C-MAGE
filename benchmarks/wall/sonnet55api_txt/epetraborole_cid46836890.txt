I read the molecule as a benzoxaborole, a benzene ring fused to a five-membered ring containing B and O.

- **Five-membered ring:** it contains O, B, two aromatic carbons and the stereocentre C. B carries an OH, and the stereocentre carbon carries an aminomethyl group (CH2–NH2).
- **Benzene ring:** the aromatic carbon next to the B-bearing carbon carries an O–CH2CH2CH2–OH chain, a three-carbon chain ending in OH. The other three ring carbons are plain CH.
- **Stereochemistry:** the only wedge is on the CH2NH2 bond, so the CH2 points toward the viewer and the implicit H points away. Viewed from the front, the substituents O → aromatic C → CH2 run clockwise. Converting this to SMILES neighbour order (CH2, H, ring-closure aromatic C, O) gives `@`.

SMILES: NC[C@H]1OB(O)c2c(OCCCO)cccc21
