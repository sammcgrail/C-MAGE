I read the structure as a tert-butylphenyl oxalamide linked to an alanine, then an aspartic acid ketone capped with a tetrafluorophenoxy group.

**Fragments, left to right:**
- A tert-butyl group on a benzene ring, with the NH on the adjacent (ortho) carbon.
- That NH is bonded to an oxalyl unit, C(=O)C(=O), which is bonded to a second NH.
- The second NH is bonded to an alanine unit, CH(CH3)C(=O), which is bonded to a third NH.
- The third NH is bonded to an aspartic-acid-derived carbon bearing a CH2COOH side chain.
- That carbon also carries a ketone, C(=O)CH2–O, which is bonded to a benzene ring.
- On that ring, going round from the oxygen-bearing carbon, the substituents are F, F, H, F, F, so the ring is 2,3,5,6-tetrafluoro-substituted.

**Stereochemistry:**
- The alanine carbon has a hashed methyl bond. With H toward the viewer, N → C(=O) → CH3 runs clockwise, so the true configuration is S (L-alanine).
- The aspartate carbon has a hashed CH2 bond. The priorities are N > ketone C > CH2, and the same reversal applies, so it is also S.
