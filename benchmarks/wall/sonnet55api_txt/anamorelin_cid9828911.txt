I read the structure as anamorelin: a tryptophan-derived amide carrying a 2-amino-2-methylpropanamide cap, linked to a 3-benzylpiperidine-3-carbonyl trimethylhydrazide.

**Fragments**
- Top right: H2N–C(CH3)2–C(=O)–NH–, an α-aminoisobutyryl amide.
- Centre: a CH bearing the NH, a C=O and a CH2–indol-3-yl group (a tryptophan unit). The CH2 bond is hashed.
- The tryptophan C=O is bonded to the nitrogen of a piperidine ring.
- Piperidine C3 is quaternary. It carries a wedged benzyl group and a C(=O)–N(CH3)–N(CH3)2 group.

**Stereochemistry**
- Tryptophan carbon: the hashed CH2 points away, so the implicit H points toward the viewer. Working through the neighbour order gives `[C@@H]` when written as `C(=O)[C@@H](Cc…)N`.
- Quaternary piperidine carbon: the wedged benzyl points toward the viewer. Viewed from the benzyl side, the C=O, ring CH2–N and ring CH2 neighbours run clockwise. In the order I wrote them, `C(=O)[C@@]1(Cc2ccccc2)CCCN(C1)…`, this gives `@@`.
