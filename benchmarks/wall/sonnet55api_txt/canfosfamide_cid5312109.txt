I read the molecule as a glutathione-like tripeptide, γ-Glu–Cys–phenylglycine, in which the cysteine sulfur is oxidised to a sulfone. The sulfone carries a 2-hydroxyethyl ester of a bis(2-chloroethyl)phosphorodiamidate (a phosphoramide mustard).

**Regions read from the drawing**
- **Mustard end:** a P=O phosphorus bears two N(CH2CH2Cl)2 groups and one O–CH2CH2–SO2 link.
- **Cysteine unit:** the sulfone sulfur is bonded to a CH2 on the cysteine α-carbon. That carbon carries an NH-acyl group and a C(=O)N amide.
- **γ-Glu unit:** the cysteine NH is acylated by C(=O)CH2CH2CH(NH2)COOH.
- **Phenylglycine unit:** the cysteine C(=O) is bonded to an NH, which is bonded to CH(Ph)COOH.

**Stereochemistry (three wedged centres)**
- **Glu α-carbon:** NH2 is on a wedge. This gives `CC[C@H](N)C(=O)O` when written from the chain side, which is the L configuration.
- **Cys α-carbon:** the CH2 is on a wedge. This gives `C[C@H](NC(=O)...)C(=O)N`.
- **Phenylglycine α-carbon:** the NH is on a wedge. This gives `N[C@H](c1ccccc1)C(=O)O`.

I checked each centre by viewing it with the implicit H pointing away from the viewer and reading the order of the other three substituents.
