I read this as a halichondrin B-type macrolide. The formula checks out: 60 carbons and 19 oxygens, with two exocyclic methylenes, one lactone C=O and a terminal triol.

**Skeleton, read from the drawing**
- **Right side:** a 1,2,4-triol side chain, CH2OH–CH(OH)–CH2–CH(OH), leads into a THF fused to a methyl-substituted pyran. That pyran is spiro-linked to the next pyran/THF unit.
- **Centre:** a second spiroketal, then two fused pyran rings. The lower pyran carries one methyl, and one ring carbon is the ester-bearing CH (the lactone oxygen position).
- **Bottom:** a pyran with an exocyclic =CH2 and a methyl, a CH2CH2 link, a THF with an exocyclic =CH2, and a CH2CH2 link running up to the ketal carbon on the left.
- **Upper left:** the caged ketal, whose quaternary carbon carries two ring oxygens. This is the ring system that fuses back to the lactone through the pyran drawn above the carbonyl.
- **Lactone:** C(=O) closes the macrocycle back to the ester oxygen.

**Stereochemistry:** I assigned each centre from the wedges and hashes, including the explicit H labels. A second cluster of doubt is the lactone-side CH, where two bonds leave in nearly the same direction.
