The drawing shows a benzene ring carrying three substituents.

- A carboxylic acid (C(=O)OH) is attached at the ring carbon at (581, 721).
- A hydroxyl group (OH) is attached at the adjacent ring carbon at (612, 993).
- An amino group (NH2) is attached at the ring carbon at (1051, 666).

Numbering the ring from the carboxylic acid carbon as C1:
- C1 (581, 721) bears the COOH.
- C2 (612, 993) bears the OH.
- C3 (864, 1102) is a plain CH.
- C4 (1084, 938) is a plain CH.
- C5 (1051, 666) bears the NH2.
- C6 (800, 557) is a plain CH, and it closes the ring back to C1.

So the OH is ortho to the acid and the NH2 is meta to the acid (at C5). The NH2 is para to the OH, since C2 and C5 are opposite each other. This is 5-aminosalicylic acid.
