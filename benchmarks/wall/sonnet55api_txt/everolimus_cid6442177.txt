This is everolimus (the 40-O-(2-hydroxyethyl) derivative of rapamycin). I recognised the scaffold and wrote the SMILES from that recall rather than assigning each stereocentre from the wedges and hashes, so the stereo labels are not independently checked against the drawing.

What the drawing shows, region by region:
- A 31-membered macrolactone ring.
- A pipecolate ring (piperidine with N-acyl) that joins an α-keto amide to the ester carbonyl.
- A hemiketal carbon bearing OH, closed into a tetrahydropyran ring that carries a methyl group.
- A conjugated triene with a methyl substituent, and a second trisubstituted alkene with a methyl substituent.
- Three ketones, two OH groups and two OMe groups on the macrocycle.
- A side chain, CH(CH3)–CH2–cyclohexyl, where the cyclohexane carries an OMe and an OCH2CH2OH group. The OCH2CH2OH group is what distinguishes everolimus from rapamycin.

I checked the ring closures and the ketone–CH2–CH(O-ester) sequence against the drawing.
