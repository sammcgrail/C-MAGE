The drawing shows a pyridine ring with the following substituents:
- N1 is the ring nitrogen.
- C2 carries the carboxamide, C(=O)NH.
- C3 carries OH.
- C4 carries OMe.
- C5 and C6 are plain CH.

The amide nitrogen is bonded to a phenyl ring at its position 1. Position 3 of that ring carries an O–aryl group, and the aryl is a 2-chlorophenyl.

Writing the SMILES from the pyridine: COc1ccnc(C(=O)Nc2cccc(Oc3ccccc3Cl)c2)c1O

Atom check:
- Pyridine: c1 (C4, bearing OMe), c (C5), c (C6), n, c (C2, bearing the amide), c1O (C3, bearing OH). This closes the ring with six atoms.
- Phenyl: c2cccc(...)c2 is six atoms, with the amide N at position 1 and the O at position 3 (meta). The ring is N-c2, c, c, c, c(OAr), c2, so the substituted atoms are separated by one CH. That is meta.
- Chlorophenyl: Oc3ccccc3Cl puts Cl on the carbon adjacent to the O-bearing carbon, which is ortho.
