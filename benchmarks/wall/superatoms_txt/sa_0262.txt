This looks like articaine, the thiophene local anesthetic, with a propylamino group on the side chain.

**Thiophene ring:** S is bonded to a CH carbon and to a carbon carrying CO2Me. That carbon (C2) is double-bonded to C3, which carries the NH amide. C3 is bonded to C4, which carries the methyl group. C4 is double-bonded to C5, the CH next to S.

**Side chain:** The amide nitrogen is attached to C(=O), which is bonded to CH(CH3). That CH carries an NH-nPr group. No wedges or hashes are drawn, so there is no stereochemistry.

SMILES check: CCCNC(C)C(=O)Nc1c(C(=O)OC)scc1C

Reading the ring in that SMILES: c1 (C3, bearing the amide N), then c(CO2Me) (C2), then s, then c (C5, the CH), then c1C (C4, bearing the methyl). This matches the drawing.
