I read the structure in three parts and wrote it as one SMILES.

**Left part.** A 6-(trifluoromethyl)pyridine-2-carboxamide is attached through its amide NH to the 6-position of a benzothiazole. The benzothiazole carries a 2-hydroxypropan-2-yl group at C5 and the cyclohexyl group at C2.

**Middle part.** The cyclohexane is 1,4-disubstituted, with a hashed bond to the benzothiazole on one carbon and a wedged CH2 bond on the other. A hash and a wedge on opposite ends means trans. The CH2 links to the nitrogen of a 2,6-diazaspiro[3.3]heptane, which is drawn as two fused cyclobutanes. The other cyclobutane carries a CH(CH3)CH2NH chain on its CH. That CH is not a stereocentre because the ring is symmetric.

**Chain stereocentre.** The methyl-bearing carbon has a hashed methyl. Its substituents rank CH2N, then the cyclobutyl CH, then CH3, with H lowest. H points toward the viewer and the CH2N → cyclobutyl → CH3 sequence appears counterclockwise, so the centre is R. In the SMILES this is written `[C@@H]`.

**Right part.** The NH is bonded to C4 of a phthalimide-type isoindoline-1,3-dione. Its ring nitrogen is bonded to C3 of a piperidine-2,6-dione, which is drawn with plain bonds and so has no stereo.

For the trans cyclohexane I used the pattern `[C@H]…[C@H]` with the ring atom written before the substituent on the second centre. The two centres are equivalent mirror images of each other, so only the relative trans arrangement matters.
