The SMILES below is my reading of the drawing, built region by region.

**Left tricycle.** A pyridazine ring carries a 2-hydroxyphenyl group on the carbon next to the ring CH. The pyridazine is fused to a six-membered ring containing an NH–CH2 unit and a bridgehead stereocentre. That ring is in turn fused to a piperazine. The piperazine's second nitrogen carries the substituent chain.

**Stereocentre.** The bold wedge goes from the stereocentre to the CH2 that bonds to NH. Its three drawn neighbours rank as follows:
1. The ring nitrogen (highest).
2. The CH2 bonded to the alkylated piperazine nitrogen. The NH-side CH2 ranks lower because that NH carries only one carbon substituent, against two on the alkylated nitrogen.
3. The wedged CH2–NH.

The hydrogen points away from the viewer. Going from the nitrogen (down) to the CH2 (right) to the wedged CH2 (up) is counterclockwise, so the centre is S. In the SMILES below, `[C@H]` written with neighbour order CH2NH, H, ring-closure N, CH2 corresponds to S.

**Linker and right-hand side.** The alkylated piperazine nitrogen is bonded to the 4-position of a piperidine. The piperidine nitrogen carries –CH2CH2–O–C(=O)–, and the carbonyl is bonded to a piperazine nitrogen. The other piperazine nitrogen is bonded to the 5-position of an isoindoline-1,3-dione. Its imide nitrogen carries a 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group, drawn with no stereochemistry, so none is specified there.
