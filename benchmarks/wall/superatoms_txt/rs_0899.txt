The molecule is a methyl ester with a 7-methylindazole on one end and a piperidine-linked dihydroquinazolinone on the other.

**Indazole.** The ring has N and NH in the five-membered ring. The CH= carbon is attached to N, which is double-bonded in the drawing, and the NH sits on the benzo ring at position 1. The methyl group is at C7, next to the C7a carbon bearing NH. The CH2 linker is attached at C5.

**Chain.** From the indazole, CH2 leads to CH(C(=O)OCH3), then CH2, then C(=O) bonded to the piperidine nitrogen.

**Piperidine and quinazolinone.** The piperidine C4 carries N3 of the 3,4-dihydroquinazolin-2(1H)-one. That ring is N3–C(=O)–NH–aryl, with the aryl ring closing back through CH2 to N3.

**Indazole SMILES.** Cc1cc(C...)cc2cn[nH]c12. Here c1 bears the methyl (C7), then cH (C6), then c(CH2...) (C5), then cH (C4), then c2 (C3a), then cH (C3), then n (N2), then [nH] (N1), and finally c12 (C7a). The ring fusion is consistent.
