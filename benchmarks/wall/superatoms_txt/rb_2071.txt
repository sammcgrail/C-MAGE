I read the molecule as a PSMA-binding Lys-urea-Glu unit joined through a suberoyl linker to a lysine–Phe–(3-iodo-Tyr) peptide, capped by a glutamic acid linked to a cyclen ring.

1. **Left end (Lys-urea-Glu):** a lysine whose α-NH and a glutamic acid α-NH are joined by a urea C(=O). The lysine carries a free α-COOH, and the glutamic acid carries two COOH groups. The lysine ε-NH is acylated.
2. **Linker:** the acyl group is a suberoyl chain, C(=O)–(CH2)6–C(=O), with eight carbons in total. Its far end is bonded to the ε-NH of a second lysine, which has a free α-COOH.
3. **Peptide:** the second lysine's α-NH is acylated by Phe (benzyl side chain), whose NH is acylated by 3-iodo-4-hydroxyphenylalanine (iodotyrosine).
4. **Right end:** the iodotyrosine NH is acylated by the γ-carbonyl of a glutamic acid, C(=O)CH2CH2CH(COOH)–. Its α-carbon is attached to one ring nitrogen of a 12-membered cyclen (four N). The other three ring nitrogens each carry a CH2C(=O)NH2 group.

No wedges or hashes are drawn, so I included no stereochemistry.
