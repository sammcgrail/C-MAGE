The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an N-thienyl isatin.

**Biphenyl part.** The upper-left ring carries HO2C at the meta position relative to the biaryl bond. The second ring has the biaryl bond at one carbon, an OH on the adjacent carbon, and the NH–N= group on the next carbon. This gives 2-hydroxy-3-hydrazinyl-biphenyl-3'-carboxylic acid.

**Hydrazone and isatin part.** The NH–N= unit is attached to C3 of an indolin-2-one. C2 is the carbonyl, and N1 bears a thiophen-2-yl group. The benzo ring is fused at the C3a–C7a bond.

**Stereochemistry.** The C=N bond is drawn with a defined geometry. The NH sits upward from the N, and the N=C bond runs down-right to C3. At C3, the carbonyl C2 is on the left and C3a is on the right. I take the NH on the N to be on the same side as the C2 carbonyl, which is the usual Z form stabilised by an NH···O=C hydrogen bond.

In the drawing, HN is at (350, 300) and the imine N is at (410, 335). C3 is at (410, 405), with C2 at left (350, 440) and C3a at right (470, 440). The N=C bond is vertical, so I compare the substituents on each end. The HN substituent lies to the left of the N (x = 350 against 410), and C2 lies to the left of C3. Both are on the same side, so the geometry is Z.

**SMILES.** Writing the hydrazone as `Nc/N=C3/...` with this geometry:

`OC(=O)c1cccc(c1)-c1cccc(N/N=C2\C(=O)N(c3cccs3)c3ccccc23)c1O`

Checking the directional bonds: `N/N=C2\C(=O)` places N (the NH) "below" the first N and C(=O) "above" the C. With the `/` before N=, the NH is on the lower side of the N. After C2, the `\C(=O)` puts the carbonyl carbon on the lower side. Both are on the same side, so this is Z, as intended.
