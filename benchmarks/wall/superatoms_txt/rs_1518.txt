I read the molecule as a 4-sulfonyl-substituted indane-2-carboxylic acid whose sulfonyl group is attached to a 2,6-dimethylpiperazine nitrogen.

- **Left part:** indane (benzene fused to a five-membered ring) with a carboxylic acid (HO–C=O) on C2 of the five-membered ring. The sulfonyl group is on the aromatic carbon adjacent to the ring fusion (position 4).
- **Linker:** the sulfonyl is S(=O)(=O), joined to a piperazine nitrogen.
- **Piperazine:** a six-membered ring N1–C–C–N4–C–C. The two carbons next to the sulfonylated N each carry a methyl on a bold wedge. Both wedges point toward the viewer, so the methyls are cis, and I assigned each centre from the drawn geometry.
- **N4 substituent:** a CH2CH2 chain leading to a para-substituted benzene ring that carries S–CF3.

Both piperazine stereocentres come out as the cis (meso-type) pair, written `[C@@H]` for the top carbon and `[C@H]` for the bottom carbon in the SMILES below.
