I read the molecule as a macrocycle fused to a benzimidazole, and wrote the SMILES in four parts.

**Left-hand pyridine.** The ring has N, C(CF3), CH, C(CH2CH2O–), C(CH2O–) and CH. The two substituents sit on adjacent carbons.

**Central benzene.** One ring carbon carries a CH2–O group (the end of the CH2CH2–O–CH2 chain from the first pyridine). The next ring carbon carries the CH2 that links to the benzimidazole. The ring carbon meta to the CH2–O carbon is bonded to the second pyridine.

**Second pyridine.** Its C2 is bonded to the benzene and its C6 carries an O–CH2 group that closes onto the first pyridine, which completes the macrocycle.

**Benzimidazole.**
- N1 carries a CH2–oxetan-2-yl group.
- C2 carries the CH2 link to the benzene.
- The benzo ring bears a CO2Me group and an OCH2CH2OMe group.

**Stereochemistry.** The only drawn stereo-element is the hashed bond from the oxetane C2 to the CH2–N group, which puts the implicit H toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are O > CH2N > ring CH2 > H. Viewed from the front the sequence runs clockwise, and reversing for the H pointing toward the viewer gives S. In the SMILES order CH2N, H, O, C3, that is `[C@@H]`.
