The drawing is an azo dye with two substituted benzene rings joined by an N=N bond.

**Left ring:** The carbon bonded to the azo group sits between two carbons that each carry a CN group. The carbon para to the azo carbon carries NO2. This is a 2,6-dicyano-4-nitrophenyl group.

**Right ring:** Number the carbon bonded to the azo group as C1. Moving clockwise in the drawing:
- C2 is the upper carbon, bearing OCH3 at the top.
- C3 is the upper-right carbon, bearing N(CH2CH2OH)2.
- C4 is the lower-right carbon, bearing OCH3.
- C5 is the lower-left carbon, bearing the other substituent.

The ring in the picture is placed with the azo-bearing carbon at the left. Its vertices are:
- upper-left: CH
- top: C(OCH3)
- upper-right: C bearing N
- bottom-right: CH
- bottom: C(OCH3)
- left: C bearing the azo group

So the azo carbon (C1) is flanked by a CH at the upper left and by C(OCH3) at the bottom. Going around from C1:
- C1 (azo)
- CH (upper left)
- C(OMe) (top)
- C(N) (right, upper)
- CH (lower right)
- C(OMe) (bottom)
- back to C1

The azo group is therefore ortho to one OMe (the bottom one) and meta to the other (the top one). The amine is para to the azo group, so the ring is 2,5-dimethoxy-4-aminophenyl.
