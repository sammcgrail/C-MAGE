I read the molecule as a pyrazin-2(1H)-one core carrying three substituents.

- **N1:** a CH bearing an ethyl group, a cyclopropyl group, and a hashed H.
- **C5:** a chlorine.
- **C3:** the nitrogen of an indoline. On the indoline's benzene ring, the methyl sits at C7 (next to the ring-fusion carbon bonded to N) and the OMe at C5.

**Stereocentre:** it is the N-bound CH. The CIP priorities are N > cyclopropyl > ethyl > H. The H is hashed, so it points away from the viewer. In the drawing, N (down), cyclopropyl (upper right) and ethyl (upper left) run counterclockwise, which gives S. Writing the neighbours in the order ethyl, H, cyclopropyl, N, this corresponds to `@@` in SMILES.

SMILES: CC[C@@H](C1CC1)n1cc(Cl)nc(N2CCc3cc(OC)cc(C)c32)c1=O
