I read the molecule as a DTPA-type chelator joined through a 6-aminohexanoyl linker to a 2,3-diaminopropionic acid unit, which carries an indazole bearing a 2-aminoimidazole side chain.

1. **Chelator end (left):** A diethylenetriamine backbone has three nitrogens linked by two –CH2CH2– units. The two terminal nitrogens carry CH2COOH groups: two on the bottom nitrogen and one on the top nitrogen. The middle nitrogen carries one CH2COOH. The top nitrogen also carries a CH2C(=O)NH– arm that leads into the linker.
2. **Linker:** The amide NH is followed by five CH2 groups, counted vertex by vertex, and then a C=O. This is a 6-aminohexanoyl unit, and it ends in an amide NH on the alpha carbon.
3. **Alpha carbon:** It bears the amide NH, a COOH, a CH2NH– group and an implicit H. The wedge runs to the CH2 group. With H pointing away from the viewer, N → CH2 → COOH runs clockwise, which gives `N[C@@H](CH2…)C(=O)O` in SMILES.
4. **Aryl amide:** The CH2NH– group is acylated by a C=O attached at C5 of an indazole.
5. **Indazole:** N1 carries a three-carbon chain, and N2=C3 is the pyrazole double bond.
6. **Side chain:** The propyl chain ends in NH attached to C2 of a 1H-imidazole, i.e. a 2-aminoimidazole.

SMILES: OC(=O)CN(CC(=O)O)CCN(CC(=O)O)CCN(CC(=O)O)CC(=O)NCCCCCC(=O)N[C@@H](CNC(=O)c1ccc2c(cnn2CCCNc2ncc[nH]2)c1)C(=O)O
