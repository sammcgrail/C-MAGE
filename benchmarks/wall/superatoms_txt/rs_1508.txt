The drawing shows a biphenyl unit joined through an amide to a pyridine, which carries a second amide to a bromopyridine.

**Left biphenyl:** A benzene ring bears SO2NH2 at the ortho position and is bonded to a para-substituted phenyl ring. That phenyl ring carries a C(=O)NH group.

**Central pyridine:** The amide NH attaches to the pyridine at C3. C2 of this ring bears a C(=O)NH group, and the ring nitrogen is adjacent to C2. The ring is therefore a pyridine-2-carboxamide with a 3-amido substituent.

**Right pyridine:** The amide NH attaches to C2 of a second pyridine. The ring nitrogen sits next to C2, and Br is on C5. In the drawing, the carbon bearing NH is bonded to the ring N, and the N-adjacent carbon on the other side is CH. Going around the ring from C2: N1, C6(H), C5(Br), C4, C3. Br is at the bottom right, para to the ring carbon that is attached to the CH next to N. So the ring is 5-bromopyridin-2-yl.
