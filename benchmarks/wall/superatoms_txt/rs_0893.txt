I read the molecule as a methanesulfonate ester on a phenyl ring, joined through a trans-propenyl linker to an aniline-type NH. That NH is attached to a second phenyl ring bearing a CH2–CH(OMe)–COOEt side chain.

- **Left end:** CH3–S(=O)(=O)–O attached to the first benzene ring (para-substituted).
- **Linker:** a trans CH=CH, then CH2, then NH (the drawing shows N with an explicit H).
- **Right ring:** a para-substituted benzene carrying CH2–CH(OMe)–C(=O)OEt.
- **Stereocentre:** the carbon bearing OMe has a bold wedge to OMe, so the implicit H points away from the viewer. The priorities are OMe > COOEt > CH2Ar > H. Going OMe (bottom) → COOEt (upper right) → CH2 (upper left) is counterclockwise with H at the back, which gives S.
- **Double bond:** the drawing shows trans geometry, written as `/C=C/`.

For the SMILES `CCOC(=O)[C@@H](OC)Cc1ccc(NC/C=C/c2ccc(OS(C)(=O)=O)cc2)cc1`, the neighbour order at the stereocentre is C(=O), H, OMe, CH2. Working through that order, `@` gives R and `@@` gives S, so I used `@@`.
