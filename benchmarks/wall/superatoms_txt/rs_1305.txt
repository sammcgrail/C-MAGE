The central benzene ring carries an OH at the top and another at the bottom. It also carries two aryl groups, a 4-methoxyphenyl at the upper right and another at the lower left.

Numbering the central ring: C1 (top) bears OH, C2 (upper right) bears the aryl group, C3 (lower right) is CH, C4 (bottom) bears OH, C5 (lower left) bears the aryl group, and C6 (upper left) is CH.

So C1 (OH) is adjacent to C2 (aryl) and C6 (H). C4 (OH) is adjacent to C3 (H) and C5 (aryl). The ring is therefore 2,5-diaryl-1,4-hydroquinone, with the two OH groups para to each other and the two aryl groups para to each other.

SMILES, written around the ring from C1: Oc1cc(-c2ccc(OC)cc2)c(O)cc1-c1ccc(OC)cc1

Checking this against the ring order: c1(O) is C1, then c is C6 (H), then c(-Ar) is C5, then c(O) is C4, then c is C3 (H), then c1(-Ar) is C2. This matches the drawing, with C1 and C4 as the OH carbons.
