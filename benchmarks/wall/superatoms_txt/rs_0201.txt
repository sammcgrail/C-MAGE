The drawing shows a central 1,4-disubstituted benzene ring. Each para position carries a CH2CH2 linker that leads to a naphthalene unit.

Each naphthalene is attached to the linker at its 1-position. Its 4-position (para to the linker) carries N(C2H5)2.

In the left naphthalene, the top ring has the N(Et)2 at the upper left and the CH2 attachment at the right, para to it. The fused benzo ring lies below. The right naphthalene is drawn the same way, mirrored.

Check on the naphthalene: c1(N)ccc(R)c2ccccc12 gives C4 with N, then C3 and C2 as CH, then C1 with R, then the fusion carbon c2, four benzo CH atoms, and the closing fusion carbon c12, which bonds back to the N-bearing carbon. This is correct.
