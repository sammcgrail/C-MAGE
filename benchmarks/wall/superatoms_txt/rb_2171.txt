I read the structure as a DOTA-type chelator (a 12-membered tetraaza ring with three carboxymethyl arms and one acetamide arm) attached to a cyclic peptide. The Ga label is drawn inside the macrocycle with no bonds to the nitrogens, so I wrote it as a separate `[Ga]` atom.

**Peptide, from the DOTA end:**
1. Cysteic acid, with a CH2SO3H side chain.
2. A lysine whose side-chain nitrogen is the amide to cysteic acid. Its α-nitrogen is the N of a 1-methyl-isoindole.
3. Tyrosine.
4. N⁶-isopropyl-lysine.
5. Arginine, with a guanidine side chain.
6. 2-Naphthylalanine.
7. Alanine.
8. Cysteine, whose sulfur is bonded to C3 of the isoindole. This closes the macrocycle, and I wrote the closure as ring bond `%10`.
9. A C-terminal N⁶-isopropyl-lysine amide.

**Stereochemistry:** I assigned each centre from its wedge or hash bond. Cysteic acid, lysine, tyrosine, N⁶-isopropyl-lysine, 2-naphthylalanine, cysteine and the C-terminal lysine all come out L. Arginine (hashed side chain) and alanine (wedged methyl) come out D.
