The drawing shows a pyridine ring with these substituents:
- OMe at C4
- OH at C3
- the amide carbonyl at C2, next to the ring N

The amide NH connects to a benzene ring. That ring carries methyl groups at both positions flanking the carbon that bears the ester oxygen. The ester is isobutyrate: O–C(=O)–CH(CH3)2.

Pyridine SMILES: n1ccc(OC)c(O)c1C(=O)N...

Full SMILES: COc1ccnc(C(=O)Nc2cc(C)c(OC(=O)C(C)C)c(C)c2)c1O

Check of the pyridine ring: c1 (C4, OMe), c (C5), c (C6), n, c (C2, bearing the amide), c1 (C3, OH). Written as COc1ccnc(C(=O)N...)c1O, this gives C4(OMe), C5, C6, N, C2(amide), C3(OH), which closes back to C4. This is correct.
