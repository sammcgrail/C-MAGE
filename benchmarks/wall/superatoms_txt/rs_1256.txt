I read the molecule as a 4-substituted benzoic acid linked through an ethylene chain to the nitrogen of a 5-oxopyrrolidine (γ-lactam). The ring carbon next to nitrogen carries an (E)-3-hydroxy-4-phenylbut-1-enyl chain.

**Connectivity**
- The benzoic acid ring is para-substituted: CO2H at one end and CH2CH2–N at the other.
- The lactam has N, a C=O carbon and two CH2 groups, plus the ring CH next to N that bears the side chain.
- The side chain runs CH=CH, then CH(OH), then CH2, then a phenyl group.

**Stereochemistry**
- The alkene is drawn trans (E).
- At the ring CH, the bond to the vinyl group is a bold wedge, so the vinyl group points toward the viewer and H points away. Reading the neighbours in SMILES order (ring CH2, H, N, vinyl) gives `@@`.
- At the carbinol carbon, the OH is on a hashed bond, so OH points away and H points toward the viewer. Reading the neighbours in SMILES order (vinyl, H, O, CH2) also gives `@@`.

SMILES: OC(=O)c1ccc(CCN2C(=O)CC[C@@H]2/C=C/[C@@H](O)Cc2ccccc2)cc1
