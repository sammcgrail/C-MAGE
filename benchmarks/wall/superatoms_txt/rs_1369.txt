- NH2 is on the upper-right carbon.
- CN is on the lower-right carbon, adjacent to the carbon bearing NH2.

I label the ring vertices: C1 top (the NH2 carbon, upper right), C2 the CN carbon (right-lower), C3 bottom, C4 lower-left (the Br carbon), C5 upper-left, C6 top.

In the image, the ring vertices run roughly as follows: top (~110,10), upper-right (~175,45) with NH2, lower-right (~175,110) with CN, bottom (~110,145), lower-left (~45,110) with Br, upper-left (~45,45).

Relative to the NH2 carbon, the CN carbon is adjacent (ortho). Going from the NH2 carbon through the CN carbon to the bottom carbon and then to the Br carbon, Br is 3 bonds away, so it is para to NH2. Going the other way, from NH2 through the top carbon and the upper-left carbon to Br, it is also 3 bonds, which confirms para.

Relative to CN, Br is two bonds away (CN carbon, bottom carbon, Br carbon), so it is meta to CN.

So the compound is 2-amino-5-bromobenzonitrile. Numbering from the CN carbon as C1, NH2 is at C2 and Br is at C5 (para to NH2, meta to CN), which fits.
