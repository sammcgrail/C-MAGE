I read the molecule as a succinyl-type diamide core, a four-carbon chain with a carbonyl at each end and one stereocentre, carrying three substituents.

- **Stereocentre (C2 of the chain):** it carries an NH (wedged bond) linked to a 5-chloro-2-nitrophenyl ring, and a C(=O) amide to the 4-(piperidin-1-yl)piperidine.
- **CH2–C(=O) arm:** the other carbon neighbour is a CH2 followed by a C(=O) amide to a piperidine nitrogen. That piperidine carries a 2-oxo-1,2,3,4-tetrahydroquinazolin-3-yl group at C4, attached through the ring N3.
- **Stereochemistry:** the NH is wedged toward the viewer and the implicit H points away. The priorities are N, then C(=O)N, then CH2, then H. Viewed from the front, N (top) to C(=O) (lower left) to CH2 (lower right) runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S. In the SMILES below, `[C@@H]` with the neighbour order C(=O), H, N, CH2 encodes S.
