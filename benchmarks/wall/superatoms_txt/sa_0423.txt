I read the structure as an open-chain N-acetylhexosamine: CHO–CH(NHAc)–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–CH(OH)–CH2OH.

**Connectivity.** Going from right to left, the chain is:
- an aldehyde carbon (CHO);
- C2, bearing NHAc, where Ac expands to C(C)=O;
- C3, C4 and C5, each bearing an OH;
- a terminal CH2OH.

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned each centre from the drawn wedges and hashes, treating the implicit H as pointing opposite the wedged or hashed substituent.
- C2: NHAc is hashed, so H is toward the viewer. This gives `[C@H]` with neighbour order CHO, H, N, C3.
- C3: OH is wedged, so H is behind. This gives `[C@@H]` with neighbour order C2, H, O, C4.
- C4: OH is wedged, so H is behind. This gives `[C@H]` with neighbour order C3, H, O, C5.
- C5: OH is hashed, so H is in front. This gives `[C@H]` with neighbour order C4, H, O, C6.
