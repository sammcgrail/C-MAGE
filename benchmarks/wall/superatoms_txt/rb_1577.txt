I read the structure in four pieces, then joined them.

- **Left end:** a tetrahydropyran-2-carboxamide. The wedge on the ring carbon puts the carbonyl toward the viewer, and the CIP priorities are O > C(=O)N > CH2 > H. With H pointing away, O → C(=O) → CH2 runs clockwise, so this carbon is R. In the SMILES, `[C@H]1CCCCO1` written after the carbonyl carbon gives R.
- **Indazole:** a 2H-indazole with OMe at C6 and the amide NH at C5. N2 carries the cyclohexyl group.
- **Cyclohexane:** a 1,4-disubstituted ring with a wedge at the carbon bearing the indazole N and a hash at the carbon bearing the CH2. A wedge on one carbon and a hash on the other means trans. I wrote this as `[C@H]1CC[C@@H](...)CC1`, which I recall as the trans pattern.
- **Linker and right end:** CH2 leads to a 7-azaspiro[3.5]nonane, with the piperidine N bearing the CH2. The cyclobutane carbon opposite the spiro centre carries a CH2CH2NH chain. That chain ends on C4 of an isoindoline-1,3-dione, whose N is attached to the 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group. The cyclobutane carbon and the glutarimide carbon have plain bonds, so I gave them no stereo.
