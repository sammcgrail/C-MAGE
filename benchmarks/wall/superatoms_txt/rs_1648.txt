The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an N-phenyl oxindole.

**Left part.** The top ring carries CO2H. Its ring carbon attached to the second ring is meta to the CO2H carbon, so this is a 3-substituted benzoic acid.

**Middle ring.** The second ring is attached to the first ring at one carbon. The adjacent carbon bears OH, and the next carbon bears NH–N=. So the ring reads C1(aryl), C2(OH), C3(NH-N=), with the remaining three ring carbons unsubstituted.

**Indolinone part.** C3 is double-bonded to N (the hydrazone). C2 is C=O. N1 carries a phenyl group. The fused benzene ring has F at the position para to C3a's neighbor. Numbering the indoline: N1, C2(=O), C3(=N), C3a, C4, C5, C6, C7, C7a. In the drawing, C3a connects up to the ring carbon at top, which is C4, then C5 at top right, then C6 at right bearing F, then C7 at bottom, then C7a attached to N. So F is on C6, which matches the 6-fluoro label.

**SMILES.** Writing the acid ring first, then the hydroxy-bearing ring, then the hydrazone to the indolinone:

OC(=O)c1cccc(c1)-c1cccc(NN=C2C(=O)N(c3ccccc3)c3cc(F)ccc23)c1O

Checking the indolinone fragment: C2 (the C3 of the indole) is bonded to C(=O), then N(Ph), then c3 (C7a), then cc(F) giving C7 and C6(F), then cc23 giving C5 and C4, closing back to C3a. The ring closure labels work out, so the SMILES is valid. The C=N geometry is not clearly defined by a wedge or cis/trans marking in the drawing, so I left it unspecified.
