The drawing shows a 3-hydroxy-4-methoxypyridine-2-carboxamide whose amide nitrogen is attached to a second pyridine.

**Left pyridine ring.** The ring nitrogen is at the bottom left. Going around the ring, C2 (bottom right of the ring) carries the C(=O)NH group. C3 carries OH. C4 carries OMe. C5 and C6 are the CH=CH at the left, with C6 bonded to N.

**Amide.** C2 is bonded to C(=O), which is bonded to NH.

**Right pyridine ring.** The NH is attached to a ring carbon, which I call C3 of this pyridine. The ring nitrogen sits at the top right, and the O-aryl group is on the bottom-right carbon next to the nitrogen, so that carbon is C2 (the OAr position). The ring runs N1, C2 (OAr), C3 (H), C4 (H), C5 (NH), C6 (H). In the drawing, the NH-bearing carbon is at the upper left of the ring, bonded to the CH that is bonded to N. So the NH carbon is C5 and the OAr carbon is C2, a 2,5-disubstituted pyridine.

**Aryl ether.** The oxygen links to a phenyl ring bearing CF3 at the para position.
