The drawing shows a benzimidazole with a thiophene-amine substituent, as the hydrochloride salt.

**Benzimidazole core.** The benzene ring carries Cl at the top-left carbon (C4, next to the ring-fusion carbon bonded to the N= nitrogen). CF3 is on the lower-left carbon (C6). The imidazole ring has N= at the top and NH at the bottom, and C2 carries the NH substituent.

**Thiophene substituent.** The thiophene is attached through its C3 position to the NH. The ring is C3 (bearing NH), C4 (bearing methyl), C5, S, C2. Reading the drawing, the NH-bearing carbon is at the left and the methyl-bearing carbon at the upper right. The bottom-left carbon is CH, S is at the bottom, and the right carbon is CH. So the ring is c1(N)c(C)csc1, which is a 4-methylthiophen-3-yl group.

**SMILES assembly.** Benzimidazole: Clc1cc(C(F)(F)F)cc2[nH]c(Nc3cscc3C)nc12. Here c1 carries Cl and is bonded to the ring-fusion carbon c12, which bears the n (the N= nitrogen). The ring-fusion carbon on the other side carries [nH] and is attached to the CH that neighbors the CF3 carbon. This matches the drawing.

The HCl is written as a separate component.
