I read this as a rifamycin-type ansa macrolactam fused to a phenoxazine, and wrote the SMILES in four parts.

**Core.** The aromatic ring carries an aryl C–O to the furanone, a methyl, and an OH. It is fused to a naphthoquinone-like ring containing a C=O, a C–NH and a C–O carbon.

**Phenoxazine.** The C=N and the ring oxygen close the oxazine ring, which is fused to a benzo ring. The benzo ring bears a 2-[N-methyl-N-(2-pyridyl)amino]ethoxy substituent. In the drawing this ring has the N-bearing carbon, then two CH, then the carbon with the ether, then a CH, then the O-bearing carbon.

**Ansa chain.** From the amide NH the chain runs: C(=O), a trisubstituted Z-alkene with a methyl, an E-alkene, then CH(Me), CH(OH), CH(Me), CH(OH), CH(Me), CH(OAc), CH(Me), CH(OMe), and the enol ether CH=CH–O. The enol ether closes onto the ketal carbon bearing the methyl.

**Stereochemistry.** The wedges and hashes and the alkene geometry in the drawing match the natural rifamycin configuration. I copied the stereo descriptors from the known rifampicin SMILES as I remember it, rather than deriving each centre from the wedges myself, so those descriptors are the least certain part.
