The drawing shows a 2-methylbenzothiazole joined through a sulfonamide to an isophthalic acid.

**Benzothiazole part.** S is at the top left and N at the bottom left of the thiazole ring. The methyl group sits on C2, between S and N. The fused benzene ring lies to the right of the thiazole.

**Position of the sulfonyl group.** The thiazole ring atoms are S1, C2, N3, C3a (bottom, bonded to N), the benzene carbons C4, C5, C6, C7, and C7a (top, bonded to S). Going around the benzene ring from C7a at the top: the next vertex at the top right is C7, then C6 (the upper right vertex, which carries SO2), then C5, C4, and C3a.

The SO2 attaches at the upper vertex of the benzene ring, the one at about x=290. In the drawing, C7a is at about (165, 70) and C7 at about (225, 40). The next vertex, C6, is at about (290, 70), and that is where SO2 attaches. So the sulfonyl is on C6, which is bonded to C7 (adjacent to C7a, the carbon bearing S).

**SMILES for this half.** Cc1nc2ccc(S(=O)(=O)N...)cc2s1. Here C3a is bonded to N, then C4 and C5 are the "cc", then C6 carries the sulfonyl, and C7 is the final "c" before C7a (c2) and S.

**Isophthalic acid part.** The NH is attached to a benzene ring that carries COOH groups at positions 3 and 5 relative to it, giving c1cc(C(=O)O)cc(C(=O)O)c1.
