The drawing shows a 3,5-dimethylisoxazole ring. The ring atoms are O1, N2, C3, C4 and C5, with N=C3 as a double bond and C4=C5 as a double bond. C3 carries a methyl group and C5 carries a methyl group (the short line at the right end of the ring).

C4 is bonded to a carbon below it. That carbon has a wavy line crossing a bond, which marks the attachment point, and a plain line going down, which I read as a methyl. The wavy line crosses a horizontal bond, so the carbon has two horizontal bonds, one to each side, plus the bond down and the bond up to the ring. One horizontal side is the wavy attachment bond, so the other side would have been cut off.

Reading it as a quaternary carbon makes the most sense: C(CH3)(CH3) attached to the ring, with a wavy bond. The wavy line is at the left and right, passing through the carbon, so the carbon bears the attachment point. The quaternary carbon would then have four bonds: ring C4, the wavy bond, a methyl down, and one more. The wavy line appears to extend both left and right of the carbon, crossing it, which suggests a single attachment bond drawn through the carbon. That leaves a carbon with only three substituents: the ring, the attachment, and the methyl going down.

Treating it as CH(CH3) with one attachment gives the isopropyl-type fragment: ring–CH(CH3)–*.

I take the wavy line as a bond at the carbon, with the carbon bearing the ring, a methyl, and the attachment, so the carbon is CH.
