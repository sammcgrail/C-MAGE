I read the molecule as a biotin–PEG-linked cyclic heptapeptide, closed through a 1,4-disubstituted triazole (a lactam-free "click" macrocycle). I assumed all residues are L-amino acids, which I checked by CIP analysis for the triazolyl-alanine residue. The same check was not done for the other residues, whose stereo I took as L, and the biotin stereocentres are written as in natural D-biotin.

**Pieces read from the drawing**
- **Biotin:** the bicyclic ureido-thiolane with a pentanoyl chain.
- **Linker:** the biotin carbonyl is amide-bonded to NH-CH2CH2-O-CH2CH2-O-CH2CH2-O-CH2-C(=O), which acylates the N-terminus of the first ring residue.
- **Ring sequence (N→C):** triazolyl-alanine, Lys, Leu, Gly, Trp, Pro, then a C-terminal primary amide residue.
- **Triazole:** C4 carries the alanine CH2 and N1 carries a chain of four CH2 groups. I counted four from the drawing.
- **C-terminal residue:** the four-CH2 chain runs from triazole N1 back to the α-carbon of this residue, which bears the C(N)=O group and is amide-bonded to the Pro carbonyl.

- The triazolyl-alanine centre has a hashed bond to its CH2 and comes out as S, i.e. L.
