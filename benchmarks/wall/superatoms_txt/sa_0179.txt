I read the structure as clomiphene (the free base), with the double-bond geometry taken from the drawing.

- **Left side:** a diethylamino group (Et₂N) connects through a two-carbon chain (N–CH₂–CH₂) to an oxygen, which is attached to a para-substituted benzene ring.
- **Right side:** the ring's para carbon is bonded to a C=C double bond. One alkene carbon carries a phenyl group and the ring (the anisyl-type aryl). The other carries a Cl and a second phenyl group.
- **Alkene geometry:** the Cl and the para-substituted aryl ring both point to the left of the double bond, so they are cis. The two phenyl groups both point right.
- **CIP priorities:** Cl outranks Ph on one carbon. The aryl ring carrying the O-substituent outranks Ph on the other. Because the two higher-priority groups are cis, the double bond is Z (zuclomiphene).

The SMILES `Cl/C(c1ccccc1)=C(\c1ccc(OCCN(CC)CC)cc1)c1ccccc1` encodes this: the `/` before the first carbon and the `\` on the aryl branch place Cl and the aryl ring on the same side.
