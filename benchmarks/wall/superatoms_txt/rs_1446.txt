I read the molecule as a 3-hydroxy-4-methoxypyridine-2-carboxamide whose amide nitrogen is bonded to a norbornane (bicyclo[2.2.1]heptane) ring. That norbornane is fused to an N-isobutyl succinimide (a 2-methylpropyl group on the imide nitrogen).

**Pyridine part.** The ring carbon next to N bears the C(=O)NH group (C2). The adjacent carbon carries OH (C3), and the next carbon carries OMe (C4). The remaining ring positions are CH, CH and N.

**Bicyclic part.**
- The carbon bearing NH is bonded to a bridgehead carbon and to a CH2.
- That CH2 leads to the second bridgehead carbon.
- The one-carbon bridge (CH2) joins the two bridgeheads.
- Each bridgehead is also bonded to one of the two ring-fusion carbons that carry the imide C=O groups.

**Stereochemistry.** Only the C–NH bond is drawn as a wedge, so only that carbon gets a stereo label. Its substituents rank N > bridgehead carbon > CH2, with H pointing away. In the drawing the sequence N → bridgehead → CH2 runs clockwise, which gives R. In the SMILES below the neighbour order at that carbon is N, H, ring-closure bridgehead, CH2, and R corresponds to `@@` for that order.
