The drawing shows a benzofuran attached at its C2 position to a benzene ring that carries the other substituents.

**Benzofuran part:** the benzene ring is fused to a furan ring. The furan oxygen is at the top. The carbon next to oxygen (C2) is bonded to the aryl ring. C3 is the CH below it, and C3 connects to the fused ring carbon.

**Substituted benzene ring:** going around the ring, the substituents are:
- C1: attached to the benzofuran C2
- C2: OCH3 (top)
- C3: O of the methylenedioxy group (upper right)
- C4: O of the methylenedioxy group (lower right)
- C5: H (bottom)
- C6: OH (bottom left)

The two oxygens at C3 and C4 are joined by a CH2, forming the dioxole ring.

**SMILES:** start with the benzofuran as c1ccc2oc(cc2c1)…, then attach the aryl group:
c1ccc2oc(cc2c1)-c1c(OC)c2OCOc2cc1O

**Check of the aryl ring:** c1 (bonded to benzofuran), c(OC), c2 (O–), then OCO, c2 (closing the dioxole), cH, c1 (OH). That gives six aromatic atoms: c1, c(OC), c2, c2', cH, c(O). The ring closes correctly, and the OH carbon is adjacent to the benzofuran-bearing carbon, matching the drawing.
