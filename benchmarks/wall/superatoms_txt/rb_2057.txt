I read this as a PROTAC-type molecule: an EZH2-inhibitor fragment joined to a VHL-ligand fragment through a piperazine amide and a long diacid chain.

**Left, EZH2 end**
- A 4,6-dimethyl-2-pyridone is attached through its CH2 to an amide NH.
- That amide sits on a central benzene ring bearing a methyl and an N-ethyl-N-(tetrahydropyran-4-yl)amino group.
- The same ring is joined to a second, para-substituted benzene whose benzylic CH2 goes to a piperazine nitrogen.

**Linker**
- The other piperazine nitrogen is acylated by a zigzag chain.
- Counting vertices along the chain gives 16 carbons including both carbonyl carbons, so it is a hexadecanedioyl unit: C(=O), 14 CH2 groups, then C(=O).
- The far carbonyl forms an amide with the NH of the tert-leucine unit.

**Right, VHL end**
- The tert-leucine nitrogen bears the (S)-tert-butyl carbon, which is acylated onto the pyrrolidine nitrogen.
- The pyrrolidine is a 4-hydroxyproline with a hashed OH, and the drawing labels C2 as (S) and C4 as (S).
- Its carboxamide NH is attached to a 4-(4-methylthiazol-5-yl)benzyl group.

**Stereochemistry**
I followed the (S) labels shown in the drawing. The C4 label differs from the usual (4R) VHL ligand. After converting each label to SMILES neighbour order, the centres come out as tert-leucine `[C@@H]`, C4 `[C@@H](O)`, and C2 `[C@H]`.
