I read the molecule as three parts joined through a sulfonamide.

- **Indane acid:** an indane (benzene fused to a five-membered ring) carries a carboxylic acid on C2 of the five-membered ring. This C2 is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label. The sulfonyl group sits on the benzene carbon next to the ring fusion (C4).
- **Piperazine:** one nitrogen is the sulfonamide N, and the carbons on either side of it each carry a methyl group. Both methyls are drawn with bold wedges, so the 2,6-dimethyl pattern is cis. The other nitrogen carries a benzyl group.
- **Benzyl group:** a benzene ring with Cl at the 3-position and OCF3 at the 4-position.

I assigned the two methyl-bearing stereocentres from the wedge geometry in the drawing. The upper one is `[C@H]` and the lower one is `[C@@H]` in the SMILES below.
