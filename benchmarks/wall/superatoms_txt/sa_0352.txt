The drawing shows "NC" on the left, joined by a bond to a central CH2 vertex, which is joined to CO2Et on the right.

The left label is written "NC" with N first, so the CH2 is bonded to the carbon of that label, and N is the terminal atom. This is an isocyanide (isocyano) group, –N≡C, not a nitrile. Ethyl isocyanoacetate has the CH2 attached to N, which would be written "CN" on the left, but here the label reads "NC". In a drawing, a label is written so that the atom bonded to the chain is the one nearest the bond. The bond attaches at the right side of "NC", at the C. So the CH2 is bonded to the C of the label, which makes it a nitrile: N≡C–CH2.

The right side is CO2Et, an ethyl ester, so the molecule is ethyl cyanoacetate: N#CCC(=O)OCC.
