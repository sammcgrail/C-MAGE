I read the molecule as a linear hexapeptide, Asp–Lys(azido)–Asp–Lys–Asp–Cys, carrying two quinoline-based difluoro-cyanopyrrolidine units.

**Peptide backbone**
- The N-terminal Asp keeps its free α-COOH. Its α-NH is acylated by a succinyl linker, and its side-chain carboxyl forms an amide with the next residue's α-NH.
- The second residue is a lysine whose ε-nitrogen is part of a 1,4-disubstituted triazole (an azidonorleucine residue). Its chain has four CH2 groups between the α-carbon and the triazole N1.
- The remaining residues are Asp (free CH2COOH), Lys (free NH2 on a four-CH2 chain), Asp (free CH2COOH), and a C-terminal Cys with a free thiol and free COOH.

**Quinoline units**
- Each unit is a quinoline carrying a 4-carboxamide and an 8-NH, with the carboxamide linked through Gly to a 4,4-difluoro-2-cyanopyrrolidine.
- The 8-NH of the lower unit is acylated by the succinyl group on the N-terminal Asp. The 8-NH of the upper unit is acylated by a second succinyl group.
- That second succinyl group is linked through NH–CH2 to C4 of the triazole.

**Stereochemistry**
- I assigned the cyanopyrrolidine carbons as S and all the amino-acid α-carbons as L. This follows the standard convention and the wedge on the CN groups, rather than a close check of each wedge or hash in the image.
