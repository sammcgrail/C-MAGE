The drawing shows an isoquinolin-1(2H)-one: a benzene ring fused to a pyridone ring that carries an NH and a C=O.

**Pyridone ring.** C1 is the carbonyl carbon (C=O at the bottom right). N2 is the NH. C3 and C4 form a double bond (the upper right of the ring). C4a and C8a are the ring-fusion carbons.

**Benzene ring.** Going around from C4a, the carbons are C5 (top), C6 (bearing OMe), C7 (bearing F), C8 (the lower vertex), then C8a, which is bonded to C1.

**Substituents.** The methoxy group is on C6, which is the upper-left carbon next to C5. The fluorine is on C7, the carbon below it. C8 is a CH that connects to C8a.

**SMILES.** Writing from the carbonyl carbon: O=C1NC=Cc2cc(OC)c(F)cc12. C1 is the carbonyl, then N, then C3=C4, then C4a (written as c2). Next come C5 (cH), C6 (c(OC)), C7 (c(F)), C8 (cH) and C8a (c12), which closes both rings.
