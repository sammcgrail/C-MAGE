The drawing shows a pyrimidine ring with these substituents:

- C4 carries Cl.
- C5 carries a phenyl group.
- C6 carries a 4-fluorophenyl group.
- C2 carries CF3.
- N1 and N3 are the two ring nitrogens. N3 sits between C4 and C2, and N1 sits between C2 and C6.

Reading the ring from the top: C(Cl) at the top right, then N at the right, then C(CF3), then N at the bottom, then C(4-fluorophenyl) at the bottom left, then C(phenyl) at the top left. The ring is therefore C(Cl)=N–C(CF3)=N–C(Ar)=C(Ph), which is a valid pyrimidine.
