The drawing shows a piperidine ring with an N-Boc group, a ketone at C4, and an exocyclic enamine (=CH-NMe2) at C3.

Ring atoms, read from the image:
- N1 carries the Boc group, C(=O)O-C(CH3)3.
- C2 is the CH2 between N1 and C3.
- C3 carries the exocyclic double bond to CH-NMe2.
- C4 is the ketone carbon, C=O.
- C5 and C6 are CH2 groups.

The double bond is drawn going down from C3 to the CH, with Me2N to the left. Because C3 has two different ring substituents (C2 and C4) and the CH carries H and NMe2, the double bond is technically a stereo element. Its geometry is shown only by the planar drawing, so I will include it.

Placing the geometry: C3 is at about (285, 210). C4 is up-right at (340, 180) and C2 is up-left at (235, 180). The CH is at about (285, 265), and its NMe2 is at lower left (240, 285).

On C3, the substituent C4 (the C=O carbon) outranks C2 (the CH2–N). C4 is on the right side of the double bond. On the CH carbon, N outranks H, and N is on the left. The two priority groups are on opposite sides, so this is E, which I write as trans.

SMILES: write C3 as C(/C=N...) form. I use O=C1CCN(C(=O)OC(C)(C)C)C/C1=C/N(C)C and check it. For C/C1=C/N: the atom before C1 is C2, written with "/" before C1, meaning C2 is below C1. After "=C", "/N" means N is above the CH. C2 is therefore on the opposite side from N, so C4 (the ring closure) is on the same side as N. That gives Z, which is not what I want.

To get E, with N opposite C4 and therefore on the same side as C2, I write C\C1=C/N. With C2 below and N above, they would be opposite, so I need N on the same side as C2. Writing C/C1=C\N puts C2 and N on the same side, so C4 is opposite N, which is E.
