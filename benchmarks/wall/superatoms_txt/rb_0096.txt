I read this as a cholesterol 3β-carbamate: cholesterol's 3-O is attached to a carbamate, then an N-linked 6-aminohexanoyl chain that ends in a tertiary amide.

- **Steroid core:** the drawing shows the usual cholesterol skeleton with the C5=C6 double bond, the 3β-oxygen on a wedge, and wedged methyls at C10 and C13. The side chain at C17 is the 6-methylheptan-2-yl group, with a wedge at C20. I took the stereochemistry from the standard cholesterol configuration, which matches the wedges drawn.
- **Carbamate:** O–C(=O)–NH links C3 to the chain.
- **Linker:** NH is followed by five CH2 groups and then C(=O), which makes a 6-aminohexanoyl unit (I counted the zigzag vertices one at a time).
- **Amide nitrogen:** it carries two substituents, CH2CH2C(=O)OEt and CH2C(=O)OEt.
