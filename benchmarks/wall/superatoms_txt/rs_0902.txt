The drawing shows a biphenyl-4-carboxamide. One phenyl ring carries SO2NH2 at the ortho position, and the other ring is the para-substituted benzoyl ring.

The benzoyl C=O is attached through NH to a central benzene ring. That ring has the following substituents:
- C1 bears the NH of the amide.
- C2 bears C(=O)NH-(5-chloropyridin-2-yl).
- NH2 sits on the carbon para to C1. Tracing the ring from the NH-bearing carbon at top left, the top right carbon is next, then the right carbon carrying NH2, then the bottom carbon (C3), then C2 bearing the carbonyl. So NH2 is on C4 relative to C1, which makes it meta to the carbonyl-bearing carbon.

The pyridine has N adjacent to the carbon attached to NH, which is C2. Cl is on C5, para to C2 across the ring.
