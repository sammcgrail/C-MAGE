This is my reading of the drawing, with the stereochemistry worked out from the wedges.

**Fragments**
- **Indazole:** the left-hand bicycle has an N=CH at the top and an NH at the bottom. The carbonyl is on the benzo carbon that sits next to the carbon beside the ring-fusion carbon bonded to NH, which makes it an indazol-6-yl group. It is joined through C(=O) to the ring nitrogen of the tetrahydroisoquinoline.
- **Tetrahydroisoquinoline:** the nitrogen carries the indazole carbonyl and has CH2CH2 on one side and a benzylic CH2 on the other. On the aromatic ring, one Cl sits next to the ring-fusion carbon on the CH2CH2 side. The next carbon bears the amide C(=O)NH. The next carbon bears the second Cl, and a CH closes the ring. This is a 5,7-dichloro-6-carboxamide pattern.
- **Amino acid:** the amide NH is attached to the α-carbon. That carbon carries CO2H and a CH2NH group that leads to a urea, NH–C(=O)–N(piperidine).
- **Piperidine:** the urea nitrogen is the ring nitrogen. The ring has two adjacent OH groups at C3 and C4, both on bold wedges.

**Stereochemistry**
- **α-carbon:** the CH2 is on a wedge, and the priorities are N > CO2H > CH2N > H. This gives S, the same arrangement as in L-alanine, so I wrote `N[C@@H](CN…)C(=O)O`.
- **Piperidine C3:** the OH is on a wedge and the priorities are OH > C4 > C2 > H. This gives S.
- **Piperidine C4:** the OH is on a wedge and the priorities are OH > C3 > C5 > H. This gives R.
- **Result:** the ring is (3S,4R), written as `N3C[C@H](O)[C@H](O)CC3`.
