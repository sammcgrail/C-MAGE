The drawing shows a cyclohexane ring in a chair conformation with two substituents, a left bond and a right bond. Each of these bonds is crossed by a wavy line, so each marks an attachment point.

Each wavy line crosses a bond close to the ring, and each bond continues past the wavy line as a short stub. The attachments could therefore be read as ring–CH2–* (a methylene between the ring and the attachment point) or as ring–* directly.

Checking the left side: the line runs from the far left at (3,72) to the ring at (130,105). The wavy line crosses it at about (65,90). The wavy line itself is a long vertical squiggle, from (80,25) to (50,150). The bond is therefore cut by the wavy line, and the line extending beyond it to the left is just the bond's continuation. The same holds on the right.

This makes the wavy line the attachment point, and the segment beyond it is part of the cut bond. The fragment is then *C1CCC(*)CC1, a 1,4-disubstituted cyclohexane.

The chair is drawn with the substituents at the 1,4 positions. The left substituent is on the lower-left vertex (130,105) and the right substituent is on the upper-right vertex (292,47). These are para to each other, so 1,4 is correct. No wedges or hashes are drawn, so I assign no stereochemistry.
