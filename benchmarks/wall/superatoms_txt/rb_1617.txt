I read the structure in four parts and joined them.

1. **Left tricycle.** A pyridazine ring carries a 2-hydroxyphenyl group. It is fused to a piperazine that bears the NH–CH2 unit, and that piperazine is fused to a second piperazine. The stereocentre sits at the ring fusion CH, which has a wedge bond to the CH2 that leads to the NH.
2. **Linker.** The second piperazine nitrogen forms a urea-type amide (C=O) with a 2-ethylpiperazine. The ethyl-bearing carbon is next to the amide nitrogen. The other piperazine nitrogen carries a CH2 that leads to the piperidine.
3. **Piperidine and isoindolinone.** The piperidine is substituted at C4 by that CH2, and its nitrogen is attached to the aromatic ring of an isoindolin-1-one. The aromatic carbon bearing the piperidine is adjacent to the CH next to the carbonyl-bearing ring carbon.
4. **Glutarimide.** The isoindolinone nitrogen is attached to C3 of a piperidine-2,6-dione. That carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so it has no stereo label.

For the wedge stereocentre, the priorities are ring N, then CH2 next to the amide N, then CH2 next to NH, then H. With the wedged bond toward the viewer, the sequence runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S. In the SMILES neighbour order used below, `[C@H]` corresponds to S.
