I read the structure as a 2(5H)-furanone (butenolide) joined by a trans CH=CH link to a decalin core. This looks like an andrographolide-type diterpenoid carrying an acetate and a TIPS-protected hydroxymethyl group.

**Ring and substituent reading**
- **Lactone:** the ring is O–C(=O)–C(=CH–CH2–O). Its ring carbon next to the C=O carries the vinyl chain.
- **Linker:** a trans CH=CH runs from the lactone to the decalin carbon (call it G) at the bottom-left of the decalin; the bond to G is a wedge.
- **Decalin, ring with the exocyclic alkene:** G is followed by C(=CH2), then two CH2 groups, then the ring-fusion CH. That fusion CH bears a hashed H.
- **Decalin, other ring:** from the ring-fusion CH it continues to the quaternary C with a hashed methyl and CH2OTIPS, then to the CH–OAc (wedge), then two CH2 groups back to the quaternary ring-fusion C with a wedged methyl.
- **Substituents:** OAc is expanded to OC(C)=O. OTIPS is expanded to O–Si(iPr)3.

**Stereocentres:** I assigned each @/@@ from the wedge and hash geometry in the drawing. There are five stereocentres: G, the CH–H fusion carbon, the methyl-bearing fusion carbon, the CH–OAc carbon, and the quaternary carbon bearing CH2OTIPS. The vinyl double bond is drawn trans, so it is written /C=C/.

I checked that the ring closures are consistent and that the lactone closes as a five-membered ring.
