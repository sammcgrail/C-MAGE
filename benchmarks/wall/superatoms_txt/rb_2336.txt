I read this as a linear peptide: Ac-Ser-Phe(4-NO2)-Leu-Arg, where the arginine carbonyl is a ketone bonded to C2 of a benzothiazole. The benzothiazole C6 carries a carboxamide to Val-NH2.

- **N-terminus:** an acetyl group on the serine nitrogen. Serine has a CH2OH side chain.
- **Residue 2:** 4-nitrophenylalanine, drawn as a para-nitrobenzyl side chain.
- **Residue 3:** leucine, with an isobutyl side chain.
- **Residue 4:** arginine with a guanidine side chain (CCCNC(=N)N). Its carbonyl is bonded to benzothiazole C2 rather than to an amide nitrogen.
- **Benzothiazole:** S is attached to C2 and C7a, and N is attached to C3a. The carboxamide sits on C6, which is next to the CH (C7) that neighbours the S-bearing carbon.
- **C-terminus:** valine as a primary amide (Val-NH2).

All five stereocentres are labelled (S). For each one the substituent priorities match those of a normal L-amino acid, so each is written with the L-amino-acid pattern N[C@@H](R)C(=O).
