I read the molecule as a benzene ring carrying nitro groups at the 3 and 4 positions. The ring is attached to a CH=C unit. The alkene carbon bears both an NH–Cbz group (NHC(=O)OCH2Ph) and a CO2Me group.

For the alkene geometry, the aryl group on the CH carbon and the nitrogen on the other alkene carbon are drawn on the same side of the double bond. The nitrogen outranks the ester carbon in priority, and the aryl group outranks H, so this is the Z isomer.

In the SMILES, `/C=C(\N…)` places the aryl group and the nitrogen cis, which gives Z.

SMILES: [O-][N+](=O)c1ccc(cc1[N+](=O)[O-])/C=C(\NC(=O)OCc1ccccc1)C(=O)OC
