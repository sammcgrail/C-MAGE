The molecule breaks into three parts: a biphenyl sulfonamide, a urea linker, and a fluorobenzamide with a bromopyridine.

**Biphenyl part.** The top-left ring carries SO2NH2 and is bonded to a para-substituted phenyl ring. That phenyl ring bears the urea NH at its para position.

**Urea.** The linker is NH–C(=O)–NH. One NH is attached to the biphenyl, and the other to the central benzene ring.

**Central benzene ring.** The urea NH sits on one carbon. The adjacent carbon carries the C(=O)NH amide. Fluorine is on the ring carbon that is para to the urea-bearing carbon, which I checked as follows:
- Number the ring with C1 as the urea-bearing carbon (top left, at about 510,290) and C2 as the carbon with the amide (about 490,355).
- Moving around the ring: C3 is at about 540,400, C4 at about 600,385 (this carries F), C5 at about 625,320, and C6 at about 570,270.
- C4 is para to C1, so the fluorine is para to the urea nitrogen and meta to the amide.

**Amide and pyridine.** The amide nitrogen is bonded to C2 of a pyridine ring. The ring nitrogen is adjacent to that carbon, and bromine sits at C5 of the pyridine (para to the carbon bearing the amide NH... more precisely, the 5-bromopyridin-2-yl pattern).
