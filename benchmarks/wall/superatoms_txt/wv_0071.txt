Reading the drawing, the fragment is HO–CH2–C(CH3)2–CH(OH)–*, with the wavy line marking one attachment point.

**Connectivity:** A terminal CH2OH is bonded to a quaternary carbon carrying two methyl groups. That carbon is bonded to a CH carrying a wedged OH, and this CH is bonded to the attachment point *.

**Stereochemistry:** Only the carbinol carbon (the CH bearing the OH) is drawn with a wedge. Its neighbours are the OH (up, wedge toward the viewer), the quaternary carbon (lower left), the attachment point * (lower right), and an implicit H pointing away from the viewer.

With H pointing away, the sequence OH → * → quaternary C runs clockwise. Viewed from the H side, the sequence H, then OH, *, quaternary C, is therefore `@`.

Writing the SMILES with the neighbour order *, H, OH, quaternary C is a cyclic rotation of the first three neighbours of that sequence, which is an even permutation, so the tag stays `@`:

*[C@H](O)C(C)(C)CO
