I read the structure as a biotin–PEG linker attached to a head-to-side-chain cyclic peptide. The ring is closed through a 1,4-disubstituted triazole.

**Fragments, left to right**
- **Biotin:** a bicyclic urea fused to a tetrahydrothiophene, with a valeryl chain, –(CH2)4–C(=O)–.
- **Linker:** NH–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2–C(=O)–NH–, a short PEG spacer ending in an amide to the first ring residue.
- **Ring residues:** the peptide runs triazolyl-Ala → Leu → Leu → Gly → Trp → Pro → Lys-derived residue, which ends in a C-terminal primary amide (C(N)=O).
- **Triazole bridge:** the Ala side chain is CH2 on triazole C4. Triazole N1 carries four CH2 groups running back to the Lys-derived α-carbon, which closes the macrocycle.

**Stereochemistry**
- I worked out the Ala(triazole) centre from its hashed bond to the CH2 and found it is L.
- The other α-carbons (Leu, Leu, Trp, Pro, Lys-derived) are drawn with wedges and match the L configuration.
- Gly has no stereocentre.
- For the biotin ring fusion I used the standard natural biotin configuration. That is an assumption from recall rather than something I re-derived from the wedges.
