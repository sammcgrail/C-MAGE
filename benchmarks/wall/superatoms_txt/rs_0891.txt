I read this as a nine-membered dilactone core (an antimycin-type scaffold) carrying a substituted picolinamide on the nitrogen.

**Core ring.** Going round the nine-membered ring: C(=O), ring O, CH(CH3), CH(O-isobutyryl), CH(CH2Ph), C(=O), ring O, CH2, CH(NH). The last CH closes back onto the first C(=O).

**Substituents.**
- The CH(NH) carbon bears an amide NH linked to C(=O) of a pyridine. The pyridine has the amide at C2, a 4-fluorobenzoyloxy group at C3, and OMe at C4. The ring N sits next to C2.
- The CH next to the ring O carries a methyl on a wedge.
- The next CH carries the isobutyrate ester, O–C(=O)–CH(CH3)2, on a hashed bond.
- The CH after that carries the benzyl group on a wedge.

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned each of the four stereocentres from the wedge and hash geometry in the drawing, with the implicit H opposite the wedged or hashed substituent where relevant:
- CH(NH): hashed N, giving [C@H].
- CH(CH2Ph): wedged benzyl, giving [C@H].
- CH(O-isobutyryl): hashed O, giving [C@@H].
- CH(CH3): wedged methyl, giving [C@H].
