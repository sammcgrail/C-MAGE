The drawing shows a benzenesulfonamide with an N-aryl group. I read it region by region.

**Sulfonyl ring.** The ring carbon bearing the sulfonyl group (C1) has Cl on the adjacent carbon (C2). The CF3 group sits on C5, which is meta to the sulfonyl carbon and para to the Cl carbon. This gives the 2-chloro-5-(trifluoromethyl)benzenesulfonyl group.

**Sulfonamide linker.** The sulfur carries two =O groups and an NH that connects to the second ring.

**N-aryl ring.** Numbering from the carbon bonded to N (C1):
- C2 carries a methyl (top).
- C3 carries the pyrrolidin-1-yl group.
- C4 carries a methyl.
- C5 is CH.
- C6 carries a methyl (bottom left).

**SMILES.** Written from the sulfonyl ring through the sulfonamide to the aryl ring:

O=S(=O)(Nc1c(C)c(N2CCCC2)c(C)cc1C)c1cc(C(F)(F)F)ccc1Cl

In the sulfonyl ring, c1 is attached to S, the next atom "cc(CF3)" puts CF3 on C5 via C6, and the ring closes through "ccc1Cl", so Cl lies on C2, adjacent to C1.
