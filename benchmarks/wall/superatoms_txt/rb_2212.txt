I read the molecule as a linear peptide-like chain. Each residue is an N-acyl amino unit, NH–CH(side chain)–CH2–N(SO2R)–CH2–C(=O), so the backbone carbonyl of the usual residue is replaced by a CH2–N(sulfonyl) unit.

**Chain, left to right:**
1. The N-terminus is acetyl (CH3C(=O)–NH).
2. The residues, in order, carry these side chains and sulfonyl groups on the following N:

| Residue | Side chain on the CH | R on N–SO2–R |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CH2CH2COOH (Glu) | CH3 |
| 2 | CH3 (Ala, *) | CH3 |
| 3 | (CH2)3NH–C(=NH)NH2 (Arg) | CH3 |
| 4 | isobutyl (Leu) | CH2CH2NH2 |
| 5 | CH(OH)CH3 (Thr) | isobutyl |
| 6 | CH3 (Ala, *) | CH2CH2NH2 |
| 7 | (CH2)3NH–C(=NH)NH2 (Arg) | isobutyl |
| 8 | sec-butyl (Ile) | CH3 |
| 9 | isobutyl (Leu) | isobutyl |
| 10 | CH3 (Ala, *) | benzyl |

3. The C-terminus is –CH2–C(=O)NH2.

**Stereochemistry:** Every α-carbon is drawn with a bold wedge to its side chain. Comparing with L-alanine, N[C@@H](C)C(=O)O, shows that this drawing corresponds to @@ when the carbon is written as N[C@@H](R)CN…. The Thr and Ile side-chain carbons are drawn with plain bonds, so they get no stereo marking. The * labels are only position markers and add no atoms.
