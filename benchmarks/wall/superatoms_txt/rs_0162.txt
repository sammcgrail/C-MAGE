The drawing shows a pyridine ring with these substituents:
- the ring nitrogen is at the bottom left
- C2 carries the carboxamide, C(=O)NH
- C3 carries OH
- C4 carries OMe
- C5 and C6 are CH

The amide nitrogen is attached to a cyclohexane ring at C1. The carbon at position 3 of that ring carries both a methyl group and an n-butyl group.

Counting the butyl chain from the quaternary carbon, the bonds go to a first carbon, then a second, a third, and a fourth at the end of the chain. That makes four carbons, so it is n-butyl.

The cyclohexane ring runs from C1 (bearing NH) to CH2 (C2), then C3 (quaternary), C4, C5 and C6.

No wedges or hashes are drawn, so I include no stereochemistry.
