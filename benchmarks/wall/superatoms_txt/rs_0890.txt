The drawing shows a biphenyl sulfonamide linked through an amide to a fluorinated anthranilamide.

**Left part.** One benzene ring carries SO2NH2. Its ortho position is bonded to a second, para-substituted benzene ring. That ring bears a C(=O)NH group.

**Central ring.** The NH is attached to a third benzene ring at position 1. Position 2, ortho to the NH, carries C(=O)NH-pyridyl. The fluorine sits on the ring carbon that is para to the NH-bearing carbon.

To check the fluorine position, number the ring starting at the NH-bearing carbon (C1, top left of the ring).
- C2 is the lower-left carbon, which bears the carboxamide.
- C3 is the bottom carbon.
- C4 is the lower-right carbon, which bears F.
- C5 is the upper-right carbon.
- C6 is the top carbon.

So F is on C4 relative to C1, which is para to the NH. Coming from the carboxamide at C2, the F is at C4, meaning it is meta to the carboxamide carbon.

Ring SMILES: c1(NC(=O)...)c(C(=O)N...)cc(F)cc1. Here c1 is the NH carbon, then c(C=O) is the ortho carbon, then c, then c(F), then c, then c1. This places F at the fourth atom from C1, which is para. This matches the drawing.

**Pyridine.** The amide NH is attached to pyridine C2. The ring N is adjacent to C2, and Cl is at C5, which is para to C2 and meta to N. Written as SMILES: c1(NC...)ccc(Cl)cn1.
