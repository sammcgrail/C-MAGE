The drawing shows a benzimidazole with a carboxylic acid on the benzene ring, an amino substituent at C2, and an HCl salt.

**Benzimidazole core.** The benzene ring carries COOH at the position next to the ring-fusion carbon that bears the N= atom (position 4). The imidazole ring has an N at the top (drawn as N=) and an NH at the bottom. C2 sits between them and carries the NH substituent.

**Thiophene substituent.** The NH is attached to thiophene C3. The ring atoms run C3, then C4 (bearing the methyl), then C5, then S, then C2 (a CH). From the drawing, the C bonded to NH is on the left and the methyl-bearing C is on the upper right. The bottom-left carbon is CH, S is at the bottom, and the right-hand CH is next to S. So the substituent is 4-methylthiophen-3-yl.

**SMILES.** Writing the benzimidazole as OC(=O)c1cccc2[nH]c(Nc3cscc3C)nc12, the thiophene is c3cscc3C. Here c3 is the attachment carbon (C3), then c is C2 (H), s is S, c is C5 (H), and c3C is C4 with the methyl, which closes the ring back to C3. The HCl is added as a separate component.
