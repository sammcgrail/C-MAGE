The drawing shows a methyl ester of an amino acid: a methyl group on an ester oxygen, a carbonyl carbon, and an alpha carbon bearing NH2. The alpha carbon connects through a CH2 to an indole ring.

**Indole ring.** The benzene ring is on the left and the pyrrole ring on the right. The CH2 is attached at the carbon at the top left of the benzene ring. That carbon is next to the ring-fusion carbon (C3a), so it is C4 or C5. I take the ring vertices as follows:
- The fusion carbon at the top, bonded to C3, is C3a.
- Going around the benzene ring from C3a, the vertex at about (290,180) is the next one, and C3a sits at about (345,210).
- The ring vertices lie at roughly (290,180), (230,210), (230,270), (290,300), (345,270) and (345,210).
- The fusion carbons are the two on the right side, (345,210) and (345,270). The CH2-bearing vertex (230,210) is attached to (290,180), which is bonded to (345,210). That makes (290,180) C4, so (230,210) is C5.

The substituent is therefore at C5. The pyrrole ring carries the CN at C3, and the NH is at the bottom right (N1).
