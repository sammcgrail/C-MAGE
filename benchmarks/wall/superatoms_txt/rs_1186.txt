I read the structure as a 9-membered dilactone core, as in antimycin analogues, carrying a substituted pyridine amide.

**Pyridine fragment.** The ring has N, then C2 bearing the carboxamide, C3 bearing OC(=O)OMe (methyl carbonate), C4 bearing OMe, then C5 and C6 as CH.

**Nine-membered ring.** Going around the ring, the atoms are:
1. the lactone C=O,
2. the ring O,
3. CH(CH3),
4. CH(O–C(=O)CH(CH3)2), the isobutyrate ester,
5. CH(CH2Ph),
6. the second C=O,
7. the ring O,
8. CH2,
9. CH(NH–), which closes back to atom 1.

**Stereocentres.** I assigned each from the wedges and hashes and then matched the SMILES chirality tags to those assignments:
- The CH bearing N, with a hashed N bond, is S.
- The CH(CH3), with a wedged methyl, is S.
- The CH bearing the isobutyrate, with a hashed O bond, is R.
- The CH bearing the benzyl group, with a wedged CH2Ph bond, is R.
