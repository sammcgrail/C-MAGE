I read the molecule as a 6-indazolecarbonyl-substituted tetrahydroisoquinoline carrying a cyanoguanidine side chain.

**Regions of the drawing**
- **Indazole:** the N=CH sits at top left and the NH at bottom left. The carbonyl is on the carbon next to the CH that borders the NH-bearing ring-fusion carbon, so this is an indazol-6-yl group.
- **Tetrahydroisoquinoline:** the indazole carbonyl is on the ring nitrogen. Chlorines are at C5 and C7, and the C6 carboxamide sits between them.
- **Amino acid unit:** the amide NH is attached to an α-carbon bearing CO2H and a CH2 that leads to the guanidine NH.
- **Cyanoguanidine:** the carbon carries =N–C≡N and a second NH. That NH is attached to CH(CH3)CH2OH.

**Stereochemistry**
- **α-carbon:** the CH2 is on a wedge and the hydrogen is implicit and pointing back. With priorities N > CO2H > CH2, this centre is S, which is written `[C@@H]` in the SMILES order N, H, CH2, COOH.
- **CH(Me)CH2OH carbon:** the methyl is on a wedge and the hydrogen is implicit and pointing back. With priorities N > CH2OH > CH3, this centre is R, written `N[C@H](C)CO`.
