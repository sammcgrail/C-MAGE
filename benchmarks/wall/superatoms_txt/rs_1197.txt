The structure is a biphenyl-4-carboxamide with a sulfonamide on the ortho position of the distal ring.

**Biphenyl part.** The top-left benzene ring carries SO2NH2 at the position next to the biaryl bond. It is joined to a para-substituted benzene ring, which bears the C(=O)NH group.

**Central ring.** The amide NH is attached to a central benzene ring. Going around that ring:
- C1 carries the NH.
- C2 (ortho to C1) carries the C(=O)NH-(5-chloropyridin-2-yl) group.
- C5 (para to C1) carries the imidazol-1-yl group.

In the drawing, the ring carbon bearing the NH is at the top left, and the carbon below it carries the carboxamide. The right-hand carbon at the lower right carries the imidazole. The ring runs C1 (NH), C2 (CONH, bottom left), C3 (bottom, CH), C4 (imidazole carbon), C5 (top right, CH), C6 (top, CH). The imidazole carbon at the lower right is therefore meta to C2 and para to C1.

**Imidazole.** The ring is attached through N1, with the other atoms as C=N-C=C, so it is n1ccnc1 as written from the attachment point.

**Chloropyridine.** The amide NH is on pyridine C2, next to the ring N. Chlorine sits at C5, which is para to C2.
