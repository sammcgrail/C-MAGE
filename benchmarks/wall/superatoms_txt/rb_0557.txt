I read the molecule as a macrocycle in which two identical benzimidazole units are joined by an (E)-but-2-ene-1,4-diyl chain and by a shared ether linker.

**Each benzimidazole unit**
- The N1 nitrogen carries the butenyl chain.
- C2 carries an NH–C(=O) amide to a 1-ethyl-3-methylpyrazol-5-yl group.
- C5 carries a primary carboxamide (CONH2).
- C7 carries an ether oxygen.

**Butenyl linker**
- N1 of the top unit is joined to N1 of the bottom unit through CH2–CH=CH–CH2.
- The two CH2 groups sit on opposite sides of the double bond in the drawing, so I assigned it as E (trans).

**Ether linker**
- Each C7 oxygen is bonded to a CH2, and both CH2 groups meet at one central CH.
- That CH also carries a CH2CH2 chain ending at C4 of a 1,3-oxazinane ring (C4–C5–C6–O1–C2–N3, with NH at position 3).
- The central CH and the ring C4 are stereocentres by connectivity, but the drawing shows only plain bonds there, so I gave them no stereo marks.

**Ring closures**
- Ring label 3 closes the macrocycle at the top unit's C7 oxygen.
- Ring labels 1 and 2 are reused for the bottom benzimidazole and the oxazinane once earlier closures are complete.
