I read the structure in four pieces and joined them into one SMILES.

- **Right-hand end:** a benzothiazole carrying a 2-hydroxypropan-2-yl group at C5 and an NH–C(=O)–(6-trifluoromethylpyridin-2-yl) amide at C6. Its C2 is bonded to a 1,4-disubstituted cyclohexane.
- **Cyclohexane:** the wedge to the benzothiazole and the hashed CH2 on the opposite carbon indicate a trans arrangement. I wrote this as `[C@H]1CC[C@@H](...)CC1`, which I recalled as the trans pattern (as in trans-4-methylcyclohexanol) rather than deriving it from the drawing.
- **Centre:** the cyclohexyl CH2 is bonded to the N of a 2-azaspiro[3.3]heptane, and the cyclobutane CH of that spirocycle carries the central CH. That CH bears two arms:
  - a CH2–NH arm leading to a 4-amino-isoindoline-1,3-dione whose ring N is attached to a 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group (the thalidomide-type end);
  - a CH2–N arm leading to a piperidine whose C4 is bonded to a phenyl ring.
- **Lower right:** the phenyl ring is para-substituted with an NH bonded to a pyrazine. The pyrazine carries a carboxamide next to the NH carbon and a piperidin-1-yl group on the other side.

The spiro CH and the central CH are drawn with plain bonds, so they get no stereo marks. The 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl CH is also drawn plain and gets none.
