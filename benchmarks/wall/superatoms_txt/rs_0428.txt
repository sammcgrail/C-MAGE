I read the molecule as a nine-membered dilactone core carrying three substituents. I assigned the stereocentres from the wedges and hashes by hand and did not run the SMILES through a parser.

**Pyridine fragment.** The ring has N, then C2 bearing the amide C(=O)NH, then C3 bearing OC(=O)N(CH3)2, then C4 bearing OMe, then C5 and C6 as CH.

**Nine-membered dilactone ring.** Going around the ring from the NH-bearing carbon:
- CH(NH–acyl), drawn with a hashed bond to N
- C(=O)
- O
- CH(CH3), with a wedged methyl
- CH(O–C(=O)CH(CH3)2), with a hashed bond to O
- CH(CH2Ph), with a wedged bond to the benzyl CH2
- C(=O)
- O
- CH2, which closes back to the CH(NH) carbon

**Stereochemistry.** Each of the four stereocentres was assigned from its drawn wedge or hash, with the implicit H taken as the opposite face. The resulting tags are @@ for the CH(CH3) carbon, @ for the CH(O-isobutyrate) carbon, @ for the CH(CH2Ph) carbon, and @@ for the CH(NH) carbon.
