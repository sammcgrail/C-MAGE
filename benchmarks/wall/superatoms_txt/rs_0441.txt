I read the molecule as an antimycin-type nine-membered dilactone carrying a pyridine amide on the ring nitrogen side.

**Ring and substituents**
- The ring runs: CH(NH–acyl), C(=O), O, CH(CH3), CH(O-isobutyryl), CH(CH2Ph), C(=O), O, CH2, back to the first CH.
- The amide acyl group is a pyridine-2-carbonyl. The pyridine carries OMe at C4 and an ester at C3, and the ester is a 4-propylbenzoate.
- The isobutyrate is on the ring carbon next to CH(CH3), and the benzyl group is on the carbon next to the lactone carbonyl.

**Stereochemistry**
- Four stereocentres have wedges or hashes: the N-bearing CH (hashed N), the CH(CH3) (wedged methyl), the CH–O-isobutyryl (hashed O), and the CH–benzyl (wedged CH2Ph).
- I assigned each @/@@ by looking at the drawn geometry with the implicit H on the opposite side from the wedge or hash, then matching the neighbour order in the SMILES.
- The result is `[C@H]` for the N-bearing carbon, `[C@H]` for the benzyl carbon, `[C@@H]` for the isobutyrate carbon, and `[C@H]` for the methyl carbon.
