The drawing shows an isoflavan: a chroman (benzopyran) with a 7-methoxy group on its benzo ring and an aryl group at C3.

**Chroman part.** The aromatic ring carries OCH3 at the top left. The ring oxygen O1 is bonded to C8a, which is the aromatic carbon at the top right of the benzo ring. Going around the pyran ring: O1, then CH2 (C2), then CH (C3, bearing the aryl group), then CH2 (C4), then C4a, then C8a. The methoxy carbon sits on the ring carbon at the upper left. That carbon is meta to C4a and adjacent to C8, so it is C7. This gives 7-methoxy.

**Aryl part.** The attached ring has a methylenedioxy group (OCH2O) on its right side and an OH at the bottom. Numbering from the attachment carbon C1':
- C1' is attached to C3 of the chroman.
- C2' is the top CH, then C3' bears O, then C4' bears O.
- C5' is the bottom CH, and C6' bears OH.

**SMILES.** Starting from the methoxy group: COc1ccc2c(c1)OCC(C2)c1cc3OCOc3cc1O

Checking the chroman ring: c1 (C7, bearing OMe), c (C8... written as "cc" for C6 and C5), c2 (C4a), then c(c1) is C8a with C8 closing the ring back to C7, followed by O, C2, C3, and C4 closing to C4a. This is consistent.

Checking the aryl ring: c1 (C1'), c (C2'), c3 (C3', bearing O), O, C, O, c3 (C4'), c (C5'), c1O (C6' with OH). This is consistent.
