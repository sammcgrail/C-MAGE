I read this as an antimycin-type nine-membered dilactone carrying a substituted picolinamide and a 2-chlorobenzyl carbonate.

**Pyridine part.** The ring has N, then C2 bearing the amide C(=O)N, C3 bearing the carbonate O, C4 bearing OMe, and C5 and C6 as CH. The C3 oxygen is OC(=O)OCH2 attached to a 2-chlorophenyl ring.

**Nine-membered dilactone.** Going around the ring from the NH-bearing carbon:
1. CH(NH-amide), the stereocentre drawn with a hashed N.
2. CH2.
3. O (ester oxygen).
4. C(=O).
5. CH(CH2Ph), drawn with a wedge to the benzyl group.
6. CH(OC(=O)CH(CH3)2), drawn with a hashed bond to the isobutyrate oxygen.
7. CH(CH3), drawn with a wedged methyl.
8. O (ester oxygen).
9. C(=O), which closes back to the NH-bearing carbon.

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned @/@@ at the four ring stereocentres from the wedge and hash geometry, taking the implicit hydrogen as pointing opposite the drawn wedge or hash. I did not independently re-check the final symbols against a second method.
