The structure is a biphenyl sulfonamide amide of a difluoroanthranilamide.

**Fragments read from the drawing:**
- Top left: a benzene ring carrying SO2NH2, attached at its ortho position to a second benzene ring (the biphenyl).
- The para position of that second ring carries C(=O)NH, an amide.
- The amide NH is bonded to a central benzene ring. Ortho to it is a C(=O)NH group leading to a pyridine.
- The two F atoms are on the central ring at the 4 and 5 positions relative to the NH, i.e. on the two adjacent carbons on the right side of the ring.
- The pyridine is attached through its 2-position to the amide NH. Its ring N is adjacent to that carbon, and Cl sits at the 5-position (para to the NH-bearing carbon's neighbour N... specifically, the carbon opposite the C2 attachment point's neighbour, as drawn at the bottom right).

**Central ring atoms:** C1 bears the NH, C2 bears the C(=O)N group, C3 is CH, C4 bears F, C5 bears F, and C6 is CH. In the drawing, the top-right carbon (F) and the right-lower carbon (F) are adjacent, and the CH atoms sit at the top and bottom. This gives c1(NH)cc(F)c(F)cc1C(=O)N.

**Pyridine:** the ring runs C2 (attached to NH), N1, C6H, C5(Cl), C4H, C3H. This gives c1ncc(Cl)cc1, i.e. n1cc(Cl)ccc1 with the attachment at C2.
