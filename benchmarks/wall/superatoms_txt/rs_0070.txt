Reading the structure region by region:

**Benzimidazolone (left).** The ring has N-methyl, a C=O between the two nitrogens, and a chlorine on the benzene ring. The other nitrogen carries the piperidin-4-yl group.

- Let N1 be the N-Me nitrogen, C2 the C=O carbon, and N3 the nitrogen bearing the piperidine.
- N3 attaches to C3a, then C4 (CH, which is also attached to C5 bearing Cl), and so on around the ring. In the drawing, the Cl-bearing carbon at the bottom is adjacent to a CH that is attached to the carbon next to N3 (C3a).
- Tracing the ring: N3–C3a, then C3a–CH (upper right), then that CH–C(Cl), then C(Cl)–CH, then CH–C7a, and C7a–N1.
- So the Cl sits meta to N3 and para to N1.

**Linker.** The piperidine nitrogen connects to CH2–CH(OH)–CH2, which attaches to pyrazole N1.

**Pyrazole.** N1 is bonded to N2, which is double-bonded to C3. C3 carries the 4-CF3-phenyl group. C4 carries the 4-pyridyl group. C5 is a CH bonded back to N1.

- Ring-closure check: N3 is followed by c2 (C3a), then c (the CH), then c(Cl), c, c, and c12 (C7a). C7a closes to N1 (ring label 1) and to C3a (label 2).
- This gives Cl on the carbon two atoms from C3a, which matches the drawing.
