I read the structure as a nine-membered dilactone ring with four stereocentres, an isobutyrate ester, a benzyl group and a 4-hydroxynicotinamide side chain.

**Ring (9 atoms, going round):** C(=O) → O → CH(CH3) → CH(O-isobutyryl) → CH(CH2Ph) → C(=O) → O → CH2 → CH(NH-acyl), which closes back to the first C(=O).

**Substituents:**
- The ester on the ring carbon next to CH(CH3) is isobutyrate, OC(=O)CH(CH3)2.
- The benzyl group is CH2–Ph, with Ph expanded to c2ccccc2.
- The amide nitrogen carries C(=O) attached to a pyridine. The ring nitrogen is at the lower left, and the OH sits on the carbon two positions from the carbonyl-bearing carbon, at C4 of the pyridine. This gives 4-hydroxynicotinoyl, c1cnccc1O.

**Stereochemistry (from the wedges and hashes):**
- The CH–N carbon has a hashed N, so it is `[C@@H]`.
- The CH(CH3) carbon has a wedged methyl, so it is `[C@@H]`.
- The CH–O-ester carbon has a hashed O, so it is `[C@H]`.
- The CH–benzyl carbon has a wedged benzyl, so it is `[C@@H]`.
