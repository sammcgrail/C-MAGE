I read the drawing as an antimycin-type nine-membered dilactone ring carrying a substituted pyridine amide.

**Pyridine part.** The ring has N, then C2 bearing the carboxamide C(=O)NH, C3 bearing the 2-ethylbutanoate ester O–C(=O)–CH(Et)₂, and C4 bearing OMe. C5 and C6 are plain CH.

**Nine-membered ring.** Going around from the amide-bearing carbon:
1. CH(NH), drawn with a hashed bond to N.
2. CH₂ attached to the ring oxygen.
3. Ring O–C(=O), the second lactone.
4. CH(CH₂Ph), drawn with a wedged bond to the benzyl group.
5. CH(O–C(=O)CH(CH₃)₂), drawn with a hashed bond to the isobutyrate oxygen.
6. CH(CH₃), drawn with a wedged methyl.
7. Ring O, then the first lactone C=O, which closes back to the CH(NH).

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned each centre from the wedge/hash drawing using CIP priorities, then converted to SMILES chirality marks. The four centres, in the order CH(NH), CH(Bn), CH(O-acyl), CH(Me), are S, R, R, S. The SMILES tags are `[C@H]`, `[C@H]`, `[C@@H]`, `[C@H]` in that order.
