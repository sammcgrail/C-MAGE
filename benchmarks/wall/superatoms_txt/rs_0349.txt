The drawing shows a tricyclic core, a linker chain, and a substituted pyridine, which I read as follows.

**Tricyclic core.** A pyridine ring is fused to a seven-membered ring that contains N, CH2 and O, and the seven-membered ring is in turn fused to a fluorobenzene.
- The pyridine ring has its N adjacent to the carbon bonded to the ring N10 (the N bearing the chain). The other fusion carbon carries the CH2.
- The seven-membered ring runs N10, pyridine C2, pyridine C3, CH2, O, benzene carbon, benzene carbon, back to N10.

**Fluorobenzene ring.** The benzene carbon attached to N is adjacent to a CH, which is adjacent to the C–F carbon. The carbon attached to O sits on the other side, next to a CH.
- Ring atoms, starting from the carbon bonded to N: c1 (N), c2 (CH), c3 (C–F), c4 (CH), c5 (CH), c6 (C–O).
- F is therefore meta to N and para to O.

**Linker.** N10 bears CH2CH2 leading to N(CH3), then CH2CH2 to the pyridine ring.

**Methoxypyridine.** The ring N is adjacent to the C–OMe carbon, then a CH, then the carbon bearing the chain (position 4). This is a 2-methoxypyridin-4-yl group.

**SMILES assembly.**
- Pyridine fused portion: c1cnc2c(c1)COc1ccc(F)cc1N2CCN(C)CCc1ccnc(OC)c1
- Check the pyridine fusion: n–c2 (bonded to N10) –c(c1)(CH2)... The ring c1cnc2c(c1)C... starts at c1 (CH), c (CH), n, c2 (bonded to N10), c (bearing CH2), c1 (CH). This closes as a six-membered ring and is correct.
- Check the benzene: c1ccc(F)cc1N2 starts at the carbon bonded to O (c1), then CH, CH, C–F, CH, then c1 bonded to N. So C–F is adjacent to a CH, which is adjacent to the carbon bonded to N. This matches the drawing.
