Reading the drawing region by region:

**Left fragment.** A benzimidazol-2-one. The carbonyl carbon bears =O. One nitrogen is NH. The other nitrogen is attached to the piperidine C4. The benzene ring carries Cl on the carbon para to the NH-side ring fusion carbon, i.e. adjacent to the CH next to the fusion carbon bonded to the substituted N.

Tracing the benzene ring: the fusion carbon bonded to N(substituted) leads to a CH, then C-Cl, then CH, CH, and the other fusion carbon bonded to NH. So Cl sits on the carbon meta to the N-substituted fusion carbon, giving a 6-chloro pattern.

**Piperidine.** C4 is bonded to the benzimidazolone N. The ring nitrogen carries a propyl chain. In the drawing the chain runs N–CH2–CH2–CH2–N1, so it is three carbons.

**Right fragment.** A 4,5,6,7-tetrahydropyrazolo[4,3-c]pyridine.
- N1 carries the propyl chain. N2 is double-bonded to C3, and C3 bears the 4-CF3-phenyl group.
- C3a is bonded to CH2 (C4), which connects to N5 carrying SO2Me. N5 connects to CH2 (C6), then CH2 (C7), then C7a, which is bonded to N1.

**SMILES:**
- Benzimidazolone: O=C1Nc2ccc(Cl)cc2N1C1CCN(CC1)...

Check of the benzene ring in the SMILES: c2ccc(Cl)cc2N1 starts at the NH-side fusion carbon, then CH, CH, C(Cl), CH, and the fusion carbon bonded to N1. Cl is therefore on the carbon two positions from the substituted-N fusion carbon (CH in between), which matches the drawing.
