The drawing shows a central tertiary amine nitrogen carrying three groups: a 4-chlorophenyl ring and two naphthalen-1-yl groups.

Each naphthalene is attached to the central N at C1. At C4 it carries a CH2CH2 linker to the para position of a phenyl ring. That phenyl ring bears an N(butyl)2 group at its para position, where each butyl is CH2CH2CH2CH3.

Each arm therefore reads: N(central)–naphthalene C1, C4–CH2CH2–C6H4–N(CH2CH2CH2CH3)2.

I wrote one arm as c1(CCc2ccc(N(CCCC)CCCC)cc2)c2ccccc2c(N...)cc1, with the naphthalene numbered so the linker carbon and the central N sit para to each other on the same ring. For the central N, the SMILES is N(c1ccc(Cl)cc1)(c1ccc(CCc2ccc(N(CCCC)CCCC)cc2)c2ccccc12)c1ccc(CCc2ccc(N(CCCC)CCCC)cc2)c2ccccc12.

Checking the naphthalene fragment c1ccc(CC...)c2ccccc12: the first atom c1 bears the N, then c and c, then the carbon bearing the linker, then c2ccccc12 closes the fused benzo ring. This places N and the linker at positions 1 and 4 of the naphthalene, as drawn.
