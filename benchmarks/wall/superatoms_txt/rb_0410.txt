I read the molecule as a C2-symmetric dimer: two identical bicyclic pyrrolo-thiazepine units, each carrying an N-methyl-alanine amide. Each unit is joined through an aminoindane ether, and the two ethers are linked by a CH2CH2–SO2–CH2CH2 bridge.

**Half-molecule (left):**
- **Side chain:** an N-methyl-L-alanine amide (CH3NH–CH(CH3)–C(=O)–NH–). The methyl is hashed, which gives the S configuration.
- **Bicyclic core:** a thiazepine ring fused to a pyrrolidine through a shared N and CH. The pyrrolidine carries a gem-dimethyl carbon next to the carbon that bears the carboxamide. The thiazepine ring holds the S atom, the lactam C=O and the NH-bearing stereocentre. Reading the wedges and hashes gives S at each of these three core stereocentres (the NH-bearing carbon, the S–CH ring-fusion carbon and the carboxamide-bearing carbon).
- **Aminoindane:** the carboxamide N is attached to C1 of a 2-oxy-indane. C1 is S (hashed N, wedged H) and C2 is R (hashed O, wedged H).

**Linker and right half:**
- The ether O on C2 of each indane connects through –CH2CH2–S(=O)(=O)–CH2CH2– to the other indane.
- The right half is drawn rotated but has the same stereodescriptors as the left. I wrote it in reverse atom order and recomputed the @/@@ tags so they match.
