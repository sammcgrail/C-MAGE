I read the molecule as a macrocyclic peptide with three amino acid residues closed through an aryl–thioether–pyrazine linker.

**Residues in the ring**
- **Ornithine:** its α-carbon carries a H2N-CH2CH2CH2 side chain, drawn on a wedge. Its α-NH is bonded to a CH2 on the pyrazine, and its C=O is bonded to the lysine NH.
- **Lysine:** its α-carbon carries a (CH2)4NH2 side chain, drawn on a wedge. Its C=O is bonded to the N-methyl nitrogen of the next residue.
- **N-methyl tryptophan:** its α-carbon carries a CH2-(indol-3-yl) side chain, drawn on a wedge. Its C=O is bonded to an NH, which is bonded to a CH2 on the benzene ring.

**Linker**
- **Pyrazine:** the ring is 2,3-disubstituted. One carbon carries the CH2 from the ornithine NH, and the adjacent carbon carries the sulfur.
- **Benzene:** going round the ring, the carbons carry CH2NH, then S, then Cl, then two CH, then a 4-carboxyphenyl group, which sits next to the CH2NH carbon.

**Stereochemistry**
- Working from the wedges, all three α-carbons come out as S, matching the natural L-amino acids.
- I converted each to the correct @/@@ in the SMILES by ordering the neighbours as written. This gives @@ for ornithine, @ for lysine and @ for tryptophan.
