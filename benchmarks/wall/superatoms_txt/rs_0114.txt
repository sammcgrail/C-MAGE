Reading the drawing region by region:

- **Benzofuran:** a benzene ring fused to a furan. The furan oxygen sits at the bottom, C2 carries an n-butyl chain (four carbons), and C3 carries a CH2 linker.
- **Biphenyl core:** the CH2 attaches to a para-disubstituted phenyl ring, which connects directly to a second phenyl ring.
- **Second ring substituents:** this ring bears an O–(CH2)3–Ph group (3-phenylpropoxy) and an NH–C(=O)–C(=O)–OEt group (ethyl oxamate) on adjacent carbons.

Numbering the second ring: C1 is the carbon bonded to the first phenyl, C2 is the CH next to it (top right, toward the O-bearing carbon), C3 carries the O, C4 carries the N, and the remaining CH carbons close the ring. In the drawing, the ring carbon at the lower left of the ring (the one bonded to the first phenyl) is attached to a CH, then to the NH-bearing carbon, then to the O-bearing carbon, then to a CH, then to another CH, and back to the start. So the O and N groups are ortho to each other, and the N-bearing carbon is meta to the biphenyl link.
