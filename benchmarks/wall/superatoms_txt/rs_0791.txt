I read the molecule as a nine-membered dilactone core (an antimycin-type skeleton) carrying a substituted pyridine amide.

**Ring core.** Going around the ring: C(=O), ring O, CH(CH3), CH(O-isobutyryl), CH(CH2Ph), C(=O), ring O, CH2, CH(NH–). That is 9 ring atoms.

**Pyridine.** The ring N sits next to C2, which carries the C(=O)NH amide. C3 carries the O-(2,4-dichlorobenzoyl) ester. C4 carries OMe, and C5 and C6 are unsubstituted CH.

**Stereocentres.** Four are drawn with wedges or hashes:
- The N-bearing CH has the hashed N.
- The CH(CH2Ph) has the bold wedge to CH2Ph.
- The CH(O-isobutyryl) has the hashed O.
- The CH(CH3) has the bold wedge to CH3.

I assigned each @/@@ from the 2D geometry, with the implicit H opposite the wedged or hashed substituent.
