The drawing shows a tetrahydrocarbazole: an indole fused to a cyclohexane ring, with the nitrogen carrying a 4-chlorobenzyl group.

**Benzene ring of the indole.** F is on the upper left carbon and Br is on the lower left carbon, next to the carbon bonded to N. In carbazole numbering, N is position 9 and C8 is next to C8a, so Br is at C8. F sits at C6, which is meta to the Br carbon. The two carbons in between are CH, with the one between F and the ring-fusion carbon (C4a) being C5. So the pattern is 6-fluoro and 8-bromo.

**Cyclohexane ring.** C1 bears the CH2CO2Et group and is bonded to C9a, the carbon attached to N. C4a is the carbon attached to the benzene ring. The remaining ring carbons are CH2 groups.

**Stereochemistry.** C1 is a stereocentre by connectivity, but the bond to it is drawn bold only at the C9a–C1 junction (a wedge-like bond that looks like a thickened line). This is not a clear wedge to a substituent, so I leave it without stereo.

**SMILES.** Writing the indole as c1(F)cc(Br)c2n(Cc3ccc(Cl)cc3)c4c(c2c1)CCCC4CC(=O)OCC:

- Ring atoms: c1(F) is C6, then c is C7, c(Br) is C8, c2 is C8a, n is N9, c4 is C9a, c(c2c1) is C4a, with the closing c1 being C5.
- C4a leads to CCC and then C4, the CH bearing the side chain, which closes onto C9a (ring label 4). That is C4a–CH2–CH2–CH2–CH(CH2CO2Et)–C9a, a six-membered ring including both fusion carbons.
