I read the structure as a methyl heptanoate chain attached to a cyclopentanone ring that carries a hydroxyl group and a trans-alkenyl side chain.

**Ring (clockwise from the C=O):**
- The ring is a cyclopentanone: C(=O), then CH2, then CH(OH), then CH bearing the alkenyl chain, then CH bearing the ester chain.
- The OH is on a wedge bond.
- The ester chain is on a wedge bond.
- The alkenyl chain is on a hashed bond.

**Ester chain:** Six CH2 groups lead from the ring to a CO2Me group, which gives a methyl heptanoate substituent.

**Alkenyl chain:**
- The chain runs from the ring as CH=CH, then CH2, then a quaternary carbon.
- The two alkene substituents point to opposite sides, so the double bond is trans (E).
- The quaternary carbon carries OH, CH3 and n-butyl (CCCC). It is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label.

**Stereo assignment:** I worked out the three ring stereocentres from the wedge and hash geometry, with the implicit H opposite the wedged or hashed substituent in each case.
