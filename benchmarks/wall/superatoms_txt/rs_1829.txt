I read the molecule as a 2-methoxy-tetrahydropyran carrying a 3-methylhepta-2,6-dienoate ester, a pent-4-enyl chain and an N-PMB thiazolidin-2-one. I assigned the stereo marks from the wedges and hashes, so they are the least certain part of the answer.

**Ester side chain.** The ester is CH2=CH–CH2–CH2–C(CH3)=CH–C(=O)–O–. In the drawing the C(=O) and the CH2 chain sit on the same side of the C=C, so the double bond is Z.

**Pyran ring (O1, C2 to C6).**
- C2 is the anomeric carbon. It carries a bold-wedged OMe and the thiazolidinone carbon.
- C3 is a CH2.
- C4 carries the ester oxygen on a bold wedge.
- C5 is a CH2.
- C6 carries the pent-4-enyl chain, CH2CH2CH2CH=CH2, on a hashed bond.

**Thiazolidinone.** The ring is C4'(H)–CH2–S–C(=O)–N, with PMB (4-methoxybenzyl) on N. The H on C4' is on a bold wedge.

**Stereo marks.** I converted the wedges and hashes into @/@@ labels by working out the handedness at each of the four stereocentres (C6, C4, C2 and C4') in SMILES atom order.
