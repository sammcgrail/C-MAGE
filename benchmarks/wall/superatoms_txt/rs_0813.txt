I read the structure as a nine-membered dilactone core (the antimycin-type ring), with a substituted pyridine amide attached through NH.

**Pyridine fragment**
- Ring atoms in order: N, C2 bearing the carboxamide, C3 bearing the ethyl succinate ester, C4 bearing OMe, then C5 and C6 as CH.
- The ester on C3 is O–C(=O)–CH2–CH2–C(=O)–OEt.
- The carboxamide is C(=O)–NH, linked to the ring carbon at the lower left.

**Nine-membered dilactone ring**, in order:
1. Ring C(=O) at the top left.
2. Ring O.
3. CH(CH3), methyl on a wedge.
4. CH bearing O–C(=O)–CH(CH3)2, on a hash.
5. CH bearing CH2Ph, on a wedge.
6. Ring C(=O) at the bottom.
7. Ring O.
8. CH2.
9. CH bearing the NH, on a hash, which closes back to atom 1.

**Stereochemistry:** I assigned the four stereocentres from the wedges and hashes, with the implicit H opposite the wedged or hashed substituent in each case. The NH carbon, benzyl carbon, isobutyrate carbon and methyl carbon come out as @, @, @@ and @ respectively in the SMILES order below.
