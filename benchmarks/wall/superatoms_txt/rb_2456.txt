I wrote this as a rapamycin-type macrolide joined through a carbamate and a PEG linker to a pyrazolopyrimidine kinase-inhibitor head group. The macrolide stereocentres are taken from rapamycin's known configuration, not read independently from the wedges in the drawing. I did not assign the stereocentre at the hydroxyl-bearing carbon next to the CH₂ that precedes the lactone oxygen, because I could not tell from the image whether that bond is a wedge or a plain line.

**Macrolide.** The ring is rapamycin-like, but the drawing differs from rapamycin at one position. Where rapamycin has a ketone, between the CH(Me) and the CH₂ next to the lactone oxygen, this structure shows CH(OH). The only remaining ketone in the upper chain is the one beside the OMe-bearing carbon. I wrote the structure to match the drawing.

**Cyclohexyl carbamate.** The cyclohexane carries an OMe and an O–C(=O)–NH group in place of rapamycin's usual 40-OH.

**PEG linker.** After the carbamate NH there are eight ether oxygens, each separated by a CH₂CH₂ unit, followed by CH₂CH₂C(=O). In SMILES this is NCCOCCOCCOCCOCCOCCOCCOCCOCCC(=O).

**Head group.**
- The linker amide nitrogen is the ring nitrogen of a 5,6,7,8-tetrahydropyrido[4,3-d]pyrimidine.
- The pyrimidine C2 carries a CH₂ that is bonded to N1 of a 4-aminopyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidine.
- C3 of that pyrazolopyrimidine bears a 2-aminobenzoxazol-5-yl group.

I checked the SMILES for ring-closure and parenthesis balance against the drawing, but did not run it through a parser.
