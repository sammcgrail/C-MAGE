I read the molecule as three parts joined by a hydroxypropyl linker.

- **Left fragment:** a 1-methylbenzimidazol-2-one (C=O at the top, N-methyl at the left), attached through its other nitrogen to the 4-position of a piperidine.
- **Linker:** the piperidine nitrogen carries CH2–CH(OH)–CH2, and the CH2 on the far side is bonded to the pyrazole N1. The OH is on a bold wedge.
- **Right fragment:** a 4,5,6,7-tetrahydropyrazolo[4,3-c]pyridine. Its C3 carries a 4-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl group, and the ring nitrogen N5 carries a methylsulfonyl group.

For the stereocentre, the OH is wedged toward the viewer, so the implicit H points away. The CIP priorities are OH > CH2–pyrazole N > CH2–piperidine N > H. The pyrazole CH2 outranks the piperidine CH2 because the pyrazole N bears (N, C) where the piperidine N bears (C, C). In the drawing, OH (bottom) → pyrazole CH2 (upper right) → piperidine CH2 (upper left) runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S. Written as `N...C[C@H](O)Cn...`, the `@` symbol corresponds to S.
