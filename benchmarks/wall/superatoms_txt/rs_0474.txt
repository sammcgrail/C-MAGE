I read the molecule as a nine-membered dilactone (an antimycin-type core built on serine) with three groups on it. The pyridine amide carries a menthyl carbonate, one ring carbon carries an isobutyrate ester, and the neighbouring carbon carries a benzyl group.

**Ring and substituents**
- **Dilactone ring:** C(=O)–O–CH(CH3)–CH(O-isobutyryl)–CH(CH2Ph)–C(=O)–O–CH2–CH(NH–), closing back to the first carbonyl.
- **Pyridine:** C2 carries the amide C(=O)NH, C3 carries the carbonate oxygen, C4 carries OMe, and N is adjacent to C2.
- **Menthyl:** a cyclohexane with the carbonate oxygen on C1, isopropyl on C2 and methyl on C5. Its wedges and hashes give the (1R,2S,5R)-menthol pattern, which matches the known SMILES of natural (–)-menthol.
