I read the structure as a lenalidomide-type glutarimide–isoindolinone joined through an NH-PEG linker and a carbamate to a statin-like hexahydronaphthalene bearing a hydroxy-lactone. I worked through the stereocentres from the wedges and hashes in the drawing, but I did not independently re-check the SMILES with software.

- **Glutarimide and isoindolinone:** the glutarimide carbon attached to the isoindolinone nitrogen has no wedge or hash, so it carries no stereo label. The isoindolinone carries the NH on the aromatic carbon next to the CH2.
- **Linker:** the chain from that NH is NH–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–NH, ending in a carbamate C(=O)–O.
- **Bicyclic core:** the carbamate oxygen sits on C1 of a hexahydronaphthalene. The left ring has CH2, then CH(CH3), then CH=C. The right ring has CH=CH, then CH(CH3), then CH carrying the side chain. The two ring-fusion carbons are the C8a CH and the alkene carbon.
- **Side chain and lactone:** an ethylene bridge leads to the lactone carbon, then a CH2, a CH(OH), a CH2 and the lactone C(=O)–O.
- **Stereocentres:** each ring CH, the two methyl-bearing carbons, the side-chain-bearing carbon, and the two lactone carbons has its configuration taken from the wedge or hash on its H or methyl.
