The drawing shows a central CH carbon with three substituents: NEt2 (up), Bz (lower left), and a methyl (lower right). HCl is drawn separately as a salt.

Bz is the benzoyl group, C(=O)c1ccccc1. The alternative reading, benzyl, is normally abbreviated Bn. Benzoyl fits well here, because the molecule is then 2-(diethylamino)propiophenone, which is amfepramone (diethylpropion), a known drug that is sold as the hydrochloride.

Connectivity: CC(N(CC)CC)C(=O)c1ccccc1, with .Cl for the hydrochloride.

The central carbon is a stereocentre by connectivity, but the bonds are drawn plain, so I give no stereochemistry. The bond to NEt2 looks like it changes shade partway along, but this is just a rendering effect and not a wedge.

SMILES: CC(N(CC)CC)C(=O)c1ccccc1.Cl
