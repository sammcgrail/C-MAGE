This is a PROTAC-type molecule: an afatinib-like EGFR warhead joined through a piperazine amide and a thioether chain to a thalidomide unit.

**Warhead (left).** The quinazoline carries a 3-chloro-4-fluoroanilino group at C4, a methoxy at C7, and an NH–C(=O) amide at C6. The amide is an (E)-but-2-enamide, NC(=O)/C=C/CH2–N, and that CH2 attaches to a piperazine nitrogen.

**Linker.** The other piperazine nitrogen is acylated: N–C(=O)–CH2CH2CH2CH2–S. Counting the chain from the carbonyl carbon, there are four CH2 groups before the sulfur. The drawing shows the C=O carbon, then vertices at about x=985, 1050 and 1110, then S at 1190. That gives three vertices between the carbonyl carbon and S, plus the carbonyl carbon itself, so the chain is C(=O)CCCCS in total, with C(=O) as the first carbon. The piperazine N–C(=O) is a pentanoyl, i.e. C(=O) followed by 3 CH2 and then S, with 4 carbons in the chain (C1 = C=O, C2, C3, C4). I take the SMILES as N–C(=O)CCCS, which is 4 carbons in total, which is not the same as what I counted first. Checking the coordinates again: the carbonyl carbon is at about x=925, the next vertices are at 985, 1050 and 1110, and then S at 1190. That makes C(=O) plus three CH2, so the chain is C(=O)CCC–S.

**Thalidomide unit (right).** The sulfur is attached at the 4-position of the phthalimide ring. The imide nitrogen carries a 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group, and the stereocentre is drawn with plain bonds, so no stereo is given.
