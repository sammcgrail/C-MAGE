I read the drawing as a linear peptide: Ac‑Ser‑Lys‑Leu‑Arg‑(α‑ketobenzothiazole)‑6‑carboxamide‑Val‑NH2. I wrote it as SMILES in these parts:

- **N‑terminus:** an acetyl group on the serine nitrogen, with a CH2OH side chain on serine.
- **Residues 2 and 3:** lysine with a 4‑carbon chain ending in NH2, then leucine with an isobutyl side chain.
- **Residue 4:** an arginine whose carboxyl is replaced by a ketone. Its side chain is three CH2 groups and a guanidine, and the ketone carbon is bonded to C2 of a benzothiazole.
- **Benzothiazole:** N3 is attached to the ring-fusion carbon at the top of the drawing. The carboxamide is on C6, para to the ring-fusion carbon next to N3's side… more precisely, on the benzo carbon reached by going C3a → C4 → C5 → C6. That carboxamide is acylated by the valine nitrogen.
- **C-terminus:** valine with an isopropyl side chain and a primary amide, C(N)=O.

All five stereocentres are labelled (S). I assigned each with the usual L‑amino‑acid pattern, N[C@@H](R)C(=O). For the arginine ketone, the ketone carbon (O,O,C) still outranks the CH2 (C,H,H), so the priorities match an ordinary acid and the same pattern gives S.
