I read this as a macrocyclic dinucleotide in which each 5′-O is joined to the other nucleoside's phosphate. Both phosphates carry an octanoyloxymethyl ester.

- **Top nucleoside:** adenine on a ribose-type furanose with a 2′-fluoro (hashed bond). The 3′-O goes to the middle phosphorus and the 5′-O goes to the bottom phosphorus.
- **Bottom nucleoside:** a furanose ring of O, CH2, C(adenine), C(3′-O) and C(CH2O). The adenine sits on the carbon next to the ring CH2, so there is no anomeric carbon bearing the base. The CH2O goes to the middle phosphorus and the 3′-O goes to the bottom phosphorus.
- **Phosphate esters:** each phosphorus is P(=O) with one O–CH2–O–C(=O)–(CH2)6CH3 group. Counting the zigzag gives 8 carbons per acyl chain, so both are octanoyl.
- **Stereochemistry:** I assigned the tags from the wedges and hashes at the four sugar stereocentres on each ring. I checked the top ring against the standard adenosine SMILES; the bottom ring I assigned directly from the drawing's geometry.
