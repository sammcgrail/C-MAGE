I read the molecule as three connected parts and wrote the SMILES from them.

**Left fragment.** An indazole joined through its 6-position to a carbonyl. The ring has an NH on the nitrogen at the bottom of the five-membered ring.

**Middle fragment.** A tetrahydroisoquinoline whose ring nitrogen carries that indazole-6-carbonyl group.
- The aromatic ring has Cl at C5 and Cl at C7.
- C6, between the two chlorines, carries a carboxamide.

**Right fragment.** The amide nitrogen is attached to a CH that bears CO2H and a CH2.
- That CH2 leads to NH–C(=N–C≡N)–NH–CH2CH2OH, a cyanoguanidine ending in a hydroxyethyl group.

**Stereochemistry.** The only wedge is the bold bond from the α-carbon to the CH2. With the CH2 toward the viewer, the CIP priorities are N > CO2H > CH2 > H. N to CO2H to CH2 runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S. In the SMILES order CH2, H, N, CO2H, that corresponds to `[C@H]`.
