The drawing shows ethyl cinnamate: EtO2C–CH=CH–Ph.

The chain runs from the EtO2C label at lower left up to a vertex at the top (about x=722, y=622), then down to a second vertex (about x=1000, y=833), and up to Ph at the right. The double bond is the one between those two vertices.

For geometry, the ester carbon sits lower-left of the first alkene carbon and the Ph sits upper-right of the second. The two substituents lie on opposite sides of the double bond, so it is trans (E).

Writing the SMILES in order: CCOC(=O)/C=C/c1ccccc1. The `/C=C/` pattern places the two substituents on opposite sides, which matches the E geometry.
