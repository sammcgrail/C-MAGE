I read the molecule as a tetrazolo-fused dihydropyrimidine carboxamide linked through an indazole, a pyridine and a propyl-bridged bis-piperazine to a thalidomide-type phthalimide.

1. **Left ring system:** a tetrazole fused to a dihydropyrimidine. The ring carbon bearing a hashed methyl is the only stereocentre. The ring also carries a C-methyl and an N-methyl, and a carboxamide sits on the carbon next to the stereocentre.
2. **Amide link:** the carboxamide NH is attached to C5 of a 1H-indazole. Indazole C3 is attached to C4 of a pyridine.
3. **Linker:** pyridine C2 carries a piperazine. Its other nitrogen is joined through a three-carbon chain to a second piperazine, which is attached to C5 of the phthalimide (isoindoline-1,3-dione).
4. **Right end:** the phthalimide nitrogen carries a 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group. Its stereocentre is drawn with plain bonds, so I gave it no stereo.
5. **Stereocentre assignment:** the methyl is hashed (pointing away) and the implicit H points toward the viewer. The priorities are N > ring C6 > CH3 > H. Going N → C6 → CH3 appears counterclockwise on the page, and the H points toward the viewer, so the true sense is clockwise, giving R. In the SMILES, neighbour order CH3, H, ring-closure N, C6 with `@@` corresponds to R.
