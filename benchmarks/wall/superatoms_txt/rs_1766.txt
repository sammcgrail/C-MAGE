I read the structure as a 2,5-dihydroxynicotinamide (pyridine ring) attached through its amide N to a nine-membered dilactone ring, an antimycin-type skeleton.

**Pyridine part:** N1, C2 bearing OH, C3 bearing the C(=O)NH group, C4 as CH, C5 bearing OH, and C6 as CH.

**Nine-membered ring**, going around from the amide-bearing carbon:
- CH–NH (the amide N-bearing carbon)
- C(=O), the lactone carbonyl at the top left
- ring O
- CH(CH3), with a wedged methyl
- CH–O–C(=O)CH(CH3)2, the isobutyrate ester, drawn with a hashed bond
- CH–CH2Ph, with a wedged benzyl group
- C(=O), the lactone carbonyl at the bottom
- ring O
- CH2, which closes the ring back to the CH–NH carbon

I assigned the four stereocentres from the wedges and hashes. Going around the ring from the CH–NH carbon, they are S (CH–NH), R (CH–benzyl), R (CH–O-isobutyrate) and S (CH–CH3). I then chose the @/@@ marks in the SMILES so they encode those assignments.
