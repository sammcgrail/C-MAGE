Reading the drawing region by region:

- **Acid chain:** HO2C–CH2–CH2– is attached to the pyridine at C2, giving a propanoic acid.
- **Pyridine:** a six-membered ring with one N. The acid chain is on one carbon next to N, and the alkene carbon is on the other carbon next to N (2,6-disubstituted).
- **Alkene:** the carbon attached to the pyridine also carries a 4-methylphenyl group. It is double-bonded to a CH, which bears a CH2 leading to the pyrrolidine N.
- **Pyrrolidine:** a saturated five-membered ring with one N, attached through N.

**Double bond geometry:**
- On the first alkene carbon, the pyridyl outranks the tolyl group because its ring carbon bears (N,C,C) versus (C,C,C).
- On the second alkene carbon, CH2N outranks H.
- In the drawing the CH2 sits on the same side as the tolyl group, so it is trans to the pyridyl. That makes the alkene E, as in acrivastine, of which this compound is the saturated-chain analogue.
