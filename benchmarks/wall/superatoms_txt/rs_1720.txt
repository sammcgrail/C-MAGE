I read the drawing as a pyrazin-2(1H)-one core carrying three substituents, plus a 7-bromo-5-methoxyindoline attached through its nitrogen.

**Pyrazinone ring.** N1 carries the chiral side chain and C2 is the C=O. C3 is bonded to the indoline nitrogen. N4 follows, then C5 bearing Br, and C6 is CH.

**Side chain on N1.** It is a CH bearing an ethyl group, a CH2OCH3 group, the ring nitrogen, and a wedged H.

**Stereocentre.** The H is on a solid wedge, so it points toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are N > CH2O > ethyl > H. On the page, N (down) → CH2O (upper right) → ethyl (upper left) runs counterclockwise. Because H points toward the viewer, the true sense is reversed, giving R. In the SMILES below, `[C@H]` with neighbour order ethyl, H, CH2O, N corresponds to R.

**Indoline.** The indoline N is bonded to C7a, whose neighbour C7 carries Br. C5 carries OMe. The ring is written N2CCc3cc(OC)cc(Br)c32.

SMILES: CC[C@H](COC)n1cc(Br)nc(N2CCc3cc(OC)cc(Br)c32)c1=O
