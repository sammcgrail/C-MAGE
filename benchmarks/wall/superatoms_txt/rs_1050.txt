The drawing shows an indole with an NH at the bottom right, a CN group at C3 (top right), and a CHO group on the benzene ring.

Locating the CHO: the benzene ring has its fusion carbons at the right side. The upper fusion carbon is C3a, which bonds to C3. The CHO sits on the upper-left carbon of the benzene ring, which is adjacent to C3a. That makes it C4 or C5, so I checked the ring vertices.

The benzene ring vertices are:
- top (about 150,50), bonded to the CHO carbon at (95,85)
- upper right, C3a (about 200,75)
- lower right, C7a
- bottom, C7
- lower left, C6
- upper left, C5 (about 95,85)

Since C3a is the upper right vertex, the top vertex is C4 and the upper left is C5. The CHO carbon at about (35,55) attaches to the vertex at about (80,110), which is the left-upper vertex. Reading the ring, C3a is at (150,95) and C4 is at (115,60) at the top. The CHO attaches to the ring at approximately (75,95), the upper left vertex, which is C5.

The pattern is: C3a, then C4 (top), then C5 (upper left, bearing CHO), then C6, C7, C7a. So the compound is 5-formylindole-3-carbonitrile.
