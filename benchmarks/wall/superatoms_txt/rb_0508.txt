I read the drawing as a 15-membered macrocycle built from three amino-acid-derived units and a diaryl sulfide linker.

**Fragments**
- **Tryptophan unit:** the indole nitrogen carries a Boc group. The α-nitrogen is N-methylated and its carbonyl is bonded to an NH–CH2 group on the benzene ring.
- **Benzene ring:** the ring carbon bearing CH2 is next to C–Br. Going round the ring, the next two carbons are CH, then CH, then C–Cl, and finally the carbon bearing S.
- **Sulfur bridge:** S joins the benzene ring to C2 of a pyridine. The pyridine C3 carries CH2–NH, and that NH is bonded to the α-carbon of the first ornithine.
- **First ornithine unit:** its side chain is (CH2)3–NH–Boc. Its carbonyl forms an amide with the NH of the second ornithine.
- **Second ornithine unit:** its side chain is also (CH2)3–NH–Boc. Its carbonyl forms an amide with the N-methyl nitrogen of the tryptophan unit, closing the ring.

**Stereochemistry (all three centres are wedged, and each works out as S)**
- Tryptophan α-carbon: the CH2 wedge points to the viewer and the neighbours N, C=O, CH2 run counterclockwise, giving S.
- Second ornithine α-carbon: the same assignment gives S.
- First ornithine α-carbon: the same assignment gives S.
- Written in SMILES, these are `[C@@H]` for the tryptophan and first-ornithine centres and `[C@H]` for the second-ornithine centre. The second one differs because its ring-closure digit changes the neighbour order.
