I read this as a PROTAC-type molecule with three parts joined in a line. The stereochemistry comes from assuming the standard configurations of the known fragments, not from reading each wedge in the image.

- **E3 ligand (left):** a VHL ligand of the VH032 type. It has a 4-methylthiazol-5-yl aryl group, a benzylamide, a 4-hydroxyproline, and a tert-leucine capped with a 1-fluorocyclopropanecarbonyl group. The aryl ring also carries an OCH2C(=O) ether linkage.
- **Linker (middle):** the OCH2C(=O) group is attached to a piperidine nitrogen. The piperidine C4 carries a three-carbon chain, CH2CH2CH2, ending in NH–C(=O)–O, which is a carbamate.
- **Statin (right):** a lovastatin-type hexahydronaphthalene core with the same ring double bonds and two methyl groups. The carbamate oxygen sits where lovastatin has its 2-methylbutanoate ester. The core carries the CH2CH2 chain to the 4-hydroxy-6-oxo-tetrahydropyran lactone.

The stereocentres on the VHL proline and tert-leucine, and on the statin core and lactone, are taken from the VH032 and lovastatin SMILES. I wrote the statin part in PubChem's lovastatin form with the ester swapped for the carbamate, then spliced it into the full SMILES.
