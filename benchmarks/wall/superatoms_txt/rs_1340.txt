The drawing shows a central benzene ring with three substituents.

- **OMe:** a methoxy group on the top carbon.
- **Amide to the chloropyridine:** C(=O)NH linked to a pyridine at its 2-position. The pyridine nitrogen sits next to that carbon, and Cl is at the 5-position (para to the carbon bearing NH).
- **NH amide to the cyanobenzene:** an NH on the ring carbon next to the carboxamide carbon, acylated by a 4-cyanobenzoyl group.

Ring positions: the carbon bearing C(=O)NH is C1 and the carbon bearing NH is C2. Going around the ring, the next carbon is C3 (CH, lower left), then C4 (CH, upper left), then C5 (top, bearing OMe), then C6 (CH, between C5 and C1). So the OMe is para to the NH-bearing carbon and meta to the carboxamide.

In the drawing, the ring carbon bearing NH is at the bottom (about x=345, y=210). The carbon bearing the C(=O) is at the right (about x=405, y=180). The carbon bearing OMe is at the top (about x=345, y=65). The ring runs bottom (NH), right-lower (C=O), right-upper (CH), top (OMe), left-upper (CH), left-lower (CH). The OMe carbon is therefore separated from the C(=O) carbon by one CH, and it is para to the NH carbon.

Pyridine: the ring carbon attached to NH is C2, the nitrogen is N1, and Cl is on C5.
