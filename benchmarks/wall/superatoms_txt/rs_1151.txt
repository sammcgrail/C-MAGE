The drawing shows a phthalimide (isoindoline-1,3-dione) whose nitrogen is attached to a three-carbon chain. That chain ends at C4 of an imidazole ring.

**Imidazole ring:**
- C2 carries a phenyl group.
- The ring N between C2 and C4 is drawn as plain N, which is N3.
- N1 is the NH, drawn at the bottom left.
- C4 (top right) carries the propyl chain.
- C5 (bottom right) carries the 4-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl group and is bonded to the NH.

**Chain:** Counting the vertices from the imidazole to the phthalimide N gives three CH2 groups.

**SMILES, written in pieces:**
- Phthalimide with its N-substituent: O=C1c2ccccc2C(=O)N1CCC...
- Imidazole: c3nc(-c4ccccc4)[nH]c3-c5ccc(cc5)C(F)(F)F

The chain carbon attaches to c3, the imidazole carbon bearing the propyl group. That carbon is bonded to n (N3) and to the carbon bearing the aryl group, which in turn is bonded to [nH]. The ring is therefore c3 n c(Ph) [nH] c3(Ar).
