I read the molecule as three parts joined in a chain.

- **Left aryl group:** a 2-oxo-benzoxazol-6-yl ring (benzoxazolone). The ring-fusion carbon bearing O is meta to the carbon carrying the CH2 group. The fusion carbon bearing NH is para to that CH2-bearing carbon.
- **Amino acid core:** the CH2 links the aryl ring to the alpha carbon. The alpha carbon carries a CO2Me group on a hashed bond, an NH, and an implicit H.
- **Urea and spiro group:** the NH is acylated as a urea, C(=O) bonded to a piperidine nitrogen. The piperidine's C4 is a spiro carbon shared with a 3,4-dihydroquinazolin-2(1H)-one ring. In that ring, the spiro carbon bonds to the fused benzene ring and to an NH, which connects through C(=O) and NH to the benzene ring.

**Stereochemistry:** only the alpha carbon is a drawn stereocentre. With the ester on a hashed bond, the implicit H points toward the viewer. The CIP priorities are N > CO2Me > CH2 > H. Viewed from the front, N → CO2Me → CH2 runs counterclockwise. Because H faces the viewer, the true sense is reversed, so the centre is R. In the SMILES order (ester carbon, H, CH2, N), this corresponds to `[C@@H]`.
