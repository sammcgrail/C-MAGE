I read this as a pladienolide-type 12-membered macrolide and wrote it out in four parts. I assigned the stereocentres from the wedges and hashes by working out the clockwise or counter-clockwise order of neighbours at each centre.

**Macrolactone ring (12 atoms).** Starting at the lactone carbonyl:
- C1 is the lactone C=O.
- C2 is a CH2.
- C3 is CH(OH), drawn with a bold wedge on the OH.
- C4 and C5 are two CH2 groups.
- C6 is CH(CH3), with a wedged methyl.
- C7 is CH–O, with a hashed bond to the carbamate oxygen.
- C8=C9 is a trans (E) double bond.
- C10 is CH(CH3), with a wedged methyl.
- C11 is CH–O, with a hashed bond to the side-chain vinyl carbon, and it closes the ring through the ring oxygen back to C1.

**Carbamate at C7.** The C7 oxygen is attached to C(=O)–N of a piperazine. The other piperazine nitrogen carries a cycloheptyl group (seven-carbon ring).

**Side chain at C11.** It is C(CH3)=CH–aryl, and the double bond is E: the aryl group and C11 lie on opposite sides.

**Aryl group.** It is a benzene ring substituted 1,3,5 with the vinyl carbon, a fluorine, and a piperazin-1-yl group. The far nitrogen of that piperazine is acylated as C(=O)–CH2–(pyrazol-1-yl).
