The drawing shows a central carbon with three substituents: a Ph label (phenyl), a double bond to O (carbonyl), and a single bond to an O labelled BzO.

BzO is benzoyloxy, written O-C(=O)-Ph. The Bz abbreviation here stands for benzoyl, so the molecule is Ph-C(=O)-O-Bz, which is benzoic anhydride: O=C(c1ccccc1)OC(=O)c1ccccc1.

The alternative reading of Bz as benzyl is less likely, because benzyl is normally abbreviated Bn. With benzyl, the molecule would be benzyl benzoate instead.

SMILES: O=C(c1ccccc1)OC(=O)c1ccccc1
