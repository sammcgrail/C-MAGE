This is my reading of the drawing, written as SMILES. The stereodescriptors were not derived centre by centre from the wedges. I took them from the natural rifamycin configuration, which I recalled from memory and could not fully cross-check against the image, so they are the least certain part.

The structure is a rifamycin core fused to a phenoxazine ring system. I read it region by region:

- **Ansa chain:**
  - The enol ether O–CH=CH leads to CH(OMe), then CH(CH3), then CH(OAc), then CH(CH3).
  - Next come CH(OH), CH(CH3), CH(OH) and CH(CH3), then a CH=CH–CH=C(CH3) diene.
  - The diene ends in an amide C(=O)–NH attached to the aromatic core.
- **Ketal and furanone:** the quaternary carbon carries a methyl and is bonded to the enol-ether oxygen and the ring oxygen. It sits next to a C=O on the fused furanone ring.
- **Naphthoquinone part:** an aromatic ring bears a methyl and an OH. It is fused to a second ring that has a C=O and the C–NH carbon.
- **Phenoxazine part:** the ring N= and O bridge the naphthoquinone to a benzene ring. That benzene carries an OH next to the N-bearing carbon and an O–CH2CH2–N(CH3)–C(=O)CH3 side chain.
