The drawing shows letrozole: a central CH carbon bonded to two para-substituted phenyl rings and to N1 of a 1,2,4-triazole.

**Cyano groups.** The labels read "NC" on the left and "CN" on the right. Each is a nitrile attached through its carbon to the ring, so each is written C#N, with the ring bonded to the carbon.

**Triazole ring.** Starting from the ring nitrogen attached to the central carbon (N1), it bonds to a second nitrogen (N2). N2 double-bonds to a CH, which bonds to a third nitrogen (N4). N4 double-bonds to another CH, which closes the ring back to N1. This gives the ring n1cncn1 pattern, written with the substituent on N1 as n1cncn1, where the atoms are N1, then C5, N4, C3, N2.

In the drawing, N1 connects to N2 on the left and to a CH on the right. That right-hand CH double-bonds to N4 at the bottom. N4 bonds to the bottom-left CH, which double-bonds to N2. This is the 1,2,4-triazol-1-yl group.

**SMILES.** N#Cc1ccc(cc1)C(c1ccc(C#N)cc1)n1cncn1

The central carbon has three different attachments in the drawing only if the two aryl groups differ, and they are identical, so there is no stereocentre.
