I read the structure as a thalidomide-type cereblon ligand joined through a pyridine linker to a simvastatin-like core, and wrote it out in three parts.

1. **Left part:** a glutarimide (2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl) on the nitrogen of a phthalimide. The phthalimide carries an O–CH2–C(=O)–NH– group at the 4-position.
2. **Linker:** the amide NH is attached to a CH2 on a pyridine ring at position 3. A second CH2 at position 5 leads to NH–C(=O)–O, a carbamate.
3. **Right part:** the carbamate oxygen is on C1 of the hexahydronaphthalene core, which has two methyl groups, two ring C=C bonds and an ethylene chain. The chain ends at a six-membered lactone bearing an OTBDMS group, written as O[Si](C)(C)C(C)(C)C.

For the stereochemistry, I did not derive each centre independently from the wedges. I took the stereo pattern of the simvastatin SMILES, which I recalled from memory and did not look up. I assumed the drawing matches natural simvastatin and did not check each wedge against it. I then swapped the ester for the carbamate and the free OH for the silyl ether, keeping the atom order so the chirality tags stay valid. The glutarimide stereocentre is drawn with plain bonds, so it has no stereo label.
