The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an N-aryl isatin core.

**Biphenyl part.** The top ring carries CO2H. It is attached to a second ring that bears an OH ortho to the biaryl bond. The NH–N= group sits on the carbon adjacent to the C–OH carbon, on the other side from the biaryl bond. So the second ring reads: C1 (biaryl), C2 (OH), C3 (NHN=), then C4, C5 and C6 as CH.

**Hydrazone and indolinone.** The hydrazone is HN–N=C3 of the indolin-2-one. The ring has C2 as C=O and N1 bearing a 3,4-dimethylphenyl group. On the fused benzo ring, C3a is bonded to C4 (Cl), C5 is CH, C6 carries CF3, and C7 is CH leading to C7a, which is bonded to N.

**Stereochemistry.** The C=N double bond is drawn with a definite geometry. The N–NH substituent points to the right and down, and the C3 carbonyl side (C2) lies to the left. Across the C=N bond, the NH on the N is drawn below the N, and on C3 the substituents are C2 (left) and C3a (right, lower). The NH is on the left-down side of the N, so it is on the same side as C2, which is the carbonyl carbon. In SMILES this gives a Z arrangement, with NH cis to C2.

Writing the SMILES as `N(…)/N=C1/C(=O)N(...)c2cc(...)cc(Cl)c12`:
- The NH is written before the bond: `N/N=C1/...`, with the `/` bond between N and N placing NH "below" the N.
- The `/` after C1 then places the next atom, C(=O), "above" C1.
- The NH is below and C2 is above, so they are trans. This is the opposite of the arrangement I want.

To get NH cis to C2, I write `N\N=C1/C(=O)...`.
