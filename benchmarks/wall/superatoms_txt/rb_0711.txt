I read the molecule as a PROTAC-type degrader: a pomalidomide-style cereblon ligand joined by a PEG linker to a KRAS G12C-type quinazoline ligand.

**Cereblon ligand (left).** A 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group sits on the nitrogen of a phthalimide (isoindoline-1,3-dione). The phthalimide benzene ring carries an NH at the 4-position. The glutarimide stereocentre is drawn with plain bonds, so I gave it no stereo.

**Linker.** From the NH the chain is CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O. The last oxygen is bonded to C2 of the quinazoline. That gives three ether oxygens plus the aryl-ether oxygen.

**Quinazoline core.**
- C2 carries the linker oxygen.
- C4 carries the piperazine nitrogen.
- C6 carries Cl.
- C7 carries a 2-fluoro-6-hydroxyphenyl group.
- C8 carries F.
- C5 is CH.

**Piperazine.** This is a 2,5-dimethylpiperazine. One nitrogen is attached to the quinazoline and the other bears an acryloyl group (C(=O)CH=CH2). One methyl is drawn with a wedge (left stereocentre) and the other with a hash (right stereocentre). Working through the CIP priorities (N > CH2 > CH3 > H) gives S for the wedged centre and R for the hashed centre. Both are written as `[C@H]` in the SMILES below, in the order the atoms appear.

SMILES: C=CC(=O)N1C[C@H](C)N(c2nc(OCCOCCOCCOCCNc3cccc4C(=O)N(C5CCC(=O)NC5=O)C(=O)c34)nc3c(F)c(-c4c(F)cccc4O)c(Cl)cc23)C[C@H]1C
