I read the molecule as a nine-membered dilactone ring (antimycin-type) carrying a substituted picolinamide on the ring nitrogen side. I worked out the stereocentres from the wedges and hashes.

**Pyridine part:** the ring nitrogen is at the bottom. C2 carries the amide C(=O)NH, C3 carries an ester O–C(=O)CH2CH2OMe (3-methoxypropanoate), and C4 carries OMe.

**Nine-membered ring**, going around: C(=O), then O, then CH(CH3), then CH(O–C(=O)CH2C(CH3)3), then CH(CH2Ph), then C(=O), then O, then CH2, then CH(NH–), which closes back to the first C(=O).

**Stereochemistry**, read from the drawing:
- The CH–N carbon has a hashed bond to N, which gives `[C@H]` in the SMILES order used.
- The CH(CH2Ph) carbon has a wedged benzyl group, which gives `[C@H]`.
- The CH–O(ester) carbon has a hashed bond to O, which gives `[C@@H]`.
- The CH(CH3) carbon has a wedged methyl, which gives `[C@H]`.
