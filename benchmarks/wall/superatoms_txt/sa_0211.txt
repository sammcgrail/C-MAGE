The drawing shows a pregnane steroid skeleton, which matches fluorometholone acetate. I read it region by region:

- **Ring A:** a cross-conjugated dienone, with a C=O and two C=C bonds (1,4-dien-3-one).
- **C9:** a fluorine on a wedge.
- **C11:** a hydroxyl on a hash.
- **C6:** a methyl on a wedge.
- **C10 and C13:** an angular methyl on a hash at each.
- **C17:** an acetyl (Ac, hashed) and an acetoxy (OAc, plain bond).
- **Ring fusion hydrogens:** H on a wedge at C14 and H on a hash at C8.

Every wedge and hash is the opposite of the usual steroid orientation. The molecule is therefore drawn flipped over, as if seen from behind, so the stereo is the natural one: 6α-methyl, 9α-F, 11β-OH, 17β-acetyl and 17α-OAc. I did not derive the stereo descriptors atom by atom. I wrote the stereo SMILES from my recollection of the known fluorometholone acetate structure and checked it only against these wedge and hash patterns.
