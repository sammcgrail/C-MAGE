Tracing the macrocycle clockwise from the top left, residue by residue:

1. N-Me-Phe: the N carries a methyl, the alpha carbon carries a CH2Ph, and its C=O leads to the next residue.
2. Phe (NH): the alpha carbon carries a benzyl group and the C=O is attached to N-Me-Phe's N. Reading in the direction N-Me-Phe N → C(=O) → Phe alpha C → NH → C(=O) → Val alpha C, the chain is:
   - N-Me-Phe: N(C)–CH(CH2Ph)–C(=O)–Pro N. The carbonyl on the left attaches to proline N.
   - The N-Me-Phe nitrogen is bonded to the C(=O) of Phe, which is attached to Phe's alpha carbon, and that alpha carbon is bonded to NH.
   - NH–C(=O)–CH(iPr)–N(Me): this is Val, with the carbonyl bonded to the Phe NH.
   - The N-Me-Val nitrogen is bonded to C(=O)–CH(sec-butyl)–O, a 2-hydroxy-3-methylpentanoic acid (Hmp) unit.
   - The O is bonded to C(=O)–CH(C(C)(C)O)–N(Me), a β-hydroxy-N-methylvaline unit.
   - That N(Me) is bonded to C(=O)–CH(isobutyl)–NH, a Leu unit.
   - The Leu NH is bonded to C(=O)–CH(iPr)–N(Me), an N-Me-Val unit.
   - That N(Me) is bonded to C(=O)–CH(sec-butyl)–NH, an Ile unit.
   - The Ile NH is bonded to C(=O)–Pro (alpha C), and the Pro N is bonded to C(=O)–N-Me-Phe alpha C.
