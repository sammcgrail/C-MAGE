Reading the structure region by region:

- **Indazole:** The ring is 1H-indazole with N1 as NH and N2 as =N. C3 is the CH, and C3a and C7a are the fusion carbons. The ethyl group is on C7, next to C7a, which bears the NH. The side chain is on C5.
- **Side chain:** The indazole C5 carries CH2, then CH(NH–C(=O)–N-piperidine)C(=O)OCH3. No wedges are drawn, so there is no stereochemistry.
- **Piperidine:** The ring nitrogen is acylated as a urea. The carbon at position 4 is attached to N3 of the quinazolinone.
- **Quinazolinone:** The ring is 3,4-dihydroquinazolin-2(1H)-one. N1 is NH attached to the benzene ring, C2 is C=O, N3 bears the piperidine, and C4 is CH2 attached to the benzene ring.

Indazole SMILES: c1(CC)cc(CC...)cc2cn[nH]c12, with the ethyl on the carbon adjacent to c12.

Full SMILES:

Checking the indazole tautomer: the drawing shows N1 as NH attached to C7a, and N2 double-bonded to C3. The ring closure c2c[nH]nc12 would put the NH on the nitrogen bonded to c3, which is wrong. The correct form is cc2cn[nH]c12: C3a is c2, C3 is c, N2 is n, N1 is [nH], and C7a is c12. The ethyl-bearing carbon is bonded to C7a, which is consistent.
