**Pyridine ring.** The N is at the bottom, with C2 bearing the C(=O)NH group. C3 carries OH and C4 carries OMe. Written as a SMILES fragment: c1(C(=O)N...)nccc(OC)c1O.

**Stereochemistry.** The alpha carbon has a bold wedge to the NH, so it is a defined stereocentre. The 2-methyl carbon in the side chain is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label.

To assign the alpha carbon, I place it at the centre with these neighbours:
- NH to the left, on a wedge (toward the viewer)
- C(=O)O above
- CH2 below
- H implied, pointing away

CIP priorities are N > C(=O)O (O,O,O) > CH2O (O,H,H) > H. With H pointing away, I read N (left) → COOR (up) → CH2 (down). Going from left to up to down is clockwise, which gives R. L-serine is S in its usual form, so this is the D-serine configuration, which is fine.

Now the SMILES. Writing the centre as N[C@H](C(=O)OC(C)C)COC(=O)..., the neighbour order is N, H, C(=O), CH2. Viewed from N, the sequence H → C(=O) → CH2 is counterclockwise for @. I need R, so I check this ordering.

With H as the second neighbour, the order N, H, COOR, CH2 is an even permutation of H, N, COOR, CH2, so the two have the same chirality sense. Looking from H, @ means N → COOR → CH2 appears counterclockwise. Looking from the opposite side (H pointing away), the same sequence appears clockwise, and that is R. So @ gives R.
