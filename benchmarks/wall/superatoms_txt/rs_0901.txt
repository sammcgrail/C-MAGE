The drawing shows a central CH carbon bonded to three groups: a piperidine nitrogen, a 2,3-dimethoxyphenyl ring, and a hydroxy-benzodioxole ring.

**Dimethoxyphenyl ring.** The ring carbon attached to CH is C1. The adjacent carbon, C2, carries OCH3 (lower right). The next carbon, C3, carries H3CO (left). So the ring is 2,3-dimethoxyphenyl.

**Benzodioxole ring.** The ring carbon attached to CH is bonded to a carbon bearing HO. The benzodioxole oxygens sit on the right side, fused to the ring through the two carbons on that side. Numbering the ring atoms:
- a1 is attached to CH.
- a2 carries OH (bottom left).
- a3 is the bottom CH.
- a4 carries O (bottom right).
- a5 carries O (upper right).
- a6 is the top CH, bonded back to a1.

**Piperidine.** The nitrogen is attached to the CH and closes a six-membered ring of five carbons.

**SMILES assembly.** Writing the CH first with its three substituents gives:

C(N1CCCCC1)(c1cccc(OC)c1OC)c1cc2OCOc2cc1O

Checking the benzodioxole portion, c1cc2OCOc2cc1O reads as:
- c1 (a1, attached to CH)
- c (a6, CH)
- c2 (a5, with O)
- OCO (the dioxole bridge)
- c2 (a4)
- c (a3, CH)
- c1O (a2, with OH)

This matches the drawing. The CH is a stereocentre by connectivity but is drawn with plain bonds, so no stereo is given.
