The drawing shows a pyridine ring with the following substituents:
- C2 carries the carboxamide, C(=O)NH–Ar.
- C3 carries an acetoxy group, OC(C)=O.
- C4 carries a methoxy group.
- N1 is the ring nitrogen, drawn at the bottom right of the pyridine.

The amide nitrogen is bonded to a phenyl ring at a meta position relative to a sulfur substituent. That sulfur links to a para-CF3 phenyl ring (a diaryl thioether).

Writing the SMILES from the pyridine ring: the carbon bearing the amide (C2) is bonded to N1, and the ring runs C2–C3(OAc)–C4(OMe)–C5–C6–N1. The SMILES is:

CC(=O)Oc1c(OC)ccnc1C(=O)Nc1cccc(Sc2ccc(C(F)(F)F)cc2)c1

Checking the meta relationship on the middle phenyl: the NH-bearing carbon is c1, followed by c, c, c, then c(S), then c1 closing the ring. The NH carbon is position 1, and the S-bearing carbon is the fifth atom, which is adjacent to the closing atom (position 6) and so sits at position 5, equivalent to meta. This matches the drawing.
