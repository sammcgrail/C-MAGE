I read the molecule as a para-substituted benzoic acid linked through a CH2CH2 chain to the nitrogen of a pyrrolidin-2-one, with a hydroxyalkenyl side chain on C5 of the ring.

**Skeleton**
- The benzene ring carries CO2H at one end and the CH2CH2 chain at the para position.
- The CH2CH2 chain is attached to the ring nitrogen of the 5-membered lactam (C=O at C2).
- Ring carbon C5 carries a trans CH=CH–CH(OH) side chain, drawn with a wedge bond at C5.
- The carbinol carbon carries a hashed OH and a straight n-pentyl chain (four CH2 groups and a terminal CH3).

**Stereochemistry**
- Ring C5: the wedged vinyl group points toward the viewer, so H points away. Viewed with H away, N → ring CH2 → vinyl runs counterclockwise. For the neighbour order in the SMILES below (ring CH2, H, N, vinyl), this corresponds to `[C@@H]`.
- Carbinol carbon: the hashed OH points away, so H points toward the viewer. Viewed from the H side, vinyl → chain CH2 → OH runs clockwise. For the neighbour order (vinyl, H, O, CH2), this corresponds to `[C@@H]`.
- The double bond is drawn trans, written `/C=C/`.

The SMILES contains the carboxylic acid, para-disubstituted benzene, lactam ring, trans alkene, hydroxyl and pentyl chain, as drawn.

SMILES: OC(=O)c1ccc(CCN2C(=O)CC[C@@H]2/C=C/[C@@H](O)CCCCC)cc1
