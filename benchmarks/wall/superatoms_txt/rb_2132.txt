I read the molecule as a Ga-labelled DOTA-type chelator joined to a cyclic peptide through an isoindole. Only the amide-bearing macrocycle carbon, the lysine alpha carbon and the peptide residues carry stereo, as read from the wedges and hashes.

**Chelator.** The 12-membered tetra-aza ring (cyclen) has three N-CH2-COOH arms. The fourth N carries CH2-C(=O)-NH-, which links to a cysteic acid residue, CH(CH2SO3H). Ga is drawn as a label inside the ring with no bonds, so I wrote it as a separate `[Ga]` component.

**Peptide chain.** After cysteic acid, an NH-(CH2)4 chain leads to the alpha carbon of a lysine-type residue. That alpha carbon is bonded to the isoindole nitrogen (a 2H-isoindole carrying a 4-nitro group and a 1-thioether). From its carbonyl the sequence runs:

1. Tyr
2. Lys(N-isopropyl)
3. Arg (guanidine)
4. 2-naphthylalanine
5. Ala
6. Cys, whose sulfur bonds to isoindole C1, closing the macrocycle
7. Lys(N-isopropyl) with a C-terminal NH2
