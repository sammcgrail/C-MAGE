I read the molecule as a 12-membered triaza-macrocycle carrying three substituents, plus a methylpiperazine sulfonamide.

**Macrocycle (12 atoms):**
- The top nitrogen bears a cyclohexylmethyl group.
- Going round the ring, it connects through three CH2 groups to the right nitrogen, which carries the sulfonyl group.
- From that nitrogen the ring continues as CH2, CH(CH3), CH2 to the lower-left nitrogen. The CH(CH3) has a wedged methyl.
- The lower-left nitrogen carries the 4-(dimethylamino)phenylsulfonyl group.
- It then connects through three CH2 groups back to the top nitrogen.

**Piperazine:**
- The sulfonyl on the right macrocycle nitrogen links to a piperazine nitrogen.
- Going round the piperazine: N(SO2), CH2, CH(CH3) with a wedged methyl, N(aryl), CH2, CH2.
- The aryl group is 4-fluoro-2-methoxyphenyl.

**Stereochemistry:** I assigned each wedged carbon by tracing its neighbours in SMILES order against the drawing, with the implicit hydrogen pointing away from the viewer. Both centres come out as `@@` in the SMILES below.
