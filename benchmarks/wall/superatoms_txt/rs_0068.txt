- The chain runs CH2, CH2, CH2, then C=O. The carbonyl sits on the other fusion carbon (C8a).
- Numbering the tetralone with C1 as the carbonyl carbon, the fusion carbon C8a is bonded to C8, then C7, then C6, then C5, then C4a.
- In the drawing, the aryl carbon is attached to the top-left ring carbon, which is bonded to the top fusion carbon (C4a). That makes it C5 if C4a is the CH2-bearing fusion carbon.
- Checking the geometry: the top fusion carbon connects to the CH2 chain, and the ring carbon at the top (adjacent to it) is CH. Next comes the carbon bearing the pyrrole, then CH at the lower left, then CH at the bottom, then the lower fusion carbon bearing C=O. So the pyrrole-bearing carbon is two bonds from the CH2-bearing fusion carbon, which makes it C6 of 1-tetralone, i.e. 6-substituted.
