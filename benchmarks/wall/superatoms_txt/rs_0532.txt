The drawing shows a pyridine ring joined through an amide to a diaryl ether.

**Pyridine ring.** The ring nitrogen is at the bottom. Going around from the nitrogen, the next ring carbon bears the C(=O)NH amide. After that comes the carbon with OH, then two CH carbons, then the carbon with Br, which is adjacent to the nitrogen. This makes it a 6-bromo-3-hydroxypyridine-2-carboxamide.

**Amide and aryl groups.** The amide nitrogen is attached to a benzene ring. The ortho position of that ring carries an O that links to a second benzene ring. That second ring has a CF3 group meta to the oxygen.

**SMILES.** Writing the pyridine as Oc1ccc(Br)nc1, with the amide carbon on the ring carbon next to the nitrogen, gives:

O=C(Nc1ccccc1Oc1cccc(C(F)(F)F)c1)c1nc(Br)ccc1O

Checking this against the drawing:
- The pyridine ring is c1 (bearing C=O), n, c(Br), c, c, c1(O). That is six atoms, with Br on the carbon next to N and OH on the carbon next to the amide-bearing carbon, as drawn.
- The CF3 sits meta to the ether oxygen on the second benzene ring, as drawn.
