This is my reading of the drawing: a 4-methoxypyridine bearing a 2-bromoethyl carbonate and an amide, joined to a nine-membered dilactone ring in the style of antimycin.

**Pyridine part.** The ring carbon next to the ring nitrogen carries the amide, C(=O)NH. The adjacent carbon carries OC(=O)OCH2CH2Br, a 2-bromoethyl carbonate. The next carbon carries OMe.

**Nine-membered dilactone ring.** Going round the ring, the atoms are:
1. C(=O)
2. ring O
3. CH(CH3)
4. CH(O-isobutyryl), where the substituent is OC(=O)CH(CH3)2
5. CH(CH2Ph)
6. C(=O)
7. ring O
8. CH2
9. CH(NH), which bonds back to atom 1

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned each centre from its wedge or hash and the CIP priorities:
- The NH-bearing carbon has a hashed N bond and is S.
- The CH(CH2Ph) carbon has a wedged benzyl group and is R.
- The CH(O-isobutyryl) carbon has a hashed O bond and is R.
- The CH(CH3) carbon has a wedged methyl group and is S.
