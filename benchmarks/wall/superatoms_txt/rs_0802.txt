The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an oxindole (isatin-type) core.

**Biphenyl part.** The top ring carries CO2H. It is attached to a second ring that has an OH ortho to the biaryl bond and an NH–N= group on the other side of the OH. The CO2H ring is therefore 3-substituted, and the second ring is 1-aryl, 2-OH, 3-NHN=.

**Oxindole part.**
- N1 carries a 3,4-dimethylphenyl group.
- C2 is C=O.
- C3 is the hydrazone carbon, C=N–NH–Ar.
- The fused benzene ring runs C3a, C4(F), C5(H), C6(CF3), C7(H), C7a.

**Stereochemistry.** The C=N double bond is drawn with explicit geometry. The N is at the lower left of C3, and the NH continues up-left from it. On C3, the C2 (carbonyl) side lies to the left and C3a to the right. The N=C bond runs from the upper-left N down to C3. The NH is attached to N and points up-left, so it sits on the same side as the C2 carbonyl (left).

I write the hydrazone as `C(=O)` on the left, with the C3 substituent order `C3(=N/NH...)`. Using the form `N1C(=O)/C(=N/Nc...)c2...`:
- For `C(=N/N...)` preceded by `/C(`, the bond `/C` goes from C2 to C3, meaning C2 is "below" C3.
- `=N/N` places the NH "above" N, so C2 and NH are on opposite sides (trans). This is E with respect to C2.

I want NH on the same side as C2, so I use `=N\N` instead.

**SMILES:** `OC(=O)c1cccc(c1)-c1cccc(N/N=C2/C(=O)N(c3ccc(C)c(C)c3)c3cc(C(F)(F)F)cc(F)c23)c1O`

Checking the stereo in this written order: `N/N=C2/C(=O)...` has NH before N with "/", so the NH is below N. Then `C2/C(=O)` places C(=O) above C3. So NH and C2 are on opposite sides (trans), which is not what I want. Changing the first bond to `\` gives `N\N=C2/C(=O)`, which puts NH and C2 on the same side (cis), as drawn.
