I read the molecule as a 9-membered dilactone ring carrying three substituents. The 3-hydroxy-4-methoxypicolinamide is on the NH-bearing ring carbon. A methyl is on the carbon next to the ring oxygen. The benzyl group is on the carbon next to the lactone carbonyl. The ring carbon between those two carries a 2,6-dimethylcyclohexanecarboxylate ester.

The ring runs: C(=O) – O – CH(CH3) – CH(O-acyl) – CH(CH2Ph) – C(=O) – O – CH2 – CH(NH-acyl) – back to the first C(=O).

Stereochemistry is set only at the four ring stereocentres, which have wedge or hash bonds in the drawing:
- **NH-bearing carbon:** the hashed NH bond puts the nitrogen behind the plane, giving `[C@H]`.
- **CH(CH3):** the bold methyl wedge gives `[C@H]`.
- **CH(O-acyl):** the hashed ester oxygen gives `[C@@H]`.
- **CH(CH2Ph):** the bold benzyl wedge gives `[C@H]`.

The cyclohexane ring is drawn with plain bonds, so it carries no stereo labels.
