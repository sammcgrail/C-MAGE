I read this as a rifamycin-type ansa macrolide fused to a phenoxazine, carrying a fluoro substituent and a 2-(dimethylamino)ethoxy side chain. The stereodescriptors and the E/Z geometry of the double bonds come from the natural rifamycin chain as I remember it. I did not verify each centre against the wedges and hashes in the drawing, so those are the least certain part of the answer.

**Regions of the drawing**
- **Ansa chain, ketal to amide:** the chain runs from the ketal carbon through O–CH=CH–CH(OMe)–CH(CH3)–CH(OAc)–CH(CH3)–CH(OH)–CH(CH3)–CH(OH)–CH(CH3)–CH=CH–CH=C(CH3)–C(=O)NH.
- **Furanone-fused aromatic ring:** the ketal carbon carries a methyl and is bonded to the ring O. The ring then has a C(CH3) and a C(OH).
- **Quinone-like ring:** this ring contains C(=O), C(NH-amide)=C(O) and a C=N carbon.
- **Phenoxazine middle ring:** it holds the N and O bridges.
- **Terminal benzene ring:** F sits next to the carbon bonded to N, and OCH2CH2N(CH3)2 sits meta to F.

**Ring-closure checks**
- The furanone ring has five atoms: the ketal carbon, its O, two aromatic carbons and the C=O.
- The quinone-like ring has six atoms, and the phenoxazine middle ring has six atoms.
