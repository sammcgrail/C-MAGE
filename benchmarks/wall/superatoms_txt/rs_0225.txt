I read the molecule as a pyrazin-2-one core with three substituents.

- **Pyrazinone ring:** N1 is bonded to C2(=O). C2 connects to C3, which is double-bonded to N4. N4 connects to C5(Br), which is double-bonded to C6, and C6 returns to N1.
- **N1 substituent:** a stereogenic carbon bearing an ethyl group, a CH2–O–CH3 group and an explicit H. The H is drawn on a bold wedge.
- **C3 substituent:** an indoline attached through its nitrogen. On the benzo ring, the carbon next to the ring-fusion carbon bonded to N carries a methyl group, and the carbon para to that fusion carbon carries OMe.

**Stereochemistry:** the H is wedged toward the viewer. Seen from the front, ethyl (upper left), CH2OMe (upper right) and N (down) run clockwise. Viewed from the H side, the neighbour order H, Et, CH2, N is therefore @@. Writing the SMILES in the order Et, H, CH2, N swaps the first two neighbours, which flips the symbol to @. That gives `CC[C@H](COC)N...`.

SMILES: CC[C@H](COC)N1C=C(Br)N=C(C1=O)N2CCc3cc(OC)cc(C)c32
