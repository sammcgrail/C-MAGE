I read the molecule as three parts: a 2-oxo-benzoxazolone, an amino-acid core, and a spiro piperidine–benzoxazinone joined through a urea.

**Benzoxazolone (top left).** The five-membered ring is O–C(=O)–NH fused to a benzene ring. The CH2 sits on the benzene carbon para to the N-bearing ring carbon, so this fragment is `O=C1Nc2ccc(C...)cc2O1`.

**Amino-acid core.** The CH2 links to the α-carbon, which carries the CO2H (hashed bond) and an NH. That NH is acylated by a C(=O) that connects to the piperidine nitrogen, forming a urea.

**Stereocentre.** The hashed bond puts CO2H behind the plane, so the implicit H points toward the viewer. Going CO2H → CH2 → NH is counterclockwise on the page, which is clockwise when viewed from the side opposite H. Written as `OC(=O)[C@@H](CH2…)N`, this corresponds to `@@`.

**Spiro piperidine.** The piperidine's C4 is the spiro carbon. It is also bonded to O of a six-membered ring and to the benzene ring: O–C(=O)–NH–aryl, i.e. a 1,4-dihydro-2H-3,1-benzoxazin-2-one. This fragment is `N1CCC2(CC1)OC(=O)Nc1ccccc12`.

Assembling the fragments gives the SMILES below.

SMILES: OC(=O)[C@@H](Cc1ccc2NC(=O)Oc2c1)NC(=O)N1CCC2(CC1)OC(=O)Nc1ccccc12
