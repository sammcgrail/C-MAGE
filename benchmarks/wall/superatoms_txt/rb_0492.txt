This is a macrocyclic STING agonist-type dimer: two benzimidazoles joined by a butene linker at N1 and by an O–CH2–CH(morpholine)–CH2–O linker at C7.

**Top half**
- Pyrazole: N1 carries an ethyl group, C3 carries a methyl, and C5 carries the carbonyl. SMILES fragment: CCn1nc(C)cc1C(=O)N.
- The amide NH is attached to C2 of the benzimidazole.
- Benzimidazole: N3 (the "N=" at top) connects to C3a. C4 is the CH bearing C(=O)NH2 at position 5, C6 is a CH, and C7 bears the O. N1 carries the butenyl chain.

**Linkers**
- Top N1 is attached to CH2–CH=CH–CH2, which ends on the bottom N1. The alkene is drawn as a double bond in the chain with no explicit geometry in the image, so I treat it as E, the usual form for this compound class (drawn as zigzag).
- The top O–CH2–CH(N-morpholine)–CH2–O links to the bottom benzimidazole C7.

**Bottom half**
- Pyrazole: N1 carries a CH2CH2F group, C3 carries a methyl, and C5 carries the carbonyl amide to C2 of the benzimidazole.
- The bottom benzimidazole mirrors the top: N1 is attached to the chain, C7 to the O, and C5 to the carboxamide.

- Closing the ring requires matching the ring-closure digits: the top N1 chain, written as n1C/C=C/C, ends on the bottom n.

Final SMILES: 
CCn1nc(C)cc1C(=O)Nc1nc2cc(C(N)=O)cc(OCC(N3CCOCC3)COc3cc(C(N)=O)cc4nc(NC(=O)c5cc(C)nn5CCF)n(C/C=C/C5)c34)c2n1C5

In this string, ring closure 5 is reused after being closed at c5, which makes it ambiguous. I use a different closure label for the macrocycle:

CCn1nc(C)cc1C(=O)Nc1nc2cc(C(N)=O)cc(OCC(N3CCOCC3)COc3cc(C(N)=O)cc4nc(NC(=O)c5cc(C)nn5CCF)n(C/C=C/C6)c34)c2n1C6

Checking the ring closure: the top n1 (last atom, "n1C6") closes the benzimidazole ring 1 and then CH2 closes 6. The bottom n(C/C=C/C6) has the chain ending in C6, which bonds to the top CH2, so the chain is N–CH2–CH=CH–CH2–N. This is correct.
