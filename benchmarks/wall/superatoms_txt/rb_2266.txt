This SMILES is my reading of the drawing, built as one cyclic peptide with two sugar-phosphonate arms. I checked the atom connectivity region by region. The stereocentres and oxime geometries were assigned by hand from the wedges and hashes, so they are the part most likely to contain an error.

**Arms.** Each arm is a cyclohexane bearing a CH2CH2P(=O)(OH)2 group, three OH groups and an O–CH2CH2–O–N=C(CH3)–CH2CH2–C(=O)– oxime linker (a levulinoyl oxime). The two arms are acylated onto the α-NH and ε-NH of the first lysine.

**Lysine branch.** The first lysine's carbonyl is an amide to the ε-NH of a second lysine. That second lysine carries a C-terminal primary amide (CONH2) and its α-NH is acylated by Gly.

**Macrocycle.** From Gly, the chain runs Cys → Pro → Ala → Glu → N-Me-Ala → biphenylalanine (Bip) → N-Me-Ala → Bip → Thr → Thr → Pro → Val → Phe. The Phe N-terminus is capped with C(=O)CH2–S, and that sulfur is bonded to the Cys side-chain CH2, which closes the ring through a thioether.

**Stereochemistry.**
- Every amino-acid α-carbon is drawn with a wedge or hash and works out as the L configuration.
- The Thr β-carbons are drawn with plain bonds, so they carry no stereo label.
- The two oxime C=N bonds are drawn differently. In the upper arm O and the chain CH2 are trans; in the lower arm they are cis. I encoded both as drawn.
