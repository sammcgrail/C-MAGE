I read the molecule as an anthracycline: a tetracyclic aglycone carrying a three-sugar chain at C1. I set stereochemistry from the wedges and hashes, working out each centre's handedness from the 2D coordinates.

**Aglycone**
- The ring system is a 1,4-dihydroxyanthraquinone-type chromophore with a further OH on the middle aromatic ring (peri to the top C=O) and two quinone C=O groups.
- The saturated ring has four substituted positions:
  - the O-glycosylated CH, drawn with a hashed bond;
  - a CH2;
  - a quaternary C bearing OH and Et, with Et on a wedge;
  - a CH bearing CO2Me on a wedge.

**Sugar chain** (from the aglycone outward)
1. An amino sugar (rhodosamine-type): ring O, C-methyl on a wedge, NMe2 on a wedge, and an O-glycosyl group on a wedge at C4.
2. A 2-deoxy sugar with a 3-OH on a wedge and a 4-O-glycosyl link on a wedge, plus a wedged C-methyl.
3. A terminal 2-deoxy sugar with two wedged OH groups and a wedged C-methyl.

The glycosidic C1–O bonds are all hashed. I wrote @/@@ for each centre from the drawn geometry, with the implicit H taken as pointing opposite the wedged or hashed substituent.
