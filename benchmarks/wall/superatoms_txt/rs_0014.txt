The drawing shows a central benzene ring with three substituents, plus an amidinophenyl amide and a chloropyridyl amide.

**Central ring.** The OMe group sits at the top. Going around the ring, the carbon bearing the carboxamide C(=O)NH-pyridyl is adjacent to the carbon bearing the NH-C(=O)-aryl group. The OMe carbon is meta to the carboxamide carbon, which makes it para to the NH carbon.

Reading the ring vertices:
- Top: C–OMe.
- Upper right: CH, which connects down to the carbon bearing C(=O)NH.
- Lower right: C bearing the carboxamide.
- Bottom: C bearing the NH amide.
- Left, going back up: two CH carbons, ending at the top C–OMe.

So the carboxamide carbon (C1) is bonded to C2 (bearing NH) and C6 (CH). C6 is bonded to C5 (bearing OMe). The OMe is therefore para to the NH carbon, since C2 and C5 are para.

**Pyridine.** The ring is attached through its 2-position to the amide NH. The N is adjacent to C2, and Cl sits at the 5-position, which is para to C2.

**Amidinophenyl.** A para-substituted benzene carries C(=N)N at one end and C(=O)NH at the other.
