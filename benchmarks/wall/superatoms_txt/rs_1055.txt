The drawing shows a 2-methylbenzothiazole whose 6-position carries an NH–CO–CH(Et)–O–aryl chain, with the aryl group a 2,4-di-tert-pentyl phenyl ring.

**Benzothiazole.** S is at the top, N at the bottom, and C2 carries the methyl group. The fused benzene ring has its substituent (the NH) on the carbon at upper right. The five-membered ring runs S1–C2–N3–C3a–C7a. The ring fusion atom attached to S is C7a, and the benzene ring then goes C7a, C7, C6, C5, C4, C3a. In the image the S-bearing fusion carbon is at the upper left of the benzene ring. The next benzene carbon at the top is C7, and the carbon bearing NH sits at the upper right, which is C6. So the NH-bearing carbon is C6, two atoms from C7a, which matches 6-amino-2-methylbenzothiazole.

**Linker.** The chain is NHC(=O)CH(CH2CH3)–O–Ar.

**Aryl ring.** The carbon bonded to O is C1. The ortho carbon (C2) bears a C(CH3)2CH2CH3 group, and the para carbon (C4) bears the second one.

**SMILES.** Writing the benzothiazole as Cc1nc2ccc(NC(=O)C(CC)Oc3ccc(C(C)(C)CC)cc3C(C)(C)CC)cc2s1:
- c1 (C2, with methyl), n, then c2 (C3a).
- c, c (C4, C5), then c(N...) (C6).
- c (C7), then c2 (C7a) bonded to s1, which closes the ring.

This gives the correct connectivity. The CH(Et) carbon is a stereocentre by connectivity but is drawn with plain bonds, so no stereo is assigned.
