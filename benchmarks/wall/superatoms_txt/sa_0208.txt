This is quinacrine dihydrochloride: an acridine core with 6-chloro and 2-methoxy substituents and a 9-amino side chain.

**Ring system.** The acridine has a central ring containing N, with the C9 carbon (bearing NH) opposite it. The left ring carries Cl and the right ring carries OMe.

**Side chain.** From C9, the chain runs NH, then CH(CH3), then CH2, CH2, CH2, then N(Et)2. That makes the pentane chain CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH2NEt2, which is the usual quinacrine chain.

**Salt.** Two HCl are drawn as separate components.

**Stereochemistry.** The CH(CH3) carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so no stereo is assigned.

**Writing the SMILES.**
- Side chain: CCN(CC)CCCC(C)Nc1c2ccc(Cl)cc2nc2ccc(OC)cc12
- Left ring: C9 connects to C9a, which leads through CH, CH to C(Cl), then CH, then C4a, which is bonded to N. This matches c2ccc(Cl)cc2n.
- Right ring: N connects to C10a, then CH, CH, C(OMe), CH, then C8a, which closes back to C9. This matches c2ccc(OC)cc12.
