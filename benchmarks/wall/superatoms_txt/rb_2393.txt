I read the molecule as a macrocyclic ester built from these parts:

- **Pyridine:** a 2-(1-methoxyethyl)pyridin-3-yl group on C2 of an N-ethylindole.
- **Indole to ester:** the indole C3 carries a CH2–C(CH3)2–CH2–O chain that closes as an ester onto the piperazic acid carbonyl.
- **Biaryl:** indole C5 is bonded to a 3-hydroxyphenyl ring, which is also attached through a CH2 to the α-carbon of the next residue.
- **Piperazic acid:** a six-membered ring (C, three CH2, N, NH). Its ring N is acylated by the next residue, whose α-carbon is the one carrying the CH2–aryl group.
- **Valine unit:** an N-methyl valine (isopropyl side chain), linked to the previous residue through an amide NH.
- **Side chain:** the N-methyl nitrogen is acylated by a piperidine-4-carbonyl. The piperidine N carries an (E)-4-(dimethylamino)but-2-enoyl group.

For the four wedge/hash stereocentres, I worked out the chirality from the wedge directions and the implicit hydrogen:

- **Methoxyethyl CH:** S.
- **Piperazic C3** (hashed bond to the carbonyl): gives `[C@@H]3` in the SMILES.
- **α-carbon bearing CH2–aryl** (wedge to NH): gives `[C@@H]`.
- **Valine α-carbon** (wedge to isopropyl): gives `[C@H]`.

The C=C is drawn trans, written as `/C=C/`.
