Reading the drawing region by region:

**Benzoxaborole (top left).** A five-membered ring B(OH)–O–CH2–C(ar)…C(ar), fused to a benzene ring. B is attached to an aromatic carbon, and the ring-closing aromatic carbon carries the CH2. The ether oxygen sits on the benzene carbon para to B's attachment point. In the drawing, the benzene carbons run: C-B (bottom left), then the CH carbons down to the C–O carbon (bottom right), with the CH2-bearing carbon at the top right. Walking the ring: c1(B) – c(CH2) – cH – c(OAr) – cH – cH – back to c1. So the oxygen-bearing carbon is meta to the CH2-bearing carbon and para to the B-bearing carbon.

SMILES for this fragment: OB1OCc2cc(O…)ccc21

**Central aryl ring.** The ring carbon bearing the aryl ether O is attached to the CH= carbon on one side and to the carbon bearing the chain on the other. The ring runs: C1(OAr), C2(chain), C3H, C4H, C5(CN), C6H. The chain carbon is ortho to the C–O carbon. CN is on the carbon that is meta to the C–O carbon (via C6H) and para to the chain carbon.

SMILES for this fragment: c1(Oar)cc(C#N)ccc1CCCCC(=O)N…

**Chain.** From the aryl ring there are four CH2 groups, then C(=O). The zigzag from the ring goes up, down, up, down to the carbonyl, which gives 4 CH2 and then C=O. This is a pentanoyl chain.
