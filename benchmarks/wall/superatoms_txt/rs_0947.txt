The drawing shows a benzodioxole: a benzene ring fused to a five-membered ring containing two oxygens and a central carbon (C2). That C2 carries two CH(CH3)2 groups, and the benzene ring carries an OH.

To place the OH, I numbered the benzene ring from the drawing. The upper fused carbon is attached to the top O, and the lower fused carbon is attached to the bottom O. The top-left vertex of the ring is bonded to the upper fused carbon, and the OH sits on the left-lower vertex. Going around the ring: top (CH), upper-right fused carbon C3a, lower-right fused carbon C7a, bottom (CH), lower-left carbon bearing the OH, upper-left CH, and back to the top. The OH carbon is therefore adjacent to the bottom CH, which is adjacent to the lower fused carbon. That makes the OH at position 5 of the benzodioxole.

SMILES, writing the ring from the OH carbon: Oc1ccc2OC(C(C)C)(C(C)C)Oc2c1

Check: c1 (the OH carbon), then c (upper-left CH), then c (top CH), then c2 (fused carbon) bonded to O, then C(iPr)2, then O, then c2 (the other fused carbon), then c1 (the bottom CH, which closes the ring back to the OH carbon). The OH carbon is therefore adjacent to the bottom CH, which is adjacent to a fused carbon, as drawn.
