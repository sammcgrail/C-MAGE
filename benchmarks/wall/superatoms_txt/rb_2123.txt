I wrote this from memory of vancomycin's isomeric SMILES and then modified it to match the drawing, so the stereocentres are not independently checked against the wedges and hashes in the image.

The structure is a vancomycin aglycone and disaccharide with a conjugated side chain. The side chain sits on the vancosamine amine and is built as follows:

1. The vancosamine NH is converted to a thiourea, NC(=S)N.
2. The thiourea leads into a tri-ethylene-glycol chain: CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2.
3. The chain ends on N1 of a 1,2,3-triazole.
4. The triazole C4 carries a CH2 that links to N9 of an adenine-type purine.
5. The purine has 6-NH2, 2-O-butyl and 8-OH substituents.

The ring-closure labels in the side chain (%10, %11, %12) are chosen so they do not clash with those already used in the vancomycin core.
