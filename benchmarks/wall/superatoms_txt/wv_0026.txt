The drawing shows a primary amide (H2N–C(=O)–) whose carbonyl carbon is bonded to a second carbon. That second carbon has two wavy lines, so it is an attachment point on both sides.

Reading the wavy lines: one wavy line crosses the bond going up and to the right, and another crosses the bond going down and to the left. The carbon at the bottom is therefore a CH bearing two attachment points, with the amide as its third substituent.

The carbon is drawn as a vertex with a wavy bond to the upper right and another to the lower left. Each wavy line is one attachment point, so there are two `*` atoms. The carbon then has two bonds to `*`, one bond to the carbonyl carbon, and one implicit hydrogen, which gives the fragment *C(*)C(N)=O.

No wedges or hashes are drawn, so no stereochemistry is included.
