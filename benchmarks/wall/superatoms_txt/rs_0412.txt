The structure breaks into three parts.

**Left ring.** A benzene ring carries an amidine at the para position, C(=N)NC, which is C(=NH)NH-CH3. The ring carbon ortho to the amide carbonyl carries F. The carbonyl-bearing carbon is C1, F is on C2, and the amidine is on C4. The amidine and F are both meta to each other, which matches the drawing.

**Central ring.** The ring carries the NH-C(=O) amide on one carbon and, on the adjacent carbon, a C(=O)NH-pyridyl group. OMe sits on the carbon para to the NH, as follows:
- C1 bears the NH.
- C2 bears the C(=O)NH-pyridyl group.
- C3 is CH.
- C4 bears OMe, which is para to C1.
- C5 and C6 are CH.

In the drawing, the OMe carbon is at the top. The ring runs from the OMe carbon down-right to the carbon bearing the carboxamide, then to the carbon bearing NH, so OMe is para to the NH carbon.

**Pyridine.** The ring is attached through its 2-position to the amide NH. The ring N is adjacent to that carbon, and Cl is at the 5-position, para to the attachment carbon.
