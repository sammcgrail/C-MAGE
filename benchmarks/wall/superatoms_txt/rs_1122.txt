I read this as an antimycin-type nine-membered dilactone carrying a substituted pyridine amide.

**Pyridine part**
- The pyridine has a 4-OMe and a 3-O-acyl group.
- The acyl group is 3,5,5-trimethylhexanoyl, written O–C(=O)CH2–CH(CH3)–CH2–C(CH3)3.
- The 2-position carries a C(=O)NH amide.

**Nine-membered dilactone ring** (going around from the NH-bearing carbon):
- CH(NH): the N bond is hashed.
- C(=O)
- O
- CH(CH3): the methyl is a wedge.
- CH(O–C(=O)CH(CH3)2): the isobutyrate bond is hashed.
- CH(CH2Ph): the benzyl bond is a wedge.
- C(=O)
- O
- CH2, which closes back to the CH(NH) carbon.

**Stereochemistry**
I assigned each centre from the wedge or hash on its drawn bond, taking the implicit hydrogen as pointing the opposite way. This gives `[C@H]` at the NH-bearing carbon, `[C@H]` at the benzyl carbon, `[C@@H]` at the isobutyrate carbon and `[C@H]` at the methyl carbon, in the order they appear in the SMILES below.
