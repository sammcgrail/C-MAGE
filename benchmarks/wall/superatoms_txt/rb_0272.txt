I read the molecule as a macrocycle fused to a substituted benzimidazole.

**Macrocycle**
- A cyanobenzene (nitrile at the top) has two meta substituents: a CH2CH2–O–CH2 chain and a CH2–O link.
- The CH2CH2–O–CH2 chain runs to the central fluorobenzene.
- The CH2–O link runs to the 2-position of a pyridine.
- The pyridine's 6-position is bonded directly to the central fluorobenzene.

**Central fluorobenzene**
- Its ring carbons carry, in order: the CH2–O chain, the CH2 linking to the benzimidazole, a CH, the fluorine, the pyridine, and a second CH.

**Benzimidazole**
- C2 carries the CH2 that links to the central benzene.
- N1 carries a CH2 attached to an oxetan-2-yl group.
- On the benzo ring, C4 bears OCH2CH2OMe, C6 bears CO2Me, and C5 and C7 are CH.

**Stereochemistry**
- The only stereo marking is the hashed bond from the oxetane C2 to the CH2 on N1.
- The priorities at that carbon are O, then CH2N, then ring CH2, with H lowest.
- With the CH2N group pointing away and H toward the viewer, the centre works out as S.
- In the SMILES, `[C@@H]` written as `n1C[C@@H]1CCO1` gives S.

SMILES: N#Cc1ccc2cc1CCOCc1cc(c(F)cc1Cc1nc3c(OCCOC)cc(C(=O)OC)cc3n1C[C@@H]1CCO1)-c1cccc(n1)OC2
