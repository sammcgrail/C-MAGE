The structure breaks down into three regions.

**Left region.** A thiomorpholine ring (S at the far left, N at the right) is attached through the nitrogen to a carbon bearing =NH, forming an amidine: N1CCSCC1 with C(=N). That amidine carbon is bonded to a benzene ring. On that ring, the carbon bearing the C(=O)NH group sits para to the amidine. The fluorine is on the carbon adjacent to the amidine-bearing carbon, which makes it ortho to the carbonyl-bearing carbon (it sits below the ring carbon that bears the carbonyl).

**Middle region.** The ring carbon bearing C(=O) is C1 and the fluorine-bearing carbon is C2. The amidine carbon is at C4, which is para to C1. In the drawing, C1 is at the right-bottom and F is on the bottom-left carbon, so F is ortho to C1 and meta to the amidine. The SMILES for this ring is c1(C(=O)N...)ccc(C(=N)N2CCSCC2)cc1F.

**Right region.** The amide NH is attached to a second benzene ring. Ortho to the NH is the C(=O)NH-pyridyl group. The OMe is para to the NH, and it is meta to the carbonyl-bearing carbon. In the ring, the carbon bearing NH is at the lower left, the carbon bearing C(=O) is at the lower right, and the carbon bearing OMe is at the top right, adjacent to the C(=O)-bearing carbon's neighbour. Taking the NH carbon as a1, the carbonyl carbon is a2, then a3 is the CH carrying OMe (the top-right carbon). So OMe is meta to the NH carbon's neighbour... checking: the ring runs a1 (NH), a2 (C=O), a3 (C-OMe), a4 (CH), a5 (CH), a6 (CH). The OMe at a3 is therefore para to a6, not to a1, and is meta to a1. The ring sequence in the drawing confirms this: the top carbon bearing OMe is linked to the right carbon, which is linked to the carbon bearing C(=O).

The pyridine is 5-chloropyridin-2-yl, attached to the amide NH.
