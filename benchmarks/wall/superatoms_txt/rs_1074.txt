I read the molecule as a tert-butyl ester of a succinate-type chain. The 1-benzothiophen-3-ylmethyl group sits on the alpha carbon, and the acyl group is attached to the nitrogen of a 4-benzyl-oxazolidin-2-one.

**Connectivity**
- The ester end is tBuO–C(=O)–CH2–.
- The next carbon is a stereocentre. It carries the benzothiophen-3-ylmethyl group (drawn on a wedge), the CH2CO2tBu arm, an implicit H, and a C(=O) that links to the oxazolidinone nitrogen.
- The oxazolidinone ring is N–C(=O)–O–CH2–CH, and the CH carries a benzyl group on a wedge.

**Stereocentre on the chain**
- CIP priorities are C(=O)N > CH2CO2tBu > CH2(benzothienyl) > H.
- The CH2CO2tBu arm outranks the benzothienylmethyl arm because its next carbon has (O,O,O) against (C,C,C).
- The wedge points the benzothienylmethyl group toward the viewer, so H points away.
- Going C(=O)N → CH2CO2tBu → CH2Ar runs counterclockwise on the page, so the centre is S.
- In the SMILES below, this is written `[C@H]`.

**Stereocentre on the oxazolidinone**
- CIP priorities are N > CH2O > CH2Ph > H.
- The benzyl wedge points toward the viewer, so H points away.
- Going N → CH2O → CH2Ph runs clockwise, so the centre is R.
- In the SMILES below, this is written `[C@H]`.
