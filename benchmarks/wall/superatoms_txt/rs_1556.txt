Reading the drawing from left to right gives a terminal vinyl group (CH2=CH–), two CH2 groups, and then a carbon bearing a methyl group that is double-bonded to a CH. That CH is attached to a carbonyl carbon (C=O) which carries OMe, so the molecule is methyl 3-methylhepta-2,6-dienoate.

For the stereochemistry of the C2=C3 double bond, the double bond is drawn vertically. At C3, the methyl points to the upper right and the CH2 chain to the upper left. At C2, the ester carbon points to the lower left and H to the right. The chain and the ester are therefore on the same side. Both are the higher-priority substituents on their carbons, so the alkene is Z.

In SMILES, `C/C(C)=C\C(=O)` places the chain carbon and the ester carbon cis, which matches this.
