This is a rifamycin-type ansamycin in which the naphthoquinone core is fused to a phenoxazine ring. The phenoxazine carries a 2-(4-methylpiperazin-1-yl)ethoxy substituent. I built the SMILES region by region from the drawing. The stereocentres and alkene geometries on the ansa chain were adapted from the natural rifamycin pattern, which I recalled from memory rather than reading each wedge and hash individually, so they are the least certain part.

- **Core ring system:** the furanone ring has a quaternary carbon (methyl, ring oxygen, enol-ether oxygen bridge) and a C=O. It is fused to the aromatic ring bearing a methyl and an OH. That ring is fused to the quinone ring (C=O and NH-amide). The quinone ring is in turn fused to the phenoxazine (N on the carbon next to the furanone side, O on the carbon next to the NH-bearing carbon).
- **Phenoxazine substituent:** the benzo ring carries OCH2CH2–N(piperazine)–N-methyl.
- **Ansa chain, from the amide C=O onward:** C(Me)=CH–CH=CH–CH(Me)–CH(OH)–CH(Me)–CH(OH)–CH(Me)–CH(OAc)–CH(Me)–CH(OMe)–CH=CH–O, closing onto the quaternary carbon.
