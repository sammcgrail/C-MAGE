I read the drawing as a DOTA-type chelator joined through a lysine to a piperazine-linked FAP-targeting quinoline unit and a 4-iodophenylbutanoyl albumin-binder arm.

- **Chelator:** a cyclen ring (four N, four CH2CH2 bridges). Three nitrogens carry CH2COOH. The fourth carries CH2C(=O)NH–, which links to the lysine side chain.
- **Lysine:** the ε-NH is acylated by the chelator and the α-carbon is (S). The α-NH carries a glutaryl linker, and the carboxyl end is an amide to the piperazine nitrogen.
- **Piperazine arm:** the other piperazine nitrogen carries –(CH2)3–O– attached at the 6-position of a quinoline. The quinoline 4-position bears C(=O)NH–CH2–C(=O)– on a pyrrolidine nitrogen. That pyrrolidine has 4,4-difluoro and a 2-cyano group with (S) configuration.
- **Albumin-binder arm:** the glutaryl linker ends in an amide to the α-amino group of a second lysine, whose COOH is free. Its ε-NH is acylated by 4-(4-iodophenyl)butanoyl.

Two readings are assumed rather than certain. The glutaryl linker is taken as five carbons (C(=O)CH2CH2CH2C(=O)), and each lysine chain as four CH2 groups. The second lysine α-carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so it carries no stereo label.

I checked both (S) centres by working out the CIP priorities from the drawing and matching them to the chirality tags in the SMILES; both come out as `[C@H]`.
