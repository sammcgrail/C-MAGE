The drawing shows an isoquinoline ring system. A CH2 group is attached to C1, the carbon next to the ring nitrogen. The wavy line is on the bond from that CH2 to the rest of the molecule, so it marks the single attachment point.

The wavy line sits on a bond that leads to a carbon with no label. That carbon is attached to the CH2, which in turn attaches to the isoquinoline C1. The wavy bond runs from the left edge to a vertex at about (75, 105). From there a bond goes up to (100, 75) and then to C1 at (155, 105).

Reading the vertices again, the wavy line crosses the bond between the left-hand attachment and the vertex at (75, 105). That vertex is the CH2, and it bonds to C1. The vertex at (100, 75) is the same CH2 as I counted a moment ago. So the chain is * – CH2 – C1 of isoquinoline, and the wavy line crosses the bond to the CH2.

The isoquinoline ring is C1(=N)…: C1 is bonded to N, and N is bonded to C3, which is bonded to C4 and then to C4a. The benzo ring is fused at C4a and C8a, and C8a is bonded to C1.
