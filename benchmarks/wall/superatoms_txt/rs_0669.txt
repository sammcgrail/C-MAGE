I read the molecule as a methanesulfonate ester of a phenol, joined through a propyl chain and an NH to a second benzene ring that carries a 2-methoxypropanoic acid side chain.

**Regions of the drawing**
- Left end: Me–SO2–O attached to a para-substituted benzene ring (a mesylate on the phenol oxygen).
- Linker: three CH2 groups run from that ring to an N–H.
- Middle: the N–H is attached to a second para-substituted benzene ring.
- Right end: that ring carries CH2–CH(OMe)–CO2H.

**Stereochemistry**
Only the carbon bearing OMe is a stereocentre, and it is drawn with a bold wedge to OMe and a hashed bond to H.
- The CIP priorities are OMe > CO2H > CH2Ar > H.
- H points away from the viewer. Going OMe (lower right) → CO2H (upper right) → CH2 (upper left) turns counterclockwise on the page.
- That gives the S configuration.
- Written as OC(=O)[C@@H](OC)Cc1..., the @@ encodes S, which matches.

SMILES: OC(=O)[C@@H](OC)Cc1ccc(NCCCc2ccc(OS(C)(=O)=O)cc2)cc1
