The drawing shows a benzoxazole with a methyl group on C2, drawn as a line going left from C2. The sulfonamide sits at position 6 of the ring system.

To place the substituent, I numbered the benzoxazole atoms: O1, C2, N3, C3a, C4, C5, C6, C7, C7a. In the drawing, O is at the top of the five-membered ring and N is at the bottom. The top benzene carbon bonded to O is C7a, and the carbon above it is C7. The SO2 group is attached to the upper-right carbon of the benzene ring, which is C6 (C7 is the top vertex, and C6 is the one to its right). C6 is therefore meta to C7a, which is consistent with the drawing.

The side chain is SO2NH-CH2-CH(C2H5)(C4H9). The CH carbon has plain bonds, so I gave it no stereo.

Writing the SMILES from the methyl group: Cc1nc2ccc(cc2o1)S(=O)(=O)NCC(CC)CCCC.

Checking the ring connectivity: c1 (C2) is bonded to n, then c2 (C3a), then c c (C4, C5), then c(S) (C6), then c (C7), then c2 (C7a), then o1. C6 is bonded to C7, which is bonded to C7a, which carries O, so the substituent position matches the drawing.
