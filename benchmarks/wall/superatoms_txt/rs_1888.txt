I read the molecule as a nine-membered dilactone ring carrying an amide-linked pyridine, a pivalate ester and a benzyl group.

**Pyridine.** The ring has N at position 1, a carboxamide carbon at C2, OH at C3, OMe at C4, and CH at C5 and C6. The C2 carbonyl carries the amide NH.

**Nine-membered ring.** Going around the ring from the NH-bearing carbon, the atoms are:
1. CH bearing the amide N. The N–C bond is hashed.
2. CH2.
3. Ring O.
4. C(=O), the lactone carbonyl.
5. CH bearing the benzyl group. The benzyl bond is wedged.
6. CH bearing the pivaloyloxy group, OC(=O)C(CH3)3. This O–C bond is hashed.
7. CH bearing a methyl. The methyl bond is wedged.
8. Ring O.
9. C(=O), which closes the ring back to the NH-bearing CH.

**Stereochemistry.** There are four stereocentres, all drawn with wedges or hashes. I worked out each @/@@ from the 2D geometry, taking the implicit H as pointing opposite the wedged or hashed substituent. The result is N[C@H], [C@H](Bn), [C@@H](OPiv) and [C@H](CH3) in the SMILES written in that order.
