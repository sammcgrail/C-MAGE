I read the molecule as a pyrazin-2(1H)-one core carrying three substituents, with one stereocentre.

- **Pyrazinone ring:** N1 is bonded to C2 (C=O). C2 is bonded to C3, which carries the tetrahydroquinoline nitrogen and is double-bonded to N4. N4 is bonded to C5 (Cl), which is double-bonded to C6 (H), and C6 closes back to N1.
- **N1 substituent:** a stereogenic CH bearing ethyl, cyclopropyl, N1 and a hashed H.
- **Tetrahydroquinoline:** the ring nitrogen is attached to C3 of the pyrazinone. The benzo ring has Br at C8 (ortho to the ring-fusion carbon bonded to N) and OMe at C6.

**Stereochemistry:** the H is hashed, so it points away from the viewer. The CIP priorities are N > cyclopropyl > ethyl > H. Going from N (bottom) to cyclopropyl (upper right) to ethyl (upper left) is counterclockwise, so the centre is S. Writing the SMILES from the ethyl side with neighbour order (H, cyclopropyl, N), the `@@` tag gives S.

SMILES: CC[C@@H](C1CC1)N2C=C(Cl)N=C(N3CCCc4cc(OC)cc(Br)c43)C2=O
