The drawing shows a cyclohexene ring joined to a pinacol boronate ester (a dioxaborolane).

**Cyclohexene ring.** The ring vertices, going around, are:
- C1, the carbon bonded to boron
- C2, the other end of the double bond (bottom, a CH)
- C3, the lower-left CH2
- C4, the upper-left carbon carrying the CF3 group
- C5, the top CH2
- C6, the right-hand CH2 between the top CH2 and C1

The double bond lies between C1 and C2.

**Boronate ester.** Boron is bonded to C1 and to two oxygens. Those oxygens connect through two quaternary carbons, each bearing two methyl groups, to form the 4,4,5,5-tetramethyl-1,3,2-dioxaborolane ring.

**SMILES.** Starting from the CF3 carbon: FC(F)(F)C1CCC(=CC1)B1OC(C)(C)C(C)(C)O1

Check: C1 (the CF3-bearing carbon), then CH2 (C5), CH2 (C6), then C(=C…) is C1 of the ring with the boron, =CH (C2), CH2 (C3), closing back to the CF3 carbon. That is six ring atoms, as drawn.

The CF3-bearing carbon is a stereocentre by connectivity but is drawn with plain bonds, so no stereochemistry is given.
