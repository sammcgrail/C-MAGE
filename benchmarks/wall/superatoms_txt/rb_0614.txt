I read the molecule in five pieces and joined them in order:

1. **Cereblon-binding end:** a 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl group on the nitrogen of a phthalimide (1,3-dioxoisoindoline). The ether oxygen sits at position 4 of the benzene ring.
2. **Linker:** O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–C(=O)–NH, a three-ether chain ending in an amide.
3. **Lysine-like core:** the amide NH is followed by four CH2 groups and then the stereocentre CH. That CH carries an NH–C(=O) group and a C(=O)NH group.
4. **Heteroaryl amide on the stereocentre:** the NH–C(=O) is attached to position 3 of a pyrrole whose ring nitrogen carries a methylsulfonyl group (SO2CH3).
5. **Other end:** the C(=O)NH is attached to position 2 of a 4-phenylthiazole.

**Stereochemistry:** the hashed bond points to the NH, so the hydrogen faces the viewer. The substituent priorities are N > C(=O)N > CH2 > H. Seen from the front, N → C(=O) → CH2 runs clockwise, and reversing for the front-facing H gives S. In SMILES, `N[C@@H](chain)C(=O)N` is the S arrangement, matching L-alanine written the same way.
