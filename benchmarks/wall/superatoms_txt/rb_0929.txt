The molecule is a PROTAC-type compound. I read it in three parts: a pyrazine carboxamide, a 3,9-diazaspiro[5.5]undecane linker, and a thalidomide-type end group.

**Left part.** The pyrazine ring has these substituents:
- C2 carries the CONH2 group.
- C3 carries the NH-aryl group.
- C5 carries the thiazol-2-yl group.
- The ring nitrogens sit at positions 1 and 4.

Going around the ring: C(CONH2), then C(NHAr), then N, then C(thiazolyl), then CH, then N, closing back to C(CONH2). In the drawing, the top-left carbon bears the carboxamide and the ring nitrogen is on the left. The carbon next to it bears the NH, then there is a nitrogen at the lower right. The carbon bearing the thiazole is at the bottom, followed by a CH at the lower left and a nitrogen on the left. As SMILES: NC(=O)c1nccc(n1)... is wrong here, so I write it explicitly as c1(C(N)=O)c(Nc...)nc(-c2nccs2)cn1.

**Middle part.** The NH is attached to a para-substituted phenyl ring. The phenyl is bonded to N of a piperidine that is spiro-fused to a second piperidine, which gives the 3,9-diazaspiro[5.5]undecane. The second piperidine nitrogen carries a CH2 group.

**Right part.** The CH2 is attached to C5 of an isoindoline-1,3-dione. The isoindoline nitrogen is bonded to C3 of a 2,6-dioxopiperidine. No stereochemistry is drawn there, so none is given.
