The drawing shows a 1,2,4-triazine ring with these substituents:

- The ring atoms are C3 (bearing SMe), N4, C5 (bearing OH), C6 (bearing the carboxamide), N1 and N2.
- The top-left carbon carries MeS, and the top nitrogen (N4) sits between that carbon and the OH-bearing carbon.
- The bottom-left atoms are N–N, with the bottom nitrogen attached to the carbon that carries the C(=O)NH group.
- The amide nitrogen is attached to a cyclooctyl ring. I counted the ring vertices: the attachment carbon plus 7 others, 8 in total.

The bond pattern is: C3=N2 (drawn as a double bond from the carbon to the left N), N1=C6, and C5=N4, with C3–N4 single. That gives a ring written as C3(SC)=N–N=C6–C5(O)=N4–C3, which is consistent.

The bond to the cyclooctyl carbon is drawn as a bold wedge, but that carbon is not a stereocentre because the ring is symmetric. So no stereochemistry is included.

SMILES: CSc1nnc(C(=O)NC2CCCCCCC2)c(O)n1
