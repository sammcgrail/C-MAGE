I read the molecule as an L-alanine amide: the carboxyl end is a tertiary amide of N-benzyl-N-(thiophen-2-ylmethyl)amine, and the alanine NH is acylated by a 4-methoxy-3-(pyrrolidin-1-yl)benzoyl group.

- **Left side:** a benzyl group (phenyl on CH2) and a thiophen-2-ylmethyl group (CH2 on the carbon next to S) are both on the amide nitrogen.
- **Centre:** the amide C=O connects to the alanine α-carbon, which carries a hashed methyl, an NH and an implicit H.
- **Right side:** the NH is bonded to a benzoyl C=O. On the benzene ring, the pyrrolidine nitrogen is at position 3 and the OMe at position 4.
- **Stereochemistry:** the methyl is hashed, so it points away from the viewer and the implicit H points toward the viewer. The priorities are N > C(=O) > CH3 > H. Viewed from the front, N → C(=O) → CH3 runs clockwise, and reversing for the H facing the viewer gives S, the L-alanine configuration. In SMILES, `C[C@H](N...)C(=O)...` corresponds to S.

SMILES: C[C@H](NC(=O)c1ccc(OC)c(N2CCCC2)c1)C(=O)N(Cc1ccccc1)Cc1cccs1
