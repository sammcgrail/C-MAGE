**Carboxylates.** Each ⁻OOC group is followed by three drawn chain vertices and then an n-decyl group (10 carbons). That gives 1 + 3 + 10 = 14 carbons, so each anion is tetradecanoate (myristate): `[O-]C(=O)` plus 13 CH2/CH3 carbons.

**Diamine.** The cyclohexane ring has six vertices. Two adjacent ring carbons each carry an NH2: one on a wedge and one on a hash, so the two amines are trans. Assigning CIP priorities (N > ring carbon bearing N > other ring carbon > H):
- The wedged carbon is R.
- The hashed carbon is also R.

The diamine is therefore (1R,2R), written as `N[C@@H]1CCCC[C@H]1N`. I checked this SMILES by hand against the drawing, and both centres come out R.
