The drawing shows a benzoxazol-2(3H)-one core: a five-membered ring fused to a benzene ring, with a CHO group on the benzene ring.

**Five-membered ring.** It contains C2 (the carbonyl carbon, C=O), O1, C7a, C3a and N3 (NH). The carbonyl carbon sits at the top, bonded to O1 on the right and to NH on the left. O1 connects to the fused carbon C7a. The NH connects to C3a.

**Benzene ring.** The fused carbons are C3a, at the left and bonded to N, and C7a, at the right and bonded to O. Going around the ring from C7a: C7 (top right, CH), then C6 (right, bearing CHO), then C5 (bottom, CH), then C4 (left, CH), and back to C3a.

**CHO position.** In the image the CHO hangs from the lower-right vertex. The vertices are C3a (upper left, about 75,150), C7a (upper right, about 135,120), C7 (about 190,150), C6 (about 190,215), C5 (about 135,250) and C4 (about 75,215). So the CHO is on the vertex two bonds from C7a, which makes it C6, para to the nitrogen-bearing carbon C3a.

**SMILES.** Writing it from the aldehyde: O=Cc1ccc2[nH]c(=O)oc2c1. Here c1 is C6, then c c are C5 and C4, c2 is C3a, [nH] is N3, c(=O) is C2, o is O1, c2 is C7a, and the final c1 is C7, which closes the ring back to C6. This matches the drawing.
