This is a PROTAC-type molecule: a sunitinib-like kinase binder joined by a linker to a pomalidomide-type cereblon ligand.

**Left fragment (sunitinib-like):**
- The oxindole carries F at the 5-position. Its C3 is double-bonded to a CH that attaches to the pyrrole.
- The pyrrole has 2,4-dimethyl groups, and its C3 bears the carboxamide.
- The amide nitrogen carries CH2CH2 leading to a piperazine nitrogen.

**Linker:**
- The other piperazine nitrogen is acylated: C(=O)CCCCN, with four CH2 groups between the carbonyl and the NH.
- That NH is attached to the aromatic ring at position 4 of the phthalimide.

**Right fragment:**
- The phthalimide N is bonded to the glutarimide ring carbon.

**Stereochemistry:** The exocyclic C=C is drawn with defined geometry. The oxindole C3 is attached to C4 of the aryl ring and to C2 (C=O). The CH= is attached to the pyrrole. In the drawing, the oxindole benzo ring sits at the top left and the pyrrole NH is below, so the pyrrole is on the same side as the C=O. This is the Z isomer, as in sunitinib.

**SMILES (written with the Z geometry):**
- Oxindole: O=C1Nc2ccc(F)cc2/C1=C\c1[nH]c(C)c(C(=O)N...)c1C
- Check of the geometry: on C3, the substituents are C2 (the C=O carbon) and C3a (the aryl carbon). On the CH, the substituents are the pyrrole and H.
- Written as `C1(=C\c3...)`, the ring-closure order makes the stereo labels easy to get wrong. Sunitinib's known SMILES is CCN(CC)CCNC(=O)c1c(C)[nH]c(/C=C2\C(=O)Nc3ccc(F)cc32)c1C, so I use that pattern.
