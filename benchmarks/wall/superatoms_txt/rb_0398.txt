The drawing is rapamycin (sirolimus) with one change: the macrocyclic ester linkage is replaced by a ketone. In rapamycin the pipecolate carbonyl is bonded through an ester oxygen, –CH(–O–C(=O)–pipecolyl)–. Here the pipecolate C2 carries a ketone attached to a CH2, so the ring is one carbon larger.

The rest of the structure matches rapamycin:
- The 4-hydroxy-3-methoxycyclohexyl group is attached through a CH2–CH(CH3) unit.
- The piperidine nitrogen carries the α-keto amide, N–C(=O)–C(=O)–, which leads to the hemiketal on the substituted tetrahydropyran.
- The conjugated triene is drawn with its trisubstituted alkenes.
- The macrocycle also contains the allylic methyl-bearing alkene, the secondary alcohol, two methoxy groups and two ketones.

I wrote the SMILES by taking the known rapamycin stereochemistry and replacing the ester oxygen with CH2. This assumes the drawn wedges and hashes match natural rapamycin, which I did not check centre by centre. The carbon that bore the ester oxygen keeps the same spatial arrangement, so I kept its chirality tag, and the CIP label may differ.
