I read the molecule as a cyclic peptide: a disulfide-bridged penicillamine–penicillamine macrocycle with an N-terminal-to-C-terminal arm and a lipidated lysine side arm. I assigned stereochemistry from the wedges and hashes where they are drawn. Where a centre has no clear wedge or hash (Trp, Asn, Thr), I assumed the L configuration.

**Arm out of the ring (C-terminal end first):**
- Glycinamide carrying an N-methyl group, with its nitrogen acylated by L-3-pyridylalanine.
- L-Asn, then L-Glu (free acid side chain).
- 4-Aminotetrahydropyran-4-carboxylic acid residue.
- L-2-naphthylalanine, then L-Tyr whose phenol oxygen carries a 2-aminoethyl group.
- This Tyr is acylated by the carbonyl of the first penicillamine, which opens the ring.

**Macrocycle (going around):**
- L-Pen (penicillamine, the first one).
- L-Lys with an acetylated side-chain amine.
- 7-Methyl-L-Trp, L-Thr and L-Asn.
- L-Pen (the second one), joined to the first Pen through the S–S bridge between the two C(CH3)2 carbons.

**Lipidated lysine on the second Pen:**
- A D-Lys whose side-chain amine is acylated by succinic acid. That succinyl group is amidated by a trimethylammonium-bearing 3-aminobutanoic acid unit, which has the R centre shown by its hash.
- The Lys α-nitrogen carries two OEG units (–C(=O)CH2OCH2CH2OCH2CH2NH–), then a γ-linked L-Glu.
- The Glu nitrogen carries a C20 diacid acyl group (C(=O)(CH2)18COOH). I estimated the chain length from the drawing's proportions, so the count of 18 CH2 groups is the least certain part.
