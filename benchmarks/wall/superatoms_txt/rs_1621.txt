The drawing is an azo dye. I read it in two halves.

**Left ring:** a benzene ring with an azo attachment at one carbon. Both ortho positions carry CN, and the para position carries NO2. This is 2,6-dicyano-4-nitrophenyl.

**Right ring:** a benzene ring with these substituents:
- the azo group at C1;
- NHAc at C2 (ortho to the azo group);
- NEt2 at C4, the carbon below the NHAc-bearing carbon and para to the azo carbon;
- OCH3 at C5.

Checking the ring layout from the drawing: the top-left carbon bears the azo group, the top-right carbon bears NHAc, the right carbon bears NEt2, the bottom-right carbon bears OMe, and the bottom-left carbon is CH. Numbering from the azo carbon (C1), the NHAc carbon is C2 and the CH next to it is C3. The NEt2 carbon is C4, the OMe carbon is C5, and C6 is the bottom-left CH. So NEt2 is para to the azo carbon and OMe is meta to it.

The diethylamino group is drawn as N with two ethyl lines, so it is N(CC)CC.

Checking the right ring in this SMILES: c1 (azo carbon), c (CH), c(OC), c(NEt2), c (CH), c1(NHAc). The azo carbon is adjacent to the CH and to the NHAc carbon. OMe is on the carbon next to the CH, and NEt2 is next to that, with a CH between NEt2 and NHAc. This matches the drawing.
