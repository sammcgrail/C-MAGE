I read the molecule as a glutamic-acid-type core (free α-amine) with two different amide arms, each ending in the same indazole–imidazole unit.

**Central core:** H2N–CH(C(=O)–)–CH2–CH2–C(=O)–. This carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo.

**Upper arm (from the γ-carbonyl):**
- The γ-carbonyl is bonded to NH–CH2CH2CH2–C(=O), a 4-aminobutanoyl (GABA) unit. I counted three CH2 groups.
- That carbonyl is bonded to NH–CH(COOH)–CH2–NH–C(=O)–, a 2,3-diaminopropionic acid unit.
- The carbonyl then attaches to C5 of an indazole.

**Lower arm (from the α-carbonyl):**
- The α-carbonyl is bonded to NH–(CH2)5–C(=O), a 6-aminohexanoyl unit. I counted five CH2 groups.
- That carbonyl is bonded to the same diaminopropionic acid unit (NH–CH(COOH)–CH2–NH–C(=O)–) and then to C5 of an indazole.

**Indazole and tail (both arms):** N1 of the indazole carries –CH2CH2CH2–NH–, which is bonded to C2 of a 1H-imidazole.

**Stereochemistry:**
- Upper diaminopropionic carbon: the CH2 is on a hashed bond, so the H points toward the viewer. The priority order N > COOH > CH2 runs clockwise, which reverses to counterclockwise, so the centre is S.
- Lower diaminopropionic carbon: the CH2 is on a wedge, so the H points away. The same priority order runs counterclockwise, so the centre is S.
- Both centres are therefore written as `N[C@H](C(=O)O)CN...`.
