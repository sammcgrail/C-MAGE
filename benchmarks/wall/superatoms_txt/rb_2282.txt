I read the structure as a macrocycle of alternating N-substituted glycine (peptoid) units and reduced amino-acid units (–NH–CH(R)–CH2–N–), closed through a thioether. I traced the ring once around, starting at the thioether sulfur.

1. The sulfur links a CH2–(para-phenylene)–C(=O) unit to a CH2CH2–C(=O)– unit on the other side.
2. Ring sequence from the benzamide carbonyl: NH–CH(CH2Ph)–CH2–N(C(=O)CH2-cyclopropyl)–CH2–C(=O)–.
3. Next: NH–CH(CH2-indol-3-yl)–CH2–N(C(=O)CH2-cyclopropyl)–CH2–C(=O)–.
4. Next: NH–CH((CH2)4NH2)–CH2–N(C(=O)CH2CH2Ph)–CH2–C(=O)–.
5. Last: NH–CH(CH2Ph)–CH2–N(CH2C(=O)NH2)–C(=O)CH2CH2–S, which closes the ring.

All four stereocentres carry a wedged side-chain bond. In each, the NH is the highest-priority substituent, then CH2N, then the side-chain CH2, and the hydrogen is behind the plane. Going NH → CH2N → side chain turns counterclockwise in every case, so all four are S. Written as N[C@@H](R)CN, each one encodes S.

I checked that the ring-closure digits are reused only after being closed and that every atom in the drawing appears once.
