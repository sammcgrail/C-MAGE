The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an oxindole (isatin-type) core.

**Biphenyl part.** The top-left ring carries CO2H at the meta position relative to the biaryl bond. The second ring is attached to the first at one carbon. Its neighbouring carbon bears OH, and the next carbon bears the NH–N= group. So the ring reads 1-aryl, 2-OH, 3-NH.

**Hydrazone and oxindole.** The NH is bonded to N, which is double-bonded to C3 of the oxindole. C2 is C=O, and N1 carries a 3,4-dimethylphenyl group. The fused benzene ring has F at the position para to C3a, which I placed as follows:
- C3 connects to C3a, and C3a is bonded to C4, which is the upper-right carbon.
- Going around the ring: C4, C5 (top right), C6 (right, bearing F), C7 (bottom), then C7a, which is bonded to N.
- F is on the carbon at the right, which is C6.

**N-aryl ring.** It is attached at the carbon bonded to N. Moving around the ring, the lower-right carbon bears a methyl (meta) and the next carbon bears another methyl (para), giving 3,4-dimethylphenyl.
