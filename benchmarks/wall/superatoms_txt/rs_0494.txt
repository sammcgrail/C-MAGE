I read the molecule as a 4-methoxy-3-acetoxypyridine-2-carboxamide whose amide nitrogen is attached to a nine-membered dilactone ring.

- **Pyridine ring:** the OMe is on C4, the acetate (OC(C)=O) on C3, and the carboxamide C(=O)N on C2.
- **Nine-membered ring**, counted atom by atom: the stereocentre CH, the upper lactone C=O, a ring O, CH2, the C(=CH2) carbon, CH2, the lower lactone C=O, a ring O, and a CH2 that closes back to the stereocentre.
- **Stereochemistry:** only the stereocentre carries a hashed bond, and it points to the NH on the left. The other substituents are the upper-right C=O and the lower-right CH2, and the implicit H points toward the viewer. Seen from the H side, N → C(=O) → CH2 runs clockwise. In the SMILES below, where N is written first and the ring closure carries the C=O, this corresponds to `@`.

SMILES: COc1ccnc(C(=O)N[C@H]2COC(=O)CC(=C)COC2=O)c1OC(C)=O
