The drawing shows a methanesulfonate ester, CH3-S(=O)(=O)-O-CH2-, attached to a benzene ring.

The benzene ring is fused to a seven-membered ring. Going around that ring: aromatic C, CH2, O, CH(Ar), O, CH2, aromatic C. That is a 1,5-dihydro-2,4-benzodioxepine, with the acetal carbon (the CH between the two oxygens) bearing a 4-nitrophenyl group.

The CH2OMs group sits on the benzene ring at the carbon next to the ring-fusion carbon on the upper side. The fused benzene carbons are the two at the lower left of the ring.

SMILES, written as the mesylate first:
CS(=O)(=O)OCc1ccc2COC(OCc2c1)c1ccc(cc1)[N+](=O)[O-]

Checking the ring atoms: c1 (bearing CH2OMs), c, c, c2 (fusion carbon), then C, O, C(Ar), O, C, c2 (the other fusion carbon), then c1 closes the benzene ring. The benzene ring therefore has the atoms c1, c, c, c2, c2', c, which makes six.

The substituent carbon is adjacent to the CH on the ring that connects to the fusion carbon. In the drawing, the substituent carbon (top) connects down-right to a CH and then to the fusion carbon at the upper right, and the left side has two CH atoms before reaching the lower fusion carbon. This matches the SMILES.
