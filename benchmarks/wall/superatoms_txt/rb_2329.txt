I read the molecule as a sulfonamide-backbone peptoid-like oligomer built from ten repeating units, N-acetylated at the left end and a primary amide at the right end. Each unit has the form NH–CH(side chain)–CH2–N(SO2R)–CH2–C(=O), with a side chain on the alpha carbon and a sulfonyl group on the backbone nitrogen.

**Units, left to right:**

| Unit | Side chain on CH | Sulfonyl group on N |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CH2CH2COOH (Glu-like) | SO2CH2CH2NH2 |
| 2 | (CH2)3-guanidine (Arg-like) | SO2CH3 |
| 3 | (CH2)3-guanidine | SO2CH3 |
| 4 | CH2CH(CH3)2 (Leu-like) | SO2CH2CH2NH2 |
| 5 | CH(OH)CH3 (Thr-like) | SO2CH2CH(CH3)2 |
| 6 | CH2CH(CH3)2 | SO2CH2CH2NH2 |
| 7 | (CH2)3-guanidine | SO2CH2CH(CH3)2 |
| 8 | CH3 (the starred carbon) | SO2CH3 |
| 9 | CH2CH(CH3)2 | SO2CH2CH(CH3)2 |
| 10 | CH2CH(CH3)2 | SO2CH2Ph, then CH2C(=O)NH2 |

**Stereochemistry:** Every alpha carbon has a bold wedge to its side chain, with the NH on the lower left and the backbone CH2 on the lower right. Working out the chirality from that geometry gives the same SMILES pattern as an L-amino acid, N[C@@H](R)C, so I used @@ at all ten alpha carbons. The Thr-like side-chain carbon bearing OH is drawn with plain bonds, so I left it unspecified.
