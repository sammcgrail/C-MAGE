I read this as a symmetric dimer: two identical bicyclic pyrrolo-fused diazocinone units joined through a benzene-1,3-disulfonyl linker.

Each half is built as follows:
- **Ring core:** A proline-derived pyrrolidine is fused through its nitrogen and its adjacent ring-fusion carbon to an eight-membered ring. The eight-membered ring contains the ring-fusion CH, two CH2 groups, a sulfonamide nitrogen, a CH2, an amino-bearing CH, and the lactam C=O on the pyrrolidine nitrogen.
- **Pyrrolidine substituent:** The pyrrolidine carbon next to the nitrogen carries a C(=O)NH–CH(Ph)2 (benzhydryl amide).
- **Amino side chain:** The amino-bearing CH carries NH–C(=O)–CH(CH3)–NH–CH3, an N-methylalanine amide.
- **Linker:** The sulfonamide nitrogen of each half is bonded to one SO2 group of the central benzene ring, with the two SO2 groups meta to each other.

The right half is a mirror reflection of the left in the drawing, with wedge and hash bonds swapped. I therefore treated the two halves as having the same configuration at each centre and wrote them with equivalent CIP descriptors.

I could not read every wedge and hash with certainty, so the stereodescriptors are partly assumed. I used the natural-type configuration: S at the alanine carbon, the amino-bearing carbon, the proline-type carbon and the ring-fusion carbon.
