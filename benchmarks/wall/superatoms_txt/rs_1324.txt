The molecule has four parts: a biphenyl carrying a sulfonamide, an amide, a fluorophenyl ring, and an aminoisoquinoline attached through an ether oxygen.

**Biphenyl part.** The top-left ring carries SO2NH2 at the ortho position relative to the bond to the second ring. That second ring is para-substituted and bears the carbonyl. This gives NS(=O)(=O)c1ccccc1-c1ccc(cc1)C(=O)N...

**Fluorophenyl ring.** The amide N is attached to a ring carbon (C1). The adjacent carbon (C2) bears the O-aryl group. Going around the ring from C1, the top-right carbon (C3) is a CH, and F sits on the next carbon (C4). The ring continues through the bottom-right CH (C5) and the bottom CH (C6) back to C2.

The ring is drawn with N-bearing C1 at the left-top, then C3 at the top, F-bearing C4 at the right-top, C5 at the right-bottom, C6 at the bottom, and C2 (bearing O) at the left-bottom. So F is para to the O-bearing carbon and meta to the N-bearing carbon. As SMILES: c1(N)cc(F)ccc1O, i.e. N–c1cc(F)ccc1O–.

**Isoquinoline.** C1 carries NH2 and is bonded to N2. Then come C3 and C4, then C4a, the benzo ring carbons C5, C6, C7 and C8, and finally C8a. The ether oxygen is on C7, which is adjacent to C8, and C8 is adjacent to C8a, which is bonded to C1. In the drawing the O-bearing carbon connects to the top-left carbon of the benzo ring, which is bonded to the ring-fusion carbon next to C1, so this is C7.

SMILES for the isoquinoline: Nc1nccc2ccc(O...)cc12. Here c1(N) is C1, n is N2, c is C3, c is C4, c2 is C4a, then c is C5, c is C6, c(O) is C7, c is C8, and c12 is C8a. This is consistent with the drawing.
