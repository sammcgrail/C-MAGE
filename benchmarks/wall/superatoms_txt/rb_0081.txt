This is the astaxanthin bis(ascorbyl phosphate) diester, with each phosphate anionic. I read it in three parts.

**Core.** Each end is a 3-oxy-4-oxo-2,6,6-trimethylcyclohexene ring (ring numbering from the carbon bearing the O–P group) joined by a conjugated polyene chain. Counting along the chain from the left ring gives CH=CH, C(Me)=CH, CH=CH, C(Me)=CH, CH=CH, CH=C(Me), CH=CH, CH=C(Me), CH=CH, then the right ring. All of these double bonds are drawn trans, so I used the E geometry throughout. The ring carbons bearing the phosphate oxygen are drawn with plain bonds, so they carry no stereo.

**Linkers.** Each ring C–O is bonded to a phosphate, P(=O)(O⁻), which is bonded through the oxygen at C6 of an ascorbic acid unit.

**Ascorbyl units.** Each is a lactone ring with a 2,3-enediol, a ring CH at C4 and a CH(OH) at C5. Both units are drawn with wedges and hashes, so I assigned them the stereochemistry of L-ascorbic acid and wrote both halves the same way: OC[C@H](O)[C@H]1OC(=O)C(O)=C1O.
