The drawing shows two substituted benzene rings joined by a thioether–propyl–ether linker, with a butanoic acid chain on the right ring.

**Left ring.** Going around the ring from the top:
- C1 carries OH.
- C2 carries nPr (propyl).
- C3 carries S.
- C4 and C5 are CH.
- C6 carries Ac (acetyl), and is adjacent to C1.

So the left ring is a 2-hydroxy-3-propyl-4-thio-substituted acetophenone: C(C)(=O)c1ccc(S...)c(CCC)c1O.

**Linker.** S–CH2CH2CH2–O, three carbons, with the O attached to the right ring.

**Right ring.** Going around the ring:
- Ca carries the O of the linker (lower left).
- Cb carries nPr (bottom).
- Cc carries O–(CH2)3–CO2H.
- Cd carries Ac.
- Ce and Cf are CH.

The ring order is Ca (O-linker), Cb (nPr), Cc (O-butanoic), Cd (Ac), Ce (CH), Cf (CH), back to Ca.

The butanoic acid chain is O–CH2CH2CH2–CO2H, which reads as O, then three CH2 carbons, then COOH. In the drawing the carbons are at 1140,808, 1232,835 and 1253,930, followed by CO2H, so the chain is OCCCC(=O)O.
