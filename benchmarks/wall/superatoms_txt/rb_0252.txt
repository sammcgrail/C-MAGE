I read this as a rifamycin-type ansa macrolactam (rifamycin SV/S skeleton) in which the naphthoquinone core is fused to an N-methylphenazine ring system.

**Core:**
- The benzofuranone carries a methyl group on the quaternary carbon. That carbon is also bonded to the enol-ether O of the ansa chain.
- The adjacent aromatic ring has a methyl and an OH.
- The central six-membered ring has a C=O and a C–NH (the amide N).
- That ring is fused to a phenazine: N= on one side and N–CH3 on the other, with a phenolic OH on the benzo ring.

**Ansa chain, from the amide C=O:**
- C(=O)–C(CH3)=CH–CH=CH–CH(CH3)
- CH(OH)–CH(CH3)–CH(OH)–CH(CH3)
- CH(OAc)–CH(CH3)–CH(OMe)
- CH=CH–O, which returns to the quaternary carbon.

I took the stereochemistry (chain stereocentres, double-bond geometry, quaternary carbon) from the natural rifamycin S configuration rather than reading each wedge in the drawing. The ring closures and bond orders were checked by hand against the drawing.
