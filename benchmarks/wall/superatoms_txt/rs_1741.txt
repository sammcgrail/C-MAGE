The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an N-methyl isatin.

**Biphenyl part.** The upper-left ring carries CO2H. Its neighbouring ring carbon (meta to the acid) is bonded to the second ring. On that second ring, the carbon bearing the biaryl bond is next to the carbon bearing OH. The next carbon after that bears the HN group.

**Hydrazone part.** The chain is NH–N=C3, where C3 is the 3-position of an indolin-2-one. This ring has C2 as C=O, N1 carrying a methyl group, and a fused benzene ring.

**SMILES.** Writing the acid ring first: OC(=O)c1cccc(c1)-c2cccc(NN=C3C(=O)N(C)c4ccccc34)c2O

Checking the second ring: c2 (attached to the biaryl bond), c, c, c, c(N…), c2(O). The ring closure puts the OH carbon next to both the biaryl carbon and the NH-bearing carbon. This matches the drawing.

The drawing does not clearly fix the geometry of the C=N bond. It looks like the N is on the same side as the C=O carbon (Z), but I have not assigned stereochemistry.
