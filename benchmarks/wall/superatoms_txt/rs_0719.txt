The drawing shows a pyridine ring with these substituents:
- C2 carries the carboxamide, C(=O)NH–.
- C3 carries OH.
- C4 carries OMe.
- N1 is the ring nitrogen, at the lower left.

The amide nitrogen is bonded to a cyclopentanone ring at the carbon next to the ketone (position 3 of the cyclopentan-1-one, i.e. 2-oxocyclopentyl). That carbon is a stereocentre by connectivity but is drawn with plain bonds, so I give it no stereo.

Writing the pyridine as c1(C(=O)NC2CCCC2=O)nccc(OC)c1O:
- c1 is C2, bearing the amide.
- n is N1.
- cc are C6 and C5.
- c(OC) is C4, bearing OMe.
- c1O is C3, bearing OH, which closes the ring back to C2.

The cyclopentanone is written N–C2CCCC2=O. The attachment carbon is C2, followed by three CH2 groups, then the carbonyl carbon that closes the ring. This gives 5 ring atoms, as drawn.

SMILES: COc1ccnc(C(=O)NC2CCCC2=O)c1O
