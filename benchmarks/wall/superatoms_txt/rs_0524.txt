I read the molecule as a 4-substituted benzoic acid joined through an ethylene linker to the nitrogen of a pyrrolidin-2-one, with an (E)-alkenyl side chain on the ring carbon next to nitrogen.

- **Lactam ring:** the five-membered ring has a C=O, an N and three CH2/CH carbons. The ring carbon next to N carries the side chain and is drawn with plain bonds, so I left it without stereo.
- **N-substituent:** N–CH2CH2–C6H4–CO2H, with the carboxylic acid para to the ethylene linker.
- **Side chain:** CH=CH is drawn trans (E). It leads to a CH2, then a quaternary carbon bearing OH, CH3 and an n-butyl group. Counting the zigzag at the end gives four carbons for the butyl, so the chain is an oct-1-enyl with 4-hydroxy-4-methyl.
- **Quaternary stereocentre:** the OH is on a hashed bond, so it points away from the viewer. The other three substituents run allyl CH2 (left), methyl (lower left), butyl (lower right), which is counterclockwise as seen from the front. Seen from the OH side this is clockwise. That gives O[C@@](allyl)(C)CCCC when O is written first. Writing the allyl carbon first swaps two neighbours and flips the tag to C[C@](O)(C)CCCC.

SMILES: OC(=O)c1ccc(CCN2C(=O)CCC2/C=C/C[C@](O)(C)CCCC)cc1
