I read the molecule as a 2-oxo-benzoxazol-6-yl group on the CH2 of an alanine-type chain. The α-carbon carries CO2H on a hashed bond and an NH that forms a urea with the nitrogen of a spiro piperidine–dihydroquinazolinone.

**Fragments**
- **Left ring system:** a 2-oxo-2,3-dihydrobenzoxazole, with O and NH in the five-membered ring and the C=O between them. It is attached through its benzene ring to a CH2.
- **Chain:** CH2–CH(CO2H)–NH–C(=O)–N. The carbonyl is a urea linking the α-nitrogen to the piperidine nitrogen.
- **Spiro system:** the piperidine's 4-position is the spiro carbon. It is also the C4 of a 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroquinazolin-2-one, bearing NH–C(=O)–NH fused to a benzene ring.

**Stereochemistry**
Only the α-carbon is drawn with a wedge or hash. Its CO2H is on a hashed bond (pointing back), with CH2 to the upper left and N below. The implicit H therefore points toward the viewer. Taking the CIP priorities N > CO2H > CH2 > H, the sequence N → CO2H → CH2 runs counterclockwise from the front. Because H faces the viewer, the true sense is clockwise, giving R. In the SMILES below, written as OC(=O)[C@@H](CH2Ar)N, the `@@` tag encodes this R configuration.

The spiro carbon has two identical piperidine arms, so it is not a stereocentre.
