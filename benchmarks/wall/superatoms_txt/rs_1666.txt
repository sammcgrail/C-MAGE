The image shows a three-component reaction (ethyl cyanoacetate + 3-nitrobenzaldehyde + S-methylisothiourea → XI), but the product XI is not drawn. The SMILES below is my inference of that product, not something read from the drawing.

I assumed a Biginelli-type condensation. The aldehyde condenses with the active methylene of ethyl cyanoacetate, and the isothiourea then adds and cyclises onto the ester with loss of ethanol. This gives a 5-cyano-6-oxo-tetrahydropyrimidine: 2-(methylthio)-4-(3-nitrophenyl)-6-oxo-1,4,5,6-tetrahydropyrimidine-5-carbonitrile.

An alternative pathway would keep the ester and cyclise onto the nitrile instead, giving ethyl 6-amino-2-(methylthio)-4-(3-nitrophenyl)-1,4-dihydropyrimidine-5-carboxylate. I chose the lactam product because the analogous reaction with thiourea is known to give the 5-cyano-6-oxo compound.

No stereochemistry is shown in the drawing, so none is included.
