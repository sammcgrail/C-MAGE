I read the molecule as a spiro compound: a phthalide (isobenzofuranone) joined at its spiro carbon to a P-methyl phosphine-oxide diaryl ring system (a phosphorus analogue of xanthene) carrying two acetate groups. An aza-crown ether is attached to the phthalide benzene ring.

- **Aza-crown:** Tracing the ring from the right-hand nitrogen gives N–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–N–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2 back to the first nitrogen. That is a 15-membered ring with three oxygens and two nitrogens. The left-hand nitrogen carries a 2-methoxyphenyl group, and the right-hand nitrogen is bonded to the phthalide ring.
- **Phthalide ring:** Its benzene ring carries the methoxy group and the crown nitrogen on adjacent carbons. The lactone C=O sits on one ring-fusion carbon, and the spiro carbon (bonded to the lactone ring oxygen) sits on the other.
- **Spiro P-oxide system:** The spiro carbon bonds to two aryl rings that are bridged by P(=O)CH3. Each aryl ring has an acetoxy group para to the carbon attached to the spiro centre, and so meta to the carbon bearing phosphorus.
