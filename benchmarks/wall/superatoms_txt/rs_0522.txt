The drawing shows a quinoline core with these substituents:

- **C4:** an NH linked to a pyridin-4-yl group.
- **C3:** fluorine.
- **C2:** a benzo[1,3]dioxol-5-yl group (the methylenedioxyphenyl ring).
- **C7:** a methoxy group.

Quinoline numbering: N1 is the ring nitrogen. C2 carries the benzodioxole and C3 carries F. C4 carries the NH and connects to C4a, which is part of the benzo ring. The benzo ring runs C4a, C5, C6, C7, C8, C8a, with C8a bonded to N1. In the drawing the MeO is on the lower-left carbon of the benzo ring, adjacent to the carbon (C8) that is bonded to C8a. That places the methoxy at C7.

Check of the quinoline portion: c1(...) is C2, n is N1, c2 is C8a, c is C8, c(OC) is C7, c is C6, c is C5, c2 is C4a (closing the benzo ring). Then c(N...) is C4 and c1F is C3, closing the pyridine ring.

Check of the aryl group: c2ccc3OCOc3c2 starts at the attachment carbon, then CH, CH, then C–O, the OCH2O bridge, C–O, and a closing CH. This gives the 3,4-disubstituted pattern of a benzodioxol-5-yl group.
