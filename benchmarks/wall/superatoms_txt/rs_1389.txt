The drawing shows a 3-hydroxy-4-methoxypyridine-2-carboxamide whose amide nitrogen is attached to a substituted cyclohexane, which carries an aryl ether.

**Pyridine ring (left).** The ring nitrogen is at the bottom left. C2 carries the C(=O)N group, C3 carries OH, C4 carries OMe, and C5 and C6 are plain CH.
SMILES for this part: COc1ccnc(C(=O)N...)c1O

**Cyclohexane ring (centre).** C1 bears the NH. Going around the ring:
- C2 is CH2.
- C3 is C(CH3)2, the quaternary carbon at the top.
- C4 is CH–O.
- C5 is C(CH3)2, at the bottom.
- C6 is CH2.

The ring is therefore N–C1H, CH2, C(Me)2, CH(OAr), C(Me)2, CH2, back to C1.
SMILES for this part: C1CC(C)(C)C(Oc...)C(C)(C)C1

**Pyridine ether (right).** The ether oxygen is attached to C2 of a pyridine, which is next to the ring nitrogen. Going round the ring: N1, C2 (O), C3 (CH), C4 (CF3), C5 (CH), C6 (CF3). This is a 4,6-bis(trifluoromethyl)pyridin-2-yl group.
SMILES for this part: c1cc(C(F)(F)F)cc(C(F)(F)F)n1 (written with the O attached at the first atom)

No wedges or hashes are drawn, so no stereochemistry is included.
