I read the molecule in four parts and then fixed the stereocentre.

1. **Left end:** a 2,6-dioxopiperidin-3-yl (glutarimide) ring is attached to the nitrogen of a phthalimide. The glutarimide carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so it gets no stereo label.
2. **Linker:** the phthalimide carries an O–CH2–C(=O)–NH group at the 4-position. This continues into a chain of six CH2 groups (counted vertex by vertex), ending at the nitrogen of a piperidine ring.
3. **Piperidine:** the ring carbon at position 3 bears an NH group on a bold wedge. The NH links to C2 of a pyrimidine. That pyrimidine has Cl at C5 and an indol-3-yl group at C4.
4. **Stereochemistry:** I assigned the wedged carbon by CIP priority. The substituents rank NH, then ring C2 (attached to N), then ring C4, then H. With the NH on the wedge, H points away from the viewer. Going NH → C2 → C4 on the page is clockwise, so the centre is R.

In the SMILES, the ring is written as N4CCC[C@@H](N…)C4. In that atom order the neighbours of the stereocentre are C4, H, N, C2, and `@@` encodes the R configuration.
