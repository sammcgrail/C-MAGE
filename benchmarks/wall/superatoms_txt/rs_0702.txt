The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an N-aryl isatin.

**Biphenyl part.** The top-left ring carries CO2H. A second ring is attached to it at the meta position. On that second ring, the carbon bearing the first ring sits next to the carbon bearing OH. The next carbon along carries the NH of the hydrazine.

**Hydrazone and isatin part.** The NH is linked by N= to C3 of the indolin-2-one. C2 is the C=O, and N1 carries a 4-isopropylphenyl group.

**Stereochemistry.** The C=N double bond is drawn with a specific geometry, so I assigned it from the image. The N= atom is at about (295, 400) and the C3 carbon is at about (325, 460). The NH is at the upper left of the N, at about (230, 380). On the C3 side, the substituents are C2 (the carbonyl, lower left, at about (290, 510)) and C3a (to the right, at about (440, 440)).

The C=N bond runs from N (295, 400) down to C3 (325, 460). The NH on N lies to the left of this axis. The C2 carbonyl on C3 also lies to the left, at x = 290. So NH and C2 are on the same side, which makes the C2 side Z with respect to the NH.

For CIP priorities, on C3 the carbonyl C2 (O, O, N) outranks C3a (C, C, C). On N, the NH outranks the lone pair. The higher-priority groups are on the same side, so the configuration is Z.
