The drawing shows a dibenz[b,e]oxepine tricycle with a piperidinylidene substituent and a 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid chain on the nitrogen.

**Tricycle.** One benzene ring (left) is bonded to the ring oxygen. The oxygen connects to a CH2, which connects to the second benzene ring (upper right). That ring carries Cl. The two benzene rings are also joined through the central exocyclic alkene carbon, giving the seven-membered ring: O, CH2, aromatic C, aromatic C, C(=), aromatic C, aromatic C.

**Chlorine position.** In the right ring, the carbon bonded to CH2 is the upper-left vertex. Going around the ring, the next vertex (top) is CH, and the Cl-bearing carbon is the vertex after that. So Cl is meta to the CH2-bearing carbon. The carbon attached to the alkene carbon is the lower-left vertex, which is adjacent to the CH2-bearing carbon in the ring. Cl is therefore para to the carbon bonded to the alkene carbon.

**Piperidine and side chain.** The alkene carbon is double-bonded to C4 of the piperidine. The nitrogen carries CH2–C(CH3)2–CO2H.
