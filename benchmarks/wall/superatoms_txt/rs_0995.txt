I read the molecule as three parts joined through a sulfonamide.

- **Indane acid:** a 2,3-dihydro-1H-indene ring (five-membered ring fused to benzene) with a carboxylic acid on C2 of the five-membered ring. The sulfonyl group sits on the benzene carbon next to the ring fusion (position 4).
- **Sulfonamide:** the S(=O)(=O) links that aromatic carbon to N1 of a piperazine.
- **Piperazine:** N1 carries the sulfonyl. The two carbons next to N1 each bear a methyl, and both methyls are drawn as wedges. The far nitrogen (N4) carries a CH2CH2 chain ending in a 4-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl ring.

**Stereochemistry:** only the two piperazine methyl-bearing carbons have wedges. Assigning CIP priorities from the drawing (N > CH2 > CH3 > H), the upper carbon is S and the lower carbon is R. That makes the 2,6-dimethyl pattern cis (meso-like). In the SMILES, `[C@@H]` gives S for the first centre and `[C@H]` gives R for the second. The indane C2 is drawn with plain bonds, so it has no stereo label.

SMILES: OC(=O)C1Cc2cccc(c2C1)S(=O)(=O)N1[C@@H](C)CN(CCc2ccc(cc2)C(F)(F)F)C[C@H]1C
