I read the structure in four parts: the xanthene dye, the benzoate ring, the diaza-crown macrocycle, and the methoxyphenyl group.

**Xanthene (rhodamine-type) core.** The top ring carries NMe2, the central ring has an O, and the right ring has a C=N+(Me)2 iminium. The C9 carbon links to the benzoate ring.

**Benzoate ring.** Going around from the carbon attached to C9 (call it C1):
- C1 bears the xanthene.
- C2 is a CH, drawn at the top left of this ring.
- C3 bears the macrocycle nitrogen.
- C4 bears OMe.
- C5 is a CH.
- C6 bears the carboxylate (COO⁻) and sits adjacent to C1.

**Macrocycle.** The ring contains two nitrogens and three oxygens. Starting at the N attached to the benzoate ring (N1):
- N1–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–N2, which covers the upper and left portion.
- N2 carries the 2-methoxyphenyl group.
- N2–CH2CH2–O–CH2CH2–N1 closes the ring at the bottom.

Reading the drawing, from N1 up and right the path is CH2CH2–O (top right), then CH2CH2–O (top left), then CH2CH2–N2. From N2 the bottom path is CH2CH2–O (bottom), then CH2CH2 back to N1. This gives 15 ring atoms: 2 N, 3 O and 5 × 2 carbons. That is a 15-membered ring, consistent with a triaza/diaza-crown (a 1-aza-15-crown-5 analogue with one O replaced by N-aryl).

Check on the xanthene: C1(=C2C=CC(=[N+](C)C)C=C2Oc2cc(N(C)C)ccc21). The C9 carbon is double-bonded to C2, a ring carbon of the right ring. In that ring C2 is followed by C=C, then C(=N+), then C=C2, which closes back to the ring-fusion carbon bearing O. This ring has six atoms: C2, C, C, C(=N), C, C2-closure. The C=C2 means the last atom is the other fusion carbon, which connects to O. The left ring is c2cc(NMe2)ccc21, with six atoms, ending at the carbon that closes to C9. This is consistent.
