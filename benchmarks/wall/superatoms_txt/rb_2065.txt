I read the molecule as an N-acetylated oligomer. Each repeat unit is NH–CH(R)–CH2–N(SO2R′)–CH2–C(=O)–, and the chain ends in a primary amide (C(N)=O).

**Sequence of units, left to right** (R = side chain on the carbon bearing the wedge, R′ = group on the sulfonyl):

| Unit | R (side chain) | R′ (on SO2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | CH2CH2COOH | CH2CH2NH2 |
| 2 | CH2CH2CH2NHC(=NH)NH2 | CH3 |
| 3 | CH2CH2CH2NHC(=NH)NH2 | CH3 |
| 4 | CH2CH(CH3)2 | CH2CH2NH2 |
| 5 | CH(OH)CH3 | CH2CH(CH3)2 |
| 6 | CH2CH(CH3)2 | CH2CH2NH2 |
| 7 | CH2CH2CH2NHC(=NH)NH2 | CH2CH(CH3)2 |
| 8 | CH(CH3)CH2CH3 | CH3 |
| 9 | CH3 (the starred carbon) | CH2CH(CH3)2 |
| 10 | CH2CH(CH3)2 | CH2Ph |

**Stereochemistry:**
- All ten α-carbons carry a bold wedge to the side chain, with NH on the left and CH2N on the right. Each is therefore written as `N[C@@H](R)CN`.
- The threonine-like CH(OH)CH3 carbon and the isoleucine-like CH(CH3)CH2CH3 carbon are drawn with plain bonds, so they get no stereo marking.
