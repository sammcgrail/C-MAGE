This is a STING agonist macrocycle of the kind found in the patent literature. I read it as two benzimidazole units joined by a butene linker on the N1 nitrogens and by a branched ether chain on the 7-oxygens.

**Top benzimidazole.** N1 carries the butenyl chain and C2 carries the NH–C(=O)–pyrazole group. N3 is the unsubstituted ring nitrogen. The carboxamide sits at C5 and the O-chain at C7.

**Pyrazole groups.** Each is 1-ethyl-3-methylpyrazole attached through its C5 to the carbonyl. In SMILES this is CCn1nc(C)cc1C(=O)N.

**Linker between the nitrogens.** N1(top)–CH2–CH=CH–CH2–N1(bottom). The double bond is drawn as cis or trans from the zigzag. The top CH2 goes down-left to a CH=, which then runs down to the next CH=, and then the CH2 goes down-left to the bottom N. This looks like a trans alkene, but the geometry is ambiguous, so I use the E form.

**Ether chain.** O(top)–CH2–CH(CH2CH2-morpholine)–CH2–O(bottom). The central CH carries a CH2CH2 chain ending at the morpholine N. It has no wedge, so no stereo is assigned.

**SMILES assembly.** The top benzimidazole is written as n1c(NC(=O)c2cc(C)nn2CC)n(C/C=C/C...)c3c(OC...)cc(C(N)=O)cc13. The macrocycle is closed with ring labels.

Top unit: `CCn1nc(C)cc1C(=O)Nc1nc2cc(C(N)=O)cc(OCC(CCN3CCOCC3)COc3cc(C(N)=O)cc4nc(NC(=O)c5cc(C)nn5CC)n(C/C=C/C5)c34)c2n1C5`

Checking the ring closures:
- Ring label 1 on the top benzimidazole (c1nc2...c2n1) closes at the last n1 before C5.
- The label 5 opens at the c5 of the second pyrazole, closes within that pyrazole (nn5), and is then reused for the linker. This is valid, since the first use closed before it was reopened.
- In `n(C/C=C/C5)c34`, the label 5 closes at the final `n1C5`, which bonds the CH2 to the top N1.
- Ring 3 is used by the morpholine and closed before `c3` is reused for the bottom benzene, so that is also fine.
- The top benzimidazole N1 is written as `n1C5` after `c2`, so its ring closure 1 matches the opening at the C2 carbon, and label 2 closes at c2.
