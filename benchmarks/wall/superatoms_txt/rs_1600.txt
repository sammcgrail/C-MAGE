Pyridine ring: N, C2 (carrying the carboxamide), C3 (OH), C4 (OMe), C5 and C6 (both CH).

Seven-membered ring: C1 (CH–NH), C2 (=C, with a methyl), C3 (=CH), C4 (=CH), C5 (CH=), C6 (C(CH3)2), C7 (CH2) back to C1.

Tracing the drawing: the CH bearing the N is bonded to the CH2 on its upper left. That CH2 connects to the quaternary C(Me)2 at the top. The quaternary carbon connects to a CH= on the right, which is double-bonded to the next CH. That CH connects to a CH= at the lower right, which is double-bonded to the C(Me) at the bottom, and that carbon bonds back to the CH–N.

So the double bonds are C(Me)=CH at the bottom and CH=CH on the right side. Two double bonds sit in the ring. Reading around from the quaternary carbon, the atoms are CH=CH–CH=C(Me), which gives the conjugated diene.
