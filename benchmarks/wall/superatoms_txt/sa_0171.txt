The drawing shows a camptothecin skeleton: a pyridone-fused lactone with a quaternary carbon bearing OH and ethyl, a fused quinoline, an OH on the benzo ring, and a TBS group on the carbon next to the benzo ring. This matches 7-TBS-10-hydroxycamptothecin.

**Substituents**
- TBS is tert-butyldimethylsilyl, `[Si](C)(C)C(C)(C)C`.
- Et is the ethyl group on the quaternary stereocentre, written `CC`.

**Stereochemistry**
- The only stereocentre is the quaternary carbon drawn with a wedge to Et.
- CIP priorities there are OH > lactone C(=O)O > ring C= > Et.
- Et is the lowest priority and points toward the viewer. OH → C(=O)O → ring C reads clockwise on the page, so the true sense is reversed, giving S. This is the natural camptothecin configuration.
- In the SMILES, `CC[C@@]1(O)C(=O)OC...` matches the neighbour order of the known (S)-camptothecin SMILES.

**Assembly**
- The lactone and pyridone part is `CC[C@@]1(O)C(=O)OCc2c1cc1n(c2=O)C...`.
- The CH2 then leads into the quinoline, which closes back on the pyridone-fused carbon: `Cc2c-1nc1ccc(O)cc1c2[Si](C)(C)C(C)(C)C`.
- The OH sits on the benzo carbon para to the ring-fusion carbon bonded to N's neighbour, and the TBS-bearing carbon is bonded to the other ring-fusion carbon, as drawn.
