This SMILES is my reading of the drawing: a bleomycin-type glycopeptide core carrying a five-residue peptoid chain and two Hoechst-type groups. I wrote it region by region and did not run it through a parser.

**Bleomycin core (bottom and right of the drawing)**
- The terminal group is H2N–CH(C(=O)NH2)–CH2–NH–, attached to the CH of the β-aminoalanine amide.
- That CH carries a CH2C(=O)NH2 side chain and sits on C2 of the pyrimidine. The pyrimidine has a 6-NH2, a 5-methyl and a 4-carboxamide.
- The carboxamide nitrogen joins the carbon that bears the imidazol-4-yl group and the glycosyl ether oxygen.
- Next come the methyl-hydroxy-valeryl unit and the threonine unit.
- These lead into the CH2CH2 link to the bithiazole.
- The disaccharide is a gulose-type ring whose C2 oxygen links to a carbamoyl-bearing second sugar. Both sugars have unspecified stereochemistry because no wedges are drawn on them.

**Linker and peptoid (middle of the drawing)**
- The bithiazole carboxamide connects through a CH2CH2CH2–NH–(CH2)4–NH– chain to the peptoid.
- The peptoid has five N-substituted glycine residues. Reading from the acetyl end, these are N(Ac)(CH2-triazole), three N-propyl residues, and a final N(CH2-triazole) residue.
- The last residue links back through CH2C(=O)NH to the butyl chain.

**Hoechst arms (top)**
- Each 1,4-disubstituted triazole carries N–CH2CH2CH2–NH–C(=O)CH2CH2CH2–O–C6H4.
- The aryl group is attached to a benzimidazole, which bears a 4-(4-methylpiperazin-1-yl)phenyl group.

**Stereochemistry**
- The wedged hydroxyl on the methyl-hydroxy-valeryl carbon is encoded as drawn.
- The glycosylated carbon and the terminal amino acid carbon are encoded as well. For the terminal carbon I assumed the L configuration.
