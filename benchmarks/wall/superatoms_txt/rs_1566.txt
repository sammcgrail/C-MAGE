The drawing shows a biphenyl carboxylic acid joined through a hydrazone to an N-aryl oxindole.

**Biphenyl part.** The top ring carries CO2H at the para position and is bonded to a second ring. On that second ring, the carbon next to the biaryl bond bears OH. The next carbon bears the NH of the hydrazone (HN–N=).

**Hydrazone and oxindole.** The chain is Ar–NH–N=C3, where C3 is the oxindole carbon. The oxindole has C2(=O) and N1, with N1 bearing a 4-(trifluoromethyl)phenyl group.

**Stereochemistry.** The drawing shows the C=N bond with a specific geometry. The NH sits at the lower left of N, and the N=C bond runs down and to the right. Going from the N, the NH substituent points to the left (up-left of the N–N bond). On the C3 end, the C2 carbonyl lies below-left and the aryl ring carbon C3a lies to the right. The NH is therefore on the same side as C2 (C=O), which is the usual Z arrangement stabilized by an N–H···O hydrogen bond.
