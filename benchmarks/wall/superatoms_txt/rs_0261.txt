The drawing shows an acrylic acid attached to a hydroxyphenyl hydrazone of an N-aryl isatin. I read it region by region.

**Acrylic acid chain.** CO2H sits on a carbon that also carries a methyl group. That carbon is double-bonded to a CH, which connects to the benzene ring. This gives HOOC–C(CH3)=CH–Ar.

The drawing shows the CH= on the left and the CO2H on top of the carbon bearing Me. The ring is attached at the lower left of the double bond, and the CO2H goes up from the right carbon. Methyl is to the right and down. On the left carbon (CH), the substituents are the ring (down) and H. On the right carbon, the substituents are CO2H (up) and Me (right-down). The ring and CO2H are therefore on opposite sides of the double bond (ring below-left, CO2H above), which is trans, so the aryl and COOH are E. In SMILES: OC(=O)/C(C)=C/Ar, where the COOH and the Ar are on opposite sides.

**Central benzene ring.** The ring carbon attached to the alkene is C1. Going around the ring, the next carbon (upper left) is CH, then C–OH (left), then C–NH (bottom), then CH, then CH. The OH carbon is meta to the alkene and the NH carbon is para to it.

**Hydrazone and oxindole.** The NH–N= unit connects to C3 of the oxindole. C3 is bonded to C2 (C=O), and C2 is bonded to N1, which carries the 3,5-dimethylphenyl group. The benzo ring bears CF3. Going round the benzo ring from C3a, the carbons are C4, C5, C6 (bearing CF3), C7, and C7a, which is bonded to N. The CF3 is drawn on the carbon that is para to C3a, which is C6.

The hydrazone C=N geometry is not clearly drawn as a defined stereo bond, so I leave it unspecified.
