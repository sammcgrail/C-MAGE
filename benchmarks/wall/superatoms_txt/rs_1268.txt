The drawing shows a phthalamic acid: a benzene ring carrying a CO2H group and, ortho to it, a C(=O)NH amide linked to a substituted aryl ring.

**Aryl ring.** The ring carbon attached to NH (call it C1) has these neighbours:
- At the top, a carbon bearing a methyl group (C2).
- At the bottom left, a carbon bearing a methyl group (C6).
- Going around from C2, the next carbon (C3) carries the pyrrolidin-1-yl group.
- The next carbon (C4) carries a methyl group.
- C5 is an unsubstituted CH.
- C6 carries a methyl group.

So the ring is 2,4,6-trimethyl-3-(pyrrolidin-1-yl)phenyl, attached through C1 to the amide nitrogen.

**SMILES.** Writing the phthalamic acid first and then the aryl ring in order C1 to C6:

OC(=O)c1ccccc1C(=O)Nc1c(C)c(N2CCCC2)c(C)cc1C

Check of the aryl part: c1 (C1, bonded to N), c(C) (C2, methyl), c(N2CCCC2) (C3, pyrrolidine), c(C) (C4, methyl), c (C5, H), c1C (C6, methyl, closing the ring). This matches the drawing.
