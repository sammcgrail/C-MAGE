The drawing shows "NC" on the left, joined by a bond to a zigzag chain of two CH2 carbons, which ends in SO2Me on the right.

The label is written "NC" with the N on the left and the C attached to the chain. Read literally, that is an isocyanide, with the carbon bonded to the CH2 and the N at the far end. This image resembles the Sigma-Aldrich drawing of 2-(methylsulfonyl)acetonitrile or 3-(methylsulfonyl)propionitrile, where the label would normally be NC, meaning a nitrile drawn with the N first.

Counting the chain: the bond from NC goes to a vertex, then to a second vertex, then to the S. That gives two CH2 carbons between the cyano group and the sulfone, so the nitrile carbon is a third carbon. The molecule is NC-CH2-CH2-SO2-CH3, which is 3-(methylsulfonyl)propanenitrile.

The label could also be read as an isocyanide, but a nitrile is the usual meaning of "NC" in such drawings, so I take it as the nitrile.
