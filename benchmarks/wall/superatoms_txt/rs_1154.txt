I read the molecule as a pyrazin-2(1H)-one core carrying three substituents.

- **Pyrazinone core:** the ring runs N1, C2(=O), C3, N4, C5(Cl), C6(H), with the C5=C6 double bond.
- **N1 substituent:** a CH bearing an ethyl group, a cyclopropyl group, the ring nitrogen and an explicit H on a wedge.
- **C3 substituent:** an indoline nitrogen. The indoline has bromine at C7, next to the ring fusion, and OMe at C5.

**Stereocentre:** the H is wedged toward the viewer, with ethyl at upper left, cyclopropyl at upper right and N below. The CIP priorities are N > cyclopropyl > ethyl > H. Viewed from the front, N → cyclopropyl → ethyl runs counterclockwise. Because H points toward the viewer, the true sense is reversed, so the centre is R.

In the SMILES below, the neighbour order at the stereocentre is ethyl, H, cyclopropyl, N, and `@` corresponds to R.

SMILES: CC[C@H](C1CC1)N2C=C(Cl)N=C(N3CCc4cc(OC)cc(Br)c34)C2=O
