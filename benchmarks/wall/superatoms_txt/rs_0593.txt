To assign chirality, I viewed the centre from the CHCl neighbour. The methyl is hashed, so it is behind the plane. The H is in front, so I took it as toward the viewer. The remaining neighbours then run H, CH(Me), methyl.

Working this through from the 2D geometry: with the H toward the viewer, the other three neighbours in the plane appear in this order:
- CHCl at upper right (angle about 40°),
- CH(Me) at left (about 165°),
- methyl at bottom (about 290°).

Going CHCl → CH(Me) → methyl is counterclockwise as seen from the front. Since H points toward the viewer, the sense seen from the side opposite H is clockwise.

SMILES order is CHCl, H, Me, CH(Me). Looking from CHCl, the sequence (H, Me, CH(Me)) must be assigned @ or @@. An equivalent way to see this is to permute the order to H first: (H, CHCl, CH(Me), Me) takes one swap from (CHCl, H, CH(Me), Me), and another swap gives (CHCl, H, Me, CH(Me)), so the parity is even overall. For the order (H, CHCl, CH(Me), Me), looking from H (which is toward the viewer), CHCl → CH(Me) → Me is counterclockwise, which is `@`. The parity is even, so the original order is also `@`.
