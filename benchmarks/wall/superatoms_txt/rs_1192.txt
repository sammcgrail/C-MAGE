I read the molecule in four parts and joined them through the central glutamic acid.

- **Left end:** a 2-sulfobenzaldehyde hydrazone, attached through N–NH to the 6-position of a nicotinoyl (pyridine-3-carbonyl) group.
- **Core:** the nicotinoyl amide nitrogen is on the α-carbon of a glutamic acid. Its stereochemistry is not drawn, so it gets no stereo label.
- **Top arm (from the γ-carbonyl):** NH–(CH2)3–C(=O), a 4-aminobutanoyl unit, then NH–CH(COOH)–CH2–NH–C(=O) to the 5-position of an indazole. The indazole N1 carries a –(CH2)3–NH–(1H-imidazol-2-yl) chain.
- **Bottom arm (from the α-carbonyl):** NH–(CH2)5–C(=O), a 6-aminohexanoyl unit, then the same diaminopropionic acid and indazole–propyl–aminoimidazole unit.

Both diaminopropionic acid centres have a wedge or hash on the CH2 bond. In each case I ranked the substituents N > COOH > CH2N > H and read the drawn direction. Both come out as S, so both are written as N[C@H](C(=O)O)CN.

The hydrazone C=N has no cis/trans geometry specified, so I left it without a label.
