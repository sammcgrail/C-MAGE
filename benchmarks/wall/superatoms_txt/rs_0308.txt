I read the molecule as a methanesulfonate ester of a 4-hydroxyphenyl ring, linked through an E-alkene and a CH2–NH bridge to a second para-substituted benzene ring. That ring carries a CH2–CH(OEt)–COOH chain.

**Fragments**
- Left end: CH3–S(=O)(=O)–O attached to a para-disubstituted benzene ring.
- Linker: the ring is joined to CH=CH–CH2–NH. The alkene is drawn trans, so I wrote it as E (`/C=C/`).
- Right ring: the NH is on one para position of a second benzene ring. The other para position carries CH2–CH(OEt)–COOH.

**Stereocentre**
The CH(OEt) carbon is the only stereocentre, and the OEt group is on a bold wedge, so the implicit H points away from the viewer. The CIP priorities are OEt > COOH > CH2Ar > H. Viewed from the front, OEt (up), COOH (lower right) and CH2Ar (lower left) run clockwise, which gives R.

In the SMILES `CCO[C@H](Cc1...)C(=O)O`, the neighbour order at the centre is O, H, CH2, COOH. Working through this order, `@@` encodes S, so `@` encodes R.

SMILES: CCO[C@H](Cc1ccc(NC/C=C/c2ccc(OS(C)(=O)=O)cc2)cc1)C(=O)O
