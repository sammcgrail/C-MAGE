The drawing shows a benzene ring with two OH groups and two identical tertiary alkyl groups, para-substituted.

Each substituent is a quaternary carbon bearing CH3, CH2CH3 (written as CH2CH3 or C2H5) and C2H5. That makes it a tert-amyl group, –C(CH3)(C2H5)2, i.e. 2-methylbutan-2-yl with the third arm written as C2H5. So the compound is 2,5-di-tert-amylhydroquinone.

Ring positions, going around the ring:
- C1 (top) carries OH.
- C2 (upper right) carries the tert-amyl group.
- C3 (lower right) carries H.
- C4 (bottom) carries OH.
- C5 (lower left) carries the tert-amyl group.
- C6 (upper left) carries H.

The OH groups are para (top and bottom). The alkyl groups are on the upper right and lower left, which are also para to each other. Each alkyl group is ortho to one OH. This gives the 2,5-pattern.
