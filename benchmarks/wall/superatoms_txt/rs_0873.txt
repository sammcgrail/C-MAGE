I read the structure in four pieces, then joined them.

- **Left end:** a 2-aminoimidazole joined through its NH to a three-carbon chain on N1 of an indazole.
- **Indazole core and linker:** the indazole carries a C5 carboxamide to the NH of a 2,3-diaminopropionic acid unit. Its α-NH is sulfonylated by a 2,6-dimethyl-4-alkoxyphenylsulfonyl group.
- **Aryl side chain:** the para oxygen carries –O(CH2)3C(=O)NH–CH2CH2–NH–, which links to a cysteic acid residue (CH2SO3H).
- **Chelator:** the cysteic acid α-NH is acylated by a DOTA-type macrocycle. This is a 12-membered ring with four nitrogens, three bearing CH2CO2H arms and the fourth bearing the CH2C(=O) link.
- **Salt:** two trifluoroacetic acid molecules.

**Stereochemistry:**
- The diaminopropionic acid carbon has a wedge to NH. By CIP priorities (N > COOH > CH2N) this is S, the L configuration. In the SMILES it is written `C(...)[C@H](N...)C(=O)O`.
- The cysteic acid carbon has a hashed bond to CH2. By CIP priorities (N > CH2SO3H > C(=O)N) this is R, the L configuration. In the SMILES it is written `C(=O)[C@H](CS(=O)(=O)O)N`.
