I read this as an antimycin-type nine-membered dilactone carrying a substituted pyridine amide.

**Pyridine part:** the ring has a nitrogen, a 2-carboxamide, a 3-OH and a 4-OMe, written `COc1ccnc(C(=O)N…)c1O`.

**Nine-membered ring**, going round from the amide-bearing carbon:
1. CH bearing the amide NH.
2. Ring CH2.
3. Ring O.
4. Lactone C=O.
5. CH bearing the benzyl group (CH2Ph).
6. CH bearing the cyclopropanecarboxylate ester, `OC(=O)C1CC1`.
7. CH bearing the methyl group.
8. Ring O.
9. Lactone C=O, which closes back onto the first CH.

**Stereochemistry:** there are four stereocentres, each assigned from its wedge or hash, with the implicit H taking the opposite side. Working through each centre gave:
- the amide-bearing CH: `@`
- the benzyl-bearing CH: `@`
- the ester-bearing CH: `@@`
- the methyl-bearing CH: `@`

SMILES: COc1ccnc(C(=O)N[C@H]2COC(=O)[C@H](Cc3ccccc3)[C@@H](OC(=O)C3CC3)[C@H](C)OC2=O)c1O
