The drawing shows a 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroquinoline. Its nitrogen carries a 3-bromobenzyl group, and a CF3 group sits on the benzo ring.

**Ring positions.** The saturated ring contains N, then CH2 (C2), CH2 (C3) and CH2 (C4). C4 joins the aromatic carbon C4a. C8a is the aromatic carbon bonded to N.

**Locating the CF3.** Going around the benzo ring from C8a (bonded to N), the next carbon is C8, an unsubstituted CH at the lower right of the junction. Then comes C7, which bears the CF3, and then C6 and C5. The CF3 sits on the bottom carbon next to the CH that is adjacent to C8a, so it is at position 7.

**Benzyl group.** The nitrogen is attached to CH2, which is attached to a phenyl ring. Counting from the attachment carbon (C1), the ring goes up to C2 (upper right), then C3 (right), which bears the Br. The Br is therefore meta, giving a 3-bromobenzyl group.

**SMILES.** Writing the tetrahydroquinoline as N1CCCc2ccc(C(F)(F)F)cc12 puts the CF3 carbon next to the CH that bonds to C8a, which is position 7. Adding the benzyl group on N gives:

SMILES: FC(F)(F)c1ccc2CCCN(Cc3cccc(Br)c3)c2c1
