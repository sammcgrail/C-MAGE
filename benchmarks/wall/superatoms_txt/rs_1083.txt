The drawing shows a carbazole core with three substituents.

**Substituents**
- The carbazole nitrogen carries a methyl group (N-methyl).
- One benzo ring carries a CH=N–N(Ph)2 group, a diphenylhydrazone.
- The other benzo ring carries an N(ethyl)(benzyl) group.

**Ring positions**
- The upper ring has N attached at its upper-left carbon (C8a). The CH=N substituent sits on the upper-right carbon, which is para to the carbon bearing N. In carbazole numbering that is C3.
- The lower ring has the amine substituent para to the carbon bearing N, which is C6.
- This gives a 3,6-disubstituted 9-methylcarbazole.

**Upper ring atom by atom**
- C8a (bonded to N) → C(upper, top-left) → C(top-right) → C(bearing CH=N) → C(lower right, CH) → C4a (the ring-fusion carbon), which bonds to C4b.
- The vertices run: N-bonded carbon, CH, CH, C(CH=N), CH, then the fusion carbon. So the CH=N carbon is meta to the fusion carbon C4a and para to the N-bearing carbon, which is C3 as stated.
