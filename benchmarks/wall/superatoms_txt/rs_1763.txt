I read the molecule as an antimycin-type nine-membered dilactone carrying three substituents.

**Pyridine fragment:** The ring has N, C2 bearing the carboxamide (C(=O)NH–), C3 bearing OH, C4 bearing OMe, then C5 and C6 as CH.

**Nine-membered ring**, going around from the amide-bearing carbon:
1. CH–NH, the amide nitrogen on a hashed bond.
2. C(=O), the upper-left lactone carbonyl.
3. O.
4. CH–CH3, with the methyl on a wedge.
5. CH–O–C(=O)C(CH3)=CH2, the methacrylate ester on a hashed bond.
6. CH–CH2Ph, the benzyl on a wedge.
7. C(=O), the lower lactone carbonyl.
8. O.
9. CH2, which closes back to the CH–NH.

**Stereochemistry:** I worked out each of the four stereocentres from its drawn wedge or hash and the implicit hydrogen. This gave @ for the CH–N carbon, @ for the CH–benzyl carbon, @@ for the CH–O-methacrylate carbon, and @ for the CH–CH3 carbon, as written in the SMILES below.
