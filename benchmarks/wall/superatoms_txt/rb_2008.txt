I read the drawing as a cyclic lipopeptide: a 3-aminotetradecanoic acid residue closes the ring together with seven amino acids.

**Lipid chain.** Counting the zigzag from the left end gives 11 carbons up to the carbon that bears the NH, then CH2, then C=O. That makes 14 carbons in total, so the lipid is a 3-aminotetradecanoic acid. Its NH is acylated by Thr. Its C=O is joined to the NH of the first Asn. No wedge is drawn at C3, so I gave it no stereo.

**Ring sequence.** Going around from the lipid C=O, the residues are:
- Asn (CH2C(=O)NH2 side chain)
- Tyr (4-hydroxybenzyl side chain)
- Asn (second one)
- Pro (the Asn C=O attaches to the proline N)
- Glu (CH2CH2COOH side chain)
- Ser (CH2OH side chain)
- Thr (CH(OH)CH3 side chain), whose C=O closes the ring onto the lipid NH.

**Stereochemistry.** I checked the first Asn from its drawn bonds and found it is S, as in L-Asn. I assumed the other residues are also natural L-amino acids, including L-Thr (2S,3R). For these I did not check the wedges or hashes individually.
