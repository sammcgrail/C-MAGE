The image shows a benzene ring with three substituents:
- a CN group at the upper right, on the ring carbon at the right of the top edge
- a Cl at the bottom right, on the carbon adjacent to the CN carbon
- a plain line at the left, on the ring carbon para to the CN carbon

The line at the left has no label, so it is a bond to an attachment point. I treat it as a methyl group, which is the usual convention for an unlabeled stub. This is a guess, since the stub could also mark a connection point to a larger structure.

The ring carbons are:
- C1 bears CN
- C2 bears Cl
- C3 is CH
- C4 bears the stub, para to C1
- C5 is CH
- C6 is CH

Writing the SMILES with the stub as a methyl group: N#Cc1ccc(C)cc1Cl
