I read the molecule as a pentapeptide-like chain, built from the N-terminus to the C-terminus:

1. **N-terminal residue:** H2N–CH(CH2Ph)–C(=O)–. The NH2 is on a wedge. With H pointing away and priorities N > C(=O) > CH2Ph, the centre is R, so this is D-Phe.
2. **Second residue:** –NH–CH(CH2Ph)–C(=O)–. The benzyl group is on a wedge. The N → C(=O) → CH2 order runs clockwise with H at the back, so it is R (D-Phe).
3. **Third residue:** –NH–CH(iBu)–C(=O)–. The isobutyl group (the "iBu" label) is on a wedge. This centre is R, so it is D-Leu.
4. **Fourth residue:** –NH–CH((CH2)4NH2)–C(=O)–. The chain is a four-carbon linker to NH2, drawn on a wedge. This centre is R, so it is D-Lys.
5. **C-terminal cap:** the Lys carbonyl is acylated onto the nitrogen of a piperidine ring. The ring carbon opposite that nitrogen carries both NH2 and CO2H, so this is 4-aminopiperidine-4-carboxylic acid. That carbon is not a stereocentre, and the plain bonds give it no stereo label.

All four stereocentres are R. For the pattern N[C@H](R)C(=O)…, where the preceding atom is the nitrogen, @ corresponds to R, so each centre is written `[C@H]`.
