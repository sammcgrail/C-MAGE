I read the molecule in three parts and joined them into one SMILES.

- **Left end:** a 6-(trifluoromethyl)picolinamide whose amide N is attached to C6 of a benzothiazole. C5 of the benzothiazole carries a C(CH3)2OH group.
- **Centre:** C2 of the benzothiazole is bonded to a 1,4-disubstituted cyclohexane. The cyclohexane's other end carries a CH2 that leads to the nitrogen of a piperidine. The 4-position of that piperidine carries a CH2CH2NH chain.
- **Right end:** the NH is attached to C4 of a phthalimide (isoindoline-1,3-dione). The phthalimide nitrogen is bonded to C3 of a 2,6-dioxopiperidine, which is a thalidomide-type group. That carbon is drawn with plain bonds, so I gave it no stereo.

On the cyclohexane, one substituent is on a wedge and the other on a hash, which means the 1,4-substituents are trans. I wrote that as `[C@H]...[C@H]` in the order used in the SMILES. I checked this against the known trans-1,4-dimethylcyclohexane pattern and accounted for the different neighbour order at the second stereocentre.
