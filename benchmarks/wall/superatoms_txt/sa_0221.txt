The drawing shows acenocoumarol, with one oddity: the side chain is labelled "Ac" attached to a CH2.

The label "Ac" means acetyl, CH3C(=O)–. In real acenocoumarol the side chain is CH2C(=O)CH3, so the CH2 attached to Ac here would give CH(Ar)–CH2–C(=O)CH3. That matches acenocoumarol and is consistent with the drawing.

Reading the structure:
- The coumarin core is a 4-hydroxy-2-oxo-chromene: O=C1Oc2ccccc2C(O)=C1.
- C3 of the coumarin carries a CH.
- That CH carries a 4-nitrophenyl group and a CH2–Ac group.
- Expanding Ac gives CH2C(=O)C.

The central CH is a stereocentre by connectivity, but it is drawn with plain bonds, so I give no stereo.

SMILES: CC(=O)CC(c1ccc([N+](=O)[O-])cc1)C1=C(O)c2ccccc2OC1=O
