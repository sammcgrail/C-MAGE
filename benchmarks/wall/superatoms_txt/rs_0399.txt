The drawing shows a pyridine ring with four substituents, which I read as follows:

- The ring nitrogen is at the bottom.
- C2 carries the carboxamide, C(=O)NH–.
- C3 carries the acetoxy group, OC(C)=O.
- C4 carries the methoxy group, OMe.
- C5 and C6 are plain CH.

The amide nitrogen is attached to a cyclohexane ring:

- C1 is the CH bearing the NH.
- Going up the ring from C1 gives a CH2, then C3, which carries both a methyl and a cyclopropyl group.
- Going down the ring from C1 gives a CH2, then a C(CH3)2 carbon.
- A CH2 between C3 and the C(CH3)2 carbon completes the six-membered ring.

The cyclohexane is therefore 3-cyclopropyl-3,5,5-trimethylcyclohexyl.
