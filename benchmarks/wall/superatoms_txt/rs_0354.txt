I read the molecule as an N-acyl glucosamine: a 3-hydroxy-4-methoxypyridine-2-carboxamide joined through the amide nitrogen to C2 of a pyranose ring. The ring OH at C1 is hashed, so I take it as the α-anomer.

**Aromatic part.** The ring is a pyridine. Starting from the ring nitrogen, C2 carries the carboxamide C(=O)N, C3 carries OH, and C4 carries OMe. The remaining two ring carbons are unsubstituted.

**Sugar ring.** It is a six-membered pyranose with ring oxygen at the bottom. The substituents and drawn bond types are:
- C1: OH, hashed
- C2: NH, hashed
- C3: OH, wedged
- C4: OH, hashed
- C5: CH2OH, wedged

**Stereo assignment.** I worked out each centre from the drawn geometry. At each centre I took the angular order of the three drawn neighbours as seen from the front, and I treated the implicit H as pointing opposite the wedged or hashed substituent. I then converted that to the SMILES @/@@ symbol for the written neighbour order. The result is O[C@H]1O[C@H](CO)[C@@H](O)[C@H](O)[C@H]1N…, with the amide written as NC(=O)c1nccc(OC)c1O.
