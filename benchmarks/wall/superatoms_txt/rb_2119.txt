I read the structure as a head-to-tail thioether-cyclised peptide and wrote it residue by residue, going around the ring.

**Sequence.** A chloroacetyl-derived carbonyl (the CH2–S thioether) bridges the N-terminal Phe to a Cys. Starting from that carbonyl and moving through the ring:

1. Phe, which closes back to the first carbonyl.
2. Arg.
3. N-Me-Phe.
4. Ser.
5. Lys.
6. Asn.
7. Tyr.
8. Sar (N-methylglycine).
9. Arg.
10. Asp.
11. Tic (tetrahydroisoquinoline-3-carboxylic acid).
12. Asn.
13. N-Me-Phe.
14. Cys, which carries the thioether S and a C-terminal Gly amide (NH–CH2–C(=O)NH2).

**Stereochemistry.** I assigned each α-carbon from its wedge or hash and the positions of its neighbours. I used the rule that, with H toward the viewer, CO→R→N clockwise means L. Every residue comes out as the L configuration, including Cys, which is the L form (R by CIP).

The wedge/hash on the left-hand Asn (between Tyr and Lys) was hard to make out in the image. I assumed it is hashed, which gives L like the other residues.

**Checks.** Ring-closure digits are opened and closed correctly, with the macrocycle on 1, the Tic ring on 2 and 3, and the phenyl rings reusing 2 after it is closed. Both Arg side chains end in a full guanidine (NC(N)=N).
